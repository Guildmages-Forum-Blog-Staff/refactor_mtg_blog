Murders at Karlov Manor (MKM) will hold its prerelease events on February 2, 2024, and be officially released a week later on February 9, 2024. Want to learn more about how the cards rate in Limited and some potentially fun synergies? Then check out this MKM Limited review series!

When it comes to Ravnica, the plane is best known not only as the second most frequently revisited plane after Dominaria, but also for its ten guilds. The guild names are now widely used as shorthand for two-color pairs in MTG, so here is a quick refresher:

- Azorius - **<font color="#023FFD">Blue</font><font color="#FED761">White</font>**
- Dimir - **<font color="#023FFD">Blue</font>Black**
- Rakdos - **<font color="#FD0214">Red</font>Black**
- Gruul - **<font color="#FD0214">Red</font><font color="#0A883B">Green</font>**
- Selesnya - **<font color="#FED761">White</font><font color="#0A883B">Green</font>**
- Boros - **<font color="#FD0214">Red</font><font color="#FED761">White</font>**
- Orzhov - **Black<font color="#FED761">White</font>**
- Golgari - **Black<font color="#0A883B">Green</font>**
- Simic - **<font color="#023FFD">Blue</font><font color="#0A883B">Green</font>**
- Izzet - **<font color="#FD0214">Red</font><font color="#023FFD">Blue</font>**

Karlov, the set's namesake, is a famous and prominent family in the Orzhov Syndicate, and the family that Teysa Karlov, a character we know well, comes from. The set revolves around a series of murders at Karlov Manor. Besides a story with a strong whodunit flavor, it brings back mechanics like investigate and Clue tokens, and introduces new ones such as the Case enchantment subtype, suspect, collect evidence, disguise, and cloak.

For the mechanics, see this article: [Murders at Karlov Manor Mechanics Explained](https://guildmagesforum.tw/MKM-mechanism/)

## Grading Scale

5.0: Must-take, unconditionally
4.0: First-pick quality
3.5: Strong enough, but not necessarily a first pick
3.0: Above average (you will really want it in your final deck)
2.5: Average, maybe around a 6th or 7th pick in a draft
2.0 to 1.0: Below average (you would rather not play it than play it)
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable (basically never play it, it will only help you lose)

The legendary veteran Agrus Kos, who still can't retire even after death, is moonlighting as a detective this time! An absurd 8/4 body for four mana, paired with its effect that when it is blocked, it comes back and fights again; when your board is ahead, you can even add the seven-mana ability so the Mole God must be blocked this turn, giving you a chance to attack again and again, rapidly lowering your opponent's defenses and life total. If you can make the Mole God indestructible and forced to be blocked, couldn't you attack until the end of time? Awesome!

OK, let's be serious. Basically, when the opponent's defensive creatures are too weak to kill the god, it is extremely painful for them: either don't block and take 8, which is no fun, or block, fail to kill it, and keep getting punched again and again. I recommend that at the start of combat you first attack with the Mole God and your other creatures that can attack profitably. If the opponent blocks your Mole God, your remaining weaker creatures can follow up with a second wave or more. If they don't block, you at least deal 8 damage to the opponent.
