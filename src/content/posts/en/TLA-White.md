## Rating Scale

5.0: Unconditional first pick, brings an almost game-winning advantage
4.5: Unconditional first pick in the right colors, brings a huge advantage
4.0: First-pick value, extremely synergistic with the color pair's theme, can be the deciding card for splashing, switching colors, or entering a color pair
3.5: Top uncommons and commons of the set and other sufficiently strong cards, synergistic with the color pair's theme, can be a key card for entering a color pair
3.0: Important cards for a color pair's theme, plus other above-average cards you want in your final deck
2.5: Average, usually a decent curve filler
2.0 to 1.0: Below average, generally leave out, can be used as filler when necessary but not too many
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable, basically don't play it, playing it only helps you lose

## Conclusion

White has two main keywords: **Airbend & Ally**

However, the application of airbend is actually quite tricky. After all, when used to exile opponents' permanents, it doesn't clean up the problem, so in the end you still need to watch whether you have enough permanents suited to being cloudshifted.

Allies, on the other hand, are very simple and powerful. This set has an extremely high concentration of Ally creatures, and many regular curve creatures carry the Ally type, so when you see cards with Ally tribal payoffs you can use them with relative peace of mind. When playing white, there should be no shortage of Allies.
