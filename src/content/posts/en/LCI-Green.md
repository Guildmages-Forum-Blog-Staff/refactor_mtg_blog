The Lost Caverns of Ixalan (LCI) prerelease events are held on November 10, 2023, with the official release one week later on November 17, 2023. Want to know more about individual card ratings in Limited and some potentially fun synergies? Then check out this LCI Limited guide series!

## Rating Scale

5.0: Unconditional must-pick
4.0: First-pick quality
3.5: Strong enough, but not necessarily a first pick
3.0: Above average (you will really want it in your final deck)
2.5: Average, maybe around the 6th or 7th pick in a draft
2.0 to 1.0: Below average (you would rather not play it than play it)
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable (basically don't play it, it will only help you lose)

![209](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/9/e/9ea7e323-f329-4928-b25a-c0b44c5ac058.jpg?1699044480)

**Jru**: 2.5

A card whose focus is easily thrown off by the high-power, low-toughness vigilance. After testing, a three-mana creature that explores on entering is very solid, but this is an evaluation based not on offense but on defense. Many people's first reaction to this card is probably that a 3/1 or 4/2 with vigilance kicking out will just trade with a bear, looking like a terrible loss. From an aggressive angle that's indeed true, but the green creatures in this set aren't designed to attack aggressively on turns two and three to race for life; the main source of power is the strong creatures from four mana onward, so the early requirement isn't to race for damage but to stabilize the board first. A 3/1 or 4/2 body going out will naturally easily be traded down with, but conversely it can also easily trade up when blocking.

So the correct role for this card is to explore once on entering to improve the stability of later operation, and after that it is a suicide squad that tries to get even or favorable trades. Don't underestimate that one explore; early explore contributes quite a bit to stabilizing the midgame. As for vigilance, treat it as a free bonus, occasionally letting you get in a free punch or two. If the creatures the opponent can block with at that moment are all worth more than this one, charge forward with confidence thanks to vigilance: if the opponent doesn't block, it's free damage, and if they block, it is still a favorable trade. Of course this fragile body dooms the score from being very high; at most it is playable, but it isn't as weak as it looks on the surface.

This set has a total of 13 lands that enter tapped, 8 of which are Caves, and there are 13 Caves in total that can trigger the life gain on entering. In addition, this card gives no help to combat on the board (a more extreme example is slamming it on a standing creature to help defend, but that situation is a bit rare). I don't recommend it before your own strategy is established. In short, it is a support card that depends heavily on the deck.
