## Before We Start

Golgari midrange has the best removal and creatures in the Standard card pool <font color="#AAAAAA">(BLB even added a Vraska + green Talent instant-kill combo)</font>, and has been one of the pillars of Standard in recent years. However, the mainstream Golgari midrange in the meta is a proactive deck that emphasizes attacking tempo, while the author prefers a slower build that values long-game resource battles. The two share quite a few powerful cards, but the underlying logic of the game plan is completely different. Conveniently, the new DSK cards include quite a few resource-engine cards that make good use of the graveyard. They struggle to have an immediate impact on the board in a short tempo battle, but they are well suited to creating enormous card advantage in a long resource battle, so I built this Golgari resource-battle deck on that foundation.

## Deck Introduction

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6684229#paper)
![List](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Golgari-Selfmill/7bd9bb409f5a.webp)

The whole deck runs on one core idea: **"card advantage"**. The main game plan is to keep drawing cards and trade resources with the opponent until the opponent's resources run dry.

Since the game plan assumes a long game, and some resource cards take a while to pay off, you will mostly be on the defensive early on. The first priority is to make sure your line isn't broken through and to drag the game safely into the mid and late game, which is when this deck truly kicks in.

But exactly when to defend and when to attack still depends on the opponent's deck and the state of the board. Although this deck can beat most opponents in a resource battle, there are always more extreme decks, and then you need to try to apply pressure early. And unlike control, many of our creatures are powerful cards that give you tempo when you want tempo and resources when you want resources; the same creature can switch freely between offensive and defensive logic depending on how you play it.

## Main Deck Breakdown

### The Say Its Name Package
![Say Its Name package](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Golgari-Selfmill/3b632bc562f7.webp)
[Say Its Name](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/197/say-its-name) (4) + [Altanak, the Thrice-Called](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/166/altanak-the-thrice-called) (1): this is actually the author's personal driving motivation for building this deck. I just wanted to play this Say Its Name package. Even among regular two-mana looting spells, Say Its Name is among the stronger ones. Note that after milling, you pick any creature or land card from the graveyard to put into your hand; there is no target when you cast it, and the card you recover isn't limited to the three you milled, which contributes a lot to the deck's consistency. As for exiling three Say Its Name from the graveyard to drop in Altanak, the Thrice-Called, that gives this looting system an extra ceiling. Although Altanak can also be dealt with by a single removal spell, it can be considered an almost free creature, and it draws a card when targeted, so sometimes just looting will inexplicably freeload a +2 card advantage. Don't forget this is a self-mill deck, so it keeps filling the graveyard anyway; in testing, gathering three Say Its Name in the graveyard is actually not hard, and because lands often end up in the graveyard, even if Altanak is in your hand you usually don't need to worry about it clogging your hand. And of course, if you're lucky enough, you have a chance to turn the board into this on turn two...

![Turn two](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Golgari-Selfmill/1840685cf2a1.webp)

However, Say Its Name & Altanak are also often the cards swapped out most in sideboarded games. At their core they are just a looting spell and its companion, support spells, and even removing them doesn't affect the original game plan. When you need to keep the main-deck removal and bring in more cards that hate on the opponent, they are always the first to give up their slots.

### Resource Engines

{% mtgcard "Overlord of the Balemurk" %}
[Overlord of the Balemurk](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/113/overlord-of-the-balemurk) (3): Overlord's graveyard-filling efficiency and card advantage need little introduction. It gains value just by entering, and if you let it live to attack and gain value again, you've usually won the game; the opponent can probably never catch up to your resource count.

{% mtgcard "Honest Rutstein" %}
[Honest Rutstein](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/207/honest-rutstein) (2): The real MVP of the deck. It often recurs Overlord or another Rutstein over and over, and almost every opponent who has experienced it has given an extremely irritated review. In addition, this deck has quite a few creature cards, so the cost reduction often lets you suddenly go off and flood the board. Don't forget that when Overlord is cast with impending it is still a creature spell, so it benefits from the cost reduction.

{% mtgcard "Bristlebud Farmer" %}
[Bristlebud Farmer](https://scryfall.com/card/big/17/bristlebud-farmer) (3): A powerful attacker and resource engine, and again one you almost can't let live to attack. Once it attacks successfully, it easily starts a resource chain. Note that this guy's recursion wording differs from Overlord's and Say Its Name's: it picks one permanent card from the three milled cards to put into your hand. Because of Bristlebud Farmer, some of the removal spells chosen are cards whose type is permanent.

### Other Creatures

{% mtgcard "Mosswood Dreadknight // Dread Whispers" %}
[Mosswood Dreadknight // Dread Whispers](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/231/mosswood-dreadknight-dread-whispers) (4): The top two-drop in Golgari; however you build, you can hardly do without it. When using it, don't be overly fixated on the standard procedure of going on an adventure and then casting it from exile. Playing it straight out at the right time sometimes lets you control the pace of the game.

{% mtgcard "Glissa Sunslayer" %}
[Glissa Sunslayer](https://scryfall.com/card/one/202/glissa-sunslayer) (2): The standard Golgari three-drop king.

{% mtgcard "Titania, Voice of Gaea" %}
[Titania, Voice of Gaea](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/193/titania-voice-of-gaea) (1): A somewhat toy-like free slot. Its back half, [Argoth, Sanctum of Nature](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/256a/argoth-sanctum-of-nature), is naturally included too, but their fusion is not the theme of this deck, just a small side combo, so don't be overly fixated on it. Because of the frequent self-milling, Titania can gain quite a lot of life when on the battlefield, and for a long-game deck life is an important asset. A three-mana 3/4 reach is also a decent body, and it can intercept flyers such as [Slickshot Show-Off](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/146/slickshot-show-off), [Faerie Mastermind](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/58/faerie-mastermind), [Deep-Cavern Bat](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/102/deep-cavern-bat), and [Zoraline, Cosmos Caller](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/242/zoraline-cosmos-caller).

{% mtgcard "Sentinel of the Nameless City" %}
[Sentinel of the Nameless City](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/211/sentinel-of-the-nameless-city) (2): A solid three-drop. A vigilant attacker performs brilliantly in matchups that value life-total trades; it has a body that isn't easily traded off, and the Map token is also a very useful thing.

{% mtgcard "Tranquil Frillback" %}
[Tranquil Frillback](https://scryfall.com/card/mat/24/tranquil-frillback) (1): In this environment, Tranquil Frillback rarely lacks a job, and it is one of the few creatures that can completely clear out [Enduring Innocence](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/6/enduring-innocence) with a single card. Also, since we are a graveyard deck, we really don't like running into graveyard hate, and most of it is artifacts and enchantments (such as [Ghost Vacuum](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/248/ghost-vacuum) and [Rest in Peace](https://scryfall.com/card/big/4/rest-in-peace)). [Ghost Vacuum](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/248/ghost-vacuum) can even be seen in some decks' main decks, so leaving all artifact and enchantment answers to the sideboard would be a bit too dangerous; the main deck needs some ability to respond as well. If the current environment has more artifacts and enchantments, you can consider swapping [Titania](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/193/titania-voice-of-gaea) for a [Scrapshooter](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/191/scrapshooter). The reason for not playing a second Tranquil Frillback is the deck's curve: although Tranquil Frillback is a three-mana creature, it is not effectively a three-drop on the curve. You don't want to slam a vanilla body on time, and you sometimes need to hold it until the target you want to hit appears before using it as removal. What's more, we also have [Honest Rutstein](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/207/honest-rutstein), who is not an active turn-three play if you haven't put creatures in the graveyard on turn two, so if we kept increasing the number of main-deck Tranquil Frillback, it would actually hurt the deck's tempo. In this respect Scrapshooter is much more proactive; at least it is a powerful three-mana 4/4 reach on the curve, and even if you just slam it, it plays an active role on offense and defense.

{% mtgcard "Sheoldred // The True Scriptures" %}
[Sheoldred, the True Scriptures](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/125/sheoldred-the-true-scriptures) (1): This is probably the card in the deck that departs most from the normal deckbuilding path. The most common options for this slot should be [Sheoldred, the Apocalypse](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/107/sheoldred-the-apocalypse) or [Hostile Investigator](https://scryfall.com/card/big/10/hostile-investigator), and that's how I built it at first, but after testing I found I wasn't actually short of win conditions. Instead, because of the high frequency of self-milling, it's easy to mill away my removal spells and be unable to recover them, so I still wanted a creature with removal built in. At first I switched to [Chupacabra Echo](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/97/chupacabra-echo). To be honest, it is just a downgraded [Ravenous Chupacabra](https://scryfall.com/card/mkc/136/ravenous-chupacabra). Although I'm a self-mill deck, in the early game the graveyard doesn't necessarily have enough permanents to provide removal reach, and drawing it under the wrong conditions really makes you want to die, but it can't be denied that once the number of permanents in the graveyard builds up enough (a single Overlord ETB may be enough), it is a super-strong resource-trading tool, and precisely choosing the target you want to remove is still impressive. But after being stuck on four-mana tempo a few times by openings with no chance to self-mill, I decided to switch to the more reliably functioning [Sheoldred, the True Scriptures](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/125/sheoldred-the-true-scriptures), which is at least a strong threat the opponent can't ignore, and can also solve my trouble with handling planeswalkers.

## Sideboarding Approach

Compared with other formats, Standard decks have more vague themes. Decks with the same name, like "XX color-pair midrange", may contain wildly different cards, and the only decks whose builds are truly specific may be extreme decks like red aggro and convoke.

So for this sideboarding section I plan to do things differently: apart from red aggro and convoke, I will use the functional traits of the imagined opponent to introduce which cards should be swapped out. I believe this is the most headache-inducing part of sideboarding for most people, while which cards to bring in is relatively clear, and you can also refer to the notes tags in the earlier sideboard card introductions.

### Responding to Graveyard Hate
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Golgari-Selfmill/f6dd7ef59a60.webp)

As a graveyard-using deck, how to handle the opponent's graveyard hate is an important part, and the graveyard hate the opponent prepares is very likely separate from the deck's main body; it simply depends on which hate card they chose. Although we are good at using graveyard resources, we aren't as pure as a graveyard-themed deck; most of the game plan still runs on the battlefield, and the cards that use graveyard resources are basically all standalone cards that can function on their own, so you can swap them out in sideboarding without hesitation.

Common graveyard hate can be roughly divided into the following four categories by mode of operation. However, whichever mode the graveyard hate uses, it disables the graveyard effect of [Say Its Name](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/197/say-its-name), so as long as you meet an opponent who can hate graveyards, you can basically take out all of [Say Its Name](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/197/say-its-name) & [Altanak, the Thrice-Called](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/166/altanak-the-thrice-called).

- One-time exile of a specified card from the graveyard (example: [Cease](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/246/cease-desist))
This level of graveyard hate is no obstacle to how this deck runs, and opponents who bring this kind of card are rare. If the opponent can interfere with the graveyard at instant speed, be a little careful when using Honest Rutstein.

- One-time exile of all cards in the graveyard (example: [Tranquil Frillback](https://scryfall.com/card/mat/24/tranquil-frillback))
Overlord & Bristlebud Farmer both mill first and then recover cards. Having the graveyard emptied has some effect, but it doesn't stop the deck from functioning. Against this kind of one-time graveyard wipe, just leave yourself a way out and make sure you can rebuild after the disaster. The idea is the same as facing a control sweeper: be careful not to fill the graveyard with too many resources and get wiped out in one go.
As long as this kind of effect stays at sorcery speed, you don't need to fear sideboard graveyard hate of this mode too much; just take out the Say Its Name package and manage the risk when playing.

- Continuous exile of specified cards from the graveyard (example: [Ghost Vacuum](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/248/ghost-vacuum))
Gix's Command's graveyard recursion has no target, and so do Overlord & Bristlebud Farmer, which can also recover resources from the cards you milled, so in theory this kind of effect can only restrain us and cannot immediately stop us from gaining resources. If it's something like [Urborg Scavengers](https://scryfall.com/card/mat/15/urborg-scavengers), which can't respond to graveyard removal at instant speed, there's nothing to fear. But against a card with the ability to respond like [Ghost Vacuum](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/248/ghost-vacuum), it is very troublesome: Honest Rutstein is shut down outright, and as soon as you see Ghost Vacuum you can take Rutstein out. Also, as long as Ghost Vacuum is on the battlefield, Mosswood Dreadknight has almost no chance to be reused, and repeatedly exiling quality creatures from the graveyard adds up to a big problem, so this matchup already requires actively bringing in answer cards.

- Continuous exile of all cards in the graveyard (example: [Rest in Peace](https://scryfall.com/card/big/4/rest-in-peace))
This is without doubt the answer card that gives us the biggest headache. Against opponents with [Rest in Peace](https://scryfall.com/card/big/4/rest-in-peace) or [Leyline of the Void](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/106/leyline-of-the-void), bring in all of Tranquil Frillback & Scrapshooter without question, take out the Say Its Name package and Honest Rutstein entirely, and depending on the situation you can even reduce the number of Overlord and Mosswood Dreadknight.
Also a reminder: against RIP and Leyline of the Void, Overlord can't recover resources from the graveyard, but Bristlebud Farmer can still recover the cards it milled; the detailed rules explanation comes later.


### Midrange Decks
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Golgari-Selfmill/cb13ec698036.webp)

In this matchup the main structure of the deck doesn't change much; just free up a few slots for the corresponding hate cards. Taking out all of [Say Its Name](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/197/say-its-name) & [Altanak, the Thrice-Called](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/166/altanak-the-thrice-called) normally frees up enough slots, and a competent midrange deck usually brings graveyard hate, so after referring to the counter-logic above, it is clear which cards should come out.

### Control Decks
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Golgari-Selfmill/bf5ffa23133a.webp)

Generally speaking, removal is the least useful in this matchup; at least Disfigure & Nowhere to Run are definitely useless. But control decks have more and more creatures now (cards that aren't creatures but can produce creature tokens count too), and many hide in the sideboard, so you can't mindlessly take out all the removal. You still need to keep a certain number of high-reach removal spells to keep the opponent in check; the exact number depends on the opponent's build. Also, control is unlikely to skip graveyard hate, especially since white-based control always has RIP, so the Say Its Name package still can't stay in.
Considering that the recent high share of white-based control is the [Caretaker's Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/6/caretakers-talent) derivative, it will mostly become a sideboarding mode where you bring in all the artifact and enchantment removal: on one hand to stop the opponent's resource engine, and on the other to avoid your own resource engine being disrupted. And against white Talent token control, you actually need to bring in Malicious Eclipse in place of regular spot removal, using a small sweeper to deal with the opponent's sea of tokens.

### Red Aggro
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Golgari-Selfmill/7b308dace5a6.webp)

| Side In | Side Out |
| :-: | :-: |
|+2 Malicious Eclipse|-3 Overlord of the Balemurk|
|+2 Disfigure|-4 Say Its Name|
|+2 Tranquil Frillback|-1 Altanak, the Thrice-Called|

In this matchup there's no time for wide-open looting; we need to make sure we can always keep an eye on the state of the game, and even after sideboarding like this, we still have quite a few card-advantage sources.

## Rules Corner: What Resource Recovery Can RIP Stop?

As mentioned above, in front of [Rest in Peace](https://scryfall.com/card/big/4/rest-in-peace), [Overlord of the Balemurk](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/113/overlord-of-the-balemurk) and [Bristlebud Farmer](https://scryfall.com/card/big/17/bristlebud-farmer) both mill and then recover resources, yet one cannot recover cards while the other can. Let's see why this difference arises:

![Cannot recover](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Golgari-Selfmill/6736e7efbca5.webp)
>[Overlord of the Balemurk](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/113/overlord-of-the-balemurk): Whenever Overlord of the Balemurk enters or attacks, mill four cards, then you may return a non-Avatar creature card or a planeswalker card from your graveyard to your hand.
>[Rest in Peace](https://scryfall.com/card/big/4/rest-in-peace): If a card or token would be put into a graveyard from anywhere, exile it instead.

Overlord's ability first mills 4 cards and then recovers one qualifying card from the graveyard. But the instant the cards are milled, they are exiled instead thanks to RIP's replacement effect, so when Overlord tries to recover a card from the graveyard after milling, you'll find your graveyard is empty and there is no qualifying card to choose, so naturally nothing is recovered.
(In addition, [Say Its Name](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/197/say-its-name) is worded with the same logic as Overlord.)

![Can recover](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Golgari-Selfmill/a35f9fe7d931.webp)
>[Bristlebud Farmer](https://scryfall.com/card/big/17/bristlebud-farmer): Whenever Bristlebud Farmer attacks, you may sacrifice a Food. If you do, mill three cards. You may put a permanent card from among them into your hand.
>[Rest in Peace](https://scryfall.com/card/big/4/rest-in-peace): If a card or token would be put into a graveyard from anywhere, exile it instead.

Bristlebud Farmer is worded slightly differently from Overlord: it mills 3 cards and then chooses one qualifying card from those 3 to put into your hand. The key here is that what this ability checks is not the graveyard but **"the cards milled by this effect"**, and for this kind of effect, rule 701.13c has a dedicated definition:

- 701.13c 
An effect that refers to a milled card can find that card in the zone it moved to from the library, as long as that zone is a public zone.

That is to say, even if the 3 cards milled by Bristlebud Farmer are exiled instead because of RIP's effect, this ability can still track those 3 cards, because the exile zone is a public zone, and in the end Bristlebud Farmer can still choose one qualifying card from those 3 to put into your hand, while the other 2 simply stay in exile.

## Conclusion

Although this deck is billed as self-mill, it isn't like Dredge, which truly throws the whole deck into the graveyard; it is just that the distinctive resource-engine cards happen to have self-mill traits, and overall the deck is still Golgari midrange that values resource volume.

When piloting this deck, there is one more key point to note: don't mill your own library out. Overlord's mill is a mandatory trigger. Fortunately, Golgari creatures all hit hard, so normally you can knock the opponent out before you deck yourself, but once the game reaches the midgame, as long as the opponent hasn't shown a clear breakthrough, you need to keep watching your library margin at all times and carefully plan how to operate Overlord.

Also, after reading the sideboarding analysis above, you'll notice that [Say Its Name](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/197/say-its-name) & [Altanak, the Thrice-Called](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/166/altanak-the-thrice-called) come out in every matchup after sideboarding, so is it really worth including them in the main deck?
The answer is still yes. In main-deck games against unspecified opponents, the generality of a card matters more, and a simple but deep looting spell like Say Its Name meets one of the definitions of generality, and the ability to proactively fill the graveyard really does help some of my cards function, raising the deck's consistency quite a bit. Altanak, the Thrice-Called is just a freebie.
Although this play style is easy to hate out in sideboarded games, I'm not a true graveyard deck to begin with, and once I switch things around in the sideboard the mode of operation actually changes, so not only can I lure the opponent into making ineffective hate choices against my deck, I can also clearly find slots to bring in my own targeted sideboard cards.
