## Quick Summary

On October 5, 2026, the Wizards of the Coast website published an announcement that starting in 2027, the contents of the current Play Booster and Collector Booster (hereafter "Collector Boosters") will be adjusted. The change takes effect with the first set of 2027, *Nauctis: The Sunken Realm*. The key updates are summarized below:

> Interested readers can read the original for themselves: [Updating Our Boosters in 2027](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/updating-our-boosters-in-2027)

**1. Booster prices will not go up!**
Super important. Compared with the price adjustment three years ago when Play Boosters replaced Draft Boosters and Set Boosters, this time Wizards has explicitly stated that the prices of Boosters and Collector Boosters will stay the same.

However, "the price stays the same" doesn't mean nothing changes in what players actually get. Collector Boosters will go from 15 cards down to 12, and Boosters will also have their foil contents adjusted. So this is more like Wizards redistributing the value structure of boosters while keeping the retail price.

> Interested readers can look back at this article, but note that when it was written, the collaborative Universe Beyond (UB) sets had not yet joined Standard, so the price expectations in it are limited to Magic's own sets: [What I Want to Say About Play Boosters...](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/About-Play-Boosters/)

**2. Play Boosters will go back to being called Boosters in 2027**
When the two kinds of boosters were merged in 2024, the name should really have been changed straight back to "Booster". Delaying it until 2027 only made the product name take an extra detour. Judging by the product line reconverging into a single "Booster", Wizards probably isn't inclined to split booster products again in the future. Of course, this is just a guess from the naming strategy, and doesn't mean the official side has ruled out releasing other special boosters in the future.

**3. Not every Booster will necessarily have a foil, and the foil slot will not show uncommons/commons**
This means the card you get in a Booster's foil slot will always be a foil rare/mythic rare. But it also means foil uncommons/commons become harder to get. For players who like foil uncommons/commons, or are used to "at least one foil per pack", this isn't necessarily good news. But for players who prefer high-rarity foils, the value density of the foil slot has indeed gone up. There are gains and losses. Japan Showcase cards, which used to appear only in Collector Boosters, will also have a chance to appear in the Booster's foil slot after the change.

**4. Collector Boosters lose 2 foil commons and 1 foil uncommon, bringing the pack to 12 cards (not counting the token)**
The official explanation is that this adjustment lowers printing costs while raising the excitement of opening packs. But the Collector Booster's price stays the same while the card count drops from 15 to 12, so by average content per card, it is effectively shrinkflation.

For players who buy Collector Boosters mainly for high-rarity and special foil cards, the impact may be relatively limited; but for players who value foil uncommons/commons, the complete foil experience, or collection completeness, it's a clear loss.

**5. God Packs introduced**

Starting with *Nauctis: The Sunken Realm* in 2027, God Packs will be introduced to all of Magic's own sets. The collaborative Universe Beyond sets are expected to join only from 2028, which means **you can't open a God Pack from any of the three UB sets in 2027.**

There will be 1 God Pack per 1,000 Boosters, roughly 1 per 6 boxes. The contents are as follows:

- 1 God Pack celebration card
- 10 nonfoil R/MR cards
- 2 nonfoil Booster Fun R/MR cards
- 2 traditional foil Booster Fun R/MR cards

Simply put, the token slot becomes a God Pack celebration card, and all the other 14 card slots are upgraded to foil and nonfoil R/MR cards!

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/About-Booster-Update-In-2027/a406cf9e0ec3.webp)

There will be 1 God Pack per 300 Collector Boosters, roughly 1 per 5 boxes. The contents are as follows:

- 1 God Pack celebration card
- 1 foil land
- 3 foil default-frame (standard frame) R/MR cards
- 3 nonfoil Booster Fun R/MR cards
- 5 foil Booster Fun R/MR cards

The change in a Collector Booster God Pack can be seen as the slots that used to hold 2 foil U/C cards each all being upgraded to foil fancy slots! Diamond-dust foil, flowing-light foil, fingerprint foil... and more special foil treatments that may appear in the future will all show up here, and they come five at a time!

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/About-Booster-Update-In-2027/88536063d5e4.webp)

## Impact on Stores

However, Wizards also published a note for WPN stores saying that God Packs may affect casual-level Limited events. In competitive-level formats run or outsourced by Wizards, when a player opens a God Pack in a draft or sealed event, the organizer will provide a brand-new, unopened Booster as a replacement. The player may keep the God Pack they opened as a souvenir, but may not use it in that event. When a player opens a God Pack in drafts, prerelease sealed events, or sealed events held by a local store, the official side lets the store allow the God Pack to stay in play for that event, only "recommending" that it be handled the same way as competitive level.

However, anyone who has played Limited knows that a single R/MR card is usually far stronger than a U/C card, let alone a God Pack giving 14 R/MR cards at once! Letting it in sealed events is extremely unfair and would very likely upset other players.

As for opening a God Pack in a draft, if it's added directly to the pool, the overall pool strength goes up. It won't give the opener a huge solo advantage the way it would in sealed, but that doesn't mean there's no risk of imbalance. If the bombs in the God Pack are especially concentrated, or fit some players' color pairs very closely, it can still cause local imbalance.

But the real problem may not be strength, but experience. The surprise and value that the God Pack opener would have enjoyed alone is forcibly shared with the other players when it joins the draft pool; rather than turning "enjoying it alone" into "enjoying it together", some players may prefer to buy another Booster from the store to join the event. Of course, some players may also think sharing the God Pack with the whole table is more fun, which ultimately depends on the store and the local players' culture.

Although the chance of opening a God Pack in a Booster is only one in a thousand<sup>[Note 1](#quote1)</sup>, it can still happen. Although the official side recommends handling it the same way as competitive events, it provides no concrete compensation measures. This also means that the potential costs of pre-event communication and dispute handling will fall on the stores.

## Conclusion: Same Price, Value Redistributed, but Is This Change Really a "Win-Win"?

This adjustment can be seen as Wizards' strategy of redistributing foil production capacity and pack-opening value while keeping the retail price. Compared with the price adjustment when Play Boosters launched in 2024, this time Wizards at least didn't raise the price outright, and it may make Boosters more attractive to some players; but the Collector Booster keeps its price while losing 3 cards, which is still effectively a content reduction.

Reducing foil U/C cards in Collector Boosters has a relatively limited impact on the customer base that buys Collector Boosters mainly for high-rarity cards and special foils; this also complements the policy of removing the foil U/C slot from Boosters. Foil U/C cards thus become rarer, which may raise their collectible value. But for players who already liked collecting those cards, it instead makes them harder to obtain.

From these two announcements, I can almost see the product managers scrambling to balance foil production capacity. I also boldly speculate that after reducing the print run of foil U/C cards, capacity could be diverted to printing more foil R/MR cards<sup>[Note 2](#quote2)</sup>, while still keeping the special fancy foil processes each set uses as a gimmick. As for God Packs, they are more likely a special pack the official side deliberately inserts at a fixed rate<sup>[Note 3](#quote3)</sup>. These changes may be Wizards' adjustment to foil production limits; but they may also be a deliberate attempt to raise the chance of special foils and high-rarity cards to boost sales. Still, this is just my own baseless guess, and if we get a chance to see the uncut foil sheets of post-2027 sets, they may offer more clues.

Is this change truly a win-win? Or is it actually a more hidden cost adjustment? Although the official side lets stores decide how to handle God Packs in casual events, it also recommends that stores remove the God Pack from the event and provide a replacement. But the problem is that if a store follows the recommendation and provides a brand-new Booster as a replacement, Wizards hasn't said where that Booster will come from. If the player who opened the God Pack is unwilling to pay for the replacement, the store will most likely still end up absorbing it to keep the event running smoothly. But the root of such disputes isn't that stores are unwilling to deal with them; it's that Wizards only "recommends" that stores follow suit, without proposing any further compensation or support mechanism, such as providing extra Boosters for replacement based on order volume and the God Pack appearance rate. I hope Wizards can propose concrete compensation measures in later announcements, to avoid leaving stores to absorb the costs and bear the resulting disputes.

Only after the new sets actually launch will we see more clearly the real impact of this adjustment on the pack-opening experience, the collectibles market, and store operations. But at least for now, one thing is certain: this is not a "win-win" change where everyone benefits unconditionally.
