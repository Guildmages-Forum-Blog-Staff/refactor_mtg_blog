## Deck Builds

### Sealed Pool 1: White-Green 3-0 @[遊戲平方](https://www.facebook.com/GameSquareTCG)

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FDN-Prerelease-Experience-Cephille/163bc577c9be.webp) 

#### Post-Event Evaluation
Even setting aside bombs like {% mtgpick "fdn" 1 tooltip=true %} , {% mtgpick "fdn" 107 tooltip=true %} , and {% mtgpick "fdn" 113 tooltip=true %} , this is a pool with a very good curve, with some small combos between the cards, and the removal is among the more important kinds for white-green.

#### Main Deck Introduction and Discussion

The two-mana mana dork {% mtgpick "fdn" 227 tooltip=true %} is super important in green-based decks, which let me build almost skipping the two-drop slot and going straight to three-mana-and-up cards. Here are some fun little combos:

1. {% mtgpick "fdn" 4 tooltip=true %} entering the battlefield triggers the ability of {% mtgpick "fdn" 9 tooltip=true %} , gaining 1 life, and if that's the first time you gained life this turn, you then make a 1/1 Cat token and gain 1 more life (which won't make another Cat this time). Pairing it with {% mtgpick "fdn" 28 tooltip=true %} to surveil 1 for every 1 life gained is far too common.
2. {% mtgpick "fdn" 107 tooltip=true %} , which has a landfall ability, and {% mtgpick "fdn" 103 tooltip=true %} pair very well with land-fetching effects and with {% mtgpick "fdn" 262 tooltip=true %} , giving you double the joy!

After playing this limited event, I feel these two green cards, {% mtgpick "fdn" 103 tooltip=true %} and {% mtgpick "fdn" 114 tooltip=true %} , are a bit too strong for commons. One makes a 1/1 Faerie Warrior token on every landfall; the other is already a supreme build as a four-mana 1/4 with deathtouch and reach, and can also serve as a mana sink to actively put counters on things and grow them (and not just on itself!?).

The green rare enchantment {% mtgpick "fdn" 113 tooltip=true %} is a truly great bomb; making 3/3 Raccoon tokens and putting counters on things are both quite useful. Often, after putting a counter or two on a flier, I start making Raccoons for ground defense while the fliers keep attacking in the air and take the game. In one game against a very coherent black-green Faerie deck, I also made 9 Raccoons with this enchantment and won with that formula.

As for removal, one white {% mtgpick "fdn" 143 tooltip=true %} and one green {% mtgpick "fdn" 214 tooltip=true %} make, I think, a very comfortable pairing. To me, their text is almost equivalent to "destroy target bomb an opponent controls." In this limited event I used them to take down bombs like {% mtgpick "fdn" 193 tooltip=true %} and {% mtgpick "spg" 81 tooltip=true %} , and I was quite satisfied XD

This red-blue is not a weak pool, but compared to the white-green main deck the power level is still quite a bit lower, which is obvious just from looking at the creatures' sizes.

As for the remaining cards, the black material is genuinely too weak. I strongly recommend that readers who see a similar black build not pick it, and not touch it even if the curve looks OK. I didn't play the 12/12 big Dinosaur {% mtgpick "fdn" 335 tooltip=true %} because it's only a win-more card.

### Sealed Pool 2: Red-Green Splashing Blue 3-0 @[紙盒子桌遊休閒館](https://www.facebook.com/profile.php?id=100057364393517)

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FDN-Prerelease-Experience-Cephille/0582678de113.webp)

After playing at 平方 in the afternoon, I ran into a player I had met before at 紙盒子 in Yonghe. They said they were running a sealed event that night, so I went over to take a look.

#### Post-Event Evaluation
I mixed in two blue-green bombs, {% mtgpick "fdn" 121 tooltip=true %} and {% mtgpick "fdn" 247 tooltip=true %} , which was completely me being greedy. A seven-mana 8/12 sea serpent basically never gets cast, but... I have a dream! Using {% mtgpick "fdn" 189 tooltip=true %} to give haste to a {% mtgpick "fdn" 121 tooltip=true %} hard-cast for seven just feels so good to think about!

#### Main Deck Introduction and Discussion

After playing one game, I swapped the main deck's {% mtgpick "fdn" 236 tooltip=true %} for {% mtgpick "fdn" 203 tooltip=true %} to be a bit more aggressive and to make Treasure; otherwise the blue really can't come together...

But once I played it, I found that the red-green curve is really beautiful, and there are some very aggressive openings early on, for example:

- {% mtgpick "fdn" 82 tooltip=true %} on turn two, then after attacking on turn three, play {% mtgpick "fdn" 87 tooltip=true %} to make it a 4/3, and from then on every {% mtgpick "fdn" 82 tooltip=true %} is a 3/2 menacing beater.
- {% mtgpick "fdn" 189 tooltip=true %} is really key in this red-green; swinging in with a {% mtgpick "fdn" 117 tooltip=true %} on turn four hurts a lot! Giving haste with {% mtgpick "fdn" 258 tooltip=true %} also works, but the downside is that it uses up one mana; giving haste with a creature is more aggressive!
- Paired with creatures that have trample, like {% mtgpick "fdn" 100 tooltip=true %}, {% mtgpick "fdn" 80 tooltip=true %} lets you reliably slam damage into the opponent's face. You don't have to use the flashback; making the opponent wary of it is good enough.
- The value of {% mtgpick "fdn" 258 tooltip=true %} lies in giving hexproof to your high-value creatures. Once it is successfully attached, it is rock solid, and you no longer have to worry about pump spells or counters. In one game I won by having {% mtgpick "fdn" 232 tooltip=true %} wear the boots and solo the opponent! (Although the slime probably has no feet... oh well XDD)

As for {% mtgpick "fdn" 1 tooltip=true %} , honestly I basically just slammed it down to speed up my opponent's surrender; the red-green base alone can play with great momentum!

#### Sideboard and Pool Optimization

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FDN-Prerelease-Experience-Cephille/4672c651ca02.webp)

The sideboard is above. Mixing in black would actually work too, but I think it isn't as explosive as blue-green. Just playing red-green is also an option, but since I opened it, it would be a shame not to play it. Honestly I also wanted to let {% mtgpick "fdn" 216 tooltip=true %} out for some air, but there really was no room for win-more cards.

> **Shop Introduction:**
> **紙盒子** is in Yonghe District, New Taipei City, about a 15 to 20 minute walk from Dingxi MRT Station. The shop started as a board game store and started promoting Magic: The Gathering with Bloomburrow (BLB). The atmosphere in the shop is quite nice, maybe because it used to be purely a board game store; while we were playing the prerelease, some parents even brought their kids in to play board games. I also saw parents bring their kids to buy a Foundations Beginner Box to play at home, or show interest in joining this weekend's new player tutorial. I hope more and more new players will come play this game with us! The atmosphere for playing cards at 紙盒子 is also much more relaxed, quite different from playing sealed at a card shop!

### Sealed Pool 3: Blue-Green 3-0 @[卡士多遊戲屋](https://www.facebook.com/CardStoreTW)

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FDN-Prerelease-Experience-Cephille/afbc300f3cee.webp)

The four-mana cards that are shiny are two copies of {% mtgpick "fdn" 31 tooltip=true %} and one {% mtgpick "fdn" 217 tooltip=true %} .

#### Post-Event Evaluation
Although I went 3-0, you can really feel how thin the two-drop curve is; luckily I didn't face a fast aggro deck with a dense curve. If I have no mana dork on the opening turn, or the mana dork gets removed, the deck could get stuck on three or four lands, unable to ramp smoothly, and lose.

#### Main Deck Introduction and Discussion

This blue-green build is quite different from the traditional limited approach built around curve and removal. It basically relies heavily on a turn-one mana dork, jumping to three and then smoothly to five mana to stabilize and push back. Most games were won by the landfall trio {% mtgpick "fdn" 247 tooltip=true %} , {% mtgpick "fdn" 103 tooltip=true %} , and {% mtgpick "fdn" 107 tooltip=true %} ; in one game {% mtgpick "fdn" 107 tooltip=true %} even grew to 32/32...

However, this build doesn't blend very well with blue's draw-two and flash themes. {% mtgpick "fdn" 152 tooltip=true %} is basically just a 2/1, and is even worse than {% mtgpick "fdn" 36 tooltip=true %} , which at least has prowess. A few games were close wins, and giving the whole team trample with {% mtgpick "fdn" 220 tooltip=true %} was a very important way to break through; that's also why I was willing to try {% mtgpick "fdn" 109 tooltip=true %} .

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FDN-Prerelease-Experience-Cephille/11c2a94c3102.webp)

The sideboard is above. In the red-white build in picture one, the shiny one-mana card is the second copy of {% mtgpick "fdn" 13 tooltip=true %} ; and in the sideboard in picture two, the shiny black cards are the two-mana {% mtgpick "fdn" 77 tooltip=true %} and the three-mana {% mtgpick "fdn" 60 tooltip=true %} .

It's a pity there was no Temur (red-blue-green) tri-land. When building I tried going three colors, but gave up when the mana was unstable. Red-green splashing blue also isn't a build that lets the strengths shine, so after thinking it over I just played blue-green.

The red-white sideboard could basically work too, but the early curve is a bit rough; the upside is that the removal is decent, and with an extra sweeper, {% mtgpick "fdn" 140 tooltip=true %} , you can catch the opponent off guard, and there are enough fliers to hold back quality creatures, wipe the board, and trade into a wave of attacks.

#### Main Deck Introduction and Discussion

The power level of both builds is a bit subtle: the blue-green creatures are solid but there are few tricks; the black-white removal is strong but the creatures are on the weak side. ## Conclusion

I played these three FDN prerelease events at three different shops and experienced different gaming atmospheres. The Friday afternoon sessions at 遊戲平方 often have many overseas players, and back during BLB I also met a visiting player from Hong Kong who made a special trip to play before leaving Taiwan, which is quite international. 紙盒子, as mentioned above, offers a very relaxed play experience, and seeing kids willing to come try Magic is also something to look forward to! As for 卡士多, it's where I exchange ideas with friends, and it's the environment I'm most familiar with!

For me, the prerelease is basically a combo event:

1. Experience a new set and verify my predictions from writing limited ratings
2. Meet new friends and catch up with old ones
3. Crack packs!
4. Take prize packs home to play sealed (even if I didn't do well this time, I can still buy up to 12 packs with the power of money)
5. Gather material to write this sealed report
6. And lots and lots more

So I'm no longer going to write about whether playing prereleases is worthwhile; that's a matter of opinion. But for me, it's just that much fun! So naturally I'll keep putting myself into it! I hope everyone can keep finding their own enjoyment in this game. Fun game, let's play together!
