## Before We Start

Ever since MH2 came out, Izzet Murktide has been one of my favorite Modern decks.
Back then [[勒格文]] was too expensive, so I shelved the idea.
After plenty of ups and downs, I finally finished building the whole Murktide deck this May. I'll get into the details of that journey when I have time.

I wrote a similar article on my own blog before, but that was back before Preordain got unbanned. If you want to look back at that old article, [click here](https://miohitokiri5474.github.io/game/Izzet-Murktide/).

## Decklist & Deck Tech

As usual, here's the [decklist](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/5829573#paper) first.
In my last article, I mentioned that I was considering two ways to add Preordain: `eight one-mana cantrips` or `replacing Consider with Preordain`.
The option I finally went with is an incomplete version of `eight one-mana cantrips`: I ended up running only seven.
Why pile on a bunch of cards that dig for things when I could just add one more of the thing I'm actually looking for, XD
For example, I recently found in testing that the deck gets stuck on lands very easily, so I swapped the fourth Consider for another land-finding card.
After all, the original slot was there to cycle an Island and find a land, and if I can't reliably find lands, I'm really playing 17 lands, rather than 19 lands with two of them being cycling lands that just sit there.

### Creatures

![](/images/Izzet-Murktide/Creatures.jpg)

The creature suite is about the same as what over ninety percent of the meta is playing: a full set of Ragavan and DRC, two Subtlety, and three Murktide Regent.
Ragavan is the strongest one-drop in the format. It feels great against tempo, midrange, and control alike, generating resources and Treasures. Awesome.
DRC is the main graveyard-filling tool, and it also filters the top of your library when you draw. After delirium it becomes a 3/3 flyer and deals damage fast. It has to attack, which is sometimes awkward, but overall the upsides outweigh the downsides.
Subtlety is there to deal with the one-mana Scam in the current environment, and with the Omnath / [[小泰]] / [[卡恩]] in four-color elementals that can't be countered.
Murktide Regent is the only big threat now. We used to have Ledger Shredder too, but ever since Orcish Bowmasters burst onto the scene, a card that requires you to draw an extra card had to be painfully cut.

### Spells

![](/images/Izzet-Murktide/Spells.jpg)

The instants and sorceries are pretty straightforward. Lightning Bolt and Unholy Heat are burn spells, used to clear the board.
Consider, Preordain, and Expressive Iteration are the cantrips.
The rest are counterspells. The current environment is a bit nasty, so we run a lot of counters (sideboard included), which feels slightly unhealthy = =
Also, I personally don't run a full playset of Lightning Bolt. In the current environment it's three at most, since even three can't handle some boards.
And when I have two burn spells in hand: before delirium, if hitting for two is enough, I'll prioritize discarding Unholy Heat; after delirium, I'll prioritize discarding Lightning Bolt, unless I'm saving it to go face for lethal.

### Sideboard

![](/images/Izzet-Murktide/Sideboard.jpg)

The sideboard is no different from last time (and there isn't much to adjust, since the format still looks about the same), so the following is copy-pasted (I'm lazy).

Two Engineered Explosives to fight Rhinos + Hammer.
Two Flusterstorm to fight cascade.
Two Cast into the Fire to fight artifacts (especially The One Ring).
Two Dress Down to fight Elementals + Urza's Saga.
One Unlicensed Hearse is just for graveyard decks (like Living End / Grinding Station).
One Blood Moon + one Magus of the Moon to fight Urza's Saga, multicolor decks, green Titan decks, Creativity, and so on. I chose to bring one Magus for games where the opponent doesn't board in their hate cards: Murktide opponents usually don't bring many removal spells (some even take a few out), so the creature version of Blood Moon is a bit better than the regular one, since it dodges counters and removal.
One Force of Negation + one Mystical Dispute \more counters/
One Fury to fight small-creature decks like Boros Burn or mirror matches.
One Commandeer to fight high-value decks like Creativity. Against Creativity, grabbing the opponent's Karns and The One Ring feels great (in the mirror, whoever lands the small Karn first wins), and green Titan decks run The One Ring now too, so taking it and drawing cards off it feels great as well.

> Although Commandeer exiles two cards from hand, and Commandeer itself is a third card, so spending three cards to steal one Ring feels like a bad deal, and it enters without any anti-everything protection (you didn't cast it).
> But think about it for a moment: the turn it comes in, you tap the Ring to draw one card, and on your own turn you tap it again to draw two, so you've effectively lost no cards, and you get a lasting draw engine (even though it keeps costing you life). Sounds pretty good, right (?

## Match Detail & Piloting Caution

### Starting Hands & Mulligan

By my understanding, Murktide opening hands are usually quite loose (in games where the opponent isn't bringing hate cards):
1 ~ 2x Lands (not color-screwed)
1 ~ 2x One Drop Creatures
3 ~ 4x Cantrip or Removal or Counters (one of each is best)

For example:
2x Lands
1x Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer
1x Dragon's Rage Channeler
1x Preordain
1x Counterspell
1x Lightning Bolt

![](/images/Izzet-Murktide/Sample-hands-01.png)

Very comfortable, a hand that can handle anything.

Or something more extreme:
1x Fetch Lands
1x Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer
2x Dragon's Rage Channeler
2x Consider
1x Spell Pierce

![](/images/Izzet-Murktide/Sample-hands-02.png)

You can keep this too, it's just a bit more painful, and there are some small differences I'll mention later.

The only hands you truly can't keep are:

1. All lands / no lands
2. Nothing but counters/burn besides lands, meaning no small creature or cantrip (though you might consider keeping in certain specific matchups)
3. One land with no cantrip

The reason you can't keep no-land or one-land-no-cantrip hands is obvious: no follow-up resources, and the land is likely to die halfway down the road.
A notable exception: against Boros Burn, Hammer, and other near-aggro decks that force Murktide to play almost like a control deck, you might consider keeping those hands on the draw (usually in sideboard games or matches with hate cards).

> Yes, that's right! Izzet Murktide is a control deck that meets the bare minimum of support: cheap removal (Lightning Bolt, Unholy Heat), hard counters (Counterspell, Subtlety, Force of Negation) plus some soft counters (Spell Pierce, Mystical Dispute), a powerful finisher (two swings from Murktide), and cheap resource engines (Consider, Preordain, Expressive Iteration). It's only missing sustained resources and a powerful sweeper (though putting a sweeper in a tempo deck is a weird idea in the first place).

Also, I personally think DRC matters more than Ragavan. In quite a few matchups (at least in my experience practicing since the end of July), the opponent was killed by a post-delirium DRC. I jokingly say I'm playing DRC aggro XD

### Piloting Caution

#### Dragon's Rage Channeler

##### DRC First?

When you have both DRC and a non-creature spell in hand, which do you cast first?
Simply put, the standard is **whether DRC will die to the opponent's removal after it enters**.
For example, if the opponent plays black and we haven't hit delirium yet, casting DRC first might get it shot down by Orcish Bowmasters.
Or if the opponent plays a deck with Unholy Heat, DRC could be hit for two before the opponent even has delirium.
Most commonly lately, against green Creativity, I wonder whether DRC will just get exiled by Warping Wail if I cast it first.
If you can dodge these threats (or the opponent's hand is already empty), then of course the most efficient play is DRC first and then the non-creature spell.

Of course, if the graveyard is one card short of delirium and you have an instant to respond with (Consider, Lightning Bolt, etc.), you can use that as leverage to bluff the opponent, but I don't recommend it personally. Playing it safe is better.

Also, we can skip the combat step and trigger delirium in the second main phase, so DRC doesn't necessarily have to swing for those three points.

##### F\*\*k You Endurance

DRC's natural predator is the same as that of our former strongest vanilla, Tarmogoyf: it gets ground into the dirt by Endurance.
Since DRC must attack once delirium is on, the opponent can respond before blockers are declared by flashing in Endurance to shuffle our graveyard away. That really hurts when we have multiple DRCs, and the opponent can combine it with other creatures (like any random 2/2) to block and trade away all of our value.
If I have an instant in hand at that point, I'll consider holding it and waiting for the opponent to act, then trying to retrigger delirium before blockers are declared.

##### Cast Murktide Before or After Combat?

Usually we cast Murktide after combat, in order to increase the board's output.
But if the opponent is playing a deck that might have Endurance, or we need a blocker, we can also make a very big Murktide before combat and throw all the problems at the opponent to deal with.

#### Mishra's Bauble

When do you look at the opponent's library, and when do you look at your own?
Most of the time you look at the top of the opponent's library, and only occasionally at your own:

1. When you have a land-finding card that can shuffle the top of your library, deciding whether to find the land before or after drawing the card from Bauble
2. When you've already looked at the opponent's top card, and you look at your own top card to decide how to play right now

Also, when I have Ragavan, I wait until Ragavan's combat damage trigger has resolved before activating Bauble, unless damage/resources/the board are really tight, in which case I decide whether to dash Ragavan to steal a card based on the opponent's top card.

> The best example here is the Boros Burn matchup: if the opponent will have lethal on board next turn and is tapped out now, I'd consider dashing Ragavan if stealing that card gives me a chance to counter-kill or stabilize, but if it's just a land I'd consider casting it normally and keeping it as a blocker.
> Another example is when the opponent is on white Hammer with no cards in hand and Sigarda's Aid on board: if I see the top card won't give them Colossus Hammer, I won't dash Ragavan, but if it will, I'll dash and steal it to see if I get another turn of beats.

The sideboarding plans here are my own, focused on commonly seen decks at tournaments, with the swap logic attached.

#### Izzet Murktide (Mirror Match)

The same cards come in for the mirror, but what comes out depends on whether you're on the play or the draw.
On the play we take out Unholy Heat to be more aggressive.
On the draw we take out Ragavan, because in the mirror it's powerless unless it survives its first turn, and after that first turn it's too easy to deal with.

What comes in is more counters, graveyard hate, and board control. We skip Force of Negation because we don't want to go down too many cards.

Take out all the Ragavans, since they will absolutely get shot down by Bowmasters.
Beyond that, just bring in Dress Down, Fury, and Cast into the Fire. On the play you can consider adding another Unlicensed Hearse.
