Bloomburrow (BLB) holds its Prerelease on July 26, 2024, and is officially released one week later on August 2, 2024. Want to know more about how each card rates in Limited and what to pick? Then check out this series of BLB Limited guides!

- For the mechanics, see this article: [Bloomburrow Mechanics Breakdown](https://guildmagesforum.tw/BLB-mechanism/)
- For a first look at the Limited archetypes, see this article: [Bloomburrow Limited Preliminary Look](https://guildmagesforum.tw/BLB-Limited-Preliminary/)

> This series of Limited guides is primarily about the ratings; some average cards, or cards with relatively simple abilities, will not get a specific comment.

## Rating Scale

5.0: Unconditional first pick
4.5: Unconditional first pick if you are in the right colors
4.0: Worth a first pick
3.5: Strong enough, but not necessarily a first pick
3.0: Above average (you will really want it in your final deck)
2.5: Average; maybe around the 6th or 7th pick in a draft
2.0 to 1.0: Below average (you would rather not play it than play it)
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable (basically never play it; it will only help you lose)

Comments:

- [Cruelclaw's Heist](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/88): A card with a very high ceiling, but effects that look at the hand generally perform mediocrely in Limited, and you have to decide whether to give the opponent a card before seeing their hand. If the opponent's hand is ordinary and you do not get a strong enough card, it is a big loss.
- [Darkstar Augur](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/90): This card needs a very long explanation, as follows:

  In the team that took part in the joint ratings, 7 people rated this card in total. Two gave it 2.5 or above and five gave it 0.5 or below (the final score is the average). I personally gave it 0. The opinions are extremely polarized, which makes it a card very much worth discussing.

  The reason for giving 2.5 or above is more intuitive: although it has the downside of losing life, as a 3/2/3 flyer with decent stats that can earn cards every turn, it brings enough threat and resources and leads to victory.

  The reasons for giving 0.5 or below are more complicated, but they mainly revolve around the life-loss downside:

  1.  Its life-loss effect is basically a copy of [Dark Confidant](https://scryfall.com/card/rav/81/dark-confidant). Dark Confidant has been studied extensively with data in the past, so I will only give the conclusion. In a format where the starting life total is 20, keeping the average mana value (lands included) between 1.2 and 1.3 lets this kind of effect work best, 1.5 and above starts to get dangerous and you should consider leaving it out, and 1.7 and above it basically cannot make the deck. In Limited the average mana value easily exceeds 1.7, so the life loss from putting it in your deck is far greater than the benefit of drawing a card.
  2.  Compared with constructed formats, high-powered cards in Limited tend to cost more. In Modern, for example, each trigger costing 2 life might bring in a [Tarmogoyf](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/235/tarmogoyf) with 4 or more power. With the expectation of flipping a card that costs 3 or less, the overall card strength in Limited is not enough to trade for a winning advantage.
  3.  Limited games easily turn into board stalls or life-total races, and in those situations this card is one that makes you "lose".

  In summary, after combining the opinions of all our writers, we still lean toward not recommending that readers play this card in Limited.
