## Before We Start

Hi everyone, I'm MarkWen, an old newbie who played through [Theros](https://scryfall.com/sets/ths), some core sets and [Khans of Tarkir](https://scryfall.com/sets/ktk), quit for real-life reasons, and came back at the beginning of this year.
I'm very happy that at the MITQ qualifier at CM on 20241215, I made Top 4 with Boros Mice and earned my spot at next year's MIT#9. The moment I qualified I really breathed a sigh of relief, and it gave all the effort I put into practicing a proper answer!
I'd also like to thank [cephille's MTG World](https://www.facebook.com/Pignosemtgworld) for inviting me to share my journey preparing Boros Mice. Getting to leave my footprints in the Guildmages' Forum community is genuinely moving ><

I chose an aggro deck to prepare for this MITQ because when I came back this year, I downloaded MTGA for the first time mid-year (back when [The Wandering Emperor](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/42/the-wandering-emperor) hadn't rotated out yet), netcdecked a Mono-Red aggro deck ([Slickshot Show-Off](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/146/slickshot-show-off) MVP) in two weeks and climbed to Mythic with it, which set the direction for my future Standard preparation. Before I started seriously building for Standard, the only paper Magic I played was Pre-release events, draft, and Commander.

I started seriously preparing for Standard after [BLB](https://scryfall.com/sets/blb) came out. At first I tried a White-Blue flyers tempo deck, but it got smashed in practice matches, so I took it apart XD
When I started browsing deck sites like MTGGoldfish, I noticed [Heartfire Hero](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/138/heartfire-hero) was being used heavily in aggro decks. I checked my collection and found I happened to have exactly four copies! So I decided to prepare mono-red Mice aggro.

Compared with the Mono-Red aggro deck from before the rotation, the two play quite differently. Mono-Red Mice depends much more on whether you have creatures on board and on the order you play your cards, and its card filtering is weaker than the old red aggro deck. So when I first practiced Mice aggro on MTGA, there was always a wall in front of me: I reached Diamond but could never climb to Mythic, and this went on for two months.

It wasn't until [DSK](https://scryfall.com/sets/dsk) arrived that I finally decided to turn mono-red Mice into Boros Mice. I want to thank Guildmages' Forum for the article [Duskmourn Standard Preliminary](https://guildmagesforum.tw/DSK-Standard-Preliminary/), which showed the [Boros Mouse](https://guildmagesforum.tw/DSK-Standard-Preliminary/#%E7%B4%85%E7%99%BD%E5%AE%B6%E9%BC%A0%EF%BC%88Boros-Mouse%EF%BC%89) list. Without that article as a core, I would never have had the follow-up ideas.

### Settling on a Direction

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Boros-Mice/e518a11140eb.webp)

The addition of [Sheltered by Ghosts](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/30/sheltered-by-ghosts) made me, someone who leaned toward mono-red Mice, move to Boros Mice. The original aggro deck mostly just kept posing problems to the opponent, but was relatively weak at removing the opponent's creatures. This Aura gives the Mice a certain amount of answering power while also applying more pressure, and since it targets, it also works nicely with the Mice's valiant.

At the same time, the arrival of [Shardmage's Rescue](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/29/shardmages-rescue) makes it easier to protect creatures on board; combined with offspring, the Mice stay alive on the battlefield even more often!

Compared to a red aggro deck that runs cards like [Screaming Nemesis](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/157/screaming-nemesis), this build gives up the ability to burst out on an empty board; still, I like the overall tempo of this deck more.

### Land Configuration
An aggro deck wants lands to be usable the moment you draw them, and I'm no exception, but among the nonbasic lands I only have a full four [Battlefield Forge](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/257/battlefield-forge). The fastland [Inspiring Vantage](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/269/inspiring-vantage) will always enter tapped as the fourth copy, so I chose to play only three.

Why only one copy of each of the other utility lands? Because when I practice, I really dislike lands that can only produce colored mana for specific targets, so I made that choice. (If you draw it, you draw it, and vice versa XD)

### Creature Choices
Like most aggro decks, [Heartfire Hero](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/138/heartfire-hero), [Emberheart Challenger](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/133/emberheart-challenger) and [Manifold Mouse](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/143/manifold-mouse) are of course at four copies. What's more unusual is that I also run a full four [Flowerfoot Swordmaster](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/14/flowerfoot-swordmaster). The reason is that after the turn-two buff, the damage it deals is on par with [Heartfire Hero](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/138/heartfire-hero); there's also a chance to make two of them on turn three, keeping the board even or even ahead. Its valiant effect also gives +1 power to every Mouse on the battlefield, which is a lot of pressure when the opponent doesn't sweep the board.

Adding [Valley Questcaller](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/36/valley-questcaller) is already strong just for giving all Mice on board +1/+1, not to mention that as long as the opponent doesn't remove it, every Mouse cast afterward can scry 1 to check whether the next card is useful. I only play two copies because in my testing that was least likely to disrupt the Mice's attack tempo, after all, this one is a Rabbit XD

The slot for [Cheeky House-Mouse](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/7/cheeky-house-mouse-squeak-by) originally held [Monastery Swiftspear](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/144/monastery-swiftspear), but after talking with a friend before the tournament, I figured that since my deck is built around Mice, I might as well switch to this card, and in the end it replaced [Monastery Swiftspear](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/144/monastery-swiftspear).
[Skrelv, Defector Mite](https://scryfall.com/card/one/33/skrelv-defector-mite), [Inti, Seneschal of the Sun](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/156/inti-seneschal-of-the-sun) and [Mabel, Heir to Cragflame](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/224/mabel-heir-to-cragflame) are each a single copy because they are legendary.
The reason I specifically included [Inti, Seneschal of the Sun](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/156/inti-seneschal-of-the-sun) is that it widens the advantage while speeding up filtering, and the trample it gives the target creature breaks board stalls in a tight position and tips the advantage to my side.

### Other Spells
In an aggro deck, [Monstrous Rage](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/142/monstrous-rage) is naturally a four-of, [Sheltered by Ghosts](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/30/sheltered-by-ghosts) is one of the core pieces of the deck and has to be a four-of, and [Might of the Meek](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/144/might-of-the-meek) is the only way to draw cards in this deck, so it also has to be a four-of. [Shardmage's Rescue](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/29/shardmages-rescue) was originally a four-of too, but after discussing with a friend I chose to add one more creature, [Skrelv, Defector Mite](https://scryfall.com/card/one/33/skrelv-defector-mite), in place of the fourth copy. As for [Rabid Gnaw](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/147/rabid-gnaw), I play two: besides being instant-speed single-target removal that triggers valiant, when I target a creature enchanted with [Sheltered by Ghosts](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/30/sheltered-by-ghosts) it also gains some extra life. Honestly it should have been swapped for [Boros Charm](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/721/boros-charm) (but I still hadn't received mine before the tournament QQ).

## Sideboard Choices

The sideboard idea is basically this: against decks like Domain or White midrange, which have lots of sweepers and whose mid-to-late-game card quality crushes ours, we bring in lots of burn and switch to a tempo burn plan. The creature count drops by a certain amount, and the goal is to take away half their life early, then finish with burn in the midgame or with the [Forge](https://scryfall.com/card/one/153/urabrasks-forge).

- [Urabrask's Forge](https://scryfall.com/card/one/153/urabrasks-forge) x3: To beat control decks that can't deal with artifacts (except mono-white tokens).
- [Ghost Vacuum](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/248/ghost-vacuum) x2: To beat graveyard decks, such as White-Blue Abhorrent Oculus or Blue-Green Otters.
- [Get Lost](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/14/get-lost) x2: To solve some tricky problems (usually brought in during aggro mirrors).
- [Shock](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/144/shock) x2: Usually used to kill creatures (a downgraded version of [Burst Lightning](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/192/burst-lightning)).
- [Lightning Strike](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/137/lightning-strike) x2: A substitute for the [Boros Charm](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/721/boros-charm) I couldn't get.
- [Exorcise](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/8/exorcise) x1: To solve some tricky problems (brought in against midrange and Domain).
- [Callous Sell-Sword](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/221/callous-sell-sword-burn-together) x1: A finisher that raises the burst ceiling (only one copy because I only owned one before the tournament).
- [Sunspine Lynx](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/155/sunspine-lynx) x1: Since there are quite a few Blue-Black control and midrange decks whose nonbasic lands usually far outnumber their basics, I chose this to replace [Screaming Nemesis](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/157/screaming-nemesis) as a final finisher.
- [Invasion of Regatha](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/148/invasion-of-regatha-disciples-of-the-inferno) x1: A finisher, but it basically never got used.

### 20241215 CM MITQ Sideboarding Strategy 

1. Take out 1 each of [Mabel, Heir to Cragflame](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/224/mabel-heir-to-cragflame), [Cheeky House-Mouse](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/7/cheeky-house-mouse-squeak-by) and [Rabid Gnaw](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/147/rabid-gnaw);
2. Take out 1 each of [Shardmage's Rescue](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/29/shardmages-rescue), [Valley Questcaller](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/36/valley-questcaller) and [Manifold Mouse](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/143/manifold-mouse)

The first three basically come out after game one, and the last three I adjust depending on the situation.

On the day of the 20241215 CM MITQ, I only sided out the first three plus 1 [Shardmage's Rescue](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/29/shardmages-rescue), mainly swapping in [Shock](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/144/shock), [Get Lost](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/14/get-lost), [Sunspine Lynx](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/155/sunspine-lynx) and [Callous Sell-Sword](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/221/callous-sell-sword-burn-together). That day I figured that forcing in [Urabrask's Forge](https://scryfall.com/card/one/153/urabrasks-forge) in a sideboarded game would actually make me lose by taking too long, so it never got a chance to play. Otherwise, against midrange or control, putting in all three Forges is completely reasonable.

The final sideboard came out like this after discussing with a friend. The reason for leaning toward burn is that the hardest matchup for my Boros Mice is mono-white tokens: with no creatures on the board it's very hard to deal damage. So my default plan was that if I really ran into mono-white, I would swap in every card that can deal damage on an empty board, but luckily I never faced mono-white that day.

## Matchup Overview

### Boros Burn
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Boros-Mice/fc0dc3bd4912.webp)
[Deck reference](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6788037#paper)

The player on the play has the advantage. Against this type of deck, as long as you can keep one creature on the board, there's basically a chance to turn the game around, so whether [Shardmage's Rescue](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/29/shardmages-rescue) is in hand matters a lot; [Sheltered by Ghosts](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/30/sheltered-by-ghosts) can directly deal with [Screaming Nemesis](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/157/screaming-nemesis) and thereby boost your life total. The rest depends on both sides' draws.

### Dimir Midrange
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Boros-Mice/d436d9807a53.webp)
[Deck reference](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/archetype/standard-dimir-midrange-dmu#paper)

If we're on the play we have a slight edge. Similar to the Boros Burn matchup, whether [Shardmage's Rescue](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/29/shardmages-rescue) and [Sheltered by Ghosts](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/30/sheltered-by-ghosts) show up on time, and whether the low-cost creatures [Heartfire Hero](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/138/heartfire-hero), [Emberheart Challenger](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/133/emberheart-challenger) and [Flowerfoot Swordmaster](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/14/flowerfoot-swordmaster) survive a turn, is crucial.

### Golgari Midrange
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Boros-Mice/e6243d828bf8.webp)
[Deck reference](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/archetype/standard-golgari-midrange-dmu#paper)

The approach is similar to the Dimir control matchup, but you don't need to worry about counterspells in particular. Overall it's somewhat easier than Dimir control, but being on the play is still more advantageous.

### Overlord Domain
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Boros-Mice/182411fd2a32.webp)
[Deck reference](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/archetype/standard-domain-dmu#paper)

Before the opponent reaches five lands, see whether you can deal with them; again, the player on the play has the advantage. The harder version is the Domain deck that plays Zur, Eternal Schemer: once the opponent gains life even once, you basically can't come back.

### Mono-White Tokens
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Boros-Mice/13817ea9e76b.webp)
[Deck reference](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/archetype/standard-mono-white-token-control-dmu#paper)

The hardest matchup, bar none. With all kinds of exile sweepers, lifegain and card draw, unless the opponent stumbles on lands and fails to draw a sweeper, you usually win less and lose more.


### Opening Hands
The best case on the play is 2 lands (one red, one white), 2 to 3 creatures (two of them one-drops), and the remaining three cards in any mix, which is usually a great start.
If you have both [Heartfire Hero](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/138/heartfire-hero) and [Flowerfoot Swordmaster](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/14/flowerfoot-swordmaster) on turn one, play the former first, since its pressure on turn two will be very strong; even if the opponent taps a land on turn one and I have [Sheltered by Ghosts](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/30/sheltered-by-ghosts), I'd still choose to cast it on an otherwise empty [Heartfire Hero](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/138/heartfire-hero), protecting the creature while also giving me more options on turn three. Also, if I have [Manifold Mouse](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/143/manifold-mouse) on turn three together with [Monstrous Rage](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/142/monstrous-rage), I can give double strike and deal 14 damage while gaining 14 life! As long as the opponent doesn't sweep the board, that's basically a win.

On the draw you get to see one extra card, but since this deck has no empty-board burst in the main deck and everything needs to pair with a creature on the battlefield, having two or more creatures in the opening hand is crucial, and in particular whether [Flowerfoot Swordmaster](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/14/flowerfoot-swordmaster) is in hand matters a lot. After that it depends on whether tricks like [Shardmage's Rescue](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/29/shardmages-rescue) can block the opponent's removal, and when [Monstrous Rage](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/142/monstrous-rage) and [Sheltered by Ghosts](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/30/sheltered-by-ghosts) show up.

### The Most Memorable Match

The match that left the deepest impression was game one of the quarterfinals against the semifinal spot, where I was on the draw and my opponent, on the play, mulliganed to two.
My opening hand was [Flowerfoot Swordmaster](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/14/flowerfoot-swordmaster), [Shardmage's Rescue](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/29/shardmages-rescue), [Monstrous Rage](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/142/monstrous-rage), [Might of the Meek](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/144/might-of-the-meek), [Cavern of Souls](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/269/cavern-of-souls), [Battlefield Forge](https://scryfall.com/card/otc/271/battlefield-forge) and [Mountain](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/274/mountain). On turn one I played [Cavern of Souls](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/269/cavern-of-souls), named Mouse, and passed.
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Boros-Mice/3de29dc399d8.webp)

I did this because I wasn't sure I'd keep having creatures later, so I planned to cast [Flowerfoot Swordmaster](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/14/flowerfoot-swordmaster) with offspring on turn three to get two creatures on the battlefield; at the same time, with [Cavern of Souls](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/269/cavern-of-souls) in play, I made sure the opponent's counterspells were basically useless. Very luckily, I drew three gas in a row over the next three turns, and with [Flowerfoot Swordmaster](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/14/flowerfoot-swordmaster) continuing to pressure the opponent, I won.

Besides the opponent's mulligan to two and my good draws afterward, my choice to endure the first two turns so that the board could keep applying pressure was also key to the win. Looking at their hand afterward, they were indeed holding a removal spell; had I chosen to play [Flowerfoot Swordmaster](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/14/flowerfoot-swordmaster) on turn one and gotten it killed, and then drawn no creatures, I would have basically lost that game. I think choosing to play slowly and only using offspring on turn three laid the foundation for this win, and let me respond more calmly in the later matches, finally reaching my first qualification.

## Conclusion
On my road with Guildmages' Forum I've met a lot of helpful people, especially since coming back this year, and having friends to invite out for a game is truly the happiest thing for a card player!

However, the most important thing in playing is how you adjust your mindset. Although there are wins and losses along the way, that's part of improving; once you've decided to play a tournament, steady practice is definitely the key, and the opinions of friends and seniors can also save you a lot of detours (I highly recommend MTGA; best-of-three practice is a blessing for introverts!)

I also hope this article can give newcomers like me, who only came back this year or just joined, some confidence: believe in your deck and practice steadily, and you will still reap rewards!
