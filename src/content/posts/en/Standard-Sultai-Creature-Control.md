## The Control Deck's Plight in the Current Format

Today's Standard is, you could say, tied with the [Ikoria](https://scryfall.com/sets/iko) era as the hardest time for control in the past ten years. And unlike last year, there is no clear format-warping deck waiting to be hit by the ban list. If you sort the current Standard by deck name and look at win rates and metagame share, everything sits in a balanced state, which also means the existing matchups will not die off on their own any time soon <font color="#A6A6A6">(and if one really does die, it only means a stronger opponent has shown up)</font>. You have to face these problems head-on.

So why is it so hard for control to survive right now? Simply put, modern threats are both efficient and tricky. Many decks are blazing fast while still generating plenty of resources, and the types of threats are very broad too. Yet compared with how quickly threats have inflated, answers have grown only a tiny bit. If you try to keep up with the threats' tempo, your removal runs into range problems and many boards become unanswerable; if you make your answers broad enough, they may be too clunky and your removal comes too late to use.

The interaction design of traditional control was basically to stuff the main deck with enough counterspells and creature removal to get decent versatility. Lots of non-creature threats used to be uncommon, and the main-deck counterspells could handle them to a degree, so that was a problem you could leave to the sideboard. That mindset no longer applies today, for two reasons:

1: Key non-creature cards have increased a lot. In many mainstream decks you can see non-creature cards that kill you outright.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/06b037169818.webp)

2: The format keeps speeding up, while versatile counterspells have basically not improved in the past two years. A one-for-one 3-mana Counterspell is completely unplayable now, and even 2-mana counters are often too slow on the draw. The strongest counterspell in Standard, [Three Steps Ahead](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/75/three-steps-ahead), also trades tempo for resources, so it works only as a late-game finisher rather than a way to hold off early pressure. Not to mention that the format has [Voice of Victory](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/33/voice-of-victory) and [Cavern of Souls](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/269/cavern-of-souls), great counterspell-killers. So pure counterspells can no longer serve as the main defensive plan; they are only a supplementary tool for specific problems.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/b3f5bfdb298c.webp)





## Analyzing the Needs

Let's first re-analyze which key threats in the common matchups of the current metagame deserve attention, and what answers they demand.

> - Primary answer needs: the deck's top design priorities
> - Secondary answer needs: nice to cover as well, but not mandatory

If a deck's key plan already falls within the scope of a traditional control deck's main-deck interaction <font color="#A6A6A6">(for example, red aggro)</font>, then regardless of the matchup I won't discuss it in this section, and will cover it together with the sideboard plans later.

> PS: For readability, some individual cards are referred to by nicknames or short names rather than their full translated names.



### Izzet Prowess: Stormchasers' Talent + Boomerang Loop

![1](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/da62694c05bf.webp)

The prowess deck's creature damage is not much of a problem for control by itself. The key is the loop of [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent) + [Boomerang Basics](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/46/boomerang-basics), which lets it quickly generate a huge amount of resources without running out of follow-up ammunition. Compared with a high-powered threat that can be answered one-for-one without losing card advantage, a threat that keeps supplying staying power like this is what gives control the biggest headache.

So enchantment removal is absolutely necessary, and ideally at instant speed. Otherwise, if the opponent has enough spare mana and enough caution, they can choose to cast [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent) only on their own turn, bounce it back with [Boomerang Basics](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/46/boomerang-basics), and just hold it in hand. Also, instant-speed enchantment removal can destroy [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent) in response to the opponent declaring a level up, wasting a lot of their mana and breaking their tempo. Instant-speed graveyard exile can also stop [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent)'s level 2 graveyard recursion, but that is more hit-or-miss: as long as [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent) is still on the battlefield the risk remains, let alone that it has the option of reaching level 3 and winning off a single card. At control's pace, games going that deep are quite common.

Also, in matchups against prowess, [High Noon](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/15/high-noon) has the destructive power of a game-winning single card, and since prowess is popular enough to deserve special respect, being able to play [High Noon](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/15/high-noon) is an effective bonus.

Since this [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent) + [Boomerang Basics](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/46/boomerang-basics) package appears in many blue decks, every deck that has invested in [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent) + [Boomerang Basics](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/46/boomerang-basics) has the same needs, so I won't list them again below.

> - Primary answer needs: instant-speed enchantment removal
> - Secondary answer needs: graveyard exile, [High Noon](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/15/high-noon)



### Izzet Lessons: Monument to Endurance

![2](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/7aa4e73c8214.webp)

The current mainstream Lessons deck wins mainly through [Monument to Endurance](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/237/monument-to-endurance), with the [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent) + [Boomerang Basics](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/46/boomerang-basics) package providing some extra support, so the key is having an answer to [Monument to Endurance](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/237/monument-to-endurance). On top of that, [Monument to Endurance](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/237/monument-to-endurance)'s discard triggers come largely from [Artist's Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/124/artists-talent), so although enchantment removal is less direct than artifact removal that just destroys [Monument to Endurance](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/237/monument-to-endurance), it can still hold it back considerably.

The Lessons package itself also relies on stuffing the graveyard to work, so exiling the graveyard causes some trouble for Lessons too. But honestly, for control, no matter how much [Combustion Technique](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/128/combustion-technique)'s removal range improves it makes little difference; the only thing that really matters is [Accumulate Wisdom](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/44/accumulate-wisdom)'s one-time card draw, so the pressure to watch the graveyard is not that heavy.

> - Primary answer needs: artifact and enchantment removal
> - Secondary answer needs: graveyard exile



### Green Landfall: Sapling Nursery + Ba Sing Se

![3](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/bfb0227156ec.webp)

The Landfall deck I mean here is specifically the version that runs [Sapling Nursery](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/192/sapling-nursery). For control, whether or not it has this card calls for two completely different answer plans. If the Landfall deck relies on its creature cards themselves for damage, that is exactly what a traditional control deck's removal should handle. Once [Sapling Nursery](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/192/sapling-nursery) is added, enchantment removal becomes an absolute must.

Speaking of powerful enchantments, there is also [Earthbender Ascension](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/175/earthbender-ascension). Its destructive power shows mainly when racing other proactive decks, or against decks like [White Token Control](https://guildmagesforum.tw/Standard-MITQ11-Mono-White-Token-Control/) that build a blocking plan by laying out lots of small chump blockers. For ordinary control, dealing with it is not much different from dealing with any other strong threat.

Another big feature of the Landfall matchup is how to answer earthbend. Just the share of Landfall decks and the special nature of earthbend are enough to determine that you must run either [Ultima](https://scryfall.com/card/fin/38/ultima) or [Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up) as your sweeper slot. The biggest killer among the earthbend cards is [Ba Sing Se](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/266/ba-sing-se), which for control is a genuine one-card nightmare. And because it is a land that never becomes a creature on its own, it is harder to remove than any threat, so you need a dedicated answer to it.

> Further reading: [A Few Things About Earthbend](https://guildmagesforum.tw/Something-About-Earthbend/)

Instant-speed graveyard exile like [Ghost Vacuum](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/248/ghost-vacuum) can also suppress the revival of earthbent creatures after ordinary destroy-based removal ([Rest in Peace](https://scryfall.com/card/big/4/rest-in-peace) cannot). However, its greater value here is mainly in suppressing [Icetill Explorer](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/192/icetill-explorer)'s ability to generate resources. Then again, that is also a creature that removal can handle, so graveyard hate does not reach the level of a primary need.

> - Primary answer needs: enchantment removal, [Ultima](https://scryfall.com/card/fin/38/ultima) or [Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up), an answer to [Ba Sing Se](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/266/ba-sing-se)
> - Secondary answer needs: graveyard exile



### Dimir Tempo: Ninjutsu Kaito + Enduring Curiosity

![4](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/66a7675f8b7d.webp)

The main pressure in this matchup basically comes from [Kaito, Bane of Nightmares](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/220/kaito-bane-of-nightmares). The design of this thing is just plain broken, so planeswalker removal is definitely a must.

Another best answer to [Kaito, Bane of Nightmares](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/220/kaito-bane-of-nightmares), and to planeswalkers in general, is always creature-to-creature combat, even with a vanilla creature that has only power and toughness. This idea applies to [Enduring Curiosity](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/51/enduring-curiosity) as well: its existence makes every instance of combat damage an unacceptable wound. Taking a hit from two 1-power creatures is no problem, but falling two cards behind is a big problem. Although control can use removal to keep its life total safe, against lots of cheap, flexible threats, expecting to kill every single one is still too much of a dream, so building your own wall of creatures is ultimately the more direct answer. So if possible, you would like to be like [White Token Control](https://guildmagesforum.tw/Standard-MITQ11-Mono-White-Token-Control/): it follows control logic, but keeps some creatures on the board.

Speaking of answering [Enduring Curiosity](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/51/enduring-curiosity), graveyard exile can do it, but the opponent's entire deck has only one [Enduring Curiosity](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/51/enduring-curiosity) that cares about the graveyard, so you can't specifically watch for it. It is best if the removal itself resolves the problem cleanly. For sweepers, again rely on [Ultima](https://scryfall.com/card/fin/38/ultima) or [Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up), both of which perform very well against Dimir Tempo, and exile-based kill spells are also a way. A second-choice answer is to kill it a second time with enchantment removal, but that basically loses card advantage already, so it is not the default plan.

> - Primary answer needs: planeswalker removal, [Ultima](https://scryfall.com/card/fin/38/ultima) or [Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up)
> - Secondary answer needs: ability to deploy creatures



### Dimir Demons: Mass Discard + Mill Combo

![5.1](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/648612396766.webp)
![5.2](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/426c15ebb49b.webp)

Honestly, this Dimir Demons deck is essentially a control deck with a combo attached, but it is also the biggest killer of traditional control, and it happens to be one of the most popular matchups in the format. You could say it is the greatest source of trouble, but also the source of inspiration for the final breakthrough.

First, the most lethal threat is that this deck has far too much discard. The combination of [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) + [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) is enough to keep any reactive deck from carrying out its defensive plan. And it is not simple one-for-one discard: after discarding, it can leave behind a creature that wins the game. For ordinary control, that alone is already unplayable.

Against the [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) + [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) discard combination, the most direct method is to shut off its ETBs. Cards that can do this are [Torpor Orb](https://scryfall.com/card/big/27/torpor-orb) and [Doorkeeper Thrull](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/13/doorkeeper-thrull). Although a [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) under [Torpor Orb](https://scryfall.com/card/big/27/torpor-orb) becomes a 2-mana 5/5 threat, which is still quite strong, for control a vanilla body is always better than being discarded from. As for graveyard hate, it only covers half of it: it does stop [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) from reusing [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit), but unless you are [Rest in Peace](https://scryfall.com/card/big/4/rest-in-peace) you may not cover it completely, and the suppression from [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) as a single card still exists.
<font color="#A6A6A6">(Making the player hexproof would actually be a way too, but the current Standard card pool has no sufficiently practical options.)</font>

The other decks mentioned above essentially only differ in their choices of removal range, but the [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) + [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) combination directly makes control's old reactive defensive logic impossible, so how to get past this is the final real decision point in the deck. <font color="#A6A6A6">(Of course, if you are confident enough in your matchups against the rest of the format, giving up this matchup outright and focusing on dodging it is also a line of thought.)</font>

Second, the Demons combo is very hard to interrupt. Attacking the library is what control fears most, because with so much discard beforehand, even if you have the answer cards you often can't hold them until the moment you need them against the combo. The combo has two stages, and you must have an answer to at least one of them.

Stage one is [Doomsday Excruciator](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/94/doomsday-excruciator)'s ETB exiling your library until only 6 cards remain. Countering the creature spell itself, or messing with its ETB ability, both work. Stage two, after your library is down to 6 cards, is using extra library-attack methods to make your library evaporate faster. Since [Doomsday Excruciator](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/94/doomsday-excruciator) itself makes its controller draw extra cards, what you need to do is withstand its library attacks and let the opponent deck themselves first. [Restless Reef](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/282/restless-reef) can be handled with removal, but if it is [Insatiable Avarice](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/91/insatiable-avarice) making you pay 3 life and draw 3, or the [Outrageous Robbery](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/97/outrageous-robbery) that may be added after sideboarding, that is somewhat impossible to stop, and only counterspells can handle it. Alternatively, you can have your own ways to attack the opponent's library, making them deck themselves faster.

> - Primary answer needs: the deck's very structure itself



### Spider-Man Reanimator: Mass Discard + Graveyard

![6](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/64312ffe3cff.webp)

The biggest problem is actually the same as the Dimir Demons above: what is truly lethal is [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) + [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man). As for the graveyard plan as the finisher, it is relatively a smaller problem for control. After all, even if you fail to watch the graveyard in time, a sweeper can still save you, unlike other proactive decks where, once the opponent overextends onto the board, you can't recover.

Also, in this matchup most of the opponent's operation relies on creature ETB abilities, so [Torpor Orb](https://scryfall.com/card/big/27/torpor-orb) is also a usable answer card.

> - Primary answer needs: graveyard exile, an answer to the [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) + [Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) combination
> - Secondary answer needs: [Torpor Orb](https://scryfall.com/card/big/27/torpor-orb)

### Summary

Looking at the decks listed above, you will find that besides lots of creatures to kill, the demand for enchantment removal is extremely high, and it is something that has to be respected in the main deck. There is also a need to answer planeswalkers and artifacts, so effectively you need removal that reaches permanents in general.

Graveyard hate, on the other hand, while it can disrupt many decks, is rarely against a deck that builds its entire plan on the graveyard, so the pressure does not need to be this heavy.

Because the format has plenty of decks that run on creature ETB abilities, or cast chains of spells in a single turn, being able to play [Torpor Orb](https://scryfall.com/card/big/27/torpor-orb) and [High Noon](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/15/high-noon) can also be a bonus.





## Rebuilding the Deck Structure

If the main removal needs are creatures and enchantments, then the white-based control deck's [Get Lost](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/14/get-lost) seems to fit the requirements perfectly. It also has [Ultima](https://scryfall.com/card/fin/38/ultima), [Rest in Peace](https://scryfall.com/card/big/4/rest-in-peace), [Kutzil's Flanker](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/20/kutzils-flanker), and other answer cards that meet the criteria above. On top of that, [High Noon](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/15/high-noon) can watch the prowess and spell-chain decks, and finishers that dodge your own [Torpor Orb](https://scryfall.com/card/big/27/torpor-orb) to lock the opponent out one-sidedly are easy to find. It all looks so natural, as if traditional Azorius or Jeskai control is still a reasonable answer... and then you discover that you can indeed answer everything, but you run out of answers before the opponent's threats are all dead.

This is a good moment to go back and introduce two important elements that make up a game: **resources** and **tempo**.

- **Resources:** Anything you can use is a resource, including cards in hand, life, mana, and so on. When resources are mentioned below, unless specifically stressed, they refer specifically to the number of usable cards, which is commonly called card advantage.
- **Tempo:** Tempo is the efficiency with which the game shifts toward your deck's game plan. When we discuss efficiency we normally use time as the unit; in MTG this unit can be converted into number of turns, or the mana you need to invest.

For example, for a race-the-life-total aggro deck like red aggro, tempo is the speed at which the opponent's life goes down. Winning on tempo means that even if you have more resources than I do, I finish executing my game plan before you can convert them into an impact on the game. The reverse, a resource advantage, is easier to understand: when both sides have enough mana to cast whatever they like, the player who can cast more cards has an obvious advantage.

Generally, the more proactive a deck is, the more it values tempo, while control usually has no tempo requirements of its own, and even sacrifices tempo in exchange for a resource advantage. When one player holds the advantage in a game, they usually lead in at least one of tempo or resources, and possibly both.

### Control's Tempo and Resources

Let's first look at what attributes control's usual tools have.

![Spot removal](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/8b723a52910b.webp)

Flexible, long-range removal is control's capital for wrecking the opponent's tempo, but note that no matter how wide the range of this removal is, the card advantage is always 0: you trade one card for one of the opponent's. One-for-one counterspells follow the same logic as these single-target removal spells.

![Sweepers](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/45059c56c3e9.webp)

Sweepers are the key reason control works at all. These are what truly let control break the opponent's tempo while gaining card advantage for itself. Conversely, if a sweeper cannot even interrupt the opponent's tempo, then our game plan already has a problem.

![Card draw](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/65192163930f.webp)

These two card-draw spells can be counted among the strongest in the past ten years of Standard. But note that although their look depth is very high, the card advantage is still only +1. You must make good use of this depth to find the cards that precisely counter the opponent or widen your advantage, and that is what lays the foundation for creating more card advantage later.

Another important concept is that simply drawing cards has no effect on the battlefield, so when the opponent has already established a threat on the board, this is the standard case of selling off tempo to buy resources.



### The Opponents' Tempo and Resources

Now let's briefly look at what the matchups introduced above actually look like:

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/da62694c05bf.webp)

- **[Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent) + [Boomerang Basics](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/46/boomerang-basics):**

**Tempo:** All of it is 1-mana cards.
**Resources:** The first [Boomerang Basics](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/46/boomerang-basics) returning [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent) creates +1 card advantage, and after that, once the level 2 loop of [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent) starts, the card advantage is capped only by when it draws out its library.



{% mtgpick "dsk" 220 %}

- **[Kaito, Bane of Nightmares](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/220/kaito-bane-of-nightmares):**

**Tempo:** Effectively a 3-mana curve, and on the turn it enters via ninjutsu it is nearly unanswerable. You usually have to wait until your next turn to deal with it, which indirectly compresses the tempo budget available to us. Choosing to make an emblem can keep pushing the damage-racing tempo.
**Resources:** On the ninjutsu turn it guarantees +1 card advantage, and every following turn it can keep drawing cards and snowballing.



{% mtgpick "dsk" 51 %}

- **[Enduring Curiosity](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/51/enduring-curiosity):**

**Tempo:** Although it costs 4, flash lets it appear at exactly the right time to maintain its own game plan, so its attacking tempo is hard to interrupt.
**Resources:** It draws so many cards I can't be bothered to count. And the fact that it comes back as a noncreature enchantment after dying once is itself an extra resource.



![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/df7fedc21a6d.webp)

- **[Badgermole Cub](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/167/badgermole-cub) decks:**

**Tempo:** Having 6 mana on turn 3 is a common sight, and with a good hand it can even reach 10 mana on turn 3, so there are all kinds of possibilities for the deck to blow up its tempo to the extreme.
**Resources:** Although it does not provide card resources itself, it contributes far too much mana, and that mana can easily be converted into any resource you want. Also, earthbending a land when it enters counts as providing a resource in the number of creatures on the board.



### Comparing Threat and Removal Performance

After actually comparing them, you find an interesting problem. Although the attackers' tempo is getting more and more out of control, in terms of removal range and cost alone, answers can still barely keep up. The opponents' advantage is tempo, but that is not what kills us. Rather, even in the amount of resources, which control is supposed to be good at, we can't match the opponents.

Look at the various threats listed above: with strong proactive tempo as a given, their resource totals are no less than those of control decks that focus on card-draw spells. Of course, because control looks deeper into its library, each card control draws has a higher expected value at the same +1 card advantage, but once the card advantage gap widens further, that expected-value edge is meaningless. After all, looking at 4 and taking 2 can never beat just drawing 4.

And don't forget that these threats already exist on the battlefield for real, while the cards you draw have no effect on the board. So even if both sides are +1 in card advantage, the opponent has conjured a battlefield tempo advantage out of thin air, and when you want to deal with the threats on the battlefield, you must invest even more tempo and resources. Going back and forth like this, control has lost who knows how much.

You could say that in this era, playing a purely reactive deck is truly thankless.



### Why Do Dimir Demons Survive?

Among the top-tier decks right now, Dimir Demons is the only one built on a control framework. So why is it, despite having a control base and lacking the many excellent white removal and hate cards, that its win rate is better across the board than traditional Azorius or Jeskai control?

Having a hard-to-interact-with winning combo is of course one reason, but the components for this combo have existed in Standard for a long time already, so the real key is not the finisher but the ability to survive long enough to go for the win.

![Creatures](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/ab5a20fd828d.webp)

[Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) is a groundbreaking powerhouse <font color="#A6A6A6">(honestly, I think it's a bit too strong...)</font>. This kind of flexible function, giving a high-cost finisher another low-cost way to be used, is exactly what control wants, equivalent to getting a free finisher slot without sacrificing the low end of the curve. Both of [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit)'s ETBs fit control's needs very well, and when they trigger together they are effectively a universal answer to any nonland permanent <font color="#A6A6A6">(bounce it, then discard it)</font>. This already somewhat breaks the color pie: it gives Dimir, which originally struggled against artifacts and enchantments, an ability equivalent to white's universal removal. Once paired with [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man), the destructive power changes qualitatively, as I have mentioned many times above.

[Harvester of Misery](https://scryfall.com/card/big/9/harvester-of-misery) should not be underestimated either. Although -2/-2 range at 2 mana looks lousy, the creatures in the current metagame are developing toward two extremes, and against the fast end, -2 range is actually enough. And like [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit), it is a low-cost play that can also be a high-cost finisher, and it can be copied by [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man). The foundation built from these three cards gives the whole deck <font color="#FF0000">no shortage of creatures that stay on the board</font>, plus a <font color="#FF0000">high density of low-cost answer cards</font>, plus <font color="#FF0000">a wide enough range of answers</font>.

Among these three advantages, which is the most crucial? Actually it is **no shortage of creatures that stay on the board**, and the qualitative change starts from that point. Having creatures means the opponent has to deal not only with the plays we make from hand but also with the creatures on the board, and we gain the options of blocking and trading in combat as well. Even though all of our cards are configured to meet control's needs, we gain the option of a midrange game against the opponent.

The biggest breakthrough that Dimir Demons brings to the control framework is not how much more it has grown in resources, but that, with the same amount of resources, it gained a proactive tempo capability that control never had before.



### Absorbing the Strengths

A framework based on [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) + [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) + [Harvester of Misery](https://scryfall.com/card/big/9/harvester-of-misery) is a good starting point, so next comes the question of how to expand it. 

First, the direction is clearly creature-based control. Since the positioning is still control rather than combo, we don't need [Doomsday Excruciator](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/94/doomsday-excruciator), and among the card-draw spells we need not consider [Insatiable Avarice](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/91/insatiable-avarice) or other components specialized for the combo. Instead we pursue creatures with enough disruption, board-clearing, and staying power. Since the creatures themselves are win conditions, there is no need to separately think about who the finisher will be.

The original Dimir Demons gained broad board-clearing ability through [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit), but there are far too many weird permanents in the format now, and relying on [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) alone is pushing the limit. This is the time to consider adding a color, and when I reviewed the answer needs listed above (a format of creatures with enchantments attached), I suddenly realized that after adding green, [Fangkeeper's Familiar](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/183/fangkeepers-familiar) perfectly satisfies all my needs! It happens to be an answer card that handles creatures and enchantments at the same time. What's more, Sultai itself is a color combination with high board-clearing range, so finding all kinds of answers later is easy.

{% mtgpick "tdm" 183 %}

Choosing the [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) + [Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) package also brought another benefit: it solves the earlier question of how to beat Dimir Demons. Since we are both on [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) + [Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man), we are on even footing, with no matchup problem. Even better, because I also have [Restless Reef](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/282/restless-reef), if the opponent dares to cast [Doomsday Excruciator](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/94/doomsday-excruciator) without leaving mana up, they could die to their own combo.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/b0eec32f06bd.webp)

Creature-based control is also half an answer to the Landfall deck's [Ba Sing Se](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/266/ba-sing-se). Originally, as long as control couldn't answer [Ba Sing Se](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/266/ba-sing-se), it would be freeloaded to death by the threats it conjures from nothing, but now that I have a board presence, I have more capital to fight it out in both offense and defense.

## Deck Introduction

### [Decklist Link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7661153#paper)
![DeckList](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/455029059b88.webp)



### Creatures

![Creatures](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/ab5a20fd828d.webp)

I've already spent a great deal of ink on this core trio above, so I won't belabor it, but there are still a few details worth mentioning.

- **[Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) (4):**

The two ETB abilities, bouncing and discarding, trigger separately. Suppose the opponent responds with an ability counter such as [Tishana's Tidebinder](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/81/tishanas-tidebinder) or [Spider-Sense](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/46/spider-sense): one card can only counter one ability. And because these are two independent triggered abilities, you can put things on the stack between them. Suppose you choose to resolve the bounce first on an opposing creature with flash, and then the discard. Before the discard resolves, the opponent has a chance to flash it back onto the battlefield.

If you run into [Torpor Orb](https://scryfall.com/card/big/27/torpor-orb), none of [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit)'s ETBs will trigger, including the sacrifice trigger on entering when you cast it with evoke.

- **[Harvester of Misery](https://scryfall.com/card/big/9/harvester-of-misery) (3):**

It is very important against opponents who tend to field lots of small creatures, such as Izzet Prowess and Dimir Tempo. Often you kill one on turn 2, then on turn 4 copy it with [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) and sweep again, and the opponent's offense will crash completely. However, the -2 range is still a bit short, and 3 copies is the limit; any more and it gets stuck far too easily.

- **[Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) (4):**

When you cast it, you don't need to declare what you plan to copy, nor choose any creature card in a graveyard as a target. Only once it is confirmed to enter the battlefield do you choose which creature it enters as a copy of.

Also, although [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man)'s copy effect keeps the name Superior Spider-Man and modifies a bit of the creature subtypes and power/toughness, everything else is overwritten with the copied creature's information, including whether it has the legendary supertype. So as long as you don't have two [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) both copying legendary creatures, you won't hit the legend rule problem; one legendary and one nonlegendary creature with the same name are not subject to the legend rule.




![Creatures](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/8f217754e791.webp)

- **[Fangkeeper's Familiar](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/183/fangkeepers-familiar) (2):**

Its three abilities fit the current format perfectly. Almost every opponent runs on creatures and enchantments, and many creatures do their work as soon as they enter, so being able to counter them outright is the cleanest answer. Although I did say earlier that a defensive plan relying on counterspells can't be the main plan now, adding a few as support is still very useful, and this isn't a plain one-for-one reactive card but a play that can gain card advantage.

Because it also has the option of surveil 3 and draw 3, this guy will never be a dead card, and when you want to be proactive it can provide proactive tempo as well. Surveil 3 is also no small depth, and may even pair with other graveyard-using cards in the deck to effectively gain cards.

In fact, I originally conceived the deck around a full playset of [Fangkeeper's Familiar](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/183/fangkeepers-familiar), but since [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) also takes up the 4-mana curve slot, and this deck's Draw-Go ability is not as strong as traditional control's, always leaving 4 mana up is not realistic for me, so I weighed the balance and settled on 2 copies.

- **[Quantum Riddler](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/72/quantum-riddler) (2):**

A powerhouse in every format; its strength needs no further comment. Originally the configuration was 1 [Quantum Riddler](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/72/quantum-riddler) + 1 [Wistfulness](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/252/wistfulness), but after testing for a while I found that the main deck seemed to respect artifacts and enchantments a bit too much, so I changed it directly to more stable card draw.

- **[Sab-Sunen, Luxa Embodied](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/221/sab-sunen-luxa-embodied) (1):**

Almost every opponent will pick this card up and read it for ten seconds.

Its role is essentially the same as [Quantum Riddler](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/72/quantum-riddler): both are 5-mana creatures with a built-in resource engine. But [Sab-Sunen, Luxa Embodied](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/221/sab-sunen-luxa-embodied) is nearly unanswerable for many decks, and even if they have answers, there may only be a few cards. Meanwhile, by repeatedly playing [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) plus other regular discard spells, I may be able to discard more than three times the number of the opponent's answers. With indestructible, it also leaves me with a powerful finisher after I finish sweeping.

Note, however, that [Sab-Sunen, Luxa Embodied](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/221/sab-sunen-luxa-embodied) can declare an attack or block only when it has an even number of counters, which sometimes opens a hole in your defenses, but overall the pros outweigh the cons. The [Blight](https://guildmagesforum.tw/ECL-Mechanism/#%E8%A1%B0%E8%90%8E%EF%BC%88Blight%EF%BC%89) from [Requiting Hex](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/116/requiting-hex) can be used at instant speed to adjust the number of counters on [Sab-Sunen, Luxa Embodied](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/221/sab-sunen-luxa-embodied).




### Card Draw

![Card draw](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/ecccf78477d0.webp)

Although this is creature-based control, relying entirely on creatures to draw cards would be a bit too clunky, so I did include a few card-draw spells.

- **[Rakshasa's Bargain](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/214/rakshasas-bargain) (4):**

Using the control staples [Stock Up](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/67/stock-up) and [Consult the Star Charts](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/51/consult-the-star-charts) in this slot is also viable, but in my view [Rakshasa's Bargain](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/214/rakshasas-bargain)'s 3-mana instant nature better fits this deck's requirements. First, because the deck packs so many creatures, its Draw-Go ability has already dropped, so I want the remaining spells to raise the instant-speed share as much as possible. This deck's 4-mana curve is rather heavy as well, and especially an on-curve turn-4 [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) is often the most dominant play, so [Rakshasa's Bargain](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/214/rakshasas-bargain) lets you stagger the curve. The graveyard filling it comes with may even conjure copy targets for [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) out of nothing.

Also, although [Rakshasa's Bargain](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/214/rakshasas-bargain) can be cast for 3 mana, a hybrid mana cost is counted by its higher side when calculating mana value, so this is a 6-mana-value card, and a single card can satisfy [Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up)'s collect evidence requirement.

- **[Winternight Stories](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/67/winternight-stories) (1):**

The deck is full of high-power creatures and graveyard-filling abilities, so [Winternight Stories](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/67/winternight-stories) has plenty of room to perform, and it is especially wonderful with a [Warp](https://guildmagesforum.tw/EOE-Mechanism/#%E8%BA%8D%E9%81%B7%EF%BC%88Warp%EF%BC%89)ed [Quantum Riddler](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/72/quantum-riddler). But there are really no slots left, so one copy is about right.




### Removal

![Removal 1](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/ffe8ec9641ab.webp)

- **[Requiting Hex](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/116/requiting-hex) (3):**

The strongest 1-mana removal in Standard black. Although its range only reaches 2-mana-value creatures, in the current format a full playset would be perfectly fine, it's just that the slots are a bit hard to squeeze out, and 3 copies seems sufficient for now.

- **[Shoot the Sheriff](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/106/shoot-the-sheriff) (3):**

Although you now bump into [Outlaw](https://guildmagesforum.tw/OTJ-mechanism/#%E7%8B%82%E5%BE%92%EF%BC%88Outlaw%EF%BC%89) creatures all the time, overall [Shoot the Sheriff](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/106/shoot-the-sheriff) is still the most elegant-range 2-mana black removal with no drawback, and most of the Outlaws you see now are ones [Requiting Hex](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/116/requiting-hex) can handle, so the two cover each other's blind spots, more or less.

- **[Bitter Triumph](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/91/bitter-triumph) (1):**

In the past, when building control I basically couldn't accept [Bitter Triumph](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/91/bitter-triumph)'s cost, but since this deck has a need to use the graveyard, it's another story. And I really do want to give [Kaito, Bane of Nightmares](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/220/kaito-bane-of-nightmares) a bit more respect.




![Removal 2](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/6147e0c5318e.webp)

- **[Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up) (3):**

Black sweepers now come in two choices, [Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up) and [Day of Black Sun](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/94/day-of-black-sun). Each has its own advantages and neither is the absolute right answer; many Dimir Demons decks also mix both in the main deck.

Here I went with all [Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up), partly out of personal preference, since I have always loved using the library-cutting effect. Another reason is that I also run an indestructible [Sab-Sunen, Luxa Embodied](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/221/sab-sunen-luxa-embodied) and stuffed in a pile of cards that can quickly supply collect-evidence ammunition, which makes [Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up) feel a bit more in line with the deck's main idea.

That said, [Day of Black Sun](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/94/day-of-black-sun)'s advantage against fast decks is obvious too, and it has a high enough return on investment in the current format, so readers can adjust the ratio according to their own preferences and understanding of the metagame.

- **[Awaken the Honored Dead](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/170/awaken-the-honored-dead) (3):**

Although it is a clunky card to cast, the stability it brings is excellent. In this age where the permanent types you need to handle are all over the place, I very much want a universal permanent removal in the main deck, and [Awaken the Honored Dead](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/170/awaken-the-honored-dead) does that very well. Its ability to move creatures around in the graveyard also raises the flexibility of my deck, which uses creatures as spells.

Also, with [Awaken the Honored Dead](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/170/awaken-the-honored-dead) added, this deck has a dream curve: turn 2 [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) to discard => turn 3 [Awaken the Honored Dead](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/170/awaken-the-honored-dead) to clear the board => turn 4 [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) copying [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit), discarding a card from the opponent and bouncing [Awaken the Honored Dead](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/170/awaken-the-honored-dead) to hand, keeping one more universal removal for later.

### Lands

Because this is a three-color deck with extremely heavy color requirements, the mana fixing needs are very demanding. I could only squeeze in 4 creature-lands (or utility lands), and the remaining slots must be filled entirely with typed dual lands + Verges.

![Creature lands](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/9e1e6f94a896.webp)

- **[Restless Reef](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/282/restless-reef) (3):**

The key to fighting Dimir Demons. Normally the opponent isn't dumb either; knowing you can mill them, they won't recklessly cast [Doomsday Excruciator](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/94/doomsday-excruciator) without an answer in hand, but being able to forcibly hold back their combo is already a big contribution in itself. What's more, [Restless Reef](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/282/restless-reef)'s overall quality is among the best of Standard's two-color creature lands, and its raw card strength is beyond reproach.

By the role it plays, it should really be a full playset, but doing so would make balancing the overall fixing needs somewhat troublesome later.

- **[Restless Cottage](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/258/restless-cottage) (1):**

Since running all [Restless Reef](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/282/restless-reef) would skew the mana fixing a bit, the fourth creature-land slot goes to [Restless Cottage](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/258/restless-cottage). Its strength is also beyond reproach, and having one more graveyard-hate tool is quite useful in the current format.


- **Verges:**
    - [Gloomlake Verge](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/260/gloomlake-verge) (4)
    - [Wastewood Verge](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/268/wastewood-verge) (3)
    - [Willowrush Verge](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/270/willowrush-verge) (2)

The three Sultai Verges have an extremely awkward point: without a typed land, none of them can produce black, so once you keep a hand with a Verge but no typed land, it is very easy to die outright. After testing, 9 Verges seems to be a relatively safe number; if you go Verge +1 and typed land -1, you feel the rate of mana accidents rise considerably.

- **Typed lands:**
    - [Watery Grave](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/261/watery-grave) (4)
    - [Overgrown Tomb](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/266/overgrown-tomb) (4)
    - [Breeding Pool](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/251/breeding-pool) (2)
    - [Hedge Maze](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/262/hedge-maze) (1)
    - Basic Swamp (1)
    - Basic Island (1)

Black is the color with the highest color requirement in this deck, and there is often a need to cast [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) on turn 2 using {B}{B}, so all shock lands with black must be maxed out.

[Hedge Maze](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/262/hedge-maze) is not essential; swapping it directly for a third [Breeding Pool](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/251/breeding-pool) is fine too. I just figured that with so many shock lands you can't always afford to pay the life and will often choose to have them enter tapped, so I'd rather play a land that still gives some benefit when it enters tapped. Considering there are already 4 creature-lands, I habitually keep truly tapped-only lands to 5 or fewer, which is why I run one Hedge Maze.

Finally, since I may still run into [Demolition Field](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/687/demolition-field), and shock lands cannot unconditionally enter untapped, I also included a few basic lands. This deck's need for green is very low and it is mainly Dimir overall, so I don't run a basic Forest.

Also, we can use [Requiting Hex](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/116/requiting-hex) to put counters on her ourselves; if the board conditions are right, we may keep the hexproof counter while also tearing apart the opponent's board.

- **[Wistfulness](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/252/wistfulness) (1):**

### Izzet Prowess

![Izzet Prowess](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/a82bb05cb09e.webp)

| Out | In | 
| :-: | :-: | 
| Quantum Riddler -1 | Duress +2 | 
| Bitter Triumph -1 | Heritage Reclamation +1 | 
| Awaken the Honored Dead -1 | Wistfulness +1 | 
| Winternight Stories -1 | Urgent Necropsy +1 | 
| Deadly Cover-Up -1 |  | 


Tempo is extremely important in this matchup, and [Harvester of Misery](https://scryfall.com/card/big/9/harvester-of-misery) is the key piece that is both offense and defense. [Fangkeeper's Familiar](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/183/fangkeepers-familiar) also doesn't have to be held in hand the whole game as a counterspell/discard; sometimes when the opponent hits a tempo break, you can flash it in directly, surveil 3 and draw 3, which gains life and puts an extra creature on the board, and that is enough to give the opponent a headache later. When necessary, simply flashing it in as a chump blocker is also fine. Not every hit of prowess is equal in value, and if you can withstand the hit that cost it the most spell resources to set up, you may throw its tempo plan into disarray.

It's true that [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent)'s long-game resource ability is very strong, but usually once we begin building a creature defense we are able to intercept the opponent's offense. So sometimes in the early-to-mid game, when the opponent has [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent) plus the Otter token it makes on the board, [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) can first target [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent), choosing to bounce the Otter token and discard a card from the opponent. This wave of disruption usually seriously interrupts the opponent's tempo, and catching the opponent in the gap while they rebuild after the damage lets you further solidify your defenses and begin dealing with the opponent's real source of resource staying power.

The answer to [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent) + [Boomerang Basics](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/46/boomerang-basics) is to tear down [Stormchasers' Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent) directly with enchantment removal, so there's no need to bring in graveyard hate especially.

The reason for taking out [Bitter Triumph](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/91/bitter-triumph) is that in this matchup its cost is a bit too painful. That said, the Izzet sideboard may show [Ral, Crackling Wit](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/230/ral-crackling-wit), and if you feel the current removal handles [Ral, Crackling Wit](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/230/ral-crackling-wit) too stiffly, you can also change it to a second [Shoot the Sheriff](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/106/shoot-the-sheriff).

### Dimir Demons

![Dimir Demons](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Creature-Control/426c15ebb49b.webp)

| Out | In | 
| :-: | :-: | 
| Harvester of Misery -3 | Duress +2 | 
| Requiting Hex -2 | Intimidation Tactics +1 | 
| Awaken the Honored Dead -3 | Disdainful Stroke +1 | 
|  | Essence Scatter +1 | 
|  | Tishana's Tidebinder +1 | 
|  | Leatherhead, Swamp Stalker +1 | 
|  | Wistfulness +1 | 

With both sides on [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) + [Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) canceling out that part of the matchup, this actually becomes a favorable matchup for us, especially once the sweeper slots start leaning on [Day of Black Sun](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/94/day-of-black-sun), because we have more creature attackers while the opponent's key mill win condition is awkward and restrictive to use. If they dare to cast [Doomsday Excruciator](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/94/doomsday-excruciator) without leaving enough room, their own library may evaporate first, but holding it forever makes it a dead card, and they can only keep taking hits passively.

The very key to winning this matchup is how to turn the opponent's [Doomsday Excruciator](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/94/doomsday-excruciator) against them. Since [Doomsday Excruciator](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/94/doomsday-excruciator) comes with forced card draw, as long as it isn't about to kill you, you don't have to rush to kill it. Based on this, how many multiples of 6 your own life total is becomes a key parameter. While watching the opponent's library-attack methods, you can let [Doomsday Excruciator](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/94/doomsday-excruciator) cut the opponent's library once or twice first, then remove it, and after that just wait to see the opponent deck themselves.

The opponent's library attacks mainly come from these:
- [Restless Reef](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/282/restless-reef): Not hard to handle if you hold instant-speed removal. The reason I kept one [Requiting Hex](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/116/requiting-hex) is also to watch the opponent's [Restless Reef](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/282/restless-reef).
- [Insatiable Avarice](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/91/insatiable-avarice): If you get the chance to see it earlier with [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit), it is actually a fairly high discard priority, and as long as [Insatiable Avarice](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/91/insatiable-avarice) may still remain in the opponent's library and hand, [Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up) can choose to pull all of them out. After sideboarding there may be an extra [Outrageous Robbery](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/97/outrageous-robbery), but usually at most one is run, so discard it if you can; if it is really hiding in the library there's not much you can do, and you can only see whether your [Disdainful Stroke](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/47/disdainful-stroke) can hold it.

Also, in this matchup whoever does more [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) ETBs has an overwhelming advantage. So when we currently have no [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) to cast but [Deceit](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/212/deceit) is already in our graveyard, [Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up) can choose to over-collect evidence and pull all the creatures in our graveyard that the opponent's [Superior Spider-Man](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/155/superior-spider-man) could use. If we can't use them, at least we avoid giving them to the opponent.

## Conclusion

Although this deck is named control, and the cards were chosen following control thinking, in essence it is already approaching midrange. Having [Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up) in the main deck is almost the last reason the deck can still be positioned as control.

This also reflects the design characteristics of cards in this new era <font color="#A6A6A6">~~(the problem)~~</font>. In the past it was hard for a card to have the best of both resources and tempo, so control, even giving up tempo entirely, could still be a viable way to win on resources alone. But now that there are all kinds of cards that can seize excellent proactive tempo while also gaining plenty of resources, control that exhausts all its effort to gain resources no longer has an advantaged position. You could even say that over the past year, control's relatively standout results came roughly when the format had been poisoned by a few cards that wouldn't survive the next ban list, to the point where no card could compare with their proactive plans, and that is what gave a control deck focused on answering them a relatively decent record.

So for the current game environment, if you still insist on playing control, my final solution is to turn it around and increase your own proactive board-establishing ability.
