## Deck Introductions

### Zur Domain
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6648940#paper)
![DeckList](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DSK-Standard-Preliminary/15eb3793bb18.webp)

The Overlords' impending is an alternative cost. It only changes what you pay to cast the spell and does not change the spell's own mana value, so when you impend [Overlord of the Hauntwoods](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/194/overlord-of-the-hauntwoods) for 3 or [Overlord of the Boilerbilges](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/146/overlord-of-the-boilerbilges) for 4, [Up the Beanstalk](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/195/up-the-beanstalk) can still draw a card.

The token lands made by [Overlord of the Hauntwoods](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/194/overlord-of-the-hauntwoods) have every basic land type, so a single card unlocks Domain. It doesn't matter that an impending Overlord isn't a creature, because [Zur, the Eternal Schemer](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/228/zur-eternal-schemer) can turn them straight into creatures to start beating down.

This time I tried swapping [Archangel of Wrath](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/3/archangel-of-wrath) for [Overlord of the Boilerbilges](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/146/overlord-of-the-boilerbilges), which pairs better with both Beanstalk and Zur. Zur can give lifelink to the enchantment creatures, which also fills the life-gain job originally handled by [Archangel of Wrath](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/3/archangel-of-wrath).

![Single card](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DSK-Standard-Preliminary/8747bbb225bb.webp)

### Mono White Token
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6648896#paper)
![DeckList](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DSK-Standard-Preliminary/d3cb0afa53c3.webp)

Since [Caretaker's Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/6/caretakers-talent) was born, white has suddenly replaced blue as the color most associated with drawing cards. [Enduring Innocence](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/6/enduring-innocence) keeps strengthening the existing mode of operation, and [Overlord of the Mistmoors](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/23/overlord-of-the-mistmoors) provides another trigger source for the draw engine, though which is better, it or [Archangel Elspeth](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/6/archangel-elspeth), remains for time to prove.

Also, white-based token control may be the best workplace for [The Wandering Rescuer](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/41/the-wandering-rescuer). Traditional convoke aggro is full of small individual creatures not worth protecting, and has no tempo to spare for flashing in a convoke creature to protect them. Ordinary midrange creatures are valuable enough, but may not have the mana to flash her in. So a token control deck that often stands behind a row of creatures without attacking aggressively is actually where the Rescuer shines. Suddenly flashing in a 3/4 double striker can easily make the opponent's attacking creatures hit a wall.

![Single card](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DSK-Standard-Preliminary/4d668199d1b9.webp)


### Mono Black Aggro
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6648902#paper)
![DeckList](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DSK-Standard-Preliminary/b728267a6d0e.webp)

Deck summary: your opponent asks you to pay twice half your life total ❤️

A turn-three [Unstoppable Slasher](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/119/unstoppable-slasher) into a turn-four [Bloodletter of Aclazotz](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/92/bloodletter-of-aclazotz) is a beautiful curve. However, contrary to its name, [Unstoppable Slasher](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/119/unstoppable-slasher) is actually very easy to block, so there's no need to run it as a dedicated combo piece. Just treat it as a Plan B bolted onto a life-grabbing aggressive deck. When every attacker puts heavy pressure on the opponent to deal with, you can force the opponent into unfavorable trades until they run out of resources, or crush them in one go on the tempo side. Treating [Unstoppable Slasher](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/119/unstoppable-slasher) as a high-power attacker with multiple lives works better.

### Mono Red Leyline Valiant (Leyline RDW)
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6648905#paper)
![DeckList](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DSK-Standard-Preliminary/37988878a31b.webp)

The new red Leyline ([Leyline of Resonance](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/143/leyline-of-resonance)) brings a very high ceiling for a turn-two kill. The deck works like this:
Open with the red Leyline, play [Heartfire Hero](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/138/heartfire-hero) or [Cacophony Scamp](https://scryfall.com/card/one/124/cacophony-scamp) on turn one, and on turn two use [Turn Inside Out](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/160/turn-inside-out) to pump it. With the Leyline's bonus it can reach 7 power as a starting point (if it's [Heartfire Hero](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/138/heartfire-hero), casting [Monstrous Rage](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/142/monstrous-rage) works too, and counting the extra valiant trigger it can also reach 7 power). After landing one hit, use the adventure of [Callous Sell-Sword // Burn Together](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/221/callous-sell-sword-burn-together) to throw it at the opponent. Since these one-drop creatures also deal damage equal to their power when they die, you can deal 7×3=21 damage by turn two.

This is the deck's highest-ceiling scenario, but even without drawing that perfectly, there is a chance to beat the opponent down to the point of being practically dead, or to find a window a few turns later for a one-shot headshot.

With this deck's curve and mana base, the Leyline is nearly a dead card if you don't draw it in your opening hand, and the valiant/heroic archetype is inherently a deck that eats up cards in hand, so the card loss after an aggressive mulligan is very painful. So when I first looked at it in the previews I didn't pay much attention, but after going back and doing the math I started to feel something was off... This is closer to a combo than to aggro. Even after an aggressive mulligan, you can still find an opening hand with a high enough payoff to go for the kill, so how red Leyline decks will affect the metagame is something I'm very worried about.

### Dimir Midrange
[Decklist link](http://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6651393#paper)
![Decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DSK-Standard-Preliminary/78c2d2c1a2ed.webp)

For the new Standard's Dimir Midrange, the individual cards we tried are [Enduring Curiosity](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/51/enduring-curiosity), [Overlord of the Balemurk](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/113/overlord-of-the-balemurk), [Kaito, Bane of Nightmares](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/220/kaito-bane-of-nightmares), and [Floodpits Drowner](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/59/floodpits-drowner). We also added one [Nowhere to Run](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/111/nowhere-to-run) to fill the removal slot. For lands, we added [Gloomlake Verge](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/260/gloomlake-verge), and used two [Undercity Sewers](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/270/undercity-sewers), two [Fabled Passage](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/252/fabled-passage), 1 Island, and 4 Swamps to make sure [Gloomlake Verge](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/260/gloomlake-verge) can produce black.

Worth mentioning is the addition of two copies of BLB's one-mana bounce spell [Into the Flood Maw](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/52/into-the-flood-maw), which gives us more confidence to maneuver against the turn-two kill ceiling of the emerging [Mono Red Leyline Valiant](#mono-red-leyline-valiant-leyline-rdw). When everyone is happily pumping their creatures, that's when the bounce should come out! And against the artifacts/enchantments that are very hard for Dimir to deal with, sometimes gifting the opponent a Fish to buy breathing room is also quite key in some matchups.

Another change worth mentioning is the removal of the two-mana [Deep-Cavern Bat](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/102/deep-cavern-bat). With [Pyroclasm](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/149/pyroclasm) reprinted in DSK, I don't want to lose to a one-for-many sweep against red-based decks.

### Rakdos Sacrifice

### Temur Overlord
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6648937#paper)
![DeckList](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DSK-Standard-Preliminary/5eb180e0817e.webp)

There is a deck that works like this: an ability flips cards from the library until it finds a spell with mana value less than or equal to X and casts it for free. The only legal target in the deck is the card-advantage engine [Up the Beanstalk](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/195/up-the-beanstalk), and the rest of the cards use split cards, adventure cards, alternative costs, and other single cards to make high-cost cards that still have something to do on the low end of the curve. One of them, a red creature with an alternative cost, deals 4 damage when it enters the battlefield.

That's right, this is the new Standard Overlord deck. Modern's Cascade Beanstalk Fury or whatever is all an illusion.

### Jund Lich-Knights' Conquest

## Conclusion

From my personal understanding of the matchups, I actually think the boost Duskmourn gave to red aggro archetypes is very dangerous... 

The first thing previewed was [Screaming Nemesis](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/157/screaming-nemesis). This card nearly single-handedly overturns the traditional answers to red aggro, namely life gain and creature blocking, and it is also a fast haste attacker with good tempo, so I deeply doubt whether this card exceeds what Standard can bear. But then the previews produced a [red Leyline](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/143/leyline-of-resonance) deck that kills you on turn two, and the topic of [Screaming Nemesis](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/157/screaming-nemesis) was set aside for the time being.

In my eyes, the red Leyline archetype of aggro (or should I say combo?) is a hypothetical enemy that deserves special respect in the early metagame. The way to respect it is also simple: in sideboard games, have at least 4 one-mana removal spells in your deck, and if the metagame develops too extremely, even 6 is reasonable (of course, some decks with especially tight slots may find it hard to do this, so do what you can). Only one-mana removal can protect you from being killed in a single turn when you are on the draw. But the red Leyline aggro deck, a single-minded deck that bets on its ceiling, also has obvious weaknesses: once the first wave of attacks is cut off, it may struggle to give strong enough pressure again. So I predict red Leyline aggro will be very popular at first, even squeezing the survival space of a large number of decks, and every deck will be forced to bring plenty of one-mana removal to deal with this problem. But after being fully respected by the metagame, its momentum will gradually fade, and it won't have monopoly-level dominance over the metagame, with traditional red aggro archetypes rising again. As for what to do about the [Screaming Nemesis](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/157/screaming-nemesis) problem by then... leave it to future me.
