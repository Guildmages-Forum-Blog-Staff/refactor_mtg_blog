4.5: Unconditional must-pick if you're in the right colors, brings a huge advantage
4.0: First-pick quality, extremely good fit with the color pair's theme, can be the deciding card to splash / switch colors or to move into the colors
3.5: Top uncommons and commons of the set and other sufficiently strong cards, good fit with the color theme, can be the key card for moving into the colors
3.0: Important cards for the color theme and other above-average cards, you want them in your final deck
2.5: Average, usually a decent curve filler
2.0 to 1.0: Below average, generally not played, can be used as filler if necessary but not too many
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable, basically don't play it, playing it will only make you lose

## Conclusion
Red cards in TDM focus on offensive tempo and high mobility. Low-cost creatures have standard bodies and useful abilities, and combined with the two core mechanics, Flurry and Mobilize, they give red aggro and tempo decks a clear strategic direction.

Although many low-cost creatures are limited in power individually, they effectively apply pressure and trigger Flurry, bringing real-game value beyond the cost curve. Removal and combat tricks lean toward low cost and many restrictions, and need specific mechanics or deck configurations to pay off. The midgame and late game center on Dragons' Storm effects and nonbasic land destruction. Although the activation threshold is high and consistency is slightly low, when it works it has strong suppression and burst, becoming the key to the endgame.

This is my first time writing a limited article, and I hoped to give those unfamiliar with limited more detailed guidance, so I wrote more text describing cards' performance and application scenarios. It can also strengthen your understanding of how decks work before you take part in limited events, and I hope this article gives you a deeper understanding of red decks.
