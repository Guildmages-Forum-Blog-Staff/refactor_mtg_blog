## The Event & My Results

The 2026 Chiba Spotlight was a large international event that gathered top players from everywhere, with 893 players in total. It ran over two days, with 9+1 rounds on Day 1 and 5 rounds on Day 2.

This time I played a 4C Omniscience Combo deck and finished with a record of **10 wins, 4 losses and 1 draw (10-4-1)**, ending the event in **50th place**.

However, behind this report there is a very valuable "control group": at this event, 4 friends of mine who also often play combo entered with exactly the same 75 cards as mine and the same sideboard strategy, yet all 4 of them were eliminated on Day 1.

Besides insufficient practice, the bigger reason for such a gap in results is probably that the underlying logic of this deck was built by me. With such a highly specialized, purpose-built weapon, the pilot needs a near-instinctive grasp of mulligans and tacit knowledge to force open a path to victory in the gap between aggro and control.

## Deck Building Analysis: Give Up Defense, Maximize Engine Density
Here is the MIT#11 deck list (damn, 9th place):
[MIT#11 Bant Omniscience decklist](https://melee.gg/Decklist/View/57333d3e-0759-402b-9df0-b4050040d53e)

Next you can see the deck Lee Shi Tian built, which went 6-4 at the PT with Simic Omniscience:
[PTSOS_Simic Omniscience](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7761360#paper)

He replaced the white removal with the cheaper soft removal {% mtglink "Bounce Off" %}, which I really like, and it also lets you reuse the creature you played early to block or to find {% mtglink "Kona, Rescue Beastie" %}: {% mtglink "Formidable Speaker" %}.

You can see that when building this 75, I chose an extreme but pure path: **minimize interaction and defense, and give the main deck's space entirely to filtering and acceleration.**
The original 7 removal spells in the main deck (3 {% mtglink "Get lost" %}, 2 {% mtglink "Seam Rip" %}, 1 {% mtglink "Day of Judgment" %}, 1 {% mtglink "Pinnacle Starcage" %}) were cut down to 5 (3 {% mtglink "Bounce Off" %}, 1 {% mtglink "Prismari Charm" %}, 1 {% mtglink "Pyroclasm" %}).

The core operation of this deck is to precisely sculpt your hand structure in the fewest turns.

For a pure combo deck, speed and consistency are themselves the strongest defense. Compromising by putting too many single-target removal spells or sweepers in the main deck only drags down the expected value of the combo running on time. And because there is less removal, it's harder to go down a more controlling route like before.

Since the whole point of splashing white was removal, I decisively dropped white; and because I changed the combo's finisher from infinitely casting {% mtglink "Scrapshooter" %} to finishing with {% mtglink "North Wind Avatar" %}, I switched to splashing red and mixed in two red removal spells for defense.

As for the filtering, since the Spotlight uses closed decklists, showing a bit of blue is less intimidating to the opponent, so I swapped {% mtglink "Opt" %} for {% mtglink "Roiling Dragonstorm" %}. As an aside, quite a few people asked why I didn't use {% mtglink "Consult the Star Charts" %} instead of {% mtglink "Roiling Dragonstorm" %}, since the former looks deeper. True, in most situations it does look deeper; but in essence, every component of our combo only wants to see 1 copy, so the loot-2-discard-1 effect is often better than look-at-N-take-1, because you can use it to adjust your hand, trading extra components for more useful lands or other components.

In addition, I added the new SOS capstone {% mtglink "Improvisation Capstone" %} precisely to deal with decks that discard a lot, like SamuelChang's version of Sultai Demon; once it resolves, the opponent has a hard time keeping up with your resources. ~~But I forgot to write it into the melee decklist, sob~~

> **Core logic**: As long as I don't die, my life total is a resource to trade for time to filter.
> **Mulligan logic**: Your goal is to find combo components, not to filter a ton. Please don't keep a hand with no components and a pile of Stock Up; that's putting the cart before the horse.
> **Switching routes**: Always consider whether this board and matchup give you a chance to kill the opponent with the various dragon brothers, and whether you still have a next turn or need to race the opponent this turn. These correspond to the two mistakes I saw from two of my teammates.

## Deck Tips
1. Because the deck covers four colors, you need to be very careful about which type you call with {% mtglink "Multiversal Passage" %}; you often need to plan ahead. Here are some simple rules of thumb to help you decide:
   - Usually green needs just one source, red needs just one, white needs just one, and the more blue the better.
   - In control matchups the priority is usually white > red, and in aggro matchups red > white.
   -  The creature type you name with {% mtglink "Cavern of Souls" %} also needs great care. It is usually **Dragon** ( {% mtglink "North Wind Avatar" %}, {% mtglink "Marang River Regent" %} ), **Human** ( {% mtglink "Voice of Victory" %} ), or **Beast** ( {% mtglink "Kona, Rescue Beastie" %} ), depending on the matchup and your hand.
3. Only the white and red Planets are useful once stationed to 12+, so station those two first.
4. With {% mtglink "Kona, Rescue Beastie" %}, discarding {% mtglink "Marang River Regent" %} resets the battlefield and puts a 6/7 flyer down. Against midrange or aggro this is a lot of pressure and is often considered as Plan B.
5. Discarding {% mtglink "North Wind Avatar" %} to Kona can't fetch from the sideboard, so watch out.
6. Kona's trigger happens at the beginning of your second main phase, so please don't cast it in your second main phase.
7. Casting the blue Regent can bounce the opponent's nonland permanent as well as your own {% mtglink "Formidable Speaker" %} or {% mtglink "North Wind Avatar" %} to keep refreshing resources (against prowess, bouncing their Otter enchantment is not recommended; I've never bounced it myself).
8. With the blue Regent and Kona on the battlefield, you can attack with the Regent and Kona (or, if you can't get through on the ground, simply station Kona) to trigger Kona's ability, then in response use {% mtglink "Prismari Charm" %} or {% mtglink "Bounce Off" %} to bounce the blue Regent back, and put it onto the battlefield again via Kona's ability to reset the board while also getting a flying blocker.
9. Note that {% mtglink "Formidable Speaker" %}'s second ability is not limited to a single player. The opponent's copy will untap our Kona during combat, so Kona's ability can't trigger in the second main phase. This is also why I originally ran {% mtglink "Grand Abolisher" %} rather than {% mtglink "Voice of Victory" %}: to prevent Sultai Reanimator, which also has Formidable Speaker, from stopping the combo, but now this deck almost never faces that.
10. {% mtglink "Emeritus of Ideation" %} is very strong against Dimir Demon and SamuelChang's Sultai Demon, because besides being hard to remove and providing extra resources, it also lets you make the opponent draw three and lose after they finish {% mtglink "Doomsday Excruciator" %}.
11. {% mtglink "Spider-Sense" %} can be cast by paying one blue mana and returning a tapped creature, so remember to station your creatures ahead of time to gain more resources.
12. Even with {% mtglink "Omniscience" %} on the battlefield you can still spend mana to cast cards from your hand. A common situation is that you don't want the spell to be counterspelled, so you still tap Cavern of Souls to cast it. Also, Omniscience only makes cards in your hand free to cast; {% mtglink "Winternight Stories" %} in the graveyard and the flashback of {% mtglink "Emeritus of Ideation" %} that casts {% mtglink "Ancestral Recall" %} still have to be paid for.

## Results Summary

These 15 rounds marked out the boundaries of the deck:

* **vs. Aggro:** 4 wins, 2 losses. Prowess has no good removal in the main deck to answer Kona, so overall it's rather manageable.
* **vs. Control:** 4 wins, 0 losses. The six-mana route is very powerful against control.
* **The deck's absolute weakness (vs. Mardu Discard):** With a 0-2 record against Mardu Discard, this deck can barely defend against a turn-three lethal strike or the opponent's direct damage that doesn't rely on creatures.
