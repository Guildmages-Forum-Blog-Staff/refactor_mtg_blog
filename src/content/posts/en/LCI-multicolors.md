The Lost Caverns of Ixalan (LCI) has its prerelease on November 10, 2023, and officially launches a week later on November 17, 2023. Want to know more about how each card rates in Limited, and some fun possible combos? Then check out this LCI Limited review series!

For the mechanics, see this article: [The Lost Caverns of Ixalan Mechanics Explained](https://guildmagesforum.tw/LCI-mechanic/)

## Rating Scale

5.0: Unconditional first pick
4.0: Worth a first pick
3.5: Strong enough, but not necessarily a first pick
3.0: Above average (you'll really want it in your final deck)
2.5: Average, maybe around the 6th or 7th pick of a draft
2.0 to 1.0: Below average (you'd rather not play it than play it)
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable (basically never play it; it will only help you lose)

As usual, I'll put the ten non-common legends, one for each color pair, up front. These ten cards are a miniature of the mechanic of each of the ten Limited archetypes in this set. To understand LCI Limited, you have to start with these ten cards.

![220](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/3/e/3ee62dd1-97d0-4e5d-8937-26c9e51e9414.jpg?1699044515)

**cephille**: 3.0
The black-green legend, a native fungus dweller of Ixalan's underground, carries two reminder texts, **Descend 4** and **Descend 8**, which tells us this color pair in LCI Limited cares about those two states. Descend cares about the number of permanent cards in your graveyard: four or more is Descend 4, eight or more is Descend 8, and once you get there you receive a bonus. It's a relatively simple, easy-to-remember theme in LCI Limited.

On its own it is a respectable 3-mana 3/3. With Descend 4 it gets an extra +2/+2 and trample (5/5 trample), and with Descend 8 it gets another +2/+2 and can't be blocked by more than one creature (7/7 trample, can't be blocked by more than one creature).

But before we go on, let's estimate how feasible these two themes are in Limited. A basic 40-card Limited deck has roughly 17 lands, 15 creatures, and 8 noncreature spells. Without counting self-mill, unless all your noncreature spells are permanents, you are only likely to hit Descend 4 in the mid game and Descend 8 in the late game.

From this estimate, **to reach Descend 4 or even Descend 8 faster in black-green, you have to specialize your deck construction: play as few nonpermanent cards as possible, and add some self-mill or ways to actively sacrifice permanents**.

> The Descend theme is actually a tribute to the Ascend theme from Rivals of Ixalan (RIX). That theme matched the story of RIX, in which several factions raced to find the Golden City, and the Ascend ability was designed around having ten or more permanents on the battlefield to obtain the city's blessing, symbolizing finding the treasure. Descend, on the other hand, cares about the number of permanents in your graveyard, which echoes the story where the factions begin to dig into the ruins beneath Ixalan's surface, discover traces of the ancient gods, and set off a chain of events.
> I wrote this note because I hope that, besides the power level of the cards and the possibilities for deckbuilding, you will gain another dimension from which to appreciate the charming flavor of how MTG's card design ties into the story. I believe it can spark your interest in many earlier sets!

![224](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/6/9/690ccdc7-6c43-4902-9d11-2f07b7a36b11.jpg?1698184730)

**cephille**: 2.5
The black-white legend's ability is **sacrificing a creature or artifact to get a bonus**, and its creature type, **Vampire**, is the most representative tribe of this color pair. As a 2-mana 2/1 it is a bit weak, but don't forget this set has plenty of artifacts and ways to make tokens to feed it. It is also a free sacrifice outlet, so it works with the Descend theme as well!

> Hm, let me seriously double-check that this is an English card. The name Bartolomé del Presidio comes from Spanish! A little trivia: the setting of Ixalan draws on Mayan mythology from South America and the Spanish colonial campaigns, so the vampires in the story, Mavren Fein and Vona, also have Spanish names. Very flavorful!

![226](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/8/e/8ea82964-fd9c-48e3-962f-94954476b31f.jpg?1699044531)

**cephille**: 3.5
The red-white faction is the Sun Empire. What the Empire has plenty of is **Humans** and **Soldiers**, and its signature set theme is **Discover**. When it attacks, you can tap two untapped artifacts and/or creatures to Discover 3. A 4-mana 4/4 body lets it attack steadily, and if your board meets the conditions you gain extra value from Discover 3 as your board keeps spreading wider and wider. It looks like red-white in LCI Limited is about going wide and generating resources.

> For a detailed explanation of the Discover ability, see this article: [The Lost Caverns of Ixalan Mechanics Explained](/LCI-mechanic/)
> The flavor of Discover feels just like archaeologists digging up ancient artifacts: the more you Discover, the deeper you dug, and the bigger the reward may be! No wonder this set's planeswalker is Quintorius Kand, the newly sparked archaeology major from Strixhaven, so well suited to go investigate Ixalan!

![227](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/1/4/14c65f5a-10bd-4f9b-b816-46c2240b11ff.jpg?1698446586)

**cephille**: 2.5
The blue-red pair is represented by the **Pirates**, notorious across Ixalan's seas, and this time LCI's theme is **a buff your Pirates get whenever an artifact enters the battlefield under your control**. As for the card itself, a 2-mana 2/2 that puts a +1/+1 counter on target Pirate you control whenever an artifact enters is basically a Pirate lord. The more you plunder (Treasures, Map tokens, and all sorts of artifacts), the more your Pirates get fat and sleek. Personally I think it's pretty strong!

![230](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/7/1/7112c366-b36a-4bc8-aa64-6bad16bebc39.jpg?1699044540)

**cephille**: 3.5
Red-green on Ixalan is of course the **Dinosaur** tribe. This 2-mana 2/3 baby dinosaur has haste, and when it enters you can pay an additional {2} to have target Dinosaur you control deal damage equal to its power to another target creature. I think it is very strong in Limited. Even if you cast it on an empty board, you can choose between a 2-mana 2/3 haste, or paying four to have it deal 2 damage to another creature. When you have another Dinosaur with higher power on board, the 4-mana mode is a premium removal creature that keeps pressure on the board. The more Dinosaurs you have, the better the enter-the-battlefield removal gets, so unless I have to, I'll cast it for four most of the time.

![232](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/c/9/c9f88a40-a6ed-4c1f-a309-011aca1acddd.jpg?1699044549)

**cephille**: 3.5
The green-white legend's first ability doesn't seem directly related to the archetype's theme; it feels more like an add-on. The archetype's theme is the second part: **bonuses when you control one or more creatures with power greater than their base power**. This card lets you draw a card whenever a creature with power greater than its base power deals combat damage to a player, which is a very strong card-advantage engine. So it is a perfect home for your pump spells, Auras, Equipment, and spells that put +1/+1 counters on creatures. Even better, it doesn't have to attack itself; any of your other creatures that qualify will draw cards. And don't forget that if a creature with first strike and one without both connect, or a creature with double strike connects, you draw two cards!
Its first ability is half a [Grand Abolisher](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/a/4/a40789d7-21e7-4d23-a904-db5dddc64051.jpg?1562653607): your opponents can't cast spells during your turn, which makes pumping your creatures while attacking much safer. And if you want to do something naughty on your own turn, casting it first guarantees that nothing, including counterspells, will interfere. Awesome!

![233](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/LCI-multicolors/c9cac38bc150.webp)

**cephille**: 3.0
The white-blue pair's theme corresponds to **Craft with artifact**, so its signature non-common card is not a legendary creature. Craft with artifact is an activated ability usable only at sorcery speed. It requires you to additionally exile **a certain number or more of artifacts and/or permanents** from the battlefield or your graveyard, and doing so transforms the card. It is basically like a sacrifice, turning the card into a stronger artifact.

As for the card itself, a 5-mana artifact that makes a 4/4 white-blue Golem artifact creature token is already worth its cost. Craft with artifact costs seven, and after transforming, you can tap it the turn an artifact enters under your control to make a 4/4 Golem, and if you can untap it you can make even more! Of course, given the high activation cost, Craft is basically a mid-to-late-game finisher in Limited. If you plan to use it, remember to bring a few artifacts or cards that make artifact tokens in your deck.

![236](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/5/e/5e6f4aba-3500-4fb7-ab78-02f63c03778a.jpg?1698603292)

**cephille**: 2.5
Blue-green on Ixalan is of course the **Merfolk** tribe, and the returning keyword **Explore** is this color pair's main theme this time. The legend itself is a 2-mana 2/3. When you explore and reveal a land, you can put a land card from your hand onto the battlefield tapped (it doesn't have to be the explored land; any land card already in your hand works). When you explore and reveal a nonland card, besides putting a +1/+1 counter on the exploring creature, you can also put one on it. I think it's somewhat ordinary: great if you have lots of explorers, but not necessarily worth playing without them. You need to build around the Explore theme for it to shine.

![242](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/a/0/a062202c-f9fb-4dd6-989a-c3083644f1c0.jpg?1698264016)

**cephille**: 3.5
Blue-black also uses the **Descend 8** reminder text, and basically goes for stacking permanents in the graveyard to get bonuses. But as I said about black-green, Descend 8 is actually fairly hard to reach, so we should still judge the card by its effect before reaching Descend 8, and treat the payoff as a bonus. The legend's creature type, **Skeleton**, also has some synergy cards in this set, which reveals this new tribe joining the Ixalan family.

As for the card itself, a 5-mana 6/4 with vigilance and menace is a very capable body, balancing offense and defense with some evasion. Once it lands, your opponent basically has to trade two creatures with it or spend a removal spell, or fall behind. Its Descend 8 ability lets you pay six at sorcery speed to return it from the graveyard to the battlefield (without even entering tapped!) with a **finality counter** on it. This is another new type of counter: a creature with a finality counter is exiled instead of dying, although if you have another way to return it to your hand and recast it, it's a live dragon again. Remember that it counts itself as one of the permanent cards for Descend 8, so you can play it mid-game, trade it off, and bring it back late.

![245](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/c/0/c06f0ff2-d42f-4854-bbe5-4b022fb26d7d.jpg?1699044586)

**cephille**: 2.5
This time the black-red theme is about **whether you descended this turn as your end step begins**. Besides being a 2-mana 2/2 deathtouch, a body well suited to trading up, if you descended this turn, each opponent discards a card or sacrifices a permanent, and if they do neither it deals 3 damage to them. Basically, as long as you can trigger Descend consistently, you can pull ahead of your opponent in hand, board, or life total. BUT, Descend only cares whether a permanent card was put into your graveyard, so tokens like a Treasure you sacrificed for mana don't count, which is worth keeping in mind.

### Delving Into the Caverns: How Caves Affect LCI Limited

> Number of **Caves** among the lands: 13
> C: 7
> U: 4
> R: 2

Before going through the lands, I think I need to add some extra explanation about Cave cards. Note that **in every LCI draft booster, the basic land slot can also show up as a common Cave**, which means in a sealed event with at least six packs you can expect to open 3 or more Caves, and in a draft, if the player upstream doesn't take them, you can pick up even more. Besides the ten two-color archetypes mentioned earlier, LCI Limited should have an **eleventh hidden archetype, "five-color Caves,"** and the drafting approach may be somewhat like the five-color gates deck in RNA Limited. That's also why so many of the Cave cards introduced below can help you fix colors.

Besides the 13 Caves introduced below, some cards also have a Cave on their back face after transforming:

> **Number that transform into Caves: 5**
> C: 0
> U: 3 (one each of black, red, green)
> R: 2 (one each of black, red)

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/LCI-multicolors/bd65de59f5c3.webp)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/LCI-multicolors/0299cff3a4dd.webp)

Although Caves obtained by transforming are relatively unreliable and conditional, so we shouldn't count on them too much for the Cave count, it's still worth watching for good opportunities to include them in your Cave theme.

Finally, if you open a decent number of Caves and Cave payoff cards in sealed, you can make this mechanic a secondary plan to reinforce your deck. In a draft, you should consciously raise the pick order of Cave cards a bit and watch whether a lot of Cave payoffs are being passed to you; with luck, you can collect those payoffs after one lap around the table and transition into the arms of five-color Caves.
