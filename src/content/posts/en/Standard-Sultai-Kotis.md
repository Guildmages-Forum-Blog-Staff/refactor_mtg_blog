## Before We Start

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Kotis/1770acb8b0b1.webp)

Sultai was also a powerful color combination in the old Tarkir Standard. The [Sidisi Whip](https://mtgdecks.net/Standard/sidisi-whip-decklist-by-shahar-shenhar-122202) deck, built around the self-milling, Zombie-making khan [Sidisi, Brood Tyrant](https://scryfall.com/card/cmm/355/sidisi-brood-tyrant) paired with [Whip of Erebos](https://scryfall.com/card/dsc/159/whip-of-erebos), could build a board, dig up big creatures, and gain life all at once, and was very hard to deal with. In this timeline's Tarkir, a new face, [Kotis, the Fangkeeper](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/202/kotis-the-fangkeeper), has become khan. His nature is completely different from the khan [Sidisi](https://scryfall.com/card/cmm/355/sidisi-brood-tyrant), so what kind of Sultai deck can he be built into?

## Card Analysis

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Kotis/13783af34107.webp)
>Kotis, the Fangkeeper
>Legendary Creature | Zombie Warrior
>Indestructible
>Whenever Kotis deals combat damage to a player, exile that many cards from the top of their library. You may cast any number of spells with mana value X or less from among them without paying their mana costs, where X is the amount of damage dealt.

Unlike [Sidisi, Brood Tyrant](https://scryfall.com/card/cmm/355/sidisi-brood-tyrant), who mills herself to send out a Zombie army, [Kotis, the Fangkeeper](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/202/kotis-the-fangkeeper) is a formidable crusader. He has built-in indestructible, and connecting with the opponent can net a big pile of resources. The only problem is that his body is too small: he is easily blocked by the opponent's creatures, and the resources he can win when he does connect are limited. In this set's limited, [Kotis](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/202/kotis-the-fangkeeper) is positioned as a target for Sultai's renew ability, pumping up his size and granting useful abilities. Can we just do the same in Standard?

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Kotis/163ecbc8c2de.webp)

Let's look at the high-rarity renew cards. The mythic [Rot-Curse Rakshasa](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/87/rot-curse-rakshasa) doesn't go the strengthening route, and its renew effect of disrupting the opponent is subtle, so let's set it aside. [Qarsi Revenant](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/86/qarsi-revenant)'s renew doesn't add size, but the abilities are all quite useful, with lifelink, trample, and deathtouch, and the creature itself is no slouch. [Lasyd Prowler](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/149/lasyd-prowler) has a decent body and mills itself, and its renew that pumps by the number of lands in the graveyard looks like it could grow things a lot, but in actual testing it's very unstable, and the creature itself has no particular advantage over other monsters at the same cost, so the verdict is pass. [Naga Fleshcrafter](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/52/naga-fleshcrafter) is a [Clone](https://scryfall.com/card/m14/47/clone) whose performance depends heavily on the board, with a lower floor than [Lasyd Prowler](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/149/lasyd-prowler). Looking it all over, only [Qarsi Revenant](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/86/qarsi-revenant) is usable. The decklist is below:

[Decklist](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7074033#paper)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Kotis/c70b711a0b49.webp)

Among self-mill-based archetypes in Standard, the best fit for [Kotis](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/202/kotis-the-fangkeeper) is probably still delirium. Other self-mill decks lean somewhat toward combo and don't need a midrange creature like [Kotis](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/202/kotis-the-fangkeeper), while ordinary midrange lacks ways to grow him, so putting [Kotis](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/202/kotis-the-fangkeeper) in there has no advantage to speak of beyond indestructible, which is pretty lame.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Kotis/207fba3ef2fb.webp)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Kotis/757795d91361.webp)

However, pumping size is exactly the strength of delirium's [Omnivorous Flytrap](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/192/omnivorous-flytrap). Casually pump [Kotis](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/202/kotis-the-fangkeeper) with it and a single punch creates a huge advantage. [Osseous Sticktwister](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/112/osseous-sticktwister) comes with lifelink and has a chance to hit directly once delirium is on, giving strong late-game recovery against aggro, and it is likewise a good target for [Omnivorous Flytrap](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/192/omnivorous-flytrap). [Patchwork Beastie](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/195/patchwork-beastie) is everyone's favorite one-mana self-mill friend, so I won't belabor it here. Next is [Qarsi Revenant](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/86/qarsi-revenant) mentioned earlier: the creature itself is a high-spec [Vampire Nighthawk](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/186/vampire-nighthawk), and once in the graveyard, its renew ability can give [Kotis](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/202/kotis-the-fangkeeper) and [Omnivorous Flytrap](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/192/omnivorous-flytrap) flying, deathtouch, and lifelink, so the opponent can't stall with ground creatures. Also, as extra firepower and a supplement to the types, I added two [Kaito, Bane of Nightmares](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/220/kaito-bane-of-nightmares). Since he has built-in hexproof when attacking, he too is a fine target for [Omnivorous Flytrap](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/192/omnivorous-flytrap) and [Qarsi Revenant](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/86/qarsi-revenant).

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Kotis/a2271d5b4ac8.webp)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Kotis/dbbd1e694465.webp)

Filling the graveyard is also Sultai's bread and butter. By adding black, we get [Awaken the Honored Dead](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/170/awaken-the-honored-dead) and [Quag Feast](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/100/quag-feast), two strong spells that are both removal and graveyard fillers, and together with green's [Seed of Hope](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/204/seed-of-hope) and [Dredger's Insight](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/159/dredgers-insight), both the removal and the graveyard-filling efficiency beat those of a Gruul-based delirium deck.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Kotis/57d4d54060eb.webp)

As for the other spells, we use [Bushwhack](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/215/bushwhack), which finds lands and doubles as removal, the delirium cost-reducing catch-all [Drag to the Roots](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/213/drag-to-the-roots), plus one exile-based answer, [Tear Asunder](https://scryfall.com/card/tdc/273/tear-asunder), as a supplement.

## Gameplay Demo

{%youtube 0jLc81T0a0M %}

The first two games in this video show the synergy of [Kotis](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/202/kotis-the-fangkeeper) with [Omnivorous Flytrap](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/192/omnivorous-flytrap) and [Qarsi Revenant](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/86/qarsi-revenant). In the first game, against Jeskai control, [Kotis](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/202/kotis-the-fangkeeper)'s indestructible made the opponent's removal useless. After [Omnivorous Flytrap](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/192/omnivorous-flytrap) pumped it up, [Kotis](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/202/kotis-the-fangkeeper) stole two [Marang River Regent](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/51/marang-river-regent-coil-and-catch), one [Rediscover the Way](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/215/rediscover-the-way), one [Stock Up](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/67/stock-up), and one [Three Steps Ahead](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/75/three-steps-ahead) in a single hit, and the opponent conceded on the spot. In the second game, although I didn't draw [Omnivorous Flytrap](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/192/omnivorous-flytrap), [Qarsi Revenant](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/86/qarsi-revenant) gave [Kotis](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/202/kotis-the-fangkeeper) flying and lifelink, gaining life while stealing the opponent's resources, and after two steals the opponent conceded. The last game was against red aggro while stuck on lands, but two [Osseous Sticktwister](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/112/osseous-sticktwister) gained plenty of life, and I still had 20-some life at the end.

## Conclusion

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Sultai-Kotis/a4480902de03.webp)

Although the new set's Sultai looks quite different in design from the old generation, both benefit from filling the graveyard and both rely on lifelink to turn the late game around, so in a sense it does carry on the spirit of the old Sultai. I also tried building around the renew theme, which was quite fun to play, but its power level doesn't match delirium. The [decklist](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7078328#paper) is here for interested readers to try modifying on their own.
