## Before We Start

Recently, inspired by a post from 寬麵ㄉㄉ, I decided to write this series of articles. This one is about how to fetch: whether you should fetch, what to fetch, why you fetch, and so on.
This series is also expected to include, but not be limited to: why should you spend your tricks? How do you spend them? On whom? And the like.

Our games are always full of small details we haven't handled well. These small details may not affect the result of a match, but in the long run your win rate always gets eaten away by them.
A card game does include some element of luck, but isn't it strange to complain about luck before doing everything within your power well?

![](/images/fetch-lands.webp)

In this article we start with fetching, the area with the least impact, since it is something you do on your own initiative, and factors like your opponent's board and hand don't affect whether you should fetch.

Generally, we fetch for no more than the following reasons:

- Thinning: reduce the number of lands in your library and increase your chances of drawing your live cards
- Color fixing: ugh, I'm color-screwed, okay?
- Triggering Revolt / Landfall / reaching thresholds / enabling Delirium: [Sheoldred, the Apocalypse](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/107/sheoldred-the-apocalypse) ouch, [Fatal Push](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/93/fatal-push) / [Unholy Heat](https://scryfall.com/card/otc/182/unholy-heat) save me

## Thinning

![](https://guildmagesforum.tw/images/memes/where-is-my-land.jpg)

Why are fetchlands so important in Modern? The main reason is thinning.
As everyone knows, most Modern decks can function with three or four lands, and with plenty of card filtering or mana cheating methods, we actually don't need that many lands most of the time, and drawing a land in the mid to late game is always awkward.
But fetchlands solve this problem: while fixing colors they also thin the deck, and having two lands in your opening hand (not necessarily fetchlands) is enough.
A more extreme example is Murktide. Current Murktide builds only run 18 to 19 lands, because there is plenty of filtering ([Preordain](https://scryfall.com/card/otc/107/preordain) and [Expressive Iteration](https://scryfall.com/card/otc/224/expressive-iteration), two real card-selection spells, plus the surveil from [Dragon's Rage Channeler](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/121/dragons-rage-channeler) and the free draw from [Mishra's Bauble](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/274/mishras-bauble)) and [Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/138/ragavan-nimble-pilferer), which makes Treasures, and all the spells in the deck cost two mana or less. That means that with just three lands the deck can basically function completely.

> The reason you need three lands is to leave one mana for extra uses. You can't just hold a [Counterspell](https://scryfall.com/card/cmm/81/counterspell) and be unable to do anything else, right?

So in the usual case, we should fetch whenever we can, to reduce the risk of drawing lands, right?

But it actually depends on the current situation.

> I won't discuss whether to still fetch when you're missing land drops here. If you're already stuck on lands, worrying about reducing your chances of drawing lands is just silly.
> The only exception is fetching a surveil land, since that really does filter the top of your library for you.

For example, suppose you have already used [Opt](https://scryfall.com/card/otc/104/opt) or [Preordain](https://scryfall.com/card/otc/107/preordain) to scry a land to the bottom. Is it good to crack a fetchland now? (assuming you have other lands available)
Obviously not, because you paid some life to fetch but didn't change your probability of drawing live cards. Unless some other effect shuffles, you shouldn't be able to draw the card at the bottom of the library.
Never mind multiple scries putting even more lands on the bottom to dig for cards: `the pile at the bottom of the library` = `cards you will probably never draw this game` (without a shuffle). Since the things you put down there can't be used, why shuffle them back up?

> If someone has shuffled and can still guarantee those cards are all at the bottom of the library, I should be able to call a judge, right?

So whether to fetch is a matter that needs calculation: what is the probability of drawing the right cards before the shuffle? Which cards did I put on the bottom with my earlier scries? Can the shuffle help me draw the right cards?

### Surveil Lands

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Tiny-Thing-ep-1/899f5f46d6f2.webp)

One extra word on surveil lands. The surveil land cycle released in MKM has land types, so they can also be fetched by fetchlands.
Because surveil lands can filter the top of the library, you can treat them as `one fewer land card` + `filtering the top of the library`; technically they thin two cards.

## Color Fixing

> In this section, lands are referred to by color plus the cycle's name, to avoid difficulty in reading.
> I believe most players, myself included, would say "Izzet shock" or "UR shock" rather than [Steam Vents](https://scryfall.com/card/rvr/288/steam-vents).

Another key point is color fixing: the order in which you fetch colors and how to split the colors of your lands.
In the environment at the time of writing, Domain Zoo and 4C Rhino should be the decks that most need to care about the order of fetching land colors.

> I won't discuss situations with [Leyline of the Guildpact](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/217/leyline-of-the-guildpact) here.

For Domain Zoo, the common combinations are [Savai Triome](https://scryfall.com/card/iko/253/savai-triome) + [Breeding Pool](https://scryfall.com/card/rvr/275/breeding-pool), or [Zagoth Triome](https://scryfall.com/card/iko/259/zagoth-triome) + [Sacred Foundry](https://scryfall.com/card/rvr/285/sacred-foundry), depending on the deck's configuration and land setup. Both combinations let you:

1. Play [Territorial Kavu](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/216/territorial-kavu), [Scion of Draco](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/234/scion-of-draco), or Dash [Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/138/ragavan-nimble-pilferer) on turn two
2. Assemble Domain
3. Always have enough for [Leyline Binding](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/24/leyline-binding) + [Stubborn Denial](https://scryfall.com/card/ktk/56/stubborn-denial)

Another example is 4C Rhino, where the Rhino combination is usually [Xander's Lounge](https://scryfall.com/card/snc/260/xanders-lounge) + [Temple Garden](https://scryfall.com/card/rvr/290/temple-garden).
Because your opening hand may have [Crashing Footfalls](https://scryfall.com/card/mh1/160/crashing-footfalls) that you want to suspend on turn one, while also wanting to keep the flexibility to make [Leyline Binding](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/24/leyline-binding) on turn two, you choose the combination of [Temple Garden](https://scryfall.com/card/rvr/290/temple-garden) + [Xander's Lounge](https://scryfall.com/card/snc/260/xanders-lounge), and this combination also supports all the required colors for [Shardless Agent](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/292/shardless-agent) or [Ardent Plea](https://scryfall.com/card/arb/1/ardent-plea) on turn three ~~and [Violent Outburst](https://scryfall.com/card/arb/63/violent-outburst)~~.

### What Should I Fetch?

I also can't avoid mentioning [Field of Ruin](https://scryfall.com/card/moc/400/field-of-ruin).
If your opponent is a mill deck or a deck like Cabal Coffers that runs Field of Ruin, you basically fetch a nonbasic land with your eyes closed.
In those two matchups, in one your life total doesn't matter and in the other the life pressure isn't on your side, so keeping your nonbasics from getting blown up matters more.
Or consider whether your opponent has [Blood Moon](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/118/blood-moon) / [Magus of the Moon](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/175/magus-of-the-moon) / [Harbinger of the Seas](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/63/harbinger-of-the-seas), and whether you need to fetch a basic land first to avoid being color-screwed.

And in the mid to late game, whether to fetch a Triome or keep it to cycle when you draw one later, and whether you will have enough mana to cycle it later, are also questions worth thinking about.

## When to Fetch

![](https://guildmagesforum.tw/images/memes/tishanas-tidebinder-is-stone-rain.png)

Usually when using a fetchland, people like to crack it at the end of the opponent's turn, which preserves the flexibility to fetch a tapped land and saves the two life a shockland costs.
But this changed after LCI. If your opponent plays blue, you also need to watch whether your opponent has enough mana to flash in [Tishana's Tidebinder](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/81/tishanas-tidebinder), and getting a land blown up to save those two life is hardly worth it.

![](https://guildmagesforum.tw/images/memes/counterspell-meme.jpg)

You also need to pay attention to whether your current situation suits fetching. Take a Murktide player holding (or bluffing to the opponent that they hold) [Counterspell](https://scryfall.com/card/cmm/81/counterspell): suppose you leave two untapped lands and one of them is a fetchland. If the opponent is on Goryo's Reanimator or another deck with instant-speed plays, at the moment you crack the fetch they may respond with [Goryo's Vengeance](https://scryfall.com/card/uma/99/goryos-vengeance), since you don't have two mana to cast [Counterspell](https://scryfall.com/card/cmm/81/counterspell), the most common counter.

> Of course you can also turn this action around to trap your opponent: when the opponent responds to our fetch with [Goryo's Vengeance](https://scryfall.com/card/uma/99/goryos-vengeance), counter them in return with [Force of Negation](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/50/force-of-negation) or [Spell Pierce](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/63/spell-pierce).

The same situation applies to [Fatal Push](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/93/fatal-push) and [Searing Blaze](https://scryfall.com/card/wwk/90/searing-blaze). If your deck has these two cards, also pay attention to whether you need to keep a fetchland on the battlefield / in hand for now, and use it when needed.

Another example: when you are going to [Grief](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/87/grief) scam, you can first put a fetchland on the battlefield, use [Grief](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/87/grief)'s ETB to look at your opponent's hand, and fetch afterward, avoiding some weird tricks such as [Archive Trap](https://scryfall.com/card/zen/41/archive-trap), or [Archive Trap](https://scryfall.com/card/zen/41/archive-trap), or also [Archive Trap](https://scryfall.com/card/zen/41/archive-trap), and [Archive Trap](https://scryfall.com/card/zen/41/archive-trap).

Also, if the land you want to fetch is one that doesn't require paying life (such as Triomes or basic lands), you can keep it until your own turn to fetch, still thinning, while keeping your mana open to handle more situations (for example, if you get hit by [Stifle](https://scryfall.com/card/cns/108/stifle) or other weird things, you can counter in response).

## Other Uses for Fetching

As everyone knows, fetching requires a shuffle, so there are also some additional applications.

Here again I'll use Murktide as the example (I can't help it, I can't think of any other Modern deck that scries these days). Suppose our opening hand has one land and on turn one we cast [Preordain](https://scryfall.com/card/otc/107/preordain) without seeing a second land. Whatever we see, we put those two cards on the bottom, even if one is [Expressive Iteration](https://scryfall.com/card/otc/224/expressive-iteration) or [Counterspell](https://scryfall.com/card/cmm/81/counterspell); we have to dutifully send them down.
When we need those cards in the mid to late game, we can use a fetchland to have them (have a chance to) get shuffled back up.

Another example is also [Preordain](https://scryfall.com/card/otc/107/preordain). Suppose we are now facing Scam, and when scrying two we see a [Subtlety](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/67/subtlety) and a random card, and we hold a [Consider](https://scryfall.com/card/clu/84/consider). We can leave [Subtlety](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/67/subtlety) on top of the library and, when the opposing side does something, decide whether to draw it or shuffle it away with a fetchland (for example, if the opponent casts [Fable of the Mirror-Breaker](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/141/fable-of-the-mirror-breaker-reflection-of-kiki-jiki) rather than a creature card).

So this also pairs with [Brainstorm](https://scryfall.com/card/mkc/96/brainstorm), shuffling cards in hand you don't currently need back into the library.
Or it can be used to hide information / prevent your top card from being manipulated, for example against cards that change the order of the top of the library such as [Jace, the Mind Sculptor](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/56/jace-the-mind-sculptor) and [Portent](https://scryfall.com/card/c18/97/portent), or cards that look at the top of the library such as [Mishra's Bauble](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/274/mishras-bauble).
