## Color Pair Themes

Basically, the color pair themes in this set are built from a hybrid-mana two-color common creature, a multicolor uncommon for that color pair, and a hybrid-mana uncommon legendary creature. Let's take a look!

> This time the color pair themes add a Takeaway chain, hoping to help readers further understand the key points of each color pair and its development possibilities
> 
> **Takeaway**: A → B → C → D
> A: Basic traits — Who we are?
> B: Color pair theme — How we stand?
> C: Theme payoff — What we want?
> D: Extended development — Where we go?

![Draft ten color pair card images](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/TLA-Multicolors/2baeb1078d46.webp)

### <span style="color:#C3B270">W</span><span style="color:#006DB3">U</span> — Flyers

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/TLA-Multicolors/2ff576ac2884.webp)

The common {% mtglink "Cat-Owl" %} looks like an ordinary flyer, but besides letting creatures block, its untap ability also supports Waterbend. The uncommon {% mtglink "Air Nomad Legacy" %} not only makes all your flyers bigger, but the Clue it creates also lets you draw cards. And the other card, {% mtglink "Sokka, Lateral Strategist" %}, draws a card as long as it attacks with another creature. If that creature is one of the flyers this color pair is built around, it's even more likely to keep attacking and pull ahead on resources.

**Takeaway: Flying creatures + vigilance → Flyer buffs → Gain more resources and cards → Waterbend**

> The Azorius pair has a heavier curve. Individual cards are strong enough, but the strategy is fairly linear, so it needs a powerful top end to raise the deck's ceiling. The pair's rares, whether {% mtglink "Aang, Swift Savior" %} or  {% mtglink "Katara, Water Tribe's Hope" %}, are both very powerful, and the Waterbend ability can blow open a board advantage.

### <span style="color:#006DB3">U</span><span style="color:#808080">B</span> — Draw Two

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/TLA-Multicolors/637d1e0e811a.webp)

The common {% mtglink "Messenger Hawk" %} comes with its own Clue token, which can actively support a draw-two effect, and its own attacking ability is quite considerable. The uncommon {% mtglink "Foggy Swamp Spirit Keeper" %} is the payoff for the draw-two theme: each time you draw your second card each turn, it creates a 1/1 colorless Spirit token, which has conditional evasion that can help you sneak in some damage. And the other card, {% mtglink "Hama, the Bloodbender" %}, has an ability that hints at the rare Bloodbending from the Avatar story. Bloodbending grew out of Waterbending, manipulating the blood and water inside other people's bodies, which is why this card's effect is also triggered through Waterbend.

**Takeaway: Create Clues → Draw-two theme → Create more small creatures → Waterbend**

### <span style="color:#808080">B</span><span style="color:#AF2321">R</span> — Fire Nation Aggro

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/TLA-Multicolors/b7304031b82b.webp)

The common {% mtglink "Vindictive Warden" %} writes all the keywords of the black-red pair right onto one card: menace gives it a measure of evasion and a better chance of surviving after attacking, and the red mana added by its attack-triggered Firebending can also be used to pay for activated abilities. The uncommon {% mtglink "Cruel Administrator" %}'s Raid benefits from the groundwork menace lays, and when it attacks, the token it creates further boosts how much mana Firebending can add. And the other card, {% mtglink "Zhao, Ruthless Admiral" %}, besides having Firebending itself, also hints that pairing with the white-black sacrifice theme will give you better payoffs.

**Takeaway: Creatures with [Firebending / menace / activated abilities] → Attack (Raid, trigger Firebending) → Create more creatures with Firebending → Sacrifice permanents (especially creatures and artifacts) for payoffs (<span style="color:#C3B270">W</span><span style="color:#808080">B</span>)**

### <span style="color:#AF2321">R</span><span style="color:#4A8A3D">G</span> — Earth Rumble Ramp

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/TLA-Multicolors/967b96e23fd6.webp)

Whether it's land creatures from Earthbend or lands entering the battlefield (Landfall), both let the common {% mtglink "Earth Rumble Wrestlers" %} meet its power 4 condition, while also giving it trample, the most important ability for medium and large green creatures. The uncommon {% mtglink "Bitter Work" %} makes the red-green pair not only have big creatures but also generate card advantage! Exhaust can only be used once, but the 4/4 land creature made by Earthbend 4 keeps this card-advantage loop running. And the other card, {% mtglink "Uncle Iroh" %}, with its Firebending and its Lesson cost reduction, is especially helpful for casting instant Lessons.

**Takeaway: Care about land creatures + Landfall → Care about power 4 or greater → Earthbend → More mana (Firebending) + Interaction with Lessons (<span style="color:#4A8A3D">G</span><span style="color:#006DB3">U</span>, <span style="color:#006DB3">U</span><span style="color:#AF2321">R</span>)**

### <span style="color:#4A8A3D">G</span><span style="color:#C3B270">W</span> — Allies

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/TLA-Multicolors/8960229af229.webp)

The common {% mtglink "Earth Kingdom Soldier" %} somehow isn't even an Ally!? And its effect of putting counters (+1/+1 counters) looks better suited to the black-green pair ~~Not elegant!!!~~. The uncommon {% mtglink "White Lotus Reinforcements" %} is simple and brutal, the [Glorious Anthem](https://scryfall.com/card/m21/21/zht/%E8%BC%9D%E7%85%8C%E7%9A%84%E8%AE%9A%E7%BE%8E%E8%A9%A9-(glorious-anthem)) for Allies. And what if you don't have enough Allies? Rely on the other card, {% mtglink "Suki, Kyoshi Warrior" %}, to keep attacking and call in friends!

**Takeaway: Put counters (+1/+1 counters) → Ally theme → Ally buffs → Care about the number of creatures (Allies) + Create more Allies (<span style="color:#AF2321">R</span><span style="color:#C3B270">W</span>)**

> Since the green-white hybrid common is really underwhelming, I have to mention another green uncommon legend, {% mtglink "Haru, Hidden Talent" %}. This is a solid payoff for the Ally theme: each time an Ally enters, you can Earthbend 1, and it also lets {% mtglink "Suki, Kyoshi Warrior" %} give up to +2 power with each attack. Don't forget to take them together in the draft!

### <span style="color:#C3B270">W</span><span style="color:#808080">B</span> — Sacrifice

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/TLA-Multicolors/73b81dcf7312.webp)

The common {% mtglink "Pretending Poxbearers" %} gets two uses out of one card. The uncommon {% mtglink "Tolls of War" %} is a major payoff for the sacrifice theme. Besides actively creating a Clue to satisfy the second Ally-creating clause, it also relies on more engines that actively sacrifice to snowball. And the other card, {% mtglink "Hei Bai, Spirit of Balance" %}, is exactly the sacrifice engine this color pair needs.

**Takeaway: Sacrifice fodder → Sacrifice theme → Active sacrifice sources (such as Clues) + Create more fodder → Sacrifice engines + Rewards for sacrificing or leaving the battlefield**

> White's Airbend can also actively make your own creatures leave the battlefield

### <span style="color:#006DB3">U</span><span style="color:#AF2321">R</span> — Combat Lessons

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/TLA-Multicolors/ddf8f2d2b4b8.webp)

The common {% mtglink "Abandon Attachments" %} is itself a Lesson, and its discard-one-draw-two effect can speed up filling the graveyard with Lessons. The uncommon {% mtglink "Dragonfly Swarm" %} encourages you to play more Lessons or noncreature cards and to put them into the graveyard early to boost its power and gain a payoff when it dies. And the other card, {% mtglink "Professor Zei, Anthropologist" %}, besides being a looting engine, can also be sacrificed if needed to reuse an instant/sorcery in the graveyard. In addition, both hybrid cards can draw extra cards, which also suits the blue-black draw-two theme.

**Takeaway: Lessons + discard + draw two → Care whether the graveyard has Lessons (instant/sorcery, or noncreature) → Gain payoffs from Lessons in the graveyard → Reuse Lessons (instants/sorceries) from the graveyard**

> The hybrid cards' draw-two effects are also very much needed by the blue-black pair, so these two color pairs will be fighting each other in the draft XD

### <span style="color:#808080">B</span><span style="color:#4A8A3D">G</span> — Counters (+1/+1 Counters)

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/TLA-Multicolors/79d817c2465f.webp)

> I really don't know how to translate "+1/+1 counters" here. A literal "+1/+1 指示物" is too boring, so I used the phrase often seen in Limited articles, "放豆" (sowing beans), and "豆豆" (beans) as the theme name. I hope readers like it.

The common {% mtglink "Earth Village Ruffians" %} has a face that screams fodder, and should also suit the white-black sacrifice theme. The uncommon {% mtglink "Dai Li Agents" %} can sow beans into soldiers when it enters. Two consecutive Earthbend 1 triggers add 2 creatures with counters on them to the board, gaining a bigger advantage in later combat. And the other card, {% mtglink "Long Feng, Grand Secretariat" %}, is even more powerful: each time a creature or land goes from the battlefield to the graveyard, you can keep placing counters, and with it around the black-green counters theme spins and can't be stopped.

**Takeaway: Creatures/lands dying + Earthbend → Care about +1/+1 counters → Add creatures with +1/+1 counters on them for payoffs → Stack all three above and roll the snowball bigger!**

### <span style="color:#AF2321">R</span><span style="color:#C3B270">W</span> — Go Wide Aggro

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/TLA-Multicolors/473bac727714.webp)

The common {% mtglink "Wandering Musicians" %} has a great body and can pump all your creatures when it attacks. The uncommon {% mtglink "Sun Warriors" %} can make good use of its Firebending ability to create more Allies. And the other card, {% mtglink "Jet, Freedom Fighter" %}, benefits from the number of creatures, and besides serving as removal, it can keep boosting teammates after it dies.

**Takeaway: Attack-triggered buffs → Go wide → Gain payoffs from increasing creature count → Encourage attacking and trading for extra value**

### <span style="color:#4A8A3D">G</span><span style="color:#006DB3">U</span> — Ramp + Lessons

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/TLA-Multicolors/e3bea50c37b5.webp)

Besides milling two cards, the common {% mtglink "Platypus-Bear" %} has a body well suited to blocking, helping ramp decks get through the early game. The uncommon {% mtglink "Hermitic Herbalist" %} is not only an excellent mana accelerator but also makes it easier for you to cast Lessons. And the other card, {% mtglink "Guru Pathik" %}, further lets you put Lessons into your hand and rewards you for casting them, extending to the two sub-themes of Sagas and Shrines.

**Takeaway: Self-mill → Ramp + Lessons → More Lessons (Sagas and Shrines) → Cast Lessons (Sagas and Shrines) and reap the rewards**

### Hybrid Mana Opens Up More Possibilities in Limited

The ten two-color pairs are a signal that helps players get started with Limited quickly, but that doesn't mean they are all of Limited, and the hybrid mana design used in this Avatar set greatly increases the playability of deck pairings! We know that for a given two-color pair, using one of its colors together with another color still leaves 6 other possible pairs. When building decks or drafting, don't forget to take these possibilities into account too!

> Take the <span style="color:#C3B270">W</span><span style="color:#808080">B</span> pair as an example. The other pairs that use <span style="color:#C3B270">white</span> are <span style="color:#C3B270">W</span><span style="color:#006DB3">U</span>, <span style="color:#4A8A3D">G</span><span style="color:#C3B270">W</span>, and <span style="color:#AF2321">R</span><span style="color:#C3B270">W</span>, 3 in all, while the other pairs that use <span style="color:#808080">black</span> are <span style="color:#006DB3">U</span><span style="color:#808080">B</span>, <span style="color:#808080">B</span><span style="color:#AF2321">R</span>, and <span style="color:#808080">B</span><span style="color:#4A8A3D">G</span>, also 3, for 7 variations in total (if you also count mono-<span style="color:#C3B270">white</span> and mono-<span style="color:#808080">black</span>, there are 9 possibilities within two colors alone). For an easy way to remember, look at the three-color group that does not contain that pair; those three colors yield 3 other two-color pairs, and 10 minus 3 tells you there are 7 possible two-color combinations. (For example, the three-color group that doesn't contain <span style="color:#C3B270">W</span><span style="color:#808080">B</span> is <span style="color:#4A8A3D">Te</span><span style="color:#006DB3">mu</span><span style="color:#AF2321">r</span> (subtracting <span style="color:#4A8A3D">G</span><span style="color:#006DB3">U</span>, <span style="color:#006DB3">U</span><span style="color:#AF2321">R</span>, and <span style="color:#AF2321">R</span><span style="color:#4A8A3D">G</span>))

## Rating Scale

5.0: Unconditional must-pick, brings a near-winning advantage
4.5: Must-pick if in the right color pair, brings a huge advantage
4.0: First-pick value, extremely good synergy with the color pair theme, can be a decisive card for splashing/switching colors or entering the color pair
3.5: Top uncommons and commons of the set and other single cards of sufficient strength, with good synergy with the color pair theme, can be a key card for entering the color pair
3.0: Important card for the color pair theme, and other above-average cards you'll want in your final deck
2.5: Average level, usually a decent curve filler
2.0-1.0: Below average, generally not played, can be used as filler when necessary but not in excess
0.5-0.0: Unplayable, basically don't play it, it will only help you lose

## Conclusion

This time Avatar returns to ten two-color themes, and during the preview it made my eyes light up! Whether it's the playability in Limited, the development potential in Constructed, or the story of this IP and its deep substance; although I'm not familiar with Avatar, this design not only makes me look forward to Limited but also makes me want to learn more about the IP. I think that, by explaining in an accessible way and blending each IP's unique elements, look, and Easter eggs into MTG's mechanics and gameplay, this is a kind of derivative creation that takes enormous effort, and I have to say the Wizards design team really made the main [TLA](https://scryfall.com/sets/tla) set excellently this time (not including [TLE)](https://scryfall.com/sets/tle)! And through such a link, MTG players and the IP's existing audience become interested in each other, which would be a win-win collaboration. I think that is what Universes Beyond (UB) was originally meant to achieve, rather than leaving both groups disappointed.

What I've said above cannot be fully experienced unless you actually play Limited. So, this weekend, gather friends you haven't seen in a long time and come experience the charm of Avatar Limited together!
