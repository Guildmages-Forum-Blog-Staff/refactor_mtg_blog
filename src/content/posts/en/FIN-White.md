4.5: Unconditional first pick if you're in the right color pair, brings a huge advantage

3.5: Top uncommons and commons of the set and other strong enough cards, good synergy with the color pair's theme, can be a key card for entering the color pair
3.0: Important cards for the color pair's theme and other above-average cards, you'll want them in your final deck
2.5: Average, usually a decent curve filler
2.0 to 1.0: Below average, basically leaning toward not playing; can be used as filler when necessary, but not too many
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable, basically don't play it, it will only help you lose

## Conclusion

The officially designated themes for white color pairs in this set are as follows:

- White-Blue: Artifacts
- White-Black: Sacrifice
- White-Red: Equipment
- White-Green: Go wide

Job select Equipment works with almost all of the themes above. White-blue and white-red have the right types directly, so I won't elaborate.

Equipment can boost small creatures to a truly threatening level, so when your Equipment provides enough bonus, going wide and then having your creatures take turns picking up Equipment to force the opponent to trade is more annoying to the opponent than a plain big body, so white-green's go-wide theme can benefit from it too.

White-black's theme is sacrifice, and the premise for sacrifice strategies to work is also a certain ability to go wide. If it's going wide with creatures, the white-green logic above applies. Also, many of the sacrifice engines in this set can eat creatures or artifacts, so from that angle Job select is a two-for-one kind of fodder, especially those Job select Equipment that can be cast for 2 mana and also serve as normal curve creatures, so you won't feel too bad about eating them.

So although many Job select Equipment are given only average, curve-filling scores based on individual card performance, they have enormous potential in strategic synergy, and you should pay more attention to this when choosing a white color pair.
