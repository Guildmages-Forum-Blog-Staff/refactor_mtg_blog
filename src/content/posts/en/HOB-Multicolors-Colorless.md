## Color Pair Themes

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/HOB-Multicolors-Colorless/e41f00edf629.webp)

HOB is a small set of 193 cards, built from 5 main two-color draft themes that correspond to the important battle at the end of the story, the Battle of the Five Armies. Players can imagine themselves representing the corresponding race, fighting the other tribes to win!

### <span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span><span style="color:#C3B270">White</span> Humans Recruit

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/HOB-Multicolors-Colorless/597995e2dc1e.webp)

How the mechanic works: When you **Recruit**, first draw a card and then discard a card. If the card you discarded is a nonland card, you additionally create a 1/1 white Human Soldier token. Mechanically it is a variant similar to Connive, but creating tokens and putting counters (+1/+1 counters) provide different board advantages in different situations.
Game plan: When you Recruit, you need to evaluate whether to keep that nonland card or discard it right away to create a 1/1 token and widen the board. Either way, the Recruit mechanic makes your deck run more smoothly, so as long as your curve isn't high, you can consider playing only 16 lands.
Synergy: Some cards in the Blue-White pair care about whether you draw a second card, such as {% mtglink "Bard the Bowman" %} , and can all be rewarded by the Recruit mechanic.

### <span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span><span style="color:#808080">Black</span> Amass Goblins

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/HOB-Multicolors-Colorless/5c5745e754d0.webp)

How the mechanic works: When you **Amass Goblins X**, if you don't control an Army token, first create a 0/0 black Army token, then put X +1/+1 counters on it, and it also has the creature type Goblin. If you already control one, put X +1/+1 counters on that token.
Game plan: Amass Goblins isn't simply a go-wide mechanic; it has the flexibility of growing a single body. You can pair it with sacrifice effects to consume the tokens you create, or keep amassing until a single Army grows large enough to change the game.

### <span style="color:#808080">Black</span><span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span> Hordes Ferocious

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/HOB-Multicolors-Colorless/6e613338daf6.webp)

How the mechanic works: As long as you control a creature with power 4 or greater, cards with the **Ferocious** ability get an extra bonus.
Game plan: This is a standard midrange approach that needs no complex combinations and can suppress the board with excellent, powerful creatures alone. However, although you need creatures with 4 or more power to trigger Ferocious reliably, don't put in too many high-cost cards. In the two-to-three-mana curve, cards like {% mtglink "Desolation Prowler" %} or {% mtglink "Duskwatch Hunter" %}, which can satisfy Ferocious on their own, will be one of the keys to whether the deck runs smoothly. Besides the main Wolves, the Black-Green pair also has Bears in green, with plenty of high-power, strong creatures. And an Army token amassed up to 4 or more can also trigger Ferocious.

### <span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span><span style="color:#C3B270">White</span> Dwarves Storied

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/HOB-Multicolors-Colorless/08be14b3c597.webp)

- How the mechanic works: When you control a combined total of at least 3 artifacts, legendaries, or Sagas, you unlock the player title **Enduring Story**. Once achieved, you keep enjoying the bonus for the rest of that game, and you won't lose the title even if the number of qualifying permanents drops.
- Game plan: Although you need a high density of artifacts and legendaries to meet the condition, don't put in mediocre cards just to fill the count. A smooth curve of creatures and quality removal are still the first priority. In addition, this color pair has a large number of Equipment, whose artifact type not only speeds up meeting the Enduring Story condition but also has many synergies with the Dwarf creatures, making them indispensable pieces. Cards like {% mtglink "Iron Hills Blacksmith" %}, which both support the game plan and streamline your card slots, are important cards for the Red-White pair.

### <span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span><span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span> Elves Landfall

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/HOB-Multicolors-Colorless/d27a34e1fa33.webp)

How the mechanic works: Whenever a land enters the battlefield under your control, you can trigger the **Landfall** ability.
Game plan: Playing lots of lands ensures you can smoothly cast midgame and late-game spells. Even if you flood late in the game, the reward cards of the Landfall mechanic can still let you benefit from it. A potential weakness is that the Blue-Green pair often lacks removal, so when building, pay attention to whether you have enough removal.

## Rating Scale

5.0: Unconditional must-pick, brings a near-winning advantage
4.5: Unconditional must-pick if you're in the right colors, brings a huge advantage
4.0: First-pick quality, extremely good fit with the color pair's theme, can be the deciding card to splash / switch colors or to move into the colors
3.5: Top uncommons and commons of the set and other sufficiently strong cards, good fit with the color theme, can be the key card for moving into the colors
3.0: Important cards for the color theme and other above-average cards, you want them in your final deck
2.5: Average, usually a decent curve filler
2.0 to 1.0: Below average, generally not played, can be used as filler if necessary but not too many
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable, basically don't play it, playing it will only make you lose

## Conclusion

Because the set has fewer cards this time, I tried putting colorless and multicolor cards in the same article, but as I wrote, I actually got the feeling of touring the Hobbit's Middle-earth for a week! From the legendary characters and the various inhabitants they met on their journey in the multicolor section, to the important treasures of the story and the protagonists' signature weapons in the colorless section, and finally revisiting the story's key scenes again in the lands section.

Writing this felt like reading a Hobbit setting book, with Magic: The Gathering cards as the vehicle for unfolding the look of Middle-earth in front of players once more. The only regret is probably that there really are too few cards this time. Why not make it a big set of 260-plus cards?
