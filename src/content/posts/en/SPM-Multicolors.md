Marvel's Spider-Man (SPM) holds its prerelease events on September 19, 2025, and launches officially a week later on September 26, 2025. Want to know more about how each card rates in Limited, with recommendations? Then check out this series of SPM Limited introductions!

Also, because of licensing issues, the physical cards and the digital cards online (MTGA, MO) in this series have different card names and different art, but the same rules text (with a few proper nouns still differing). So this Limited primer shows both versions of the card art side by side for readers to compare.

- For the mechanics, see this article: [Marvel's Spider-Man Mechanics Breakdown](https://guildmagesforum.tw/SPM-Mechanism/)
- The physical set is named Marvel's Spider-Man ([SPM](https://scryfall.com/sets/spm))
- The online set is named Through the Omenpaths (tentative translation; [OM1](https://scryfall.com/sets/om1))

## Color Pair Themes

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SPM-Multicolors/c4da3f6306eb.webp)

Unlike past Limited formats, the draft themes of this SPM Limited are reduced so that only the 5 allied color pairs have themes, while the other 5 enemy pairs have none. So when evaluating enemy-color cards in Limited, we adjust a little; of course, strong cards like {% mtglink "The Spot, Living Portal" %} are still worth splashing for.

### <span style="color:#C3B270">White</span><span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span> — Modified

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SPM-Multicolors/6c7de325e1d9.webp)

The Modified theme focuses on these three key points:
- Putting +1/+1 counters on creatures
- Attaching Auras to creatures
- Equipping creatures with Equipment

Take the white-blue hybrid common {% mtglink "Skyward Spider" %}: it is not much on its own, but as long as you can make it modified, it becomes a threatening flier. {% mtglink "SP//dr, Piloted by Peni" %} brings its own source of modification, and also turns every attack by a modified creature into an endless stream of card resources. And the rare Equipment {% mtglink "Biorganic Carapace" %} is outrageous: besides boosting stats, damage dealt by a single creature draws a considerable number of cards.

### <span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span><span style="color:#808080">Black</span> — Villain Connive

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SPM-Multicolors/a88fbac1032e.webp)

Connive needs a target creature to work. Whether it is {% mtglink "Mob Lookout" %} or {% mtglink "Prowler, Clawed Thief" %}, they can trigger connive once or many times. Beyond pairing with the madness-like Mayhem mechanic of the upcoming black-red pair, the cards you discard to the draw-then-discard, such as {% mtglink "Morbius the Living Vampire" %}, which has extra effects in the graveyard, can be boldly discarded depending on the situation and have their value squeezed out in later turns XD Of course, discarding also speeds up filling the graveyard, so some Villains like {% mtglink "Doc Ock, Sinister Scientist" %} benefit from it as well.


### <span style="color:#808080">Black</span><span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span> — Mayhem

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SPM-Multicolors/3dbafddb215b.webp)

The black-red pair's new mechanic, Mayhem, relies heavily on discarding as the trigger. So the black-red hybrid common is a sorcery, {% mtglink "Pumpkin Bombardment" %}: you can simply pay 3 mana, or discard a card as an additional cost to cast it for just 1. And if the card you discard happens to be {% mtglink "Scarlet Spider, Kaine" %}, you can pay its Mayhem cost to cast it from the graveyard later. The other card, {% mtglink "Green Goblin, Revenant" %}, besides being a source of discards, may also let you trade for more cards in hand to keep your snowball rolling.

> Note: A Mayhem cast still has to follow the timing restrictions of the card itself.

### <span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span><span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span> — Large Spell

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SPM-Multicolors/057cc2864bfc.webp)

This refers to spells with mana value four or higher (does it remind you of the [blue-red mechanic in FIN](https://guildmagesforum.tw/FIN-Multicolors/#%E8%97%8D%E7%B4%85-%E2%80%94-%E5%9B%9B%E8%B2%BB%E6%88%96%E4%BB%A5%E4%B8%8A%E9%9D%9E%E7%94%9F%E7%89%A9%E5%92%92%E8%AA%9E%EF%BC%88Big-Noncreatures%EF%BC%89)?). Since the focus is on large spells, mana accelerants are naturally indispensable, and the red-green hybrid common {% mtglink "Spider Manifestation" %} is an important little helper for this pair. The uncommon {% mtglink "Kraven, Proud Predator" %} can cash in on large spells to boost its attack. Another uncommon, {% mtglink "Rhino, Barreling Brute" %}, is a capable finisher for this pair.

### <span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span><span style="color:#C3B270">White</span> — Web-Slinging

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SPM-Multicolors/b7eeb619561b.webp)

In the green-white pair, the new Web-slinging mechanic is a play style with plenty of this pair's flavor. To make it work, you need the following components working together:

- Tapped creatures: achieved by attacking with creatures, activating tap abilities, Crew, or Convoke (only the mythic rare {% mtglink "Web of Life and Destiny" %} has it)
- Creatures with the Web-slinging mechanic in hand
- A Web-slinging cost you can pay

The green-white hybrid {% mtglink "Gallant Citizen" %} draws a card on entering, which is well suited to being returned and reused. However, its body is weak, so it can basically hardly meet the tapped condition by attacking. The uncommon {% mtglink "Spider-Girl, Legacy Hero" %} has trample and puts out a 1/1 token on leaving, making it a more proactive source for Web-slinging. And I especially want to introduce the mythic rare {% mtglink "Silk, Web Weaver" %}, which works extremely well with this mechanic. Web-slinging helps her come out early, and when you return a source and slam it down again it can fill the board, and the five-mana activated ability can turn the board into lethal damage.


## Rating Scale

5.0: Always take it; brings an advantage close to winning the game
4.5: Always take it if you are in the right colors; brings a large advantage
4.0: Worth a first pick; synergizes extremely well with the color pair's theme and can be the deciding card for splashing, switching colors, or entering the pair
3.5: Top uncommons and commons of the set, and other cards that are strong enough; synergize well with the color pair's theme and can be the key card for entering the pair
3.0: Important cards for the color pair's theme, and other above-average cards; you want them in your final deck
2.5: Average; usually a decent curve filler
2.0–1.0: Below average; basically you lean towards not playing it, and it can be used as filler when necessary but not too many
0.5–0.0: Unplayable; basically don't play it, it will only help you lose

## Conclusion

Looking at multicolor overall, the white-blue Modified theme should still have the most supporting cards, bolstering stats while also topping up your card resources; the black-red Mayhem, with a moderate mix of discard sources and Mayhem cards, should also snowball into an unstoppable advantage. Green-white Web-slinging depends heavily on pairing with that mechanic, and how to tap creatures effectively without slowing your tempo will be one of the keys to building green-white, but don't worry too much, since green's excellent bodies can still solve most problems. As for blue-black Villain Connive and red-green Large Spells, they lean towards traditional Limited play, and if the three elements of curve, removal and bombs are all in place, they can naturally make full use of their color pair's advantages.
