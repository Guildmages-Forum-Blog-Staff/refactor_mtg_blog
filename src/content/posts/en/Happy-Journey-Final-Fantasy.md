## We Met Because of Magic
Our fate of becoming friends through fighting took shape in the small Windy City. At first Requiem_Black was just a little sister working at a board game shop, and my husband was a taciturn customer whose default face looked fierce. Fate strangely made us husband and wife. Yet there has never been a game where his wife wasn't beaten black and blue. I think what's needed is not a restraining order, but swapping every card in my husband's deck for lands and then asking him whether he wants to concede.

Hello everyone, this is Requiem_Black. Thanks to [cephille](https://www.facebook.com/Pignosemtgworld) for the invitation to write, and thanks to everyone I've met in the Magic world!

## Too Timid for an Independent Trip
It's not that we had never been abroad. We have set foot in Japan and the US, but joining a tour group has always been our safe card as a couple. With a solution everywhere and everything relying on the tour guide, it's a comfortable choice for travel. We, too timid to go independent, kept staring at the officially released "Magic Spotlight: Final Fantasy".

"Is there any possibility...?"

## I Love My Fellow Magic Planeswalkers
Is there any possibility that when a certain plane calls out to us strongly, we will finally answer that passionate invitation?

Yes. We got up and looked for planeswalkers heading to the same plane to form a group. Three planeswalkers from the Hsinchu area joined, and we opened a LINE group to dig through posts, book hotels, and look for flights together. Requiem_Black fed all her dreams to AI and generated the boldest campaign itinerary ever. When Magic met Final Fantasy, it triggered the first independent trip of our lives! (Report from the convention: using AI to plan an itinerary is a very efficient, time-saving method, strongly recommended by Requiem_Black)

Thanks to Hsinchu-area planeswalkers Jawa, manamagic, and 阿奇. Without you, our courage would never have grown, and this trip would never have departed.

## Flying and Dashing
The two of us did our homework carefully: a checklist for going abroad and preview practice for the tournament. We watched the professional tournament from the week before our flight together, agonized over the deck until we were nearly bald from scratching our heads, and in the end still went to 新世紀 to borrow Prowess cards from fellow players (heartfelt thanks to 老宋, my Prowess Superman). Requiem_Black played Izzet and my husband played mono-red. The tournament announcement said the decklist had to be uploaded by 6/27, and we finished uploading on 6/25, ready to welcome the day of the flight.

The Magic Spotlight: Final Fantasy event ran for three days, 6/27-29 (Fri-Sun). We flew on 6/26 (Thu). On the day we took off, a young typhoon, Sepat, was also heading toward Japan, which worried us greatly, but luckily it didn't affect the flight schedule.

We arrived at Narita Airport at noon. Holding the itinerary guide we had planned in advance and printed out, we became agile the moment we entered the battlefield, quickly transferring to a bus and rushing to the lobby of the APA Hotel Tokyo Bay Makuhari to leave our luggage. Relieved of the burden of luggage, we dashed 900 meters to the JR, switching from line to line, and here we had to praise how amazingly useful Suica is, practically Japan's EasyCard! With one card in hand, there are no ticket-buying worries! You only need to make sure you're on the right line and in the right direction, and you'll eventually arrive where you want to go.

## Hareruya Takadanobaba Store
As an MTG player, how could we not visit Hareruya?

Transferring and transferring, we arrived near Takadanobaba Station, looking for the famous Hareruya3-Takadanobaba, the largest Magic specialty store in all of Japan!

On a first visit, if you use Google Maps you'll find that there are two Hareruya on the same street in Takadanobaba. One is Hareruya3, which has no Magic; the other is the Tournament Center, with a sign clearly marked with the Magic logo, which is the Magic specialty store, so don't go to the wrong one!

![12](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Happy-Journey-Final-Fantasy/eece738e79c8.webp)

As soon as you enter, the staff's energetic greeting meets you, glass display cases full of cards beckon to you, and the variety of booster packs, merchandise, sleeves, deck boxes, and playmats is dazzling. Continue inward and even the vending machines sell booster packs, which was very novel for us from Taiwan!

## 6/27 - Magic Spotlight Special Exhibit Area
After flying into Japan the day before and running around Shinjuku and Shibuya, we broke our single-day record with over twenty thousand steps and my feet were about to fall off. I couldn't help admiring how good Japanese people are at walking; Requiem_Black might be better suited to rolling. After a good night's rest, we welcomed the first day of the event, the side events. Queueing to enter was very orderly, and at the entrance there were large standees of Cloud and Sephiroth, super cool! Inside the entry-only, exit-only gate was an amazing, spacious magical venue!

![28](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Happy-Journey-Final-Fantasy/95529bfb48e3.webp)

At the first stop at the entrance, we were immediately drawn to the special exhibit area. Combining two powerful IPs, Magic: The Gathering and Final Fantasy, the vast audiences they have accumulated over more than thirty years were all eagerly anticipating such a grand event. The most eye-catching thing in the exhibit area is without a doubt the HARDY DAYTONA that Cloud drives when escaping the Shinra Building. As a cool vehicle familiar to players, it is displayed life-size right before you. The guys must all want to buy the car on the spot, right? ## 6/27 - Magic Spotlight Side Event: Challenge Standard
The main event was on the second day, so today was more like a warm-up. Players streamed into the venue, and vendors had set up early, waiting for customers to look for rare and unusual items.

![37](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Happy-Journey-Final-Fantasy/ffbea410e426.webp)
![38](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Happy-Journey-Final-Fantasy/17a95ec0be8a.webp) 
 
We signed up for Challenge Standard, with an entry fee of 2500 JPY per person:
1-	Queue
2-	Join the event using Companion (staff walk around holding a sign for queued players to scan the event QR code or manually enter the event number)
3-	Pay (cash only)
4-	Staff verify the player's app name to confirm they have joined the event
Once these are done you can leave the queue, wait for pairings and a seat, and then you can start fighting.

My husband, the strong one, nicely took three wins, and his mono-red [[大鼠]] blitz did well in the warm-up.
Requiem_Black, with Izzet Prowess, got one win, slowly getting a feel in the event, and, not understanding Japanese, calling a JUDGE first got more and more natural (?)!

In Challenge Standard events, players with three wins get an FFXIV twin-panel promo, the left panel a Magic card and the right panel a card from the Final Fantasy Trading Card Game published by SQUARE ENIX.

## 6/29 - Magic Spotlight Main Event Day 2: Side Event Team Sealed
 
The main event's second day kicked off with great fanfare, and our travel companion from the Hsinchu-area planeswalkers, manamagic, advanced to the fierce Day 2.

![47](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Happy-Journey-Final-Fantasy/f968f3dfc5d5.webp)

Jawa dominated Day 1 with Omniscience. We as a couple were both excited and worried. Excited that our teammate's outstanding performance could take him straight to Day 2, and worried that the pre-registered three-person team Limited open was the couple + Jawa, so if Jawa advanced to Day 2 the team would be one person short, and we might have to stand at the door holding a sign to recruit a teammate, hahahaha!

Requiem_Black, my husband, and Jawa played the pre-registered Team Limited Open as scheduled. Team Limited Open is made up of three players registered in advance, with no on-site registration. The three players are numbered A, B, and C and use Companion for match pairings. At check-in, the organizer guides each team to take red, green, and blue wristbands, instructing players to wear them on their wrists following the rule A-red, B-green, C-blue, for easy identification.

![48](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Happy-Journey-Final-Fantasy/107b590e1a68.webp)

Format: Sealed
Participation prize: Promo Swords to Plowshares《剣を鍬に》(FOIL), Promo Alphinaud
(FINAL FANTASY Trading Card Game/Japanese/Foil)
  
![49](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Happy-Journey-Final-Fantasy/2b32c07edaa8.webp)

On site, the organizer provides 12 opened and checked booster packs of cards, with an orange registration sheet already completed by the organizer listing all the cards of this pool. There are also 3 blank white registration sheets for the team to fill out after building decks. The cards from these 12 boosters need to form 3 decks for players A, B, and C to play. Since we are weak in Japanese, the organizer very thoughtfully asked each table in advance and offered a choice of English boosters. Since the event's official announcement only said that Japanese boosters would be used, this extra service made us feel very touched.
 
![50](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Happy-Journey-Final-Fantasy/bdc82663e3e7.webp)

Each table has two teams with 6 players in total. Each player plays against the player with the same wristband color (no swapping), best of three, and as soon as one team at the table has 2 winners, it is the winning team for that round. Players who finish their match may give tactical guidance and advice to their teammates, which a judge confirmed is allowed to help teammates. Since we had encountered an opposing B who kept coaching teammate C in the middle of a game, my B (Requiem_Black) and my husband A saw this and also swapped seats, letting my husband A support teammate C, Jawa. At this point, the opposing B saw this and directly stopped a passing judge, asking in Japanese:
"Excuse me, can players change seats?"
This meant that my husband A and Requiem_Black B swapped seats after finishing their matches. From the wristband colors you could indeed quickly tell that we weren't sitting in the player seats assigned by the organizer, but we hadn't left the table. The opposing B tried to get third-party support to pressure the supporter, my husband A, to return to his original seat, to interrupt our support. And the opposing B could then use their home advantage to keep feeding output to their teammate C next door.

The judge answered: "After finishing the match, it's allowed (to swap seats), and players can give their teammates advice."
And after replying in Japanese, they explained to us again in English translation.

![51](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Happy-Journey-Final-Fantasy/c7f58c15df94.webp)
![52](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Happy-Journey-Final-Fantasy/5c83c2497248.webp)
 
Seeing that all players have considerable determination to win, no matter the country. Requiem_Black's feeling about this incident is that if the conditions are equal, I'm happy to follow and obey the competitive rules, but this case was relatively unequal, and it felt clearly not very comfortable. It also let me see different sides of players at competitive-level events.

We were there to take part. Although we didn't place in Team Limited Open, the whole process was truly great fun, and during the games you could even get stuck on lands together with your opponent until it was silly, and then laugh together at the end. Even if our languages were different, we met each other through participating in the event, and treasuring this process of player exchange, this kind of joy is precious.
