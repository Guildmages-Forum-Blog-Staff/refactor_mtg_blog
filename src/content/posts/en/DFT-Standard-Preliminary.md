## Deck Introductions

### Mono Green Ramp

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6912387#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/a011a74cd3f0.webp)

This deck embraces the full Mono-Green aesthetic: ramp into big creatures and beat down. [Lumbering Worldwagon](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/168/lumbering-worldwagon) and [Overlord of the Hauntwoods](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/194/overlord-of-the-hauntwoods) both provide board presence while quickly raising your Forest count, and [Awaken the Woods](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/170/awaken-the-woods) snowballs further and sets up a defense. As long as you have enough creatures and Forests on the battlefield, a single [-7] from [Nissa, Ascended Animist](https://scryfall.com/card/one/175/nissa-ascended-animist) ends the game on the spot.

![Key cards](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/4004168f38bd.webp)




### Mono Green Dinosaur

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6912375#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/0bffa221798d.webp)

The biggest advantage Dinosaurs have over other green decks is the second set of one-mana ramp creatures, [Ixalli's Lorekeeper](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/194/ixallis-lorekeeper). The awkward part before was that, while jumping from one mana to a three-mana Dinosaur is powerful, good three-drop Dinosaurs were scarce, basically just [Pugnacious Hammerskull](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/208/pugnacious-hammerskull) ([Tranquil Frillback](https://scryfall.com/card/mat/24/tranquil-frillback) is a three-mana creature card, but not really a three-drop for curve purposes). Now that [Regal Imperiosaur](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/177/regal-imperiosaur) fills the slot with a simple, brutal three-mana beater, the Dinosaur curve is finally complete.

As for those four [Wastewood Verge](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/268/wastewood-verge), what are they for? They are a small bonus that powers up the sideboard [Tear Asunder](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/183/tear-asunder). It costs nothing, and gives a few more color-fixing opportunities anyway.

![Key cards](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/e7c2293e7404.webp)

### Gruul Delirium

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6915048#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/2355cf3a3ef2.webp)

[Afterburner Expert](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/150/afterburner-expert) almost single-handedly holds up the [Exhaust](https://guildmagesforum.tw/DFT-mechanism/#%E7%AB%AD%E7%B5%95%EF%BC%88Exhaust%EF%BC%89) strategy. It's a solid high-power attacker on its own, and the fact that after exhausting once it can return from the graveyard to the battlefield with no drawback gives it terrifying staying power. [Draconautics Engineer](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/121/draconautics-engineer) is excellent by itself, and additionally provides a cheap Exhaust recursion point. Also, when you activate [Draconautics Engineer](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/121/draconautics-engineer)'s first Exhaust ability, [Afterburner Expert](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/150/afterburner-expert)'s return trigger resolves first, and only then does the Exhaust ability resolve to give all your teammates haste. This lets the returned [Afterburner Expert](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/150/afterburner-expert) rush out together with the rest. You'll see these two show up again and again in other decks later, as if glued together.

Back to the deck itself: because [Afterburner Expert](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/150/afterburner-expert) can come back from the graveyard, decks that can fill the graveyard make better use of it. Gruul Delirium is exactly that kind of graveyard-filling aggressive deck.

### Mono Red Goblin

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6915010#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/7b2977c39723.webp)

It looks like Wizards really wants to push the Goblin tribe lately. You can almost build the whole deck from just the two newest sets, and there are so many quality three-drops that the former war god [Squee, Dubious Monarch](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/146/squee-dubious-monarch) can't find a slot. Because I liked the efficient board development and mana production of [Howlsquad Heavy](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/146/squee-dubious-monarch) + [Krenko, Mob Boss](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/146/squee-dubious-monarch), I also experimentally included one [City on Fire](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/135/city-on-fire) to play as an ultimate.

### Redshift Combo

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6915072#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/91c876c47f8e.webp)

Yes, it's [Agatha's Soul Cauldron](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/242/agathas-soul-cauldron) Exhaust time again, this time in a combo build. [Agatha's Soul Cauldron](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/242/agathas-soul-cauldron) + [Redshift, Rocketeer Chief](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/218/redshift-rocketeer-chief) + [Sleep-Cursed Faerie](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/66/sleep-cursed-faerie) can turn any creature with power 3 or greater into an infinite mana source usable only for activated abilities. If [Marketback Walker](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/235/marketback-walker) is also around, you can convert that infinite mana into an infinitely, controllably growing creature. Even if you can't get through in combat, you can use [Voldaren Thrillseeker](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/171/voldaren-thrillseeker) to hit them in the face directly for the win <font color="#AAAAAA">(but if Marketback Walker itself is the one on the battlefield and you want to Fling yourself to kill someone, remember to keep its size within what your own library can handle)</font>. Creatures led by [Draconautics Engineer](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/121/draconautics-engineer) + [Afterburner Expert](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/150/afterburner-expert) provide frontal combat ability, giving the deck a midrange mode so it doesn't rely entirely on the combo to win.

### Selesnya Gearhulk

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6912878#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/fb8fe67028ad.webp)

[Brightglass Gearhulk](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/191/brightglass-gearhulk), as one of the first preview cards of the set, has always drawn plenty of discussion, such as finding [Urza's Saga](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/259/urzas-saga) + [Amulet of Vigor](https://scryfall.com/card/wwk/121/amulet-of-vigor) in more recent formats. In Standard, though, what it can find looks a bit awkward. A deck that truly packs in one-drop creatures may squeeze its curve so tight there's no room for four-drops. There are plenty of good one-mana artifacts and enchantments, but many of them are support pieces, and there aren't that many slots available in the deck (especially in the main deck).

After looking around, using X-cost creatures to adjust the curve seems more reasonable. Those creatures all have +1/+1 counter abilities, which is exactly the specialty of the Green-White color pair, so it naturally turned into a Selesnya counters deck <font color="#AAAAAA">(it almost changed jobs a second time into Selesnya Mounts)</font>. The sideboard also holds quite a few one-mana answers as a toolbox for [Brightglass Gearhulk](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/191/brightglass-gearhulk). Perhaps the ability to reliably find sideboard cards in post-board games will make it look even better than in game one.

![Key cards](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/a7888b5275a6.webp)

### Izzet Vehicle Pirate

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6912372#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/5993f9215762.webp)

[Lifecraft Engine](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/234/lifecraft-engine), which is both a Vehicle and a tribal bonus, naturally went into the deck too.

Forcing a pairing just for Chandra would put the cart before the horse, so in the end single-card value came first.

### Izzet Discard Aggro

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6912369#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/2d5e40e9d4ce.webp)

The discard triggers in this set each trigger once per discarded card. If you can guarantee frequent triggers, [Marauding Mako](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/138/marauding-mako) and [Scrounging Skyray](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/60/scrounging-skyray) grow at a very impressive rate. Standard also has no shortage of fast looting sources like [Kiora, the Rising Tide](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/45/kiora-the-rising-tide) and [Brass's Tunnel-Grinder // Tecutlan, the Searing Rift](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/135/brasss-tunnel-grinder-tecutlan-the-searing-rift). Since drawing and discarding are about equally strong here, you can also look forward to the bonus from [Proft's Eidetic Memory](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/67/profts-eidetic-memory). As for [Monument to Endurance](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/237/monument-to-endurance), although it is also a powerful payoff for discarding, it doesn't quite match this deck's intended rhythm of deploying damage sources quickly, so it went to the sideboard as a Plan B win condition that goes around creature removal.

### Rakdos Engine Lizard

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6921443#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/ef24051a9107.webp)

The [Unholy Annex // Ritual Chamber](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/118/unholy-annex-ritual-chamber) shell gains a new friend in [The Speed Demon](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/105/the-speed-demon). If you haven't reached max speed yet when the end step trigger happens, you can order the stack yourself: resolve [Unholy Annex // Ritual Chamber](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/118/unholy-annex-ritual-chamber)'s drain and draw first to trigger the speed increase, and then resolve [The Speed Demon](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/105/the-speed-demon)'s draw-and-lose-life.

Of course, you don't want to wait until you play [The Speed Demon](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/105/the-speed-demon) on turn 5 to [Start Your Engines](https://guildmagesforum.tw/DFT-mechanism/#%E7%99%BC%E5%8B%95%E5%BC%95%E6%93%8E%EF%BC%81%EF%BC%88Start-Your-Engines-%EF%BC%89), so you still need some suitable sources of starting your engines early. [Gastal Thrillseeker](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/205/gastal-thrillseeker) and [Momentum Breaker](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/97/momentum-breaker) are fairly standard curve-fillers that are easy to understand, but things get interesting when we look at [Hazoret, Godseeker](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/133/hazoret-godseeker). Besides starting your engines itself, it can give a creature with power 2 or less "can't be blocked", and [Unstoppable Slasher](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/119/unstoppable-slasher) and [Bloodletter of Aclazotz](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/92/bloodletter-of-aclazotz) both fit that condition. This is exactly one of the well-known companion combos of the [Unholy Annex // Ritual Chamber](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/118/unholy-annex-ritual-chamber) shell, and now [Unstoppable Slasher](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/119/unstoppable-slasher) is truly **unstoppable**.

### Azorius Mill Control

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6912390#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/8fe803a70205.webp)

Although this is a blue control deck, it has all but given up on being a real control deck. Its wins basically come from the [Riverchurn Monument](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/57/riverchurn-monument) + [Jace, the Perfected Mind](https://scryfall.com/card/one/57/jace-the-perfected-mind) mill package. Few cards in the current Standard can answer a mill strategy from the defensive side, so apart from control decks, the only answer is basically racing. In the past, [Jace, the Perfected Mind](https://scryfall.com/card/one/57/jace-the-perfected-mind) alone could already end a game, but it still demanded that you stabilize the board. Now that [Riverchurn Monument](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/57/riverchurn-monument) is added, you have more capital to race. Against midrange or slower opponents, one or two sweepers might stall their offense and let you go straight into your own lethal rhythm. What's left is figuring out how to survive against aggro decks, and white was chosen precisely for the excellent anti-aggro ability of [Beza, the Bounding Spring](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/2/beza-the-bounding-spring) and [Authority of the Consuls](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/137/authority-of-the-consuls).

### Azorius Affinity

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6912854#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/136cd608ee73.webp)

The new Affinity for Artifacts cards this time, [Voyage Home](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/227/voyage-home) and [Memory Guardian](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/49/memory-guardian), are both straightforwardly good. Although the existing artifact decks already had strong card-advantage tools like [Braided Net // Braided Quipu](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/47/braided-net-braided-quipu) and [Mazemind Tome](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/676/mazemind-tome), they demand more mana and are easier to break through because of tempo gaps. On that point, [Voyage Home](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/227/voyage-home) refills your hand and restores life more promptly, which improves the tempo weakness to a degree. [Memory Guardian](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/49/memory-guardian) is a 5-mana artifact creature with 4 toughness, which lets it dodge a bunch of common removal, and thanks to Affinity's cost reduction it usually effectively costs just 1 mana. This gives [Simulacrum Synthesizer](https://scryfall.com/card/big/6/simulacrum-synthesizer) more chances to flood the board quickly in a single turn.

But because of the Affinity, the deck also needs more artifacts on the battlefield than before. For cheap removal, for example, it plays more restrictive removal like [Glass Casket](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/16/glass-casket), and there's no room to mindlessly fill the deck with [Get Lost](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/14/get-lost) first like before.

![Key cards](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/ff3d9588ce09.webp)




### Dimir Artifact

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6912842#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/21d03b8045d9.webp)

This build starts from [Winter, Cursed Rider](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/228/winter-cursed-rider). Every artifact has ward-pay-2-life, which sounds fierce, but if you spend the early game laying down non-threatening artifacts like the two decks above, never mind how much pressure that 2 life gives. It's hard to say whether the opponent even has a way to target your pile of noncreature artifacts and trigger ward. So you have to raise the number of artifact creatures and build in a traditional creature-midrange structure. That also gives [Oildeep Gearhulk](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/215/oildeep-gearhulk), which has nearly become a meme card, a little work, and lets us try the new two-mana flyer [Diversion Unit](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/41/diversion-unit) along the way.

Incidentally, although the ward life-loss chain of [Winter](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/228/winter-cursed-rider) + [Phyrexian Fleshgorger](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/121/phyrexian-fleshgorger) can be fierce in some life-total-trading matchups, as soon as the opponent plays a [Nowhere to Run](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/111/nowhere-to-run), [Winter](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/228/winter-cursed-rider) becomes almost a joke.

### Golgari Insidious Roots

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6919052#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/9484c153e39a.webp)

[Molt Tender](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/171/molt-tender) provides graveyard filling, graveyard removal, and ramp all in one card, which makes it a perfect fit for an [Insidious Roots](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/208/insidious-roots) deck, and [Bloodghast](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/77/bloodghast) makes the graveyard filling pay off even more.

The deck's other theme is the [Agatha's Soul Cauldron](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/242/agathas-soul-cauldron) shell. [Agatha's Soul Cauldron](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/242/agathas-soul-cauldron) and [Insidious Roots](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/208/insidious-roots) complement each other: [Insidious Roots](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/208/insidious-roots) supplies plenty of creatures with +1/+1 counters for the Cauldron, and the [Cauldron](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/242/agathas-soul-cauldron) supplies a trigger source for exiling from the graveyard. After eating [Loot, the Pathfinder](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/212/loot-the-pathfinder) and [Voldaren Thrillseeker](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/171/voldaren-thrillseeker), you can use their abilities for all kinds of fancy plays. For example, when [Tyvar, Jubilant Brawler](https://scryfall.com/card/one/218/tyvar-jubilant-brawler) is on the battlefield, your creatures can activate abilities as though they had haste, so the plants produced by [Insidious Roots](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/208/insidious-roots) can tap for one mana on the turn they enter. If the [Cauldron](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/242/agathas-soul-cauldron) has already eaten [Voldaren Thrillseeker](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/171/voldaren-thrillseeker), you can use that one mana to [Fling](https://scryfall.com/card/j25/553/fling) it at the opponent, and [Loot, the Pathfinder](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/212/loot-the-pathfinder)'s ability works the same way.

Because both [Agatha's Soul Cauldron](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/242/agathas-soul-cauldron) and [Insidious Roots](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/208/insidious-roots) come with some color-fixing ability, plus [Molt Tender](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/171/molt-tender) as a source of mana of any color, you don't need to worry about color screw even though the deck has no red or blue lands.

### Orzhov Ketramose

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6918999#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/33d268660dfc.webp)

The new Orzhov god [Ketramose, the New Dawn](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/209/ketramose-the-new-dawn) was also a heavily discussed card during previews (though the discussion focused on its pairing with [Psychic Frog](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/199/psychic-frog) in Modern). This build mixes in an enchantment aggro theme led by [Optimistic Scavenger](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/21/optimistic-scavenger) and [Hopeless Nightmare](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/95/hopeless-nightmare), and ties the two themes together with [Scrollshift](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/34/scrollshift). The main deck includes [Rest in Peace](https://scryfall.com/card/big/4/rest-in-peace) to strengthen exiling, so it can trigger [Ketramose](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/209/ketramose-the-new-dawn)'s draw ability more often. Also, many recent decks have at least some graveyard usage or death triggers, so [Rest in Peace](https://scryfall.com/card/big/4/rest-in-peace) isn't a completely dead card when [Ketramose](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/209/ketramose-the-new-dawn) isn't around.

### Simic Turtle

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6927455#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DFT-Standard-Preliminary/7f92ff426a43.webp)

A super let-loose deck. Although it's named Turtle, it isn't really a pure tribal deck; Turtles just happen to make up a fair share. The key card of this build isn't even a Turtle, but a 0/8 legendary vanilla, [Caelorna, Coral Tyrant](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/40/caelorna-coral-tyrant). The deck's main damage comes from the toughness-based damage of [Bedrock Tortoise](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/176/bedrock-tortoise) and [Streetwise Negotiator](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/207/streetwise-negotiator). [Azure Beastbinder](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/41/azure-beastbinder) provides the precious board disruption in Simic, and since every creature in the deck has more toughness than power, it maximizes the card-advantage ability of [Fecund Greenshell](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/171/fecund-greenshell).

Against control or removal-heavy opponents, where our few attackers are easily dealt with but the opponent's offense isn't strong either, try stalling in post-board games with a pile of big-butt creatures and mill the opponent out directly with [Jace, the Perfected Mind](https://scryfall.com/card/one/57/jace-the-perfected-mind).

As a reminder, although [The Pride of Hull Clade](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/172/the-pride-of-hull-clade) lets Defenders attack as though they didn't have defender, it is not the common toughness-based damage effect.

## Conclusion

With the single cards from Aetherdrift added, new ideas such as Izzet Discard Aggro and all kinds of Vehicle decks, and even existing Azorius artifacts, Golgari Insidious Roots, and Cauldron decks have all gotten new toys. Some tribes have also taken shape under the old card framework thanks to key single cards. This Standard first look is only a starting point, so let's keep using our imagination and iterate forward!
