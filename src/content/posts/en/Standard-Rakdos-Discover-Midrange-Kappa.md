## Deck Introduction

First things first, here's the decklist: [Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6143220#paper)

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Rakdos-Discover-Midrange-Kappa/99dfe1be57a3.webp)

Standard Rakdos Discover is a deck that, after Standard Rakdos lost three mainstays in the last ban announcement, [Fable of the Mirror-Breaker // Reflection of Kiki-Jiki](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/141/fable-of-the-mirror-breaker-reflection-of-kiki-jiki), [Reckoner Bankbuster](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/2/7/279acd17-6c17-427b-a69d-fc02442ff4a3.jpg?1685368706) and [Invoke Despair](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/3/5/35af9d5c-4449-4549-b549-c3ba4a67dee0.jpg?1685368727), was upgraded through a pile of powerful LCI single cards such as [Deep-Cavern Bat](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/6/9/69c68c95-b788-43b1-9f22-1b22c5a00b25.jpg?1699044121), [Preacher of the Schism](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/8/9/89345f55-2b32-4356-945a-d56dded39909.jpg?1699044158), and [Trumpeting Carnosaur](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/e/d/edc035ca-f0a3-4814-9405-d6dc6f048315.jpg?1699044357), and has once again joined the ranks of top environment decks.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Rakdos-Discover-Midrange-Kappa/ecb321afcd9b.webp)

## Environment Overview

There are plenty of guides online about how to play and operate this deck, so in this article I want to start from the current Standard environment and explain why I chose Rakdos. I think the LCI environment can be defined by two color groups: one is five-color, the other is black.

![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/4/a/4a1f905f-1d55-4d02-9d24-e58070793d3f.jpg?1676519555)

### Domain

Resources that win the game the moment they land, plus the must-answer huge body of [Atraxa, Grand Unifier](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/4/a/4a1f905f-1d55-4d02-9d24-e58070793d3f.jpg?1676519555).
The cheapest one-mana exile, [Leyline Binding](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/3/c/3c3ac3dd-35db-447f-8674-37b4680a1ef7.jpg?1673306500), which has had a job from Standard all the way to Modern; [Herd Migration (5 Dogs)](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/b/0/b0244a1f-e696-4223-9c14-22c2ca3cb738.jpg?1673307696), which fixes colors and gains life early and can close the game late; [Archangel of Wrath](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/2/d/2d00bab2-e95d-4296-a805-2a05e7640efb.jpg?1673306308), which at six mana fires off 2 shocks, gains 4 life, and whose body keeps gaining life with lifelink; and so on, plus all kinds of cost-skipping and sweepers. This deck has held a top spot in the environment since the start of the LCI environment. The advantage is of course the unmatched single-card power in fair matchups: while others are still using strong 4- and 5-mana cards, five-color Domain may already have slammed down finishers like Atraxa. Against creature decks it has sweeper answers like [Sunfall](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/3/2/32e29c7d-ed4b-4eff-b3c2-d99e5b63ef8d.jpg?1682202915), and against aggro decks led by red aggro, it can desperately gain life with the [5 Dogs](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/b/0/b0244a1f-e696-4223-9c14-22c2ca3cb738.jpg?1673307696) and Archangel of Wrath to hold off the damage and then counterattack for the win.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Rakdos-Discover-Midrange-Kappa/5a423215b3f7.webp)

However, this kind of deck has recently not achieved great results in major competitive events abroad and in Taiwan, because of the rise of Bant Poison, which uses poison as its win condition, negating Domain's life-gain tactics, and, led by [Skrelv's Hive](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/f/f/ffbd77ec-fc81-41d5-934f-c3dd844cb053.jpg?1675956934), unfolds a sea of insects that can quickly rebuild even after being swept. Most importantly, poison is different from life loss: Standard currently has no way to recover from it (only [Melira, the Living Cure](https://scryfall.com/card/one/209/melira-the-living-cure) can act as a vaccine, but you still get one poison counter every turn), which is also why, after analysis, I think there's a risk of getting caught and killed when using Domain in the current tournament scene.

### Black

![Black villains](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Rakdos-Discover-Midrange-Kappa/dbf41103fc09.webp)

> I call them the Three Great Villains of Black.

The other color defining the environment is black. Why only black and not Rakdos? Because mono-black alone can complete this environment-defining play. 2-mana [Deep-Cavern Bat](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/6/9/69c68c95-b788-43b1-9f22-1b22c5a00b25.jpg?1699044121), 3-mana [Gix, Yawgmoth Praetor](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/2/c/2c76f7e0-37e7-4e87-93a3-a25ba0674645.jpg?1675567458), 4-mana [Sheoldred, the Apocalypse](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/d/6/d67be074-cdd4-41d9-ac89-0a0456c4e4b2.jpg?1674057568): the Bat strips the opponent's answers, then Gix's effect and its own lifelink let you draw cards painlessly, and Sheoldred further stabilizes the ground defense and, by drawing cards one-sidedly, widens the life gap.
Even if you don't assemble this golden combination, it doesn't matter; black has plenty of removal to clear out the opponent's key creatures and forcibly hold the line, and the not-yet-mentioned [Preacher of the Schism (324)](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/8/9/89345f55-2b32-4356-945a-d56dded39909.jpg?1699044158) is also a premier strong card of this LCI. With 4 toughness and deathtouch, most ground creatures can't trade favorably with it, and it can even create a situation where it can't be attacked through; this body also lets it attack with confidence to gain board or card advantage, and you can use pain lands to manage your life total, striving to trigger both the Vampire-making and card-drawing effects at once. (Being too versatile also put a certain [black werewolf](https://scryfall.com/card/mid/104/graveyard-trespasser-graveyard-glutton) out of a job.)
To go even more extreme, there's the tactic of drawing a pile of Bats, stripping the opponent's entire hand, praying the opponent doesn't draw an answer, and then forcibly beating them to victory with a pile of Bats; all I can say is it's worthy of being the strongest color in Standard right now, as mono-black alone can use quite a few strong cards and suppress the opponent.

## So Why Choose Rakdos?

Simply put, there's no reason to play mono-black in Standard now: [Invoke Despair](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/3/5/35af9d5c-4449-4549-b549-c3ba4a67dee0.jpg?1685368727) is gone, and Standard now has fast lands, slow lands, pain lands and other excellent dual lands coexisting, even tri-lands. Without a special reason, it naturally trends toward multiple colors to seek good cards from other color groups and play the midrange deck with the greatest single-card value. And because Standard now has two utility lands that can blow up nonbasic lands ([Field of Ruin](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/1/4/143147d2-2eec-41e7-b78a-592288b38630.jpg?1682210418) and [Demolition Field](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/d/9/d9c88546-13c9-4d7e-a618-cb2ccd1dbc0f.jpg?1674422181)), mana bases for three colors have become even less stable, and moreover among current three-color decks only the Esper color group has a clear reason to use three colors (because of [Raffine, Scheming Seer](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/7/1/716a44b4-f6b0-4f14-a270-6442aed3251f.jpg?1664413581)), so the natural train of thought develops toward two-color decks.

Among the colors that combine with black, Dimir and Rakdos draw the most attention in competition. To describe the two simply, the former emphasizes mobility and the latter emphasizes explosiveness. The former keeps costs low, uses low-cost blue flyers to assist damage, and supports combat with [Tishana's Tidebinder](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/9/0/907b3d1d-8c85-4707-80b5-c4d832df9846.jpg?1699043973) and various counterspells, forcibly beating the opponent when they're not fully functional. But playing Dimir feels like walking a tightrope; there are many options each turn, and a slight misstep easily lets the opponent push through a big play, causing a chain reaction and collapse.

For more about Dimir midrange, you can read this article: [cephille's Standard Dimir Midrange Analysis and Tournament Report](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/Standard-Dimir-Midrange/)

In terms of difficulty of operation, Rakdos has fewer worries, because the cards used are also strong cards at their cost, for example [Bloodtithe Harvester](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/f/0/f0192cf7-3391-4720-b9c8-72dec5dde01e.jpg?1643593808), the stalwart of the old Rakdos midrange, whose cost is also appropriately pulled up to 6. Use this deck's golden combination or [Preacher of the Schism](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/8/9/89345f55-2b32-4356-945a-d56dded39909.jpg?1699044158) to gain cards and smoothly increase mana, then convert the extra mana into large creatures and crush the opponent directly with board advantage. Many of this deck's creatures also have ETB effects, absorbing the strengths of the old Rakdos midrange: resource volume and immediacy, which this deck also aims for. The opponent's only effective solution is counterspells, but thanks to LCI reprinting [Cavern of Souls](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/3/a/3aad15a2-8a1b-4460-9b06-e85863081878.jpg?1706137652), the success rate of Rakdos creatures entering the battlefield is greatly increased.

A brief summary: Rakdos is a deck that wins by slamming cards in order by cost, with strong snowball power; once advantage accumulates you keep drawing cards, suitable for players who like this exhilarating feeling and those who like simple brutality.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Rakdos-Discover-Midrange-Kappa/f297700126e3.webp)

The biggest beneficiary of red in LCI this time is the 2 discover creatures. As a variant of the notorious cascade ability, discover raises flexibility: even if the player doesn't cast the spell immediately it can be taken to hand, meaning that as long as a discover creature lands successfully, the best case is an extra spell and the worst case is still one more card in hand; on the side of explosiveness, there's also a side of gaining resource value.

> It feels so good to have [Bloodbraid Elf](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/e/2/e2f12f6f-9383-47e6-a44f-2834ad130e51.jpg?1673148385) back in Standard!

Of course there are still differences: discover requires the creature to enter the battlefield before the ability can trigger, unlike cascade, where one-for-one is determined on cast; but as mentioned above, discover in Standard has Cavern of Souls to escort it, so the success rate is actually not low.

## Pros

1. Simple, exhilarating, brutal to operate
2. Snowball through the golden combination, easy win
3. Plenty of explosive power; depending on what discover flips, there's the power to turn the board in one shot
4. Doesn't over-rely on card synergy; each card has a certain amount of power fighting alone
5. The main deck can win through big plays, and after sideboarding you can match the opponent's game speed, using [Duress](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/3/5/3557e601-9b71-4ce9-9047-1a8baa72e574.jpg?1675957024), Bat, and [Extract the Truth](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/2/b/2bdb9df1-4475-45e7-bc9c-40937c8c1722.jpg?1664410922) to pull away high-cost threats to keep your costs low and fight them
6. Bloodbraid Elf fans rejoice
7. Every discover feels like drawing a card, so good!

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Rakdos-Discover-Midrange-Kappa/8a9fa5dcf3d5.webp)

1. The inescapable fate of creature decks: sweepers.

## Small Features of the Build

This time I used the common balanced 25-land build, and used the combination of [The Cruelty of Gix](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/3/d/3d6e5eb3-25ab-4e9e-96e8-655e0ce74675.jpg?1673307042) + [Trumpeting Carnosaur](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/e/d/edc035ca-f0a3-4814-9405-d6dc6f048315.jpg?1699044357), increasing the chance of digging it back from the graveyard to get Carnosaur out early, adding a few unfair plays to a fair deck. Compared with common builds online, I changed only a few parts, briefly described below:

1. The reason the main deck doesn't run a full 4 [Restless Vents](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/e/6/e628e89b-bee9-408d-bb05-1784fda6b8a1.jpg?1699044708):
   Rakdos's creature lands are quite strong this time: at 4 mana it's a 2/3 menace that can rummage when attacking. But considering that after sideboarding there are many plays that start at one mana, such as Duress and Cut Down, in those matchups whether you can play an untapped land on turn one is your lifeline, so I'd rather use one more Swamp than add risk of tapped lands, and so used 3 to strike a balance.

2. The sideboard uses 1 [Virtue of Persistence (the black Virtue)](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/f/1/f1e5cafb-b0e6-4ee5-8c58-6f8e5ef2b9da.jpg?1692937996) and 2 [Lithomantic Barrage](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/c/4/c45a5f4a-2174-4885-aa5a-c4c24cc732f0.jpg?1682204356), taking out in exchange the extra [Go for the Throat](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/5/4/5446e1ba-c745-45b2-ad05-b22abf04daec.jpg?1682209037), [Anoint with Affliction](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/f/7/f7dc55a8-290f-4666-90ce-aa632e87c5e7.jpg?1675957006), and [Abrade](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/4/7/47f39b5e-2e85-4f31-bbab-0b0bf58f701d.jpg?1699044218):
   Starting with the former, it's for use against red aggro; I'll take any 2 life I can drain, but there are too many 4-toughness creatures in the currently popular decks, so you can swap back to Go for the Throat if needed. The other two cards are entirely aimed at [Raffine](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/7/1/716a44b4-f6b0-4f14-a270-6442aed3251f.jpg?1664413581), where there are actually few places to use them, so if needed you can swap back the other 2 general-purpose removal spells. You could say all 3 of these cards target the tournament environment I wanted to enter at the time, and readers can adjust them as needed.

### R1: VS Domain XOX (1-2)

- Game 1: Although I was on the play, I mulliganed once and also didn't draw a 2-mana creature. The only time I hit the opponent's face in the whole game was one punch from [Preacher of the Schism](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/113/preacher-of-the-schism), then I got hit by [Sunfall](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/40/sunfall). Later I drew [Deep-Cavern Bat](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/102/deep-cavern-bat), but seeing two [Atraxa, Grand Unifier](https://scryfall.com/card/one/196/atraxa-grand-unifier) there was nothing I could do. Finally Atraxa landed, took 5 cards including [Nissa, Ascended Animist](https://scryfall.com/card/one/175/nissa-ascended-animist), and after a look I conceded right away.

**Brief explanation:**
Referring to overseas strategy articles, there are only two ways to fight Domain: one is to wreck their hand early so they can't ramp, and kill them while they're not fully functional; the other is to compete with them on the weight of big plays. This sideboard leans toward the former, but I also worried there was a chance of not finishing the game in time, so I prepared 2 big plays.

- Game 2: On the play, mulligan once, but the sideboard cards all showed up: turn-one [Duress](https://scryfall.com/card/one/92/duress), turn-two [Bat](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/102/deep-cavern-bat), turn-three Extract the Truth, successively stripping or discarding [Depopulate](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/c/5/c53c1898-9107-4bf8-b249-d0502fb9596d.jpg?1664409589), [Negate](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/68/negate), and [Chrome Host Seedshark](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/51/chrome-host-seedshark). The opponent never drew a powerful answer, and I went crazy gaining resources with the Bat + [Gix, Yawgmoth Praetor](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/95/gix-yawgmoth-praetor) combination; a few turns later I had 7 cards in hand while he had just 1, and the opponent realized there was nothing to be done and conceded. At the time I hadn't yet noticed what he had sided out...
- Game 3: This time not as many sideboard cards came, and with only 2 Bats I couldn't effectively strip all of the opponent's hand, especially since on seeing 2 [Herd Migration (5 Dogs)](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/165/herd-migration) in the opening hand, I felt I couldn't lock down his mana costs and so could only change to stripping Sunfall. In the middle of the game I noticed he'd used the 5 Dogs only once to find a land, and roughly realized he wanted to use the 5 Dogs to close the game. Although I desperately hit with Bats, I still couldn't beat the opponent topdecking a Sunfall, and in the end, as expected, I was pushed back and killed by the 5 Dogs. Only then did I realize that the opponent's sideboard plan was probably that, seeing I had [The Cruelty of Gix](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/87/the-cruelty-of-gix), he sided out all the Atraxas. In Game 2, when I had no hand, I didn't plan to drag it out because without Atraxa there was no way for a one-shot reversal, and now looking at another card in my sideboard I thought that having it would have wiped out the 5 Dogs all at once; I could only take this lesson along with the loss.

### R2: VS Dimir Midrange OO (2-0)

- Game 1: The opponent used flyers like [Spyglass Siren](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/78/spyglass-siren), [Faerie Mastermind](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/58/faerie-mastermind), and Bat as main attackers, with backup from stalwart creatures like [Sheoldred, the Apocalypse](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/107/sheoldred-the-apocalypse), [Ertai Resurrected](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/199/ertai-resurrected), and [Tishana's Tidebinder](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/81/tishanas-tidebinder). For me, the most annoying thing in this matchup was not being able to use the Bat's one-sided lifelink to widen the life gap. Game 1 was also a back-and-forth between both sides' creatures, with both life totals dropping to single digits together. I also tried to use the Bat to strip the opponent's [Sheoldred](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/107/sheoldred-the-apocalypse) and answers, but the opponent had [Cut Down](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/89/cut-down) so I couldn't keep it up for long. I tried several times to use Sheoldred to recover life, but it was either answered or neutralized by [Tidebinder](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/81/tishanas-tidebinder). After dragging on for about 25 minutes, finally through the combination of [Preacher of the Schism](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/113/preacher-of-the-schism) and pain lands, I gained both creature advantage and a card-resource gap, and used the little Vampires it made to suicide-attack for lifelink, opening a gap in the opponent's defense. Because [Restless Reef](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/282/restless-reef)'s activation cost was too high, the opponent didn't have enough defenders, and in the end couldn't hold, and I pushed everything through to kill them.

### Finals: VS Red Aggro OO (2-0)

- Game 1: The opponent from the Swiss round 4 ID, an unavoidable fate in the end. I think this deck has only 2 technical points against red aggro: one is the die roll, and the other is whether to keep or mulligan. Red aggro on the play basically depends on whether the opponent's hand is smooth; if the opponent plays out the smoothest combination, no matter how good my hand is I still lose; since the finals can't decide the former, I could only do my best on the latter. Fortunately the opponent mulliganed once, and I saw a 1-mana Cut Down, a 2-mana removal, and a 3-mana Preacher in my opening hand, a perfect hand. The opponent opened turn one with [Kumano Faces Kakkazan // Etching of Kumano](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/152/kumano-faces-kakkazan-etching-of-kumano), making my turn-one Cut Down temporarily useless. In the middle of this game, if I had used Cruelty to dig the opponent's Goddric and race life, it should have ended nearly 10 minutes sooner...
