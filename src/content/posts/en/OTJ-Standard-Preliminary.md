## Before We Start

With the release of OTJ, Standard has reached the largest card pool in its history. At a moment when constructed offers the most options ever, what changes can the new set bring to Standard? Let's Brainstorm!

![Cover](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/OTJ-Standard-Preliminary/92a9f3044a2d.webp)

> Note:
> This article focuses on creative lists that were rarely seen in the previous metagame, and on decks that have improved significantly thanks to new individual cards.

## Deck Introductions

### Golgari Midrange

[Golgari Midrange decklist](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6302065#paper)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/OTJ-Standard-Preliminary/05cb8a945c3c.webp)

Outlaws of Thunder Junction brings back five enemy-color fast lands, which completes the mana curve of enemy-color midrange decks. The addition of [Blooming Marsh](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/266/blooming-marsh) lets Golgari develop more smoothly in the early game. On the draw, you no longer face the cruel choice of whether your first land should be a Golgari tapped land or a Swamp, or whether to keep black mana open so you can use [Cut Down](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/89/cut-down) at instant speed to kill your opponent's two-drop and regain tempo. Basically, this is still the existing Golgari Midrange build with new OTJ cards tried out, so let's see what's new!

For spells, it uses two new Spree cards and a new two-mana black removal spell:

- [Insatiable Avarice](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/91/insatiable-avarice) can act as a weakened [Grim Tutor](https://scryfall.com/card/m21/103/grim-tutor) without paying 3 life; or you can pay 3 life to draw 3. Paired with [Sheoldred, the Apocalypse](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/107/sheoldred-the-apocalypse) it can become "gain 3, draw 3", or "drain your opponent for 9 and draw 3" to finish the game. Basically I chose this card to maximize how often I find Sheoldred and the effect it has. I want at least three Sheoldreds in my deck, but I don't want to draw three of them XD
- The other card, [Smuggler's Surprise](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/180/smugglers-surprise), is very flexible. I can pay three to mill four and take two creature/land cards into my hand, or pay two to give a creature with power 4 or greater hexproof and indestructible until end of turn. In the more extreme case, there is a cap of six mana at instant speed to put two creatures from my hand onto the battlefield. That probably won't come up often, but it should be useful against control decks, and even if it gets Countered, I can just slam creatures down next turn to regain tempo.
- I also tried one [Shoot the Sheriff](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/106/shoot-the-sheriff). Cards like [Power Word Kill](https://scryfall.com/card/afr/114/power-word-kill) always run into the awkward situation where you can't use them when you want to, but since most of the strong cards from the last metagame are not Outlaws, I figured I'd put in one copy to test.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/OTJ-Standard-Preliminary/70de50f1f742.webp)

For creatures, these are the ones I want to prioritize:

- [Honest Rutstein](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/207/honest-rutstein): The new version of [Old Rutstein](https://scryfall.com/card/vow/244/old-rutstein) can pick up cards from the graveyard and make creature spells cost one less. I believe it should perform well in Golgari, a color pair that earns advantage and resources through quality creatures!
- [Vraska, the Silencer](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/237/vraska-the-silencer): A test card. It doesn't die to Cut Down, and our removal can turn the opponent's creatures with abilities into treasures for us. A two-mana removal spell, then one more mana to gain your opponent's [Sheoldred, the Apocalypse](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/107/sheoldred-the-apocalypse)? Try it!
- [The Gitrog, Ravenous Ride](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/206/the-gitrog-ravenous-ride): A 6/5 trampling haste frog for five mana. Haste is strong against control! As the best Mount in OTJ, the frog can also sacrifice the creature riding it to draw cards and ramp, so the four-drop slot brings back [Archfiend of the Dross](https://scryfall.com/card/one/82/archfiend-of-the-dross) to raise the deck's ceiling.
- [Harvester of Misery](https://scryfall.com/card/big/9/harvester-of-misery): Another new card from a subset: The Big Score (BIG). It gives us denser early removal, but mainly it is for annoying creatures like [Grand Abolisher](https://scryfall.com/card/big/2/grand-abolisher) and [Thalia, Guardian of Thraben](https://scryfall.com/card/vow/38/thalia-guardian-of-thraben) that your opponent may have: kill them for two mana and then it's our turn! The -2/-2 ability also gets around the opponent's Counterspell and Duress more easily. Slamming it down as a 5/4 for five mana as a small sweeper is also acceptable, which is why this Golgari list doesn't use cards like [Deep-Cavern Bat](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/102/deep-cavern-bat). What? Kill something on turn two, then on turn three bring [Honest Rutstein](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/207/honest-rutstein) back to get it again? So nice!

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/OTJ-Standard-Preliminary/a0126ee3f99c.webp)

### Azorius Flash

[Azorius Flash decklist](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6305591)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/OTJ-Standard-Preliminary/e593552d18e9.webp)

Azorius Flash already exists in the current Standard. It mainly relies on [Errant and Giada](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/224/errant-and-giada), [The Wandering Emperor](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/42/the-wandering-emperor), and a whole bunch of flash creatures to keep the opponent running around in circles. Let's see what new reinforcements Outlaws of Thunder Junction brings us!

- [Aven Interrupter](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/4/aven-interrupter): A 2/2 flash for three that exiles a spell and plots it. The opponent has to pay two more when casting it later, which is well suited to suppressing midrange decks that only cast one spell per turn, or, when your own spell is about to get countered, you can exile it with Aven and use it later. One issue is that if the opponent's spell is a sweeper or another finisher, Aven may only buy you one turn, and you still have to face the free cast next turn, so when using it you need to consider whether you will still have answers next turn.

  > Don't forget that as long as Aven is on the battlefield, spells your opponent casts from exile or the graveyard cost two more. If the opponent doesn't have enough mana, you can also consider playing several Avens so almost all of their plotted spells can't be cast! You might even catch flashback or impulse draw effects (exile the top X cards, and you may play them until the end of your next turn)!

- [Stoic Sphinx](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/71/stoic-sphinx): A 5/3 flash flyer for four mana. It has hexproof as long as you haven't cast a spell, which is a great fit for a flash deck that doesn't take the initiative. It replaces two of the original list's copies of The Wandering Emperor as a mid-sized finisher to test.
- [Three Steps Ahead](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/75/three-steps-ahead): A three-mana [Cancel](https://scryfall.com/card/m21/46/cancel), or loot two, are both fairly average but useful options, but the biggest value of this card is the four-mana instant mode that makes a copy of a creature or artifact you control. That makes it very flexible to use!
- [Lost Jitte](https://scryfall.com/card/big/23/lost-jitte): The new Jitte is much weaker than the [old one](https://scryfall.com/card/bok/163/umezawas-jitte), but the upside is that its casting and equip costs are also halved, which suits small evasive aggro creatures well. The current Azorius Flash build looks like a good home, so I put in two copies to test. The ability to make a creature unable to block may not be needed in a deck full of flyers (it can be used in mirror matches), but the other two let your flyers add mana at instant speed or untap a land, which boosts Azorius Flash's tempo. The only question is whether you can reliably put charge counters on it. If Jitte still isn't strong enough in testing, it can be cut later.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/OTJ-Standard-Preliminary/4aa5891dc092.webp)

However, considering that [Grand Abolisher](https://scryfall.com/card/big/2/grand-abolisher) will be in the Standard environment, Azorius Flash's tempo will become very clunky once Abolisher arrives. And since it comes down at sorcery speed, most of your creatures are smaller than the opponent's by more than a little. Whether you can find an effective way to deal with Grand Abolisher right away or stop it from entering will be an indicator of whether Azorius Flash can survive in the new Standard.

### Boros Prowess

[Boros Prowess decklist](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6333444#paper)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/OTJ-Standard-Preliminary/932c9150d770.webp)

Outlaws of Thunder Junction brings us [Slickshot Show-Off](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/146/slickshot-show-off), a more explosive [Kiln Fiend](https://scryfall.com/card/jmp/338/kiln-fiend). Combined with the previously great red aggro creatures such as [Monastery Swiftspear](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/144/monastery-swiftspear) and [Fugitive Codebreaker](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/127/fugitive-codebreaker), plus the powerful white heroic creature [Illuminator Virtuoso](https://scryfall.com/card/snc/17/illuminator-virtuoso), along with plenty of cheap spells and, don't forget, your [Inspiring Vantage](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/269/inspiring-vantage), ta-da! Boros Prowess takes shape!

As for the support spells, besides the must-play prowess spells, it uses [Twinferno](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/149/twinferno) for an extra burn, or the traditional one-of in prowess decks, [Temur Battle Rage](https://scryfall.com/card/cmm/264/temur-battle-rage), to let the red bird punch through the sky. Both perform very well. I also experimentally included one [Mishra's Command](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/141/mishras-command). Whether this long-neglected card is the final piece of the prowess puzzle or just another failed attempt is up to players to find out through testing.

As a side note, in testing this deck occasionally still ends up with the prowess and heroic axes not meshing. [Illuminator Virtuoso](https://scryfall.com/card/snc/17/illuminator-virtuoso) can't grow when your hand holds only burn spells, and without haste it is also one of the creatures in this deck that takes longer to make an impact. Cutting Virtuoso for a pure prowess build might be more harmonious; however, there are no other haste prowess creatures that can be cast for two mana in the current Standard, and cutting Virtuoso would also leave a shortage of creatures.

### Mono Red Aggro

[Mono Red Aggro decklist](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6329208#paper)
![Mono Red Aggro](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/OTJ-Standard-Preliminary/5322f82b8447.webp)

[Fugitive Codebreaker](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/146/slickshot-show-off) in the last set already amazed me, and I didn't expect this set to bring an even nastier [Slickshot Show-Off](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/146/slickshot-show-off), pushing red-based prowess decks to a whole new level. A two-mana flying haste pseudo-prowess creature that gets +2/+0 once per trigger is efficient enough as a beater by itself, and Plot is the finishing touch!

Some people might wonder why I would skip a turn and give up a hasty attack. If the opponent has no interaction like removal or Counterspell, it truly makes no difference, but if you judge that the opponent has the answer ready to deal with you, you can choose to plot Slickshot Show-Off first. That way, the mana the opponent held up for a turn to cast their removal may be wasted, and in later turns you get to make multiple threats in a single turn for a small amount of mana. Even if the opponent has enough removal in hand, they may not have enough mana to use it all. The addition of Slickshot Show-Off, without a doubt, raises both the explosiveness and the flexibility of red-based aggro by a notch.

### Mono Red Reanimator

[Mono Red Reanimator decklist](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6329205#paper)
![Mono Red Reanimator](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/OTJ-Standard-Preliminary/9687cd53cc34.webp)

### Mono Green Aggro

[Mono Green Aggro decklist](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6329210#paper)
![Mono Green Aggro](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/OTJ-Standard-Preliminary/41363467aa41.webp)

Mono green gained two new cards this time, [Goldvein Hydra](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/167/goldvein-hydra) and [Railway Brawler](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/175/railway-brawler). Both fit green's style: plain, no-frills, brutally large bodies, and both have trample, so they won't be held up by throwaway small creatures from cards like [Wedding Announcement](https://scryfall.com/card/vow/45/wedding-announcement-wedding-festivity) that can chump block without regret.

Goldvein Hydra's X-cost casting and its ability to create X Treasures when it dies make it a good curve play whenever you draw it. Even when cast early with X=2 or 3, it isn't an attacker the opponent can ignore, and when it dies it bursts into a pile of Treasures, so the opponent may face an even more violent explosive board next turn. On top of that, it has vigilance, so the opponent always has to think about the consequences of trading with it.

### Mono Black Aggro

[Mono Black Aggro decklist](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6336227#paper)
![Mono Black Aggro](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/OTJ-Standard-Preliminary/15e7dcdc16c7.webp)

[Dark Confidant](https://scryfall.com/card/rvr/71/dark-confidant)'s pay-life-to-draw design is one of the classic black designs, and this set has a new [Dark Confidant](https://scryfall.com/card/rav/81/dark-confidant) variant: [Caustic Bronco](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/82/caustic-bronco), which keeps refilling your resources while you attack. Although saddling it avoids the drawback of losing life yourself, and can even make your opponent lose life instead, it still requires extra support, so the overall deck design still follows the idea of keeping your own mana curve low. That includes cards like [Insatiable Avarice](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/91/insatiable-avarice), which is actually a high-cost spell in practice, but as a card it counts as a one-mana Spree card.

Also, to make it easier to meet Caustic Bronco's Saddle 3 requirement, the three-drop slot doesn't use the recently popular [Preacher of the Schism](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/113/preacher-of-the-schism) but returns to the embrace of the old friend [Graveyard Trespasser](https://scryfall.com/card/mid/104/graveyard-trespasser-graveyard-glutton). Although Graveyard Trespasser hasn't shown up much lately, it still performs well in aggro decks, and its graveyard exile ability satisfies the commit-a-crime condition and also supports [Forsaken Miner](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/88/forsaken-miner) and [Kaervek the Punisher](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/92/kaervek-the-punisher).

### Dimir Bloodletter

[Dimir Bloodletter decklist](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6329219#paper)
![Dimir Bloodletter](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/OTJ-Standard-Preliminary/146426a935d1.webp)

A variant of Dimir tempo that hands the heavy responsibility of the deck's final damage to [Bloodletter of Aclazotz](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/92/bloodletter-of-aclazotz). Paired with the third mode of the new card [Rush of Dread](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/104/rush-of-dread), which makes the opponent lose half their life total (rounded up), doubled, it simply drains the opponent no matter how much life they have. In other words, it is a one-turn-kill combo.

The pairing of Bloodletter and [Mindlink Mech](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/62/mindlink-mech) is also fun. Mindlink Mech on turn three and crewing it with Bloodletter on turn four makes your Mindlink Mech a 4/3 copy of Bloodletter. Because you have two Bloodletters, the opponent will lose four times as much life this turn, which could easily kill them in one punch. In addition, [Deep-Cavern Bat](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/102/deep-cavern-bat) and [Preacher of the Schism](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/113/preacher-of-the-schism) are also quality pilots for Mindlink Mech.

Considering that Rush of Dread is effectively a high-cost card, and may be unreliable without Bloodletter to go with it, the list only puts in one for now. If you're excited about this theme, you can raise the count of Rush of Dread yourself. After all, its ceiling is a one-turn kill, so the reward is high enough.

### Dimir Legendary

## Conclusion

We are at a historic moment, exactly as the introduction said: Standard has the largest card pool ever.

Ever since Dominaria United in 2018, when rotation-by-block ended and every set became its own independent large set, the design philosophy of cards has changed greatly. The clearest sign is that closed keyword mechanics or themes, lacking enough sets to supply cards, usually never get substantially strengthened again after their set. At most they get an upgraded dual land or utility land, or a new card in the same role replaces something like removal. Furthermore, cramming a more closed mechanic entirely into one set tends to polarize its power level: either it is so weak it is pointless, or so mindlessly strong that the game becomes hard to interact with on equal terms.

So today's card design emphasizes versatility. It rarely requires an entire deck to run on a single thematic mechanic. Instead, cards with various functions that happen to cooperate are matched together, as long as the whole deck's game plan is consistent. This is also why Standard deck names today are often just a classification like **XX color pair + aggro/midrange/control**, rather than a very specific theme name.

Whether this change is good or bad depends on personal taste. As for the author, I very much like the current Standard. To use an analogy: if a more closed theme is like a jigsaw puzzle, where you piece together components that have already been decreed to go together within a restricted frame, then today's Standard is more like Lego. You can freely combine pieces in your own style and find the play style that best expresses yourself. Of course, what you assemble still has to pass the test of balance, and that can be continuously optimized through repeated testing and tuning.

This kind of constructed environment also brings one characteristic: many decks tend to become collections of powerful individual cards, seeking extra synergy between those powerful cards <font color="#AAAAAA">(the author's own preferred way of building also falls into this category)</font>. As the Standard card pool expands to three years, the pool easily becomes saturated, so once some decks have gathered quality individual cards to a certain point, new sets may no longer change them significantly. But that doesn't mean things never change again; the form of change just becomes more subtle.

If I were asked to evaluate the biggest difference between Standard's construction logic and that of other formats, I would say it is **the clarity of the proactive game plan**. In the mainstream decks of other formats, aside from traditional control, there is basically a very clear operating theme, and the proactive game plan is built around that theme. Standard is less clear in this regard: although many midrange decks have various small synergies between individual cards, it is hard to say what theme runs through the whole deck. The focus of the game lies less in emphasizing a proactive theme and more in how to gain an edge over the opponent in the back-and-forth of interaction, and the depth of play decisions has also increased significantly compared with before. Of course, I'm not saying either style of game has an absolute advantage, but at least for me personally, I love this kind of interaction-focused environment.
