It's been a long time since I wrote a meta analysis article (?

### Azorius Control

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/f547f080c8e5.webp)

UWx Control today can be called the answer-to-everything of the format, beating Phoenix on the left and kicking Rakdos Aggro on the right. The build is basically similar to the earlier structure, but the winning list has more removal than card filtering, so against the two decks mentioned above you don't need to spend the first two or three turns looking for answers. After all, Rakdos Aggro can kill you by turn three now, and still doing {% mtgcard "Deduce" tooltip=true %} on turn two is a little bit (a hundred million bits) bad...
But the price is that you should expect to feel uncomfortable in the Control mirror and against 5C Enigmatic Incarnation, where {% mtgcard "Portable Hole" tooltip=true %}, for example, is utter trash in those matchups...

{% mtgcard "High Noon" %}

Also worth noting is that {% mtgcard "High Noon" tooltip=true %} now shows up in nearly every main deck and sideboard. Only being able to cast one spell per turn suits control and the current meta very well. Although it also limits you to one spell on the opponent's turn, the tempo control wants most is trading one-for-one and dragging the game into the late game, while also stopping the explosive first three turns of Phoenix and Rakdos Aggro.

### 5C Enigmatic Incarnation

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/43b404a75152.webp)

Although the list still says Enigmatic Fires, nobody actually plays {% mtgcard "Fires of Invention" tooltip=true %} anymore.
The main reason is the same as for Azorius Control above: as a slow deck, {% mtgcard "High Noon" tooltip=true %} suits it very well, likewise stopping the explosive starts of Phoenix and Rakdos Aggro, and when needed it can also be eaten by {% mtgcard "Enigmatic Incarnation" tooltip=true %} to find the three-mana creature toolbox.

In addition, the red Overlord {% mtgcard "Overlord of the Boilerbilges" tooltip=true %} and the green Overlord {% mtgcard "Overlord of the Hauntwoods" tooltip=true %} are both a great fit for this deck. For one, they fit the tempo, and they pair with {% mtgcard "Up the Beanstalk" tooltip=true %} to clear the board plus draw cards. They can be found by {% mtgcard "Enigmatic Incarnation" tooltip=true %}, serve as its fodder, and pair with {% mtgcard "Fable of the Mirror-Breaker // Reflection of Kiki-Jiki" tooltip=true %} for setup or a kill. A real multi-purpose card.

> By the way, will Overlord plus {% mtgcard "Up the Beanstalk" tooltip=true %} get banned in Standard or even Pioneer? Turn-two {% mtgcard "Up the Beanstalk" tooltip=true %}, turn-three {% mtgcard "Overlord of the Hauntwoods" tooltip=true %} to draw a card and ramp. Somehow this feels awfully familiar ({% mtgcard "Uro, Titan of Nature's Wrath" tooltip=true %}: let me out).

### Rakdos Aggro

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/e464aa67cf8b.webp)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/b609d6ee99a4.webp)

Rakdos Aggro is also the new darling of the current meta. You can basically see it as a Gruul Aggro with no protection and no follow-up resources, but in exchange it gets a higher damage ceiling.
Although, as in Standard, pairing it with the new Leyline {% mtgcard "Leyline of Resonance" tooltip=true %} can reach a higher ceiling, given that Pioneer has more cheap removal than Standard ({% mtgcard "Fatal Push" tooltip=true %}, {% mtgcard "March of Otherworldly Light" tooltip=true %}, {% mtgcard "Play with Fire" tooltip=true %}), the payoff for chasing that ceiling is too low.

These two builds are much the same: the main deck chases the ceiling and only the sideboard plays defense. But probably because they expected many mirrors, both main-deck two {% mtgcard "Fatal Push" tooltip=true %} and two {% mtgcard "Reckless Rage" tooltip=true %}.
Also, to deal with {% mtgcard "High Noon" tooltip=true %}, both chose to put {% mtgcard "Feed the Swarm" tooltip=true %} in the sideboard.
For midrange and control matchups they pick {% mtgcard "Ob Nixilis, the Adversary" tooltip=true %}. Dragging out the game, Ob Nixilis compresses the opponent's life total while also clocking them, which feels really good.

### Rakdos Transmogrify

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/6065c9d9190b.webp)

Rakdos Transmogrify uses {% mtgcard "Transmogrify" tooltip=true %} to turn a cheap creature into {% mtgcard "Atraxa, Grand Unifier" tooltip=true %}, and suppresses the opponent's actions with lots of removal and discard.
It can be seen as the Rakdos version of {% mtgcard "Indomitable Creativity" tooltip=true %}, but resolving it doesn't win on the spot. In exchange you get a more comfortable floor and ceiling.

As for finishing, you can hit with {% mtgcard "Atraxa, Grand Unifier" tooltip=true %} once or twice and be done, or find {% mtgcard "Ob Nixilis, the Adversary" tooltip=true %}, sacrifice {% mtgcard "Atraxa, Grand Unifier" tooltip=true %}, and use the copy of {% mtgcard "Ob Nixilis, the Adversary" tooltip=true %} to ultimate and hit the opponent for seven.

I also really like {% mtgcard "Rakdos Joins Up" tooltip=true %}: reanimate to get value again, and if it gets killed it still deals damage to the opponent.

> Some decks choose to use the new card {% mtgcard "Valgavoth, Terror Eater" tooltip=true %} instead. I think each has pros and cons: {% mtgcard "Atraxa, Grand Unifier" tooltip=true %} gains resources as soon as it enters but eats some removal, while {% mtgcard "Valgavoth, Terror Eater" tooltip=true %} has no immediate resources on entering but is harder to kill.

### Rakdos Midrange

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/abc652d7a96b.webp)

This version of Rakdos Midrange is tailored to the meta. It uses {% mtgcard "Archfiend of the Dross" tooltip=true %} to clock the opponent quickly and effectively suppress Sacrifice decks, and adds more spot removal and more {% mtgcard "Graveyard Trespasser // Graveyard Glutton" tooltip=true %} to handle Rakdos Aggro.

Compared with {% mtgcard "Sheoldred, the Apocalypse" tooltip=true %}, the advantages of {% mtgcard "Archfiend of the Dross" tooltip=true %} are:
1. It doesn't die to a single {% mtgcard "Lightning Axe" tooltip=true %}
2. It flies, so it isn't afraid of all sorts of chump blockers
3. It beats most of the reach creatures in the format
4. It can be copied with {% mtgcard "Fable of the Mirror-Breaker // Reflection of Kiki-Jiki" tooltip=true %} to reach a single-turn damage ceiling

But its weaknesses are also very obvious:
1. It is very afraid of {% mtgcard "Heartless Act" tooltip=true %} pulling off three oil counters
2. It is also a clock on yourself. If you haven't killed the opponent within three turns, you get killed instead, and when necessary you have to point your removal at your own creature
3. Against Phoenix, as long as the opponent can keep flying Phoenix back to chump, you lose if you're blocked for three turns

The rest I think is a very solid build, though I personally don't like the five 4-drop configuration. Without {% mtgcard "Sheoldred, the Apocalypse" tooltip=true %} as a life gain source, it doesn't feel like quite enough.

Also, the two {% mtgcard "Hidetsugu Consumes All // Vessel of the All-Consuming" tooltip=true %} and one {% mtgcard "The Meathook Massacre" tooltip=true %} in the sideboard are big weapons against Sacrifice.

### Jund Sacrifice

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/9b78488bd8a8.webp)

BLB's two new cards {% mtgcard "Ygra, Eater of All" tooltip=true %} and {% mtgcard "Scavenger's Talent" tooltip=true %} greatly bolster Sacrifice. The former provides an infinite combo, and the latter combines `fodder`, `filling the graveyard`, and `reanimation` in one card.

### Dimir Rouges

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/3fff00839a32.webp)

Dimir Rouges made the Top 8.
... Dimir Rouges made the Top 8?!
It's the year 4202, how is anyone still playing Dimir Rogues?!

Basically the build is a very standard gathering of Rogues. They also chose main-deck two {% mtgcard "Archfiend of the Dross" tooltip=true %} and sideboard two {% mtgcard "The Meathook Massacre" tooltip=true %} to fight Sacrifice.
This deck is also a good choice against control, as nearly all its creatures have flash and it has discard and counterspells too, all things control finds uncomfortable.
But in some matchups (for example Phoenix, and Phoenix, or Phoenix, and Phoenix, plus a Greasefang) it's quite uncomfortable, since filling the opponent's graveyard for them isn't necessarily a good thing.

### Rakdos Cauldron

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/08f7e8a4905a.webp)

Basically this is a midrange deck with an off-board combo. For the combo, you use {% mtgcard "Agatha's Soul Cauldron" tooltip=true %} to eat {% mtgcard "Tree of Perdition" tooltip=true %}, pair it with other creatures to lower the opponent's life total, then use {% mtgcard "Voldaren Thrillseeker" tooltip=true %}'s Backup ability to give another creature the throw ability and kill the opponent. You can also use {% mtgcard "Agatha's Soul Cauldron" tooltip=true %} to eat it and then kill with a creature that has a +1/+1 counter.

I should also mention that in the final-round win-and-in match, the opponent made a very ugly play.
Anyway, it was a Rakdos mirror, one side playing Cauldron and the other Transmogrify. The Transmogrify player had {% mtgcard "Unlicensed Hearse" tooltip=true %} on the board, and the Cauldron side had {% mtgcard "Agatha's Soul Cauldron" tooltip=true %} but was stuck on lands.
Because of the land screw, Cauldron could only grit their teeth and loot away {% mtgcard "Tree of Perdition" tooltip=true %}. But the Transmogrify player voluntarily activated {% mtgcard "Unlicensed Hearse" tooltip=true %} at end of turn to exile {% mtgcard "Tree of Perdition" tooltip=true %} from the graveyard, and the Cauldron side responded by activating {% mtgcard "Agatha's Soul Cauldron" tooltip=true %} to eat {% mtgcard "Tree of Perdition" tooltip=true %}. Combo piece collection progress (1/2).
Then the Transmogrify side killed a creature on their own turn and activated {% mtgcard "Unlicensed Hearse" tooltip=true %} to exile it, and on their own turn the Cauldron side discarded {% mtgcard "Voldaren Thrillseeker" tooltip=true %} to the graveyard and ate it with {% mtgcard "Agatha's Soul Cauldron" tooltip=true %}. Combo piece collection progress (2/2).

So you see, {% mtgcard "Unlicensed Hearse" tooltip=true %} has to play passively when there is an {% mtgcard "Agatha's Soul Cauldron" tooltip=true %} around. If {% mtgcard "Unlicensed Hearse" tooltip=true %} never activates, the first target of {% mtgcard "Agatha's Soul Cauldron" tooltip=true %} will certainly fail. The Cauldron side can only activate {% mtgcard "Agatha's Soul Cauldron" tooltip=true %} on the opponent's turn first to force them to tap {% mtgcard "Unlicensed Hearse" tooltip=true %}, discard {% mtgcard "Voldaren Thrillseeker" tooltip=true %} and eat it on their own turn, then find the second and third {% mtgcard "Tree of Perdition" tooltip=true %} and trick the opponent once in the same way to succeed.

### Selesnya Company

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/9ef4fd980a28.webp)

This is basically Legacy's Death & Taxes, but a Pioneer version mixed with green.

###  Azorius Lotus Field

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/d4f74ea3b9e4.webp)

Essentially a control deck, but it plays {% mtgcard "Lotus Field" tooltip=true %} and {% mtgcard "Strict Proctor" tooltip=true %} as mana cheating, and also hangs three {% mtgcard "Thespian's Stage" tooltip=true %} to be greedy all the way.
Although it's called control, this deck only plays a single counterspell, {% mtgcard "Censor" tooltip=true %}, with the rest being removal.
But I think this is an understandable choice. I guess they felt the current meta doesn't need that many counterspells and threats can all be handled with removal. After all, this deck might tap out on turn three for {% mtgcard "Strict Proctor" tooltip=true %} + {% mtgcard "Lotus Field" tooltip=true %}, and counterspells can't deal with threats in later turns while removal can, so they swapped the counterspell slots for removal.
Similarly, decks in the current meta either can't be swept in time or don't need sweeping, so they play only three {% mtgcard "Farewell" tooltip=true %} as a way to fight Enigmatic Incarnation.

## Wonderful Decks

### Sans-Red Enigmatic Incarnation

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/675fa047139c.webp)

The main Enigmatic Incarnation builds now all play red, pairing {% mtgcard "Fable of the Mirror-Breaker // Reflection of Kiki-Jiki" tooltip=true %} for filtering with {% mtgcard "Overlord of the Boilerbilges" tooltip=true %} as board clearing and a final finisher.

This deck instead uses the blue Overlord {% mtgcard "Overlord of the Floodpits" tooltip=true %} in the three-drop slot to replace the looting of {% mtgcard "Fable of the Mirror-Breaker // Reflection of Kiki-Jiki" tooltip=true %}, and it can also trigger {% mtgcard "Up the Beanstalk" tooltip=true %}.
It also uses the blue Overlord {% mtgcard "Overlord of the Floodpits" tooltip=true %} and the black Overlord {% mtgcard "Overlord of the Balemurk" tooltip=true %} to fill the graveyard, providing fodder for {% mtgcard "Kroxa and Kunoros" tooltip=true %} to produce an endless board.
Removal is mainly black: besides {% mtgcard "Fatal Push" tooltip=true %}, there is DSK's {% mtgcard "Nowhere to Run" tooltip=true %}, and {% mtgcard "Nowhere to Run" tooltip=true %} suits this deck very well, since after killing a creature it can become fodder for {% mtgcard "Enigmatic Incarnation" tooltip=true %} to sacrifice.

### Abzan Greasefang w/ Overlord of the Balemurk & Say Its Name

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/ed74184f3ec0.webp)

Uses the new DSK cards {% mtgcard "Say Its Name" tooltip=true %} and {% mtgcard "Overlord of the Balemurk" tooltip=true %}, and BLB's {% mtgcard "Cache Grab" tooltip=true %}, to replace the earlier {% mtgcard "Grisly Salvage" tooltip=true %} and {% mtgcard "Witherbloom Command" tooltip=true %} as the new ways to fill the graveyard.
Also, when there are three {% mtgcard "Say Its Name" tooltip=true %} in the graveyard, you can exile all three to bring back an {% mtgcard "Altanak, the Thrice-Called" tooltip=true %} from your library/graveyard/hand, and it has an activated ability of its own, so when drawn you can at least discard it and use it to ramp, so it isn't a completely dead draw.

### Boros Token Control

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/301f23b34620.webp)

Basically a standard Boros Token Control, plus {% mtgcard "Fable of the Mirror-Breaker // Reflection of Kiki-Jiki" tooltip=true %} and {% mtgcard "March of Otherworldly Light" tooltip=true %}, and some people play {% mtgcard "Wedding Announcement // Wedding Festivity" tooltip=true %}.
It feels good against UW Control and midrange matchups, and its resources are hard to cut off.

### Sultai Landfall Combo

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Pioneer-Meta-Inspection-PreMIT-7/31ba26d40830.webp)

Lots of ramp up front, then {% mtgcard "Port of Karfell" tooltip=true %} reanimates {% mtgcard "Lumra, Bellow of the Woods" tooltip=true %} onto the battlefield, and the enter effect of {% mtgcard "Lumra, Bellow of the Woods" tooltip=true %} also returns {% mtgcard "Port of Karfell" tooltip=true %} to the battlefield.
If there is a second {% mtgcard "Lumra, Bellow of the Woods" tooltip=true %} in the graveyard, you can repeat this to mill the whole deck into the graveyard and return all the lands, including {% mtgcard "Festering Gulch" tooltip=true %}, a land that deals 1 damage to the opponent when it enters, and use {% mtgcard "Lotus Field" tooltip=true %} to eat it and {% mtgcard "Lotus Field" tooltip=true %} itself, then return them in an infinite loop.
However, this combo is a tiny bit (a hundred million bits) fragile. If {% mtgcard "Festering Gulch" tooltip=true %} gets killed, you can only attack to kill the opponent. Although {% mtgcard "Lumra, Bellow of the Woods" tooltip=true %} is huge, it has no trample itself, so besides dying to removal it can also be chump-blocked by small creatures. The deck also doesn't seem to have a way to shuffle the graveyard, so if you unluckily mill {% mtgcard "Festering Gulch" tooltip=true %} only at the very end and it gets exiled in response, the whole deck seems to be GG.
Personally I suggest considering one {% mtgcard "Turn the Earth" tooltip=true %}, just in case the opponent has graveyard hate in the main deck.

## Meta Predictions

Finally, meta predictions. I think Taiwan will mainly consist of Mono-Black Demons, UWx Control, Izzet Phoenix, and Rakdos Aggro.
Mono-Black Demons is a midrange deck with a combo, and even just putting either of the two combo pieces on the board alone makes a very strong clock/defender.
UWx Control, because quite a few Taiwanese players like playing control, and Phoenix, because quite a few Taiwanese players already own the deck.
And Rakdos Aggro may attract some players who already have Rakdos Midrange to build it.

The rest should be decks like Rakdos Midrange, Rakdos Transmogrify, and Selesnya Company.
I also think Sacrifice isn't a good choice, mainly because it's too easy to target. Even though it now has win conditions besides {% mtgcard "Mayhem Devil" tooltip=true %}, what can't change is that it is extremely uncomfortable against {% mtgcard "Leyline of the Void" tooltip=true %} or {% mtgcard "Rest in Peace" tooltip=true %}.
