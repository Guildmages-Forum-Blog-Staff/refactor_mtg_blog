## Forgetful Fish Basics

First, let me introduce `Forgetful Fish`. It is a casual format whose core creature is {% mtglink "Dandân" %}, and unlike other formats, both players share the same mono-blue deck and graveyard.
The format was created by Nick Floyd in 1997, but it wasn't widely known until Rhystic Studies introduced it in 2023. Together with the recent controversy around the SLD, the format got an even bigger push.

A `Forgetful Fish` deck is basically made up of 10 {% mtglink "Dandân" %} and 8 {% mtglink "Memory Lapse" %}, which is also where the name `Forgetful Fist` comes from, plus many blue instants, aiming to beat your opponent using the top of the library, the stack, and {% mtglink "Dandân" %}.

The rules are the same as most Magic constructed formats: starting at 20 life, seven-card hands, the player on the play doesn't draw, maximum hand size of seven, drawing from an empty library loses the game, and so on. Besides the shared library and graveyard, in `Forgetful Fish` you can mulligan repeatedly until your opening hand contains at least two lands and two spells (you do have to reveal your hand to your opponent, of course), or mulligan following the standard mulligan procedure.

Also, opening hands are dealt alternately, like dealing poker cards, and if some effect makes the players draw cards at the same time, the active player goes first.

Finally, about `controller`/`owner`: for some effects (returning to hand), the card returns to the hand of its `caster`, while `into your library` or `from your graveyard` refers to the shared graveyard and library.

As for how to win, it's basically hitting your opponent five times with {% mtglink "Dandân" %}, or locking things down so hard that your opponent has to draw from an empty library (though that should be extremely hard to pull off).

## The War for the Top of the Library

In `Forgetful Fish`, I think the most important thing is controlling the top card of the library.

{% mtgcard "Memory Lapse" "EMA" %}

For example, {% mtglink "Memory Lapse" %} counters a spell and puts it on top of the library, which means a player can counter the opponent's spell onto the top of the library and then draw it into their own hand by any draw effect. You can also use things like {% mtglink "Predict" %} or {% mtglink "Brainstorm" %} to wreck the top of the library your opponent has set up, or hand the junk cards in your hand to your opponent when they're about to draw.

{% mtgcard "Predict" "C18" %}

In a game against a friend, for example, I used {% mtglink "Memory Lapse" %} to counter his {% mtglink "Dandân" %} onto the top of the library and wanted to use {% mtglink "Predict" %} to gain two cards, but he got ahead of me by cycling {% mtglink "Lonely Sandbar" %} to wreck the top of the library, leaving me one card short.

## "You don't control a Mountain now? Then your Dandân dies"

Although the whole deck is mono-blue, that doesn't mean there's no removal.
...For example, I change all the rules text on your {% mtglink "Dandân" %} to Mountain.

{% mtgcard "Mind Bend" "MIR" %}

> Mind Bend
> U, Instant
> Change the text of target permanent by replacing all instances of one color word with another or one basic land type with another.
> For example: change "nonblack creature" to "nongreen creature", or "Forestwalk" to "Islandwalk". This effect doesn't end at end of turn.

Since you don't control a Mountain, by the rules you must immediately sacrifice {% mtglink "Dandân" %}.
The only ways to defend are to use {% mtglink "Unsubstantiate" %} to return {% mtglink "Dandân" %} to hand first, or to use {% mtglink "Dance of the Skywise" %} to wipe its abilities first and save it for a turn.

> As for whether a card can still have its text changed after it `loses abilities`, the answer is yes, because `losing abilities` doesn't turn the whole card into a vanilla; the card's own characteristics still include that text, it just isn't taking effect.
> Also, text-changing effects are in Layer 3 while adding and removing abilities (losing/gaining abilities) is in Layer 4, so even if {% mtglink "Dandân" %} has its abilities wiped by {% mtglink "Dance of the Skywise" %}, it will still have its text changed by {% mtglink "Mind Bend" %}, and then in the cleanup step of that turn's end step it loses the effect of {% mtglink "Dance of the Skywise" %} and gets sacrificed.

I originally thought I could use {% mtglink "Vision Charm" %} to change the basic land types back to the corresponding ones to keep {% mtglink "Dandân" %} from being sacrificed, but after thinking about it, whether in response to {% mtglink "Mind Bend" %} I cast {% mtglink "Vision Charm" %}, or after {% mtglink "Mind Bend" %} resolves I cast {% mtglink "Vision Charm" %}, there is a moment in between when {% mtglink "Dandân" %} doesn't meet its survival condition and gets sacrificed.
But on the flip side, you can also use {% mtglink "Vision Charm" %} as a one-mana sweeper to wipe out every {% mtglink "Dandân" %} on the battlefield.

## Conclusion

`Forgetful Fish` is very fun to play, and it can also help newer players build an understanding of the stack, or help them get to know and practice how to handle blue mirror matches.
In theory `Forgetful Fish` could be extended to any color, but I think none of them compare to mono-blue; frankly, other colors can't change the top of the library as often as blue does, so they lose the fun of the shared library.
