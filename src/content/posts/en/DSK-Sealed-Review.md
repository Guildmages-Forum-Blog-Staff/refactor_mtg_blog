## Before We Start

Here's another new experiment from Guildmages’ Forum: a brand-new column.

For more than a year now, in the five working days before every Sealed event, the Guildmages’ Forum team has been analyzing furiously and racking our brains to write reviews that are easy to read and understand, pushing so hard that we barely get to catch our breath until the last minute before the event. Once the article goes out, we drag our exhausted bodies to the card shop for the Sealed party we've been looking forward to and play our hearts out!

And then we really did go wild, and the more we played, the more we learned. But the team members, temporarily suffering from Limited-analysis PTSD, kept their butts glued to the card shop chairs and didn't want to return to the screen and keyboard too soon. So it wasn't until today that we realized there was something we could do and hadn't done yet: now that we're more familiar with the flavor of Sealed and draft, why not create an opportunity for an **exciting look back** and **rethinking**?

So let's get started, beginning with a recollection of the first Sealed event I took part in at [遊戲平方](https://www.facebook.com/GameSquareTCG)!

## A Sealed Party: Relaxed First, Tense Later

Compared with the Limited analysis articles we publish before Sealed events, this piece takes a more relaxed tone. It won't spend much space laying out the reasoning and details behind every step, and will focus on memorable individual cards and moments.
Also, a bit of background: over the past month I had my first taste of Modern, and bravely ~~recklessly~~ stepped right onto the MITQ stage. I learned a lot of details under pressure, but I also happened to be mentally and physically drained and in need of a breather, so this **Sealed party** came at exactly the right time for me.

Together with site member [cephille](/en/authors/cephille/), I headed to 遊戲平方 and began exploring Duskmourn with a four-person pod in the afternoon. In the first event, counting the prerelease promo card, I opened two copies of [The Rollercrusher Ride](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/155/the-rollercrusher-ride), and on top of that came powerful cards like [Hedge Shredder](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/183/hedge-shredder), [Peer Past the Veil](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/226/peer-past-the-veil), and [Unidentified Hovership](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/37/unidentified-hovership), plus two stellar [Baseball Bat](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/209/baseball-bat)s. That made me go into Naya (red-green-white) without hesitation. Backed by a steady five green fixing cards and two dual lands, I truly believed it was a very competitive deck. Two [The Rollercrusher Ride](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/155/the-rollercrusher-ride)s in a row could end the game, and even if my opponent was stubborn I'd get delirium going along the way. Seeing that perfect combination, I let out a little sly chuckle, heh heh.
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DSK-Sealed-Review/3aaddf6f2b46.webp)
In the first round I was paired against [cephille](/en/authors/cephille/). I reminded myself this was a time to enjoy the game and to take it with a relaxed mindset ~~maybe I'd even play better that way, right~~, and the result was that I lost, which still left a small knot of gloom in my chest, sigh...
> I think cephille's performance in this series was truly excellent, for an extremely simple reason: he is serious about it and enjoys it. For his more professional perspective, see [cephille's DSK Prerelease Sealed Experience](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/DSK-Prerelease-Experience-Cephille/).

I took the second round steadily with a 2-0. In the third round, because of my overly loose mindset, I missed a trigger on [Acrobatic Cheerleader](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/1/acrobatic-cheerleader) at a moment I should have checked it. Only after that did I start playing carefully and tensely, and in the deciding game 3 I flooded out badly and just got knocked out, finishing at one win and two losses. Frankly, I think it's a real shame, because that was a deck worth going 3-0 with, and it made me realize that **relaxed first, tense later** is a terrible **psychological curve**: if there's a competitive streak inside you, don't deliberately ignore it.

I learned a valuable lesson that day.

After grabbing a quick bite with [cephille](/en/authors/cephille/), I went back to 遊戲平方 for the evening session. I cracked open six boosters again, and this time the card pool was far more miserable than the afternoon's...

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DSK-Sealed-Review/62c2f0dba694.webp)
> Well, I really did my best. I badly wanted to go home at that point.

In the end I built a green-white weakling deck with three rares as its bombs: [Rip, Spawn Hunter](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/228/rip-spawn-hunter), [Twitching Doll](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/201/twitching-doll), and [Ghost Vacuum](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/248/ghost-vacuum) (!?). My head was swirling with thoughts like "how great would those two [Baseball Bat](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/209/baseball-bat)s from the afternoon be right now..." and "how great would it be if I'd made the most of that afternoon match...", and I went on to a record that moved backward instead of forward: one win, one loss, and one draw. One of the losses was once again a thorough bloodbath at the hands of [cephille](/en/authors/cephille/), so ridiculous that everyone burst out laughing XD, which was actually a lot of fun ~~after all, with a clown at the table the party atmosphere gets stronger~~.

> I think cephille's performance in this series was really dominant XD. I just wanted to say it one more time.

Through this event I got a glimpse of the ceiling of the green-white survival archetype. When you reach a stalemate on even footing against a blue-green deck, the opponent can sit back passively and wait for the end step to loot, while a survival deck, without actively tapping creatures, has no choice but to force a path through. Otherwise, quietly waiting while the resource gap widens is also a dead end. I think "survival" is an ability whose weak spot is easy to hit, and I'm neither able nor very willing to master it.

## A Moment of Quiet Magic

After dealing with most of the site's business the day before, on Saturday I wanted to give myself a chance to play quietly: no thinking about proving my improvement in front of my teammates, just play quietly, rest, and enjoy the pure joy of cracking packs and playing.
That day I chose the afternoon session at [貓腳印東門店](https://www.facebook.com/@CatFootPrintDongMen.tw), and unexpectedly there were as many as sixteen players (!?), with quite a few familiar faces XD. My original plan failed, but at least my head wasn't cluttered with stray thoughts. After opening six boosters, I quietly realized the cards in front of me could form the three-color combination I was most optimistic about. So let's go, this looks kind of interesting.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DSK-Sealed-Review/cf3523c6ae84.webp)
> Frankly, there was some luck involved too. Against an aggro deck I'd have had to swap in a lot of sideboard cards.

In the end I put together an Abzan (white-black-green) three-color deck. Its bombs included [Enduring Innocence](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/6/enduring-innocence), [Unidentified Hovership](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/37/unidentified-hovership), [Enduring Tenacity](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/95/enduring-tenacity) (I happened to have three life-gain cards worth playing, so I committed to it), and the super bomb, super flashy [Valgavoth's Onslaught](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/204/valgavoths-onslaught), but none of that was the most crucial factor. In a set that is so often decided off the top of the library, it's the commons and uncommons that guide players deeper into the haunted house that are the deciding factor in a best-of-three, and my MVP card was none of the bombs mentioned above. Please allow me to keep you in suspense for a moment.

>I'm quite optimistic about the Abzan color combination in this set, because when the card pool has no clear theme, **green's creature quality and fixing are comparatively outstanding**, while white-black has a fair number of cards that dig creatures out of the graveyard directly, not to mention that black has some demons that put you close to winning the moment they enter the battlefield. You can carve out a "drop it down, pick it back up" route of your own. I personally think it's worth trying and a lot of fun, and I'd been looking forward to it for a long time.

In the first round I faced a player surnamed 謝 playing black-green, and I smoothly took the win with a 2-0. After the match, while chatting, I realized my opponent was a beginner on their second day of playing (!!). Their skill was still a bit green, but already fairly proficient. They said they had experience with PTCG and had done some homework online before coming to play. The other players in the shop must have overheard our conversation, and they joined in one after another, kindly offering all sorts of advice to the newcomer. When I heard that 謝 (I presumptuously guess they must still be quite young XD) intended to play Standard in the future, I just handed over all my unused cards, and only later realized I'd put myself at a disadvantage with no sideboard... showing off comes at no small price.
What impressed me most was 謝's "heart". Their attitude toward losing is healthier than most people's, and they actively dig into every detail of the play. I can almost picture them becoming a formidable player in the future. I wish 謝 all the best! A truly, truly impressive young person, and I'm glad and honored to have met them at this time ~~from now on it'll be their turn to win, so let me take advantage while I can~~.

In the second round I met a player from Hong Kong whose deck was Sultai (black-green-blue). We fought it out to 1-1, and just as it was about to be my turn to play first, my opponent had a flight to catch (!) and chose to leave early. We exchanged contact details and verbally agreed to meet again for another match when the chance arises. After the match, I enjoyed quite a few of their sketches on Instagram. They are a modest, courteous player who knows how to enrich their life, and I'm really looking forward to the day we rematch.  This match made me feel the joy of Sealed all over again: exchanging ideas in a friendly way and getting to know the new cards together, mixed with high-level play ~~mind games~~. It was truly a wonderful time for broadening friendships and relaxing at once.

In the third round I ran head-on into an old opponent from MITQ, a player surnamed 徐, who that day played Bant (green-white-blue). By this point **every player I'd faced had chosen green**, which is enough to show how important and powerful that color is in this set.
The key moment of this match was probably this: my opponent flashed in [Enduring Curiosity](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/51/enduring-curiosity) and finished blocking, leaving me with one lone low-cost creature standing on the board, two lands, and [Break Down the Door](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/170/break-down-the-door) in hand, but with nothing I could do. In a flash of inspiration I asked to take a look at the card, and quietly exclaimed "oh~ toughness 3, then go for it." My opponent, probably worried about a trick, didn't dare push hard, and when my turn came I simply used [Break Down the Door](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/170/break-down-the-door) to clean up that cute but scary cat completely.
In the end I took the match 2-0 and made a plan with 徐 to play again in the future. Our record against each other currently stands at one win and one loss, and in my heart he is a respectable opponent worth overcoming.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/DSK-Sealed-Review/011f2906ffbd.webp)
> The cute kitty got its door broken down and exiled this time.

Without overthinking, my 3-0 moment finally arrived! I'm really happy to have also had the experience of winning on the first table outside the world of EDH.
And my MVP card of this event was actually two copies of [Fear of Surveillance](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/11/fear-of-surveillance). Opponents easily misjudge its power: because attacking triggers it and does the surveil 1, they get the illusion of "why trade a 2/2 with an ability for a vanilla 2/2?" Letting [Fear of Surveillance](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/11/fear-of-surveillance) surveil over and over meant I drew into bombs and removal, and I repeatedly cast [Valgavoth's Onslaught](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/204/valgavoths-onslaught) with X=2 or X=3 to set up my win. That said, a lot of people opened [Valgavoth's Onslaught](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/204/valgavoths-onslaught) in that event, so a plain X=2 doesn't necessarily win outright, and you still have to push through the manifested creatures with a few tricks ~~it somehow felt like playing Banqi~~.

## Excellent Commons and Uncommons in Every Color

Following on from the above, in a set so often decided off the top of the library, the commons and uncommons that guide you toward exploring the depths are crucial. Below are the cards I personally rate highly, along with a tribute to their shining moments in Sealed.

1. [Fear of Surveillance](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/11/fear-of-surveillance), my MVP favorite. Surveil 1 plus vigilance may look unable to dominate the game, but if it comes down on curve and your opponent isn't paying attention, it becomes a hidden bomb that keeps pressing your advantage. Your opponent might not even understand which card they lost to. Being unremarkable is exactly what makes it strong.
2. [Twist Reality](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/77/twist-reality), I didn't touch blue in any of the five Sealed events I've played, but since this set is often decided off the top of the library and the pace tends to be slow, holding a Counterspell effect to answer bombs is a damaging choice. Plan B also lets you manifest dread at instant speed, which in a way makes it a trick plus surveil. I'd want to put one copy in my own blue-touching decks.
3. [Vile Mutilator](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/122/vile-mutilator), fierce, painful, and most importantly worth digging for. There are plenty of ways to put it onto the battlefield without casting it, avoiding the side effect. Once it enters, besides being dominant on its own, it forces your opponent to sacrifice an enchantment and a creature. In the worse scenario both sides keep removing each other's threats and get stuck in a stalemate. If you draw it, bite the bullet and try to hard-cast it; it's still worth the price, and slowly grinding forward is also an ugly way to win the game.
4. [Ragged Playmate](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/150/ragged-playmate), it has a role when racing for life totals at critical moments, a role when breaking through at a single point, and even a role when you're forcing survival to trigger. When your opponent can't stand it anymore and decides to remove it, it's extremely helpful for the delirium theme, and it has a role while sitting in the graveyard too. A good player should easily find how it connects within a deck.
5. [Break Down the Door](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/170/break-down-the-door), **exile** is a crucial topic in this set, and this instant can also be seen as a card that exiles creatures (there are often powerful enchantment creatures on the board). When you're ahead and want to close out the game quickly, or when you're behind and need a miracle, it can also be used to manifest dread at instant speed. Apart from its somewhat high cost, it's a godly card.

## Conclusion: The Road from 0-3 to 3-0

The original intention behind starting this Limited Review column is that I only got into Limited a year ago, with The Lost Caverns of Ixalan (LCI), and through [the articles Guildmages’ Forum has produced](/en/categories/Limited/), I went from a **0-3** record all the way to today's **3-0**. I'm on the older side, and my learning and competitive form are past their peak, but since I could improve through effort, I believe other players can feel this joy too. That is my biggest reason for writing this article.

Magic: The Gathering is truly a fun game. Perhaps we can bring out the spirit of [Ubuntu](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E4%B9%8C%E7%8F%AD%E5%9B%BE), spending a bit more energy cherishing and encouraging one another, so that the culture and strength of the Chinese-speaking community can rise against the wind and we can find a style of our own. This is my slightly cringey grand vision.

As for the story of how I advanced to the second stage as the second seed at the Sealed + draft MITQ and then immediately crashed out... I'll save that for later. That should be the first chapter of the next story, so please look forward to it.

In any case, I'd like to encourage any friends who are still watching Limited from the sidelines to take the step and try it. Losing is perfectly normal. Most players at the event have already built up a lot of experience. The strength of the cards is certainly worth paying attention to, but in the end it's a top-level contest between players, and as long as you keep accumulating experience and enjoying it, one day you'll feel an unbelievable thrill at your own progress. I personally enjoy this series' Sealed events a lot ~~I also love the art style~~!
