## Before We Start

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Izzet-Otters/a0c3595a12ec.webp)

*Bloomburrow* has ten charming tribes. If you ask which is the most challenging to build, aside from the Frogs mentioned in the [previous article](https://guildmagesforum.tw/Standard-Simic-Frog/), it has to be the Otters. At first glance it is a traditional prowess route that burns and beats down, yet it also contains the possibility of wild turns with convoke. Which route is more stable?

## Card Analysis

Generally speaking, the best way to see how a tribal deck plays is its legend, but the legendary Otter [Alania, Divergent Storm](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/204/alania-divergent-storm) is clearly not designed for Standard, so the core card that fits both approaches above is [Valley Floodcaller](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/79/valley-floodcaller):

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Izzet-Otters/3fe1d3482214.webp)

> Valley Floodcaller
> Creature | Otter / Wizard
> Flash
> You may cast noncreature spells as though they had flash.
> Whenever you cast a noncreature spell, Birds, Frogs, Otters, and Rats you control get +1/+1 until end of turn. Untap them.

As a walking, flashing [Jeskai Ascendancy](https://scryfall.com/card/ktk/180/jeskai-ascendancy), [Valley Floodcaller](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/79/valley-floodcaller) gives the prowess route extra bonuses and makes all noncreature spells flash, and the team-wide untap lets you pay the cost of noncreature convoke spells for free. It looks like you could go either way? In reality that is not the case.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Izzet-Otters/b326f69bff26.webp)

Compared with red aggro, which also takes the prowess route, the offensive Otters led by [Stormcatch Mentor](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/234/stormcatch-mentor) seem a bit mild, and cannot match red aggro in either speed or staying power. Going the convoke route, the board-building ability is somewhat lacking, and convoking middling spells like [Stoke the Flames](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/166/stoke-the-flames) and [Meeting of Minds](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/66/meeting-of-minds) feels clunky. To pull off a wild board turn you still have to rely on [Valley Floodcaller](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/79/valley-floodcaller) and [Stormsplitter](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/154/stormsplitter), and depending on specific cards to function means that when you cannot meet the prerequisites, the game experience turns to mush.

So what is my answer?

[Decklist](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6594091#paper)

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Izzet-Otters/d412e0bd1328.webp)

As you can see, this is an Otter convoke deck that both controls and beats down. A powerful web of fire woven from [Torch the Tower](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/153/torch-the-tower), [Lightning Strike](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/137/lightning-strike), [Nahiri's Warcrafting](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/155/nahiris-warcrafting), and [Ill-Timed Explosion](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/207/ill-timed-explosion) suppresses the opponent in the early and mid game, and once the opponent is weakened, [Valley Floodcaller](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/79/valley-floodcaller) leads the [Otter](https://scryfall.com/card/tblb/25/otter) tokens to give the opponent a beating.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Izzet-Otters/45f288f7f8b9.webp)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Izzet-Otters/3514d5276925.webp)

The convoke target is the deck's apex, [City on Fire](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/135/city-on-fire). Paired with [Valley Floodcaller](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/79/valley-floodcaller), it can come down as early as turn four, taking the opponent out in one punch with triple damage. Even without the [Floodcaller](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/79/valley-floodcaller), casting it with [Otter](https://scryfall.com/card/tblb/25/otter) tokens and normal land drops is not hard, and it can also be fertilizer for an 8-point sweep with [Ill-Timed Explosion](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/207/ill-timed-explosion). As for sources of [Otters](https://scryfall.com/card/tblb/25/otter), here I use [Ral, Crackling Wit](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/230/ral-crackling-wit), [Stormchaser's Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent), and [Otterball Antics](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/63/otterball-antics) to build the board, paired with the Otter-specific spell [Pearl of Wisdom](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/64/pearl-of-wisdom) to refill the hand.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Izzet-Otters/534b37e52481.webp)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Izzet-Otters/75172b48d390.webp)

For lands, like the Frog deck, I packed three sets of rare dual lands: [Spirebluff Canal](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/270/spirebluff-canal), [Shivan Reef](https://scryfall.com/card/otc/320/shivan-reef), and [Restless Spire](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/260/restless-spire). The difference is that for tribal lands I only used [Lilypad Village](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/255/lilypad-village). That is because the deck has only four creature cards, the [Floodcaller](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/79/valley-floodcaller), and putting in too many tribal lands would get in the way of the deck's operation, so after evaluating I chose the surveil 2 that helps more in the long game.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Izzet-Otters/faa4ab689f66.webp)

## Gameplay Demo

{%youtube FJpyPxNu1l0 %}

The opponent is white-black midrange, seemingly a deck that earns resources by blinking [Beza, the Bounding Spring](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/2/beza-the-bounding-spring). Early on the opponent drew plenty of removal, but everything removed was a token I made with [Otterball Antics](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/63/otterball-antics), so I took no damage at all, and I also delivered an on-time [Nahiri's Warcrafting](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/155/nahiris-warcrafting) to the opponent's [Preacher of the Schism](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/113/preacher-of-the-schism). Later the opponent built an air advantage and a life lead, and also had [Aven Interrupter](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/4/aven-interrupter) to disrupt, but did not account for that monster [City on Fire](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/135/city-on-fire). With the [Otter](https://scryfall.com/card/tblb/25/otter) tokens made by [Ral](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/230/ral-crackling-wit) paired with the [Floodcaller](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/79/valley-floodcaller), I slammed it down and sent the opponent home with one punch.

{%youtube WejpBJ9aKM4 %}

The opponent is the mono-black discard deck common on MTGA. Although under the pressure of [Ruthless Negotiation](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/108/ruthless-negotiation) I discarded [City on Fire](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/135/city-on-fire) first, for a deck that keeps making [Otter](https://scryfall.com/card/tblb/25/otter) tokens, can hit planeswalkers directly, and draws cards, it is hard for mono-black discard to suppress completely. On top of that, the opponent was careless and let [Sheoldred, the Apocalypse](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/107/sheoldred-the-apocalypse) get answered by [Torch the Tower](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/153/torch-the-tower). In the end [Stormchaser's Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/75/stormchasers-talent) reached level three, the second [Ral](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/230/ral-crackling-wit) also saved up to his ultimate, and that same turn a triple [Pearl of Wisdom](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/64/pearl-of-wisdom) and a quadruple [Lightning Strike](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/137/lightning-strike) badly hurt the opponent and won the game.
