Murders at Karlov Manor (MKM) holds its prerelease events on February 2, 2024, and launches officially a week later on February 9, 2024. Want to know more about single-card Limited ratings and some potentially fun synergies? Then check out this series of MKM Limited overviews!

For the mechanics, see this article: [Murders at Karlov Manor Mechanics Explained](https://guildmagesforum.tw/MKM-mechanism/)

## Rating Scale

5.0: Unconditional must-pick
4.0: First-pick value
3.5: Strong enough but not necessarily a first pick
3.0: Above average (you will very much want it in your final deck)
2.5: Average, maybe around the 6th or 7th pick of a draft
2.0 to 1.0: Below average (you would rather not play it than play it)
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable (basically don't play it, it will only help you lose)

## White

![002](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/b/d/bd6e71a1-713e-4eca-bd65-9f0638c16794.jpg?1705953227)

**Jru:** 2.5

For a flier, a 4/3 body for five mana is middling. Whether the enters ability that removes suspicion is good or bad is hard to say, and depends on whether your game plan is more proactive or reactive. Suspect is essentially an ability that strongly encourages both sides to attack. When you are behind on tempo or want to lengthen the game, this enters ability may be a lifeline, but if your game plan is more aggressive, or you even have other suspect cards of your own, it may be like dropping a rock on your own foot. So depending on your game plan, this card's score may swing by ±0.5.

Either way, its death ability is unquestionably beneficial. The suspect when it dies is under your control, and you can decide based on the board whether to suspect the opponent's creature.

![003](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/f/5/f5bcb21a-8559-4791-8cd0-482e7b8dcfd2.jpg?1705496848)

**Jru:** 4.0

The simplest way to figure out how many points [Assemble the Players](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/3/assemble-the-players) is worth is to ask how many cards you can expect it to earn you. The first is tempo-losing card filtering, the second is already a gain, though if you care more about tempo cost you can call it even, and everything from the third on is pure profit.

This set has 139 creature cards in total, of which 65 have power 2 or less, 47% of the total. I believe this number is much higher than many people imagine, caused by the large number of low-power, high-toughness creatures in this set.

But that is not the full range of this card's benefit. Read the condition on Assemble the Players closely: it checks the **characteristics of the spell**, not the characteristics of the card. When a spell is cast face down with this set's Disguise ability, the face-down characteristics replace the original ones, and those face-down characteristics are a 2/2 creature, which satisfies Assemble the Players' condition. That means you can use Assemble the Players' ability to cast a Disguise spell face down from the top of your library, even if the front of that card is a creature with power greater than 2, or not even a creature at all (for example [Branch of Vitu-Ghazi](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/258/branch-of-vitu-ghazi)). For a similar effect, see how [Void Winnower](https://scryfall.com/card/bfz/17/void-winnower) stops the opponent from casting Morph spells.

![004](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/5/9/5901dff4-e09b-4747-9297-797a1a057cd5.jpg?1705423253)

**Jru:** 5.0

The spiritual successor to [Angel of Serenity](https://scryfall.com/card/c21/83/angel-of-serenity). Even just cast normally it is a powerful flying attacker, and while the 4/2 body is easier to kill, ward 2 makes up for that to a degree. What's more, if you got it by turning it face up from Disguise, you may come out way ahead on cards, and getting back even two cards counts as pure profit.

But treating it merely as a card-drawing machine with a leaves-the-battlefield trigger would sell it short. The highest ceiling of Aurelia's Vindicator is to clear the opponent's entire board and then go all in to hit them in the face. Even if the opponent manages to remove this creature immediately, those cards only return to their hand rather than the battlefield, and the opponent still has to spend mana to slam the creatures back down, which in most cases is too slow to rebuild the defense.

There is one more rules detail to add about Aurelia's Vindicator:
Its turn-face-up ability and its leaves-the-battlefield ability are two separate triggered abilities. You can respond to your own turn-face-up trigger by getting it off the battlefield some other way (for example sacrificing it to [Agency Coroner](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/75/agency-coroner)). Then the leaves-the-battlefield ability goes on the stack and resolves first, but at that point no cards have been exiled by Aurelia's Vindicator, so no cards are returned to any player's hand. Afterward the turn-face-up ability resolves and exiles X targets, and because the Aurelia's Vindicator that could bring those cards back is already gone, those exiled cards are stuck in exile forever.

**Jru:** 2.0

Passable as a combat trick that may earn you a card, but a combat trick always carries the risk of being responded to for a two-for-one. Using it on a Disguise creature with ward 2 reduces some of that risk.

![006](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/f/5/f5148def-cf1a-460e-8dfd-856103940892.jpg?1706038757)

**Jru:** 2.5

White two-mana cards that dig for three-drops are showing up more and more lately, and the extra flying counter is a decent boost. If your build has important two- or three-mana creatures, consider including this one.

![007](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/7/0/70a52038-9d1c-4be1-8dbe-6f0ee916ba94.jpg?1706241461)

**Jru:** Non-Case theme: 1.5 / Case theme: 3.5

Look at six and take one is very deep, and it is not limited to Cases, since any enchantment can be taken, but you still need enough Cases for this card to be sufficiently well incentivized. When you don't have enough enchantments, she is just a three-mana 1/4 vanilla creature. Even if you don't have many Cases, you should have at least a certain number of enchantment cards, so she has a reasonable chance of earning at least one card when she enters.

![008](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/0/8/0862bf07-8a76-4e80-bba2-20d22f8eee30.jpg?1706050691)

**Jru:** 3.5

The enters ability is removal with a range limit, but in Limited that is mostly good enough. Also note the wording: "each creature you control deals 1 damage to it", so if any of your creatures have effects tied to dealing damage, such as deathtouch or lifelink, they all apply.
The solve condition is not hard to meet in Limited, especially since this set also has suspect, which raises your creatures' survival rate after attacking, and the ability after solving is plain and simply useful.

![009](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/3/2/32927bf2-63c1-4402-99dc-3a0f2f8e0f9c.jpg?1705431642)

**Jru:** 2.5

Of the 139 creature cards in this set, 46 are Detectives, almost exactly a third, a number even higher than most tribal environments, so tribal-payoff cards for Detectives in this set tend to get bigger score boosts than in a normal environment.
Putting +1/+1 counters and making Clues are both intuitively beneficial, and in a Detective environment you can look forward to the ceiling. Of course this card's performance is somewhat unstable, with issues of sequencing and the number of Detectives. In a sealed pool, if you want to use this card, remember to check the number of Detective cards and token-making abilities in your pool. In a draft it can be a key card for a Detective plan.

![010](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/a/c/ac63941b-3f78-4bd3-8b05-ca12aaaa006c.jpg?1706050757)

**Jru:** 1.5

Meeting the solve condition is a bit hard in Limited. You basically still need lifelink creatures with some punch to hit it, and even if you manage to solve it, whether you profit comfortably depends on your available mana and what cards are in your graveyard.

**Jru:** 3.5

As mentioned above, this set has a high proportion of creatures with power 2 or less, so it is easier to find enough trigger sources. Because Disguise creatures are 2/2, on offense you can also pair it with face-down Disguise creatures, so the opponent can only block with low-power creatures, and after blockers are declared you turn yours face up, giving you more chances to make favorable trades.

Used well it can make the opponent miscalculate and collapse, though most of the time it will probably be used as a protection spell that can also earn a card. The body is also an incredibly sturdy wall, and it can block basically any ground creature without deathtouch.

**Jru:** 2.5

The classic white tapper of Limited, with the activation cost at the benchmark three mana. It can put a stun counter on both itself and the target creature, giving this classic ability new room to play. On defense, this kind of tapper naturally targets the same creature every turn; by choosing to put one stun counter on each, the result is still that the same creature stays tapped for two turns, but on the second turn you have an extra 3 mana to use. Or, if you activate on your own turn to force a path through, you can ensure that the opponent's most troublesome blocker can't act during these two attacks, or at least won't hit back at you next turn. That one turn of reset difference plus the 3 mana saved might make a considerable difference in the game.

By the standard of a three-mana creature, a 2/4 flying vigilance is already a god of war combining offense and defense, and this combination of body and abilities alone gives it a good base score.

Equipment cast and equip costs are both reasonable, and the +1/+1 vigilance buff is quite practical in Limited. The instant-speed tap ability at an activation cost of three mana is also a benchmark value for commons and uncommons. The bonus Clue type can help you adjust your hand or pair with other Clue/artifact payoff cards in this set. Although each element on its own isn't especially outstanding, a card that gives this many effects is above average.

## Afterword

When I first started studying MKM's mechanics, I expected this to be a relatively slow set: Cases take time to unlock their full functionality, Collect Evidence takes time to gather graveyard fuel, and Disguise is plainly sacrificing tempo for flexibility. The only mechanic that speeds up tempo is suspect, but because of its drawback I didn't pay it much attention at first, and in the end the early previews even showed a whole bunch of creatures that looked like they were pushing a defenders or low-power theme.

But once the full card list was revealed, I began to doubt my first impression. First, the suspect mechanic: on closer thought it can actually have a very strong effect on the tempo of attack and defense. Also, from the creatures in this white article you can tell that low-cost creatures actually include plenty of attackers suited to racing for tempo, with a fair number of related buff cards.

So looking at this set's Limited again now, it may be a set where fast and slow decks diverge especially sharply: the fast ones feature all kinds of flying, menace, and can't-block effects, trading blows right to the flesh; the slow ones are like walking through a minefield, probing at what creature is under each of the opponent's Disguises, and how to play to avoid getting trapped yourself.
