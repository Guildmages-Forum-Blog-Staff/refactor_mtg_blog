## If You Still Don't Know What Draft Is

Since this article is promotion-oriented, please allow us to briefly introduce "draft" once more.

Getting straight to the point: Magic drafts are **insanely fun**. You can crack packs, pick from them, snag a good card nobody else took (usually because it's not in the color pair they're playing this time, so they reluctantly pass it to you), build a deck, battle, take home souvenirs provided by the store, play casually and trash-talk with a few friends; or play seriously, so seriously that you can even keep going at the Pro Tour.

## Players' Thoughts

### GY_Player's Thoughts:

#### What do you think was the most important pick today, and why? Please name the card.

[Fire Nation Cadets](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/134/fire-nation-cadets) at P1P9. For my first eight picks I was basically forcing red, because red in this set is really strong, and I wanted to cut red off from everyone else.

After my first pack went around the table and came back, I noticed [Fire Nation Cadets](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/134/fire-nation-cadets) had wheeled, and I took it as a signal that red was open. But I didn't notice that the third red single in that pack, [Boar-q-pine](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/124/boar-q-pine), had been taken, so it wasn't that nobody was taking red; they just didn't take [Fire Nation Cadets](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/134/fire-nation-cadets).

This misreading of the signal left me with very little red material in the next two packs, but luckily [Dragonfly Swarm](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/215/dragonfly-swarm) at P2P2 and [Lost Days](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/62/lost-days) at P3P1 gave me a lifeline to switch colors. I consider those two picks very important as well.

#### What were the key turns today like?

The turn where [cephille](/en/authors/cephille/)'s [Hama, the Bloodbender](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/224/hama-the-bloodbender) stole my [Firebending Lesson](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/138/firebending-lesson) left too deep an impression; I never expected to run into a State Base Action (SBA) interaction in Limited. I highly recommend interested players read the article Jru wrote, [What Are State-Based Actions (SBA)](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/What-is-SBA/)!

#### How would you recommend drafting?

I really love drafting this set! For the past few days, every night before bed I draft a deck on MTGA, finish playing it the next day when I find time, then draft a new one in the evening, repeating the cycle and liking it more and more. All ten two-color archetypes in this set have their own themes and charm, and if you look carefully, most commons and uncommons can serve multiple draft themes, which greatly increases flexibility in deck building during the draft, so you don't waste earlier picks when switching colors.

And in-person drafts are even more fun~ You can chat, discuss, and play with friends, and discover each other's blind spots or misunderstandings on the spot, correct them early, and improve together!
Go find friends and play a draft today!

#### Do you like drafting? Any memorable draft memories?

I love it! MTG booster packs cost more than twice what a single pack costs in most other games, and what makes them expensive is that MTG packs can be used for drafts or Limited play. My friends and I have always thought that with the current product design that includes Collector Boosters, people who really want to crack packs would get far more joy from cracking Collector Boosters than regular ones. Think about it: if you open a booster just for one particular R/MR card or special card, the odds are low, and basically at least 12 to 13 cards in that pack have nothing to do with you, which is quite wasteful. If those boosters could be used with friends for a draft, spending a pleasant afternoon/evening together, how great would that be.

I hope more and more players get to experience the fun of drafting, making the whole community more willing to play and organize drafts. You may occasionally feel tired of playing Constructed, but you'll definitely never get tired of drafting!

### [Jru](/en/authors/JruMTG/)'s Thoughts:

#### What do you think was the most important pick today, and why? Please name the card.

Removal cards. In this draft, the green signals were good, and all kinds of good green curve creatures were still visible in the middle of the packs, but removal was very tight, so from the second pack on I greatly increased the priority of removal, and even drafting that way I only just reached 8 removal spells. On the other hand, I passed so many creatures along that in the end, apart from the 3-mana curve being a bit thin, I had so many good creatures at the other costs that I had a headache deciding what to cut. Afterward, when discussing builds with other players, I found another person who also went red-green-based but had not a single removal spell; I estimate the packs this time really did have a thin supply of removal, and if you hadn't raised removal picks, your deck's ability to interact would probably have been terrible.

#### What were the key turns today like?

In the first match I was behind all the way and got pressed to death, and in the second I was ahead all the way and pushed to a win, so there really weren't any special key situations, but both winners had something in common: after establishing a strong enough clock in the early-to-mid game, they threw out removal in succession so the opponent couldn't block effectively, and the player who was behind also had no removal, so they were punched through instantly.

#### Do you like drafting? Any memorable draft memories?

From the description above it's not hard to see that I value removal in Limited. Although removal is indeed important to begin with, the weight I place on removal is probably on the particularly high side. This style of mine in Limited was probably established starting from [the Hour of Devastation set](https://scryfall.com/sets/hou), and it was also the period of my highest Limited win rate; across the dozen or so Friday drafts in that whole set, I went undefeated in best-of-3 throughout. Of course, that was just a local store's weekly event level, so it's not that valuable, but the biggest change at the time was that I began to consciously prioritize removal picks, while demanding less of creatures, as long as they were basically usable for offense and defense.

#### How would you recommend drafting?
Among this year's sets, this Avatar Limited format has relatively high demands on theme synergy.

The Lesson theme focuses heavily on a few key cards, and has a very high ceiling once it comes together, but if you don't pick up a complete set, you'll wonder whether you've been playing a pile of [Grizzly Bears](https://scryfall.com/card/10e/268/grizzly-bears) and [Skywinder Drake](https://scryfall.com/card/m12/75/skywinder-drake); Allies are the creature type this set is least short of, but if you choose to play a true Ally theme, then besides the key tribal payoff cards, the choice of the overall creature curve also has quite a few requirements; not to mention the sacrifice theme, an old regular in Limited that demands a lot from engines and fodder.

So when drafting this set, I recommend planning which direction you want your game plan to go within the first 5 picks of the first pack; if you want to enter a specific theme, you should start aggressively grabbing the theme cards. That said, there are still builds that don't need much synergy, like my red-green this time, where I just put in creatures and removal in proportion and was done. After all, Earthbend, even without any synergy, produces a creature with offense and defense out of thin air, which guarantees combat strength; for beginners who lack the confidence to build a deck with complete synergy in a draft, this is a relatively easy pick pattern to get started with.
