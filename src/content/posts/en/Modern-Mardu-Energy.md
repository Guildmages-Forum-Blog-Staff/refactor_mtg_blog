### Q: It's already a mainstream deck, but how does it actually play?

A: It overpowers the opponent with raw card power. (Seriously.)
The new MH3 cards are just that strong.

For example, using the energy package alone, you build an early board advantage with [Amped Raptor](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/114/amped-raptor) or [Ocelot Pride](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/38/ocelot-pride), punish the opponent's card draw with [Orcish Bowmasters](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/103/orcish-bowmasters), pick apart their hand with [Thoughtseize](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/334/thoughtseize), and while they can't do anything, clear the way with various removal spells and crush them. A plain tempo beatdown route.

Pair [Guide of Souls](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/29/guide-of-souls) with [Ocelot Pride](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/38/ocelot-pride) to mass-produce cats. With 3 Ocelot Prides, gaining life once each turn makes 14 cats appear at end of turn, gaining 14 life and 14 energy. You beat the opponent on the lifegain route.

Use [Chthonian Nightmare](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/83/chthonian-nightmare) to keep pulling valuable cards like [Ajani, Nacatl Pariah](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/237/ajani-nacatl-pariah-ajani-nacatl-avenger) or Ocelot Pride back onto the battlefield. A quasi-combo route.

Flip [Ajani, Nacatl Pariah](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/237/ajani-nacatl-pariah-ajani-nacatl-avenger) into a planeswalker, and add the sideboard [Ob Nixilis, the Adversary](https://scryfall.com/card/snc/206/ob-nixilis-the-adversary) for a long-distance planeswalker war route.

And of course, we can't leave out blowing up your card draw with [The One Ring](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/246/the-one-ring) and crushing the opponent with card advantage. The Ring route.

The Mardu energy shell looks like an ordinary midrange deck, but it actually contains many routes to victory, and you adjust your plan flexibly depending on your hand and the opponent's deck.

### Q: But the results sites show that Boros (red-white) energy has a higher win rate than Mardu. Why still play Mardu?

The short answer first: Boros additionally gets [Blood Moon](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/118/blood-moon) as an extra route that punishes multicolor decks. If you only want to play The One Ring, Boros makes more sense (fewer shocklands, so you don't have to worry about losing too much life), and you don't have to worry about mana issues either. It loses the Chthonian Nightmare combo route, can't take cards from the opponent's hand, and can't punish card draw with [Orcish Bowmasters](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/103/orcish-bowmasters), but the floor it provides is very high.

Mardu is somewhat prone to color problems. On 22 lands you also have to fit various utility lands. If you don't have enough black sources you can't use [Chthonian Nightmare](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/83/chthonian-nightmare) to its full effect, and because it's three colors, you inevitably sacrifice some consistency in the red-white cards. Not to mention that against a Boros opponent you get punished by Blood Moon (you can only run three basic lands at most, and because of the escape on [Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/197/phlage-titan-of-fires-fury), every land has to produce at least one of red or white, so no basic Swamp).

So why play Mardu anyway? Again, it's the multi-angle attack: the Bowmasters punishment, the hand information from Thoughtseize, the possibilities Chthonian Nightmare provides, and the Ob Nixilis planeswalker war are all very worthwhile. I would rather raise the ceiling and lower the floor, so I'm going with Mardu.

### Q: Can you explain what you'd swap in if you don't play The One Ring?

A: [Fable of the Mirror-Breaker](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/141/fable-of-the-mirror-breaker-reflection-of-kiki-jiki), I'd say. In my original reference list, I swapped out one [Goblin Bombardment](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/279/goblin-bombardment), one [Chthonian Nightmare](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/83/chthonian-nightmare), and two [Ob Nixilis, the Adversary](https://scryfall.com/card/snc/206/ob-nixilis-the-adversary). You can also increase the number of [Static Prison](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/44/static-prison), or go up to a full four [Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/197/phlage-titan-of-fires-fury). Those are flex slots anyway, so basically just use your own creativity.

If you remember [Amped Raptor](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/114/amped-raptor), you'll remember that the energy deck was originally built around it, so every card costs 3 or less, so that when you cast Raptor for 2 mana you can reliably hit any card in the deck. But our [The One Ring](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/246/the-one-ring) is simply too good. It's like putting Tabasco on spaghetti or extra garlic on ramen: sure, you can add it, but it doesn't match the spaghetti or ramen at all... Raptor can now whiff on turn 2, but the Ring is just too tempting...

If you're obsessive about a deck's thematic consistency and aesthetics, don't play [The One Ring](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/246/the-one-ring). Every time I use it, I feel like a foreign object has slipped in, but it really is strong.

[Fable of the Mirror-Breaker](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/141/fable-of-the-mirror-breaker-reflection-of-kiki-jiki) is the closest in function, but I always laugh that the Ring can draw 10-plus cards while Fable can only draw 2 and discard 2, so how could it compare? The Ring's life loss? We have Guide of Souls, Phlage, and Ocelot Pride to gain it right back!

## The MH3 Crew

### [Guide of Souls](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/29/guide-of-souls)

<!--- ![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/7/6/76c3cad2-1e25-4abe-878d-9194de6fcc27.jpg?1717011433) --->
{% mtgpick "mh3" 29 %}

Nickname: GoS
This is what [Soul Warden](https://scryfall.com/card/mm3/24/soul-warden) looks like after training in the mountains for 10 years, and it's the unsung hero of the energy deck. Whether it's storing energy for the [two](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/122/galvanic-discharge) [removal spells](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/44/static-prison), preparing energy for [Amped Raptor](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/114/amped-raptor), making a pile of creatures so your life total is healthy enough to pay life, or even, when you have too much energy and nobody to spend it, using its own ability to add power and fly over to deny ground combat, it does a three-heads-six-arms level of work in this deck.

In particular, there's the combo with [Ocelot Pride](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/38/ocelot-pride) mentioned earlier. With GoS on the battlefield, whenever you gain life, you make cats according to the number of Ocelot Prides, and the new cats trigger GoS again, adding even more life and more energy. GoS can also make a flier every turn to sneak across, and the best target is of course Ocelot Pride itself. Attack with lifelink to trigger the end-of-turn effect, and once it's the city's blessing, the cats you make keep growing and the lifegain snowball keeps getting bigger (the Ocelot Pride quick-reference table is in the next section). This creates an irreversible gap in life total and board, a seamless combination.

Because of this, if energy gets nerfed next month, the thing I fear most is that they hit GoS. Pull one thread and the whole thing moves: it indirectly reduces the output of both removal spells, and it loses one route to victory (lifegain). That cut would hurt enormously, so I hope they don't go this way... ~~Wizards, would you take [Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/197/phlage-titan-of-fires-fury) as a sacrifice instead? ### [Ocelot Pride](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/38/ocelot-pride)

<!---![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/8/9/89cf6f57-230f-497e-a14e-ad1e8737fd42.jpg?1717011506)--->
{% mtgpick "mh3" 38 %}

The culprit that knocked [Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/138/ragavan-nimble-pilferer) off his pedestal (although [Tamiyo, Inquisitive Student](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/242/tamiyo-inquisitive-student-tamiyo-seasoned-scholar) already seems to have punched Ragavan once).
Being a 1-mana first striker with lifelink isn't enough; it also has an ability text so long you don't want to read it. Simply put, if you gain life, you make 1 cat at end of turn, and once you have the city's blessing, at the end of your turn, all tokens that entered this turn are copied again. The scariest part of this ability is that other tokens get copied too. At the light end, there's the Orc Army token from [Orcish Bowmasters](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/103/orcish-bowmasters) (the copy enters without any +1/+1 counters and immediately dies), and the 2/1 cat from [Ajani, Nacatl Pariah](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/237/ajani-nacatl-pariah-ajani-nacatl-avenger). At the heavy end, even a copy of [Ob Nixilis, the Adversary](https://scryfall.com/card/snc/206/ob-nixilis-the-adversary) (its loyalty is determined by the value when Casualty was paid) can be made again, and if you run [Fable of the Mirror-Breaker](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/141/fable-of-the-mirror-breaker-reflection-of-kiki-jiki), the Goblin, Treasure, and even the flipped copy token can be made one more time. This is where the magic starts. I already covered the combo with GoS above, so Ocelot Pride is the second card I fear getting banned; without it, there's no more magic. That said, what reassures me a bit is that it's a mythic rare, ~~and Wizards still wants to sell cards, so they'll probably let it slide.~~

Ocelot Pride quick-reference table
Number of 1/1 cats made once you have the city's blessing:
- 1 Ocelot Pride: 1 + 1 = 2
- 2: 2 + 1 + 3 = 6
- 3: 6 + 1 + 7 = 14
- 4: 14 + 1 + 15 = 30
- 5 or more only happens in Naya with [Springheart Nantuko](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/171/springheart-nantuko), or with [Fable of the Mirror-Breaker](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/141/fable-of-the-mirror-breaker-reflection-of-kiki-jiki), so I'll skip it here; the formula is right there and it's simple anyway.

So you can see how big a life-total gap you can roll up each turn with 1 GoS and 3 or 4 Ocelot Prides. No wonder every deck with red has been bringing [Pyroclasm](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/149/pyroclasm) in the sideboard lately.

Tokens other than 1/1 cats:
- 1 Ocelot Pride: 1 becomes 2
- 2: 2 becomes 4
- 3: 4 becomes 8
- 4: 8 becomes 16
It's basically just simple x2 math.

### [Ajani, Nacatl Pariah](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/237/ajani-nacatl-pariah-ajani-nacatl-avenger)

<!---![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/0/d/0d16e8e0-31b2-4389-afd6-783c501f6fa0.jpg?1718635371)
![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/back/0/d/0d16e8e0-31b2-4389-afd6-783c501f6fa0.jpg?1718635371)--->
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Mardu-Energy/be55eec94e6c.webp)

Ajani comes in with a 2/1 cat and goes into a rampage whenever another Cat dies. The token it brings along was deliberately made nonlegendary to avoid breaking the legend rule (the cat is probably modeled on [Jazal Goldmane](https://scryfall.com/card/c14/9/jazal-goldmane), Ajani's older brother), but it still couldn't withstand the creative minds of players.
Watch his brother get shot out by [Goblin Bombardment](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/279/goblin-bombardment) and go berserk. Watch his brother become the Casualty cost for [Ob Nixilis, the Adversary](https://scryfall.com/card/snc/206/ob-nixilis-the-adversary) and go berserk. Use Bowmasters to stage the Jazal assassination yourself. Or even make Ajani see a second version of himself, die to the legend rule, and then go berserk. ~~How is it just a single mentally disturbed cat~~
Having said that, Ajani is the strongest card in this deck on its own merits. On the front side he's a 3/3 in total, and while Ajani is on the battlefield he deters the opponent from using removal on [Ocelot Pride](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/38/ocelot-pride). When you ram into the opponent with cats, they have to think about whether to block, to stop Ajani from flipping.
What stands out after flipping is of course the [+0] ability: besides painlessly making 1 cat, as long as you control another red permanent, it deals damage equal to the number of creatures you control to any target, handling the board and going for the head at the same time.
> Because the [+0] ability is so strong, I've basically never used the other two abilities. Well, pressing [+2] to force the opponent to hurry up and kill Ajani, or else risk the [-4], is also a tactic.

But this is where Mardu's weakness comes in. The red permanents that can stick around in the main deck are only [Amped Raptor](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/114/amped-raptor), [Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/197/phlage-titan-of-fires-fury), [Goblin Bombardment](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/279/goblin-bombardment), and the companion [Jegantha, the Wellspring](https://scryfall.com/card/iko/222/jegantha-the-wellspring). By the time Phlage can stay on the battlefield, you can already escape it and decide the game, and if you're casting Jegantha straight up, your hand must be in terrible shape, so normally you only look at the other two. Raptor is a creature and easily answered in response, and Goblin Bombardment is too weak on its own. This is one of Mardu's weaknesses: Ajani is easier to flip, but there are fewer situations where you get to use the full [+0] ability. Making one 2/1 cat each turn is already strong, but firepower of [Lightning Bolt](https://scryfall.com/card/clu/141/lightning-bolt) or better every turn would obviously be more useful, wouldn't it?
After sideboarding, the possible red permanents grow to include [Obsidian Charmaw](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/137/obsidian-charmaw) and Ob Nixilis, and the latter in particular can sacrifice a cat on demand while providing a red permanent, making it Ajani's best partner. (Like Goblin Bombardment, it's a card for Ajani, and Ob Nixilis is exclusive to Mardu.)

### [Amped Raptor](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/114/amped-raptor)

<!---![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/1/a/1ac0e78b-0fdd-44f9-8b7b-c4f28a32782e.jpg?1717012124)--->
{% mtgpick "mh3" 114 %}

It should be one of the reasons to play the energy deck, but Raptor is fading more and more into the background. Simply put, it flips cards from the top of your library until you hit a nonland card, and lets you cast it using energy in place of its mana cost, a bit like Cascade. Getting two for one is powerful, but if you flip the wrong card on the fastest turn, or even whiff completely, it gets awkward. To prevent that, the deck keeps its mana values at 3 or less, so that after one [Aether Hub](https://scryfall.com/card/kld/242/aether-hub) or GoS, a 2-mana Raptor can hit any card. Except... [The One Ring](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/246/the-one-ring)... never mind...

Two-for-one is certainly strong, and even if you flip [Galvanic Discharge](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/122/galvanic-discharge), you can target nothing in particular and still gain the energy (note that [Static Prison](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/44/static-prison) can't necessarily do the same thing; more on that later).
Remember to pay energy when you hit one, especially with Galvanic Discharge, which is very easy to forget.
Note that the sideboard [Obsidian Charmaw](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/137/obsidian-charmaw) might eat too much energy, and you can choose not to cast it. Also, all lands flipped over during the process get exiled, which quietly reduces the lands you draw later, so watch out for that.

### [Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/197/phlage-titan-of-fires-fury)

<!---![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/e/4/e419cd0b-2449-4cc5-9ead-b9e45e271700.jpg?1717012789)--->
{% mtgpick "mh3" 197 %}

A new member of the [Titan](https://scryfall.com/card/thb/229/uro-titan-of-natures-wrath) [series](https://scryfall.com/card/thb/221/kroxa-titan-of-deaths-hunger), whose price has finally dropped back to a normal level recently. It's a [Lightning Helix](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/218/lightning-helix) that walks. Before escaping, a sorcery-speed Lightning Helix costing 3 is admittedly weaker, but once escaped it's a huge 6/6, and each attack shoots another Lightning Helix, which is incredibly powerful. The problem is that there isn't much room for it to shine in current Modern; against combo decks it's just a liability, and it gets awkward if the opponent has too many creatures with 4 toughness. You also have to pick the timing for escaping, so you don't eat a removal spell and go straight back to the graveyard. It's been one of the first cards sided out in many matchups recently, but even so, it's still incredibly strong in energy mirrors or against midrange decks that aren't too fast, and 3 copies are still necessary.

Also, with [Arena of Glory](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/215/arena-of-glory), after escaping it can run out and attack immediately, raising the ceiling of surprise attacks. Suddenly hitting the opponent's face for 12 still hurts.

### [Galvanic Discharge](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/122/galvanic-discharge)

<!---![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/3/2/32aa6e33-221f-414c-9b51-850d97a7e051.jpg?1717012186)--->
{% mtgpick "mh3" 122 %}

An energy removal spell. It provides 3 energy itself, so (as long as the opponent has no [Suncleanser](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/39/suncleanser) on the battlefield) it's guaranteed to be at least a 3-damage removal for 1 mana, and apart from not being able to hit face, it's better than [Lightning Bolt](https://scryfall.com/card/clu/141/lightning-bolt). Note that after targeting, you don't have to announce how much damage right away. You decide how much energy to spend only after both players pass priority and the spell resolves, and the opponent can't respond to it. Using this, you can target your own creature, declare paying 0, and just happily take 3 energy.
After pulling off the [GoS](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/29/guide-of-souls) + [Ocelot Pride](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/38/ocelot-pride) combo, I call pointing at an opposing creature and firing 20-plus damage the [Lightning of Laputa](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A9%E7%A9%BA%E4%B9%8B%E5%9F%8E).

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Mardu-Energy/ae2c56ee5271.webp)

### [Static Prison](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/44/static-prison)

<!---![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/d/d/dd16222e-349c-4a2b-a7c8-8eb35a8ab332.jpg?1717011554)--->
{% mtgpick "mh3" 44 %}


The energy world's [Leyline Binding](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/24/leyline-binding). You don't have to pay the energy right away either: you're only asked whether to pay starting next turn. The sorcery speed is a little sluggish, but it still covers Galvanic Discharge's weaknesses from the side. Too many would be clunky, so the main deck only runs 2.
Its main enemies are [Suncleanser](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/39/suncleanser) and troublesome noncreature permanents (for example, [Amulet of Vigor](https://scryfall.com/card/wwk/121/amulet-of-vigor)).

Rules gotchas:
Note that when the opponent has nothing to exile, you **can't take the energy**.
Note that the time to pay the energy is **the beginning of your precombat main phase**.
Note that after exiling Suncleanser, you do take the 2 energy.

### [Chthonian Nightmare](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/83/chthonian-nightmare)

<!---![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/c/e/ce5dd2c1-b6e0-4914-b5c9-7dd451c29e22.jpg?1717011882)--->
{% mtgpick "mh3" 83 %}

The [Recurring Nightmare](https://scryfall.com/card/exo/72/recurring-nightmare) of 2024, 1 mana cheaper but with an energy restriction. It's Mardu's cheat code. For example, after [Ajani, Nacatl Pariah](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/237/ajani-nacatl-pariah-ajani-nacatl-avenger) himself gets killed and only the 2/1 cat is left sitting there alone, you play Nightmare and sacrifice the cat to bring Ajani back. Incredible! Ajani makes another cat, and it only cost two mana. The 2/1 cat is still there but Ajani is back! (And you made 1 energy on top of that!)
Other uses include turning excess [Ocelot Pride](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/38/ocelot-pride) tokens back into the real Ocelot Pride, urgently pulling [Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/197/phlage-titan-of-fires-fury) back before it can escape to fire a Lightning Helix, sacrificing Ajani's cat to actively drive Ajani insane, and, after sideboarding with enough energy, repeatedly pulling up [Obsidian Charmaw](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/137/obsidian-charmaw) to blow up lands. Surely the opponent won't side in graveyard hate for one trick card? If they do, you came out ahead!

Note that returning [Amped Raptor](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/114/amped-raptor) this way also doesn't trigger the two-for-one effect. (The Raptor's effect is a casting trigger issue.)
Note that you can't directly bring back a creature whose cost you just sacrificed. (You can't target it.)
The more black lands you have, the more times you can use Nightmare in a turn. The most extreme case I tried was returning three Ocelot Prides in one turn, so watch out for the details of color-changing before using it.
Since the ability is sorcery-speed only, if the energy trigger on entering gets responded to by destroying Nightmare, you can't do the trick either.

## The Lord of the Rings Crew

### [Orcish Bowmasters](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/103/orcish-bowmasters)

<!---![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/7/c/7c024bae-5631-4e20-ac69-df392ac9e109.jpg?1686968669)--->

The second-biggest villain of The Lord of the Rings, which punishes the opponent's card draw. To MTG players, this nobody who appears in the movie for less than 5 seconds matters more than all the legendary characters of the Lord of the Rings world.

### [Suncleanser](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/39/suncleanser)

<!---![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/3/6/3644df41-b690-4581-ac7d-c85cec75411f.jpg?1562301661)--->
{% mtgpick "m19" 39 %}

The final weapon against energy decks. How many you run depends on how much you hate energy mirrors. Once its ability targeting the opponent resolves, they can never get energy again until Suncleanser leaves the battlefield, and when the ability resolves their energy is wiped out completely. That means [Galvanic Discharge](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/122/galvanic-discharge) can only hit for 0, [Guide of Souls](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/29/guide-of-souls) turns straight back into a slightly bigger Soul Warden, and [Amped Raptor](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/114/amped-raptor) can only ever flip for 0. The only one that avoids this is [Static Prison](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/44/static-prison). This time I really hated the mirror so I ran 3, but I never faced a single energy deck, so I adjusted the number afterward.

Another thing to note: if the opponent has 1 or more energy, you can still deal with it by killing Suncleanser with Galvanic Discharge before the ETB ability resolves. (It still wipes out all of the opponent's remaining energy, though.)

Aside from that, in the rarer cases you can also target a creature of the opponent's that will carry a pile of counters, such as in [Heliod, Sun-Crowned](https://scryfall.com/card/cmm/29/heliod-sun-crowned) [Walking Ballista](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/306/walking-ballista) and [Hardened Scales](https://scryfall.com/card/lcc/243/hardened-scales) decks, which see this situation more often. But against the latter, there are lots of cases where they respond by sacrificing the targeted creature and moving the counters to another creature or to [The Ozolith](https://scryfall.com/card/iko/237/the-ozolith), so treat Suncleanser as just one sideboard option and don't trust it too much.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Mardu-Energy/8e7cb7cee814.webp)

For Domain, Goblin Charbelcher decks (keeping the opponent from casting [Goblin Charbelcher](https://scryfall.com/card/ddt/57/goblin-charbelcher) and just waiting a turn), [Grinding Station](https://scryfall.com/card/5dn/127/grinding-station), [Primeval Titan](https://scryfall.com/card/ima/183/primeval-titan), [Hardened Scales](https://scryfall.com/card/lcc/243/hardened-scales), [Colossus Hammer](https://scryfall.com/card/afc/202/colossus-hammer), Affinity decks, and so on.

### [Ob Nixilis, the Adversary](https://scryfall.com/card/snc/206/ob-nixilis-the-adversary)

<!---![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/8/0/80226520-6b0a-48ea-a868-a29eb3fbd403.jpg?1664413416)--->
{% mtgpick "snc" 206 %}

For decks like [Murktide Regent](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/52/murktide-regent) (& [Abhorrent Oculus](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/42/abhorrent-oculus)), Jeskai Control, and Izzet Wizards. The imagined enemy is draw-go decks with plenty of counterspells, or slower control decks.
The Casualty effect is naturally suited to dodging counterspells, so use it when you expect the opponent to play a slow game. Note that in combo matchups it's awkward in the same way as [Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/197/phlage-titan-of-fires-fury): sacrificing your own creatures slows your tempo a little. Also, against decks that discard cards (for example [Goryo's Vengeance](https://scryfall.com/card/uma/99/goryos-vengeance)), it may make the opponent discard exactly what they wanted to dig up, completing a friendly combo of helping them with their graveyard. (In effect, a fancy way of raising your hand in surrender.)

When you notice that the opponent's deck leans on the graveyard to some degree, you should avoid siding this in. (So be careful about this against Murktide or Oculus.)

I didn't expect them to quickly draw a second [Static Prison](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/44/static-prison), and my 2 Spheres were answered by 2 [Static Prisons](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/44/static-prison), but under my aggro pressure the opponent still couldn't get going, and had to [Grapeshot](https://scryfall.com/card/dmr/125/grapeshot) for storm 10 to clear the board. In the end I drew 3 cards with the Ring, found enough creatures to enter, and let the [GoS](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/29/guide-of-souls) on the battlefield have enough energy to fly over and kill the opponent.

Game 3 we started right away since time was short. The opponent mulliganed three times. I planned to take them out with one wave of aggro, but Static Prison dragged out the time, and with just one minute to spare, I was one turn short of killing the opponent. Considering that a draw here would send both players into the top four, they let me go up in the end.

[Temple Garden](https://scryfall.com/card/rvr/290/temple-garden)

### Finals: VS Hardened Scales OXO

The opponent was a well-known [Hardened Scales](https://scryfall.com/card/lcc/243/hardened-scales) player, and honestly my familiarity with Scales is really lacking, so I expected a tough fight. I did at least have an idea in my head of how I could win, which was to complete the [GoS](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/29/guide-of-souls) + [Ocelot Pride](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/38/ocelot-pride) combo and win on the life-total gap, which can ignore the opponent's offense to a certain degree (and I did pull it off twice).

I was on the draw. In game 1, the opponent's turn-1 Scales and turn-2 [Walking Ballista](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/306/walking-ballista) took out my [Ocelot Pride](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/38/ocelot-pride), forcing me to trade with [Bowmasters](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/103/orcish-bowmasters). Then the opponent played [Patchwork Automaton](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/254/patchwork-automaton), [Welding Jar](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/307/welding-jar), [Shadowspear](https://scryfall.com/card/thb/236/shadowspear), [The Ozolith](https://scryfall.com/card/iko/237/the-ozolith), and other common Scales cards, growing the Automaton to be super huge, and all I could do was cast the Ring and activate it to look for a solution.
At one point my life total dropped to 4 when I drew [Static Prison](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/44/static-prison). Looking at the opponent's Ozolith and the [Inkmoth Nexus](https://scryfall.com/card/mbs/145/inkmoth-nexus) behind it, a flash of inspiration hit: instead of exiling the Automaton, I exiled the Shadowspear. (I'd realized the Ozolith transfers counters when it leaves the battlefield.)
I had 3 mana left at the time and 1 [Galvanic Discharge](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/122/galvanic-discharge) in hand. If I had Prisoned the Automaton itself, I'd be tapped out, and next turn the Ozolith would move all the counters onto the Inkmoth land and I could get killed by 10 poison right away. I remembered that in Scales lists Shadowspear usually has only one copy, and an Automaton without its evasion can only be fed one creature per turn.
On the key turn I already had [GoS](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/29/guide-of-souls) on the battlefield, one [Ocelot Pride](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/38/ocelot-pride) in hand, and two [Ocelot Prides](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/38/ocelot-pride) in the graveyard. I activated the Ring and drew [Chthonian Nightmare](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/83/chthonian-nightmare). I've been waiting for you for so long! I first cast the Ocelot Pride in my hand, then cast Nightmare twice in a row to trade 2 creatures for Ocelot Prides, and with 3 Ocelot Prides plus 14 cats at end of turn, I went from 7 life to 21 in one go.
Next turn I calculated that an all-out attack still couldn't kill the opponent (they were at 22), so I decided to only fly the Ocelot Prides over for one hit and make 14 more cats, then prayed that the opponent had no way of sacrificing Patchwork Automaton to give the counters to Inkmoth Nexus. Luckily they didn't draw it, and I successfully turned the game around.

Side in:
[Static Prison](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/44/static-prison) x 2
[Wear // Tear](https://scryfall.com/card/cmr/456/wear-tear) x 2
[Obsidian Charmaw](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/137/obsidian-charmaw) x 2

Side out:
[Orcish Bowmasters](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/103/orcish-bowmasters) x 1
[Thoughtseize](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/334/thoughtseize) x 3
[Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/197/phlage-titan-of-fires-fury) x 1
[Goblin Bombardment](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/279/goblin-bombardment) x 1

The opponent was too fast, but since it wasn't an infinite combo, I could still endure by feeding creatures until [The One Ring](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/246/the-one-ring) came out, opening up a big gap in hand size in one go, and as long as I could complete the [GoS](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/29/guide-of-souls) + [Ocelot Pride](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/38/ocelot-pride) combo, I would win. Static Prison and Wear // Tear need no explanation, since there are many targets. As for Charmaw, the opponent's deck has quite a few colorless lands, such as [Inkmoth Nexus](https://scryfall.com/card/mbs/145/inkmoth-nexus), [Urza's Saga](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/259/urzas-saga), and [Phyrexian Tower](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/303/phyrexian-tower), and each one is key: Inkmoth is the finisher, Saga can open a midrange route on its own, and Tower is one of the combo pieces, so I had to punish them by blowing up lands.
Because life total matters, by elimination I first pulled all the Thoughtseizes. Considering that this deck basically has no time to attack the opponent early on, even after completing the combo and making 6 or 14 cats (30 cats is too hard) I couldn't kill the opponent that same turn, so I took out Goblin Bombardment too. Phlage is sluggish, but at least it gains life, so I only took out 1. For the last slot I originally wanted to take out Chthonian Nightmare, since I'd shown it once in front of the opponent and their [Agatha's Soul Cauldron](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/242/agathas-soul-cauldron) would surely focus all its attention on my graveyard's Nightmare, but in the end I felt Nightmare still raises the ceiling, so I took out 1 Bowmasters instead (the opponent doesn't draw extra cards).

Game 2, I don't remember very well; the life-total sheet doesn't have any life changes written on it. I only remember doing something wrong that let the opponent block my GoS to death, and then after a round of their combo the opponent produced a Walking Ballista with 12 counters, used it to shoot away all my blockers, then hit my face and shot me dead. Loss.

Game 3, no changes. I mulliganed once, and the opponent started the game with [Gemstone Caverns](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/280/gemstone-caverns). Early on I kept getting beaten down by [Hangarback Walker](https://scryfall.com/card/ori/229/zht/%E8%A1%8C%E8%B5%B0%E6%A9%9F%E5%BA%AB) with counters, while [Zabaz, the Glimmerwasp](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/243/zabaz-the-glimmerwasp) was also on the battlefield, making the opponent's combos more varied. While I blocked with creatures, once I had enough mana I used [Charmaw](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/137/obsidian-charmaw) to blow up the opponent's Phyrexian Tower, stopping them from suddenly sacrificing Hangarback Walker to produce 10-plus Ornithopter tokens. (I also learned that a Gemstone Caverns with a luck counter doesn't count as a colorless land, so Charmaw can't be reduced in cost.)
After feeding Charmaw to the opponent's Hangarback Walker, the opponent played [Agatha's Soul Cauldron](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/242/agathas-soul-cauldron) and used the legend rule to make Zabaz kill itself. My turn: I tried sneaking in an attack with Ajani to see whether the opponent would blow themselves up; the opponent activated the Cauldron to eat Zabaz, which gave their Hangarback Walker a way to blow itself up, and suddenly 11 Ornithopter tokens appeared to block my Ajani & the 2/1 cat to death. Great, the Cauldron couldn't be activated any more, so I cast the Chthonian Nightmare in my hand, ate GoS to bring that Charmaw back and destroyed the Gemstone Caverns, and after that the opponent had only a Forest left. The opponent drew only one Urza's Saga, while I activated the Ring and drew Wear // Tear (for the Cauldron & Urza's Saga), GoS, Ocelot Pride, and a pile of other material, which I slowly cast one by one, once again pulling my life total up to 27, and the opponent lost to the hand-size gap the Ring created.

Victory! The winning tactic in the last game against Scales was "if you raise your life total high enough, you won't get killed"! (You still have to watch out for Inkmoth Nexus, though.)
