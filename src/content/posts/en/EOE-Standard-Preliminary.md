## Deck Overview

### Mono White Token Control
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7257368#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/5d4b7d405b16.webp)

In this Standard rotation, Mono White Token Control lost only one card, [Lay Down Arms](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/11/lay-down-arms). It was a strong one-mana removal spell, but we still have [Elspeth's Smite](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/493/elspeths-smite), which hasn't rotated, and the set's newcomer [Seam Rip](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/34/seam-rip) (an enchantment version of [Portable Hole](https://scryfall.com/card/afr/33/portable-hole)). As one-mana removal, those two are above average, so the overall structure took no major hit. There's even a bit of a silver lining: without [Lay Down Arms](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/11/lay-down-arms), you no longer have to agonize over your Plains count and can fully enjoy the high ceiling of a mono-colored deck's land base.

Speaking of white removal, I have to mention [Pinnacle Starcage](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/27/pinnacle-starcage), the successor to [Temporary Lockdown](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/36/temporary-lockdown) in this set. It can only deal with creatures and artifacts, but that's not entirely a downside, because our sideboard also runs [Authority of the Consuls](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/137/authority-of-the-consuls) and [Rest in Peace](https://scryfall.com/card/big/4/rest-in-peace). These targeted enchantments won't hurt us by accident either. Against aggro in particular, [Authority of the Consuls](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/137/authority-of-the-consuls) often shows up alongside it. The new [Seam Rip](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/34/seam-rip) is also cleverly designed as an enchantment, so the two anti-aggro removal pieces can coexist.

> In fact, [Pinnacle Starcage](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/27/pinnacle-starcage) has one more advantage over [Temporary Lockdown](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/36/temporary-lockdown): it can exile artifact lands. That said, this is basically negligible in Standard.

### Mono Green Aggro
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7254438#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/26584a23529c.webp)

A green aggro deck themed around +1/+1 counters. [Ouroboroid](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/201/ouroboroid) has a very high ceiling for board explosions, but placing +1/+1 counters on its own is still a bit too slow. It only shows its true strength alongside a series of counter-placing support cards like [Warden of the Grove](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/166/warden-of-the-grove) and [Innkeeper's Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/180/innkeepers-talent). [Terrasymbiosis](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/210/terrasymbiosis) also provides valuable resource refueling for the counters package.

### Mono Green Eldrazi
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7259860#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/e30b7a0d221a.webp)

A pie-in-the-sky meme deck. At its core it's a green ramp deck, but with [Sledge-Class Seedship](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/208/sledge-class-seedship) and [Kona, Rescue Beastie](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/187/kona-rescue-beastie), it has a chance to drop a big creature onto the battlefield immediately. The two even work together: any random Vehicle or Spacecraft can help [Kona, Rescue Beastie](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/187/kona-rescue-beastie) satisfy its survival condition. However, this strategy can be stopped as soon as the opponent has a bit more removal, so the sideboard dutifully carries [Smuggler's Surprise](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/180/smugglers-surprise), letting you switch back to the traditional instant-speed drop plan.

### Mono Black Aggro
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7259144#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/d1fc9452698a.webp)

[Sunset Saboteur](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/116/sunset-saboteur) was clearly born for aggro: high power, hard to block, and killing it costs you card advantage. The Skeleton package is another relatively aggressive archetype in black, but since Standard doesn't have enough quality Skeletons, that actually freed up some slots in the creature curve. [Elegy Acolyte](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/97/elegy-acolyte) serves as a near-substitute for [Gix, Yawgmoth Praetor](https://scryfall.com/card/tdc/181/gix-yawgmoth-praetor), supplying extra staying power.

### Orzhov Demon
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7251720#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/cefb60a5460a.webp)

The biggest impact of this rotation on the [Unholy Annex // Ritual Chamber](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/118/unholy-annex-ritual-chamber) archetype is the loss of [Archfiend of the Dross](https://scryfall.com/card/one/82/archfiend-of-the-dross) and <font color="#999999">a card that isn't a Demon but does a Demon's job,</font> [Sheoldred, the Apocalypse](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/107/sheoldred-the-apocalypse). To keep playing [Unholy Annex // Ritual Chamber](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/118/unholy-annex-ritual-chamber), the most intuitive option is to bring back the classic pairing of [Bloodletter of Aclazotz](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/92/bloodletter-of-aclazotz) and [Unstoppable Slasher](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/119/unstoppable-slasher). Built on that premise, you could actually just go mono black. But another big blow to black decks in this rotation is the disappearance of a large batch of useful cheap removal. Conveniently, [Godless Shrine](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/254/godless-shrine) was reprinted this time and [Concealed Courtyard](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/268/concealed-courtyard) hasn't rotated yet, so splashing white costs little in mana base stability while patching the removal weakness, and the sideboard gets broader coverage as well.

Another benefit of adding white is access to [Enduring Innocence](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/6/enduring-innocence) as an extra resource engine. After all, both core pieces, [Unstoppable Slasher](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/119/unstoppable-slasher) and [Bloodletter of Aclazotz](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/92/bloodletter-of-aclazotz), are 2-power creatures, and other strong curve plays like [Voice of Victory](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/33/voice-of-victory) and [Preacher of the Schism](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/113/preacher-of-the-schism) also qualify. Even ignoring the combo that can kill in one swing, it performs well as a plain midrange grind deck.

### Orzhov Control
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7259078#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/7a8f7535a7c4.webp)

A control deck built around [Ketramose, the New Dawn](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/209/ketramose-the-new-dawn) (commonly called the Black-White God). It picks generically useful exile-related cards as much as possible to extract value from the Black-White God. The pairing of [Cease // Desist](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/246/cease-desist) and [Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up) is practically tailor-made for [Ketramose, the New Dawn](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/209/ketramose-the-new-dawn). When [Cease](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/246/cease-desist) draws cards, it can also exile a graveyard, and that one card then satisfies [Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up)'s collect evidence 6. If you collect evidence when casting [Deadly Cover-Up](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/83/deadly-cover-up), exiling from graveyards happens once as a cost and once on resolution, which lets [Ketramose, the New Dawn](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/209/ketramose-the-new-dawn) draw two cards, and it won't wipe out [Ketramose, the New Dawn](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/209/ketramose-the-new-dawn), which has indestructible.

Given that you're pairing with the Black-White God, among one-mana removal spells the new [Seam Rip](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/34/seam-rip) is indeed better than [Cut Down](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/89/cut-down) and [Elspeth's Smite](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/493/elspeths-smite). This is a rare exception where, after the black decks lost their cards, one-mana removal actually got better. However, [Seam Rip](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/34/seam-rip) is only sorcery speed, so it can't save you in certain tempo-sensitive board states, which is why the sideboard still dutifully keeps [Elspeth's Smite](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/493/elspeths-smite).

### Izzet Vivi Cauldron
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7256954#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/c88d2cfff520.webp)

Honestly, this build uses no new cards at all. It's just post-rotation reconstruction. The biggest pain point of this rotation for Vivi Cauldron is losing [Voldaren Thrillseeker](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/171/voldaren-thrillseeker), so you can no longer have a creature commit seppuku at the slightest provocation, [Fling](https://scryfall.com/card/eld/126/fling) itself out, and go for the head with out-of-nowhere damage. I took a quick pass through the creatures in current Standard with a win-the-game activated ability, and [Draconautics Engineer](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/121/draconautics-engineer) seems like the more viable option. This card already showed up as a Plan B in the sideboard of some older Vivi Cauldron lists, and its [Exhaust](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/DFT-mechanism/#exhaust) can also provide a win condition from a tricky angle, keeping the Vivi Cauldron deck's strength of having many attack angles.

### Boros Burn
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7259018#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/464c67521206.webp)

I have to say, although the rotation of [Monastery Swiftspear](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/144/monastery-swiftspear) doesn't hurt creature-based aggro too badly, it still matters quite a bit for spell-based aggro like burn. I originally wanted to try the new [Lightstall Inquisitor](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/24/lightstall-inquisitor) in the slot of [Greasewrench Goblin](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/132/greasewrench-goblin), but on reflection the tempo didn't seem right. Setting aside the floor case where the opponent exiles it and plays a land tapped, meaning we get no ETB (because against burn, a shockland entering untapped just gives us a free [Shock](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/144/shock), and whether the opponent wants to do that is sometimes hard to say), burn's tempo was never as tight as creature-based aggro, and the most annoying thing about burn is that often the opponent has no effective interaction at all. Weighing everything, Boros Burn doesn't seem to be the stage where [Lightstall Inquisitor](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/24/lightstall-inquisitor) can shine, so I switched back to the old one-mana curve for now.

Overall, apart from rebuilding the mana base with [Sacred Foundry](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/256/sacred-foundry), it uses no new cards at all, so it's just post-rotation reconstruction of the old list.

### Boros Tannuk
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7251716#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/acba5261cff4.webp)

[Tannuk, Steadfast Second](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/162/tannuk-steadfast-second) can cast powerful artifacts and red creatures cheaply with [Warp](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/EOE-Mechanism/#warp). To reach the ceiling while keeping the deck stable, the most intuitive approach is to pick creatures with powerful ETB abilities that are expensive on their own but have a flexible cheap alternative cost. [Trumpeting Carnosaur](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/171/trumpeting-carnosaur) and [Overlord of the Boilerbilges](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/146/overlord-of-the-boilerbilges) both fit perfectly.

Even so, packing that many expensive cards is still a bit scary, so I mixed in a number of looting effects. The deck has a decent number of card types, so [Fear of Missing Out](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/136/fear-of-missing-out) can reach delirium without trouble, and big creatures that get discarded can be dug back onto the battlefield with [Push // Pull](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/250/push-pull) for an explosive attack. To keep its left half from becoming a dead card, the deck lightly splashes white, which also fixes red's feeble removal suite.

### Boros Artifact
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7253966#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/af04bafa48e1.webp)

The core of the deck is the supply chain of [Weapons Manufacturing](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/168/weapons-manufacturing) + [Legion Extruder](https://scryfall.com/card/big/12/legion-extruder), which continuously builds the board and deals damage. When people talk about artifact decks, they usually think of splashing blue for [Simulacrum Synthesizer](https://scryfall.com/card/big/6/simulacrum-synthesizer), but the blue side of the archetype is already quite complete. Also, that route is merely good at producing artifacts, and to get the most out of [Weapons Manufacturing](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/168/weapons-manufacturing) it also demands a number of sacrifice outlets. Furthermore, the [Simulacrum Synthesizer](https://scryfall.com/card/big/6/simulacrum-synthesizer) route leaves very little slot room for artifacts that aren't three mana or more, so there's simply no room to play the [Weapons Manufacturing](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/168/weapons-manufacturing) + [Legion Extruder](https://scryfall.com/card/big/12/legion-extruder) package. Switching to red-white actually works out better.

### Azorius Control
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7256721#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/29682a9b0316.webp)

My past verdict on [Memory Deluge](https://scryfall.com/card/inr/75/memory-deluge) was "the pinnacle of four-mana instant-speed card advantage spells in the Pioneer card pool, and I doubt whether a draw spell that rivals it will ever appear." And this set really did deliver a successor on the same level: [Consult the Star Charts](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/51/consult-the-star-charts).

In terms of total card advantage, [Memory Deluge](https://scryfall.com/card/inr/75/memory-deluge) is still higher, but the depth and flexibility of [Consult the Star Charts](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/51/consult-the-star-charts) are really too good. In long matchups it practically picks up half your library to search through, and in fast games it also offers a two-mana card-filtering option. Unlike traditional powerful draw spells, it won't leave you with a full grip you can't use, too clunky to cast, who then dies before stabilizing the board.

That said, although Azorius Control gets a super powerful card-draw spell this time and white removal is barely affected by the rotation, there is a big hole in the mana base. [Adarkar Wastes](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/243/adarkar-wastes) rotated, and [Seachrome Coast](https://scryfall.com/card/one/258/seachrome-coast), which you may have made do with before, rotated along with it. [Hallowed Fountain](https://scryfall.com/card/rvr/280/hallowed-fountain) wasn't reprinted this time, and [Starting Town](https://scryfall.com/card/fin/289/starting-town) is not something a two-color control deck can afford to play, so all you can do is increase your tapped lands and basic lands and make do.

By the way, although the maindeck [Ultima](https://scryfall.com/card/fin/38/ultima) and the sideboard [Pinnacle Starcage](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/27/pinnacle-starcage) seem to be conflicting sweepers, the matchups where you need [Pinnacle Starcage](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/27/pinnacle-starcage) are probably so fast that you won't have time to wait until five mana for [Ultima](https://scryfall.com/card/fin/38/ultima), and when sideboarding they will most likely be swapped for each other. If you want to keep the number of maindeck sweepers the same while also adding [Pinnacle Starcage](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/27/pinnacle-starcage), change all the maindeck [Ultima](https://scryfall.com/card/fin/38/ultima) into [Day of Judgment](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/140/day-of-judgment).

### Dimir Control
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7251734#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/565b2d542afe.webp)

Dimir Control, like the Azorius Control above, is one of the decks that painfully lost its adjacent-color dual lands. But Dimir is lucky in that [Watery Grave](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/261/watery-grave) was reprinted this time, so there's no need to worry about the mana base at all.

Although the lands are fine, the spells have plenty of problems. After [Cut Down](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/89/cut-down) rotated, the only real unconditional one-mana instant-speed removal left is [Stab](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/71/stab), yet there are simply too many key low-cost threats with 3 toughness in the current environment. So for the one-mana removal slot I turned to [Caustic Exhale](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/74/caustic-exhale). As long as you can [Behold](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/TDM-mechanism/#behold), it's also a one-mana removal spell, and at worst it's a two-mana removal spell. Removing slowly beats not removing at all. And since we're already running Behold, it's only natural to build a Dragon control deck, and [Dispelling Exhale](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/41/dispelling-exhale) <font color="#999999">(the Behold counterspell)</font> also fills the gap in Dimir's rather weak two-mana defensive counterspells.

But Dimir Control has another big problem: its ability to deal with planeswalkers. Regular planeswalkers can be handled by counterspells plus specialized removal like [The End](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/87/the-end), but the current environment happens to have [Kaito, Bane of Nightmares](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/220/kaito-bane-of-nightmares), a particularly troublesome outlier. [Sheoldred's Edict](https://scryfall.com/card/one/108/sheoldreds-edict), the best answer to it, rotated as well, so all I could do was try [Feed the Cycle](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/94/feed-the-cycle), which has a more flexible cost, and use [Nowhere to Run](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/111/nowhere-to-run) to turn off ward, to see whether [Shoot the Sheriff](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/106/shoot-the-sheriff) can force through an answer on the opponent's turn.

### Dimir Tempo
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7259094#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/ce04730c3e9d.webp)

The two-mana curve uses the new [Sunset Saboteur](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/116/sunset-saboteur). It has no flash and no flying, but this guy's attacking tempo is just too good, and for a two-drop curve, menace is sometimes not any easier to block than flying. As for the drawback of putting +1/+1 counters on the opponent's creatures, it can be mitigated by the stun effects that Dimir is good at.

Also, the Rogue creature type of [Sunset Saboteur](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/116/sunset-saboteur) brings up a detail worth watching in post-EOE Standard: the blind spot of [Shoot the Sheriff](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/106/shoot-the-sheriff). After [Go for the Throat](https://scryfall.com/card/j25/447/go-for-the-throat) rotates, [Shoot the Sheriff](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/106/shoot-the-sheriff) is expected to become its main successor, but there are actually quite a few [Outlaw](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/OTJ-mechanism/#outlaw) creatures in Standard, so [Shoot the Sheriff](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/106/shoot-the-sheriff) often stumbles in odd places. This is especially interesting in Dimir decks: logically they are the main victims of black's strong removal rotating out, yet Dimir happens to be a heavy user of [Outlaw](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/OTJ-mechanism/#outlaw) creatures, so in the new environment Dimir creatures have, oddly, become slightly more resistant to removal. When designing the removal ratios for black decks, I recommend carefully weighing backup answers against [Outlaw](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/OTJ-mechanism/#outlaw) creatures.

### Golgari Melds
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7256818#paper)

Although it carries the Meld banner, the underlying structure is a midrange grind deck built on self-mill plus graveyard recursion. Honestly, the original core idea of this deck was [Cosmogoyf](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/215/cosmogoyf) plus a collect evidence package, but the more I built it the more I felt that a plain recursion-based grind deck would be better, so I moved [Cosmogoyf](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/215/cosmogoyf) down to the sideboard as a countermeasure against [Rest in Peace](https://scryfall.com/card/big/4/rest-in-peace). ### Temur Ferocious
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7251715#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/5dd747947cb2.webp)

Ferocious decks have always lacked good proactive two-drops. If it's a creature, you want it to be a Ferocious creature with at least 4 power, and [Llanowar Elves](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/227/llanowar-elves) + [Herd Heirloom](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/144/herd-heirloom) provide plenty of acceleration already, so no more mana creatures are needed. [Quantum Riddler](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/72/quantum-riddler) unexpectedly meets the job requirements here: when you have nothing to do at two mana, you can use [Warp](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/EOE-Mechanism/#warp) to cycle through your deck, and still keep the guarantee of calling down a Ferocious creature later.

Of course, this set also printed [Stomping Ground](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/258/stomping-ground) + [Breeding Pool](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/251/breeding-pool), which is part of what makes Temur color combinations playable. Getting two shocklands that both happen to produce green also ensures that a turn-one [Llanowar Elves](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/227/llanowar-elves) has green mana to cast it. Because [Herd Heirloom](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/144/herd-heirloom) + [Starting Town](https://scryfall.com/card/fin/289/starting-town) can both produce any color, [Twinmaw Stormbrood // Charring Bite](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/232/twinmaw-stormbrood-charring-bite) is not just a five-damage burn spell with [Omen](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/TDM-mechanism/#omen), and there is also a chance to cast the creature side.

### Temur Greenshell
[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7254037#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/EOE-Standard-Preliminary/6b1dcb00eb97.webp)

I don't know what I was on when I put together this unhinged deck. The core idea of the whole deck is to get the most out of [Fecund Greenshell](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/171/fecund-greenshell): stuff in every creature with toughness greater than power, picking members with good general-purpose value where possible. But once it was all stuffed in, I found that, surprisingly, there are quite a few synergies among these members.

For example, [Biotech Specialist](https://scryfall.com/card/eoe/214/biotech-specialist) wants more sacrificable artifacts in the deck, and the adventures of [Sentinel of the Nameless City](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/211/sentinel-of-the-nameless-city) and [Kellan, Inquisitive Prodigy // Tail the Suspect](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/212/kellan-inquisitive-prodigy-tail-the-suspect) can also create artifact tokens. In turn, [Kellan, Inquisitive Prodigy // Tail the Suspect](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/212/kellan-inquisitive-prodigy-tail-the-suspect) can blow up the artifact tokens made by the creatures above to draw cards, or pair with [Kitesail Larcenist](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/61/kitesail-larcenist) to turn the opponent's creature into an artifact and force a board clear. [Azure Beastbinder](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/41/azure-beastbinder) likewise gives the removal-light Temur color combination a few more disruption options, and paired with various red damage sources it can force through big creatures.

## Conclusion

Standard deckbuilding for this set turned out to be surprisingly hard. I saw a great many powerful single cards that excited me during preview season, but when I actually tried to assemble them into decks, no amount of tuning made them fit together.

[Warp](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/EOE-Mechanism/#warp) is a powerful ability, but cards with good enough general-purpose value are very scarce. [Warp](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/EOE-Mechanism/#warp) creatures have a high curve to begin with, so the number you can put in one deck is limited, and as luck would have it most of them are designed to shine only under specific themes. The result is that even though it's the set's headline feature, it's hard to build a deck that makes the most of it. [Station](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/EOE-Mechanism/#spacecraft--station) is in a similar situation: it runs too clunkily, and I don't know how to adjust it.

The other obstacle in deckbuilding this time was the mana base. All the pain lands plus the adjacent-color fastlands rotated at once, but shocklands were only half replenished, and only two of those were adjacent-color shocklands. As a result, for some adjacent-color decks that demand good tempo, I'd figured out the whole list, but looking left and right the lands just couldn't keep up, so I painfully put them on ice until the rest of the lands are filled in.

For example, when I saw the cards below during preview season, a White-Blue artifact aggro deck had already taken shape in my mind. But when you want to guarantee color fixing while also not tolerating early tempo loss, the current card pool's lands simply can't support it <font color="#999999">(or rather, the expected value doesn't pass)</font>, so the White-Blue decks that survived are all ones that aren't picky about color requirements or tempo.
