## Rating Scale

5.0: Unconditional first pick
4.5: Unconditional first pick if you're in the right color pair
4.0: Worth a first pick 
3.5: Strong enough but not necessarily a first pick 
3.0: Above average (you'll really hope it makes your final deck)
2.5: Average, maybe around the 6th or 7th pick of a draft
2.0 to 1.0: Below average (you'd rather not play it than play it)
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable (basically don't play it, it will only help you lose)

## Conclusion

Having previewed the white cards, my general impression is: there are lots of 2-power creatures, and flying is important. This time, however, white's commons don't include a two-mana {% mtgcard "Pacifism" "m20" tooltip=true %}; instead there's the three-mana {% mtgcard "Trapped in the Screen" tooltip=true %}, so relying on removal to clear the way is less efficient, and giving fliers Auras may be the more viable way to play white aggro. For black-white and blue-white, the eerie theme that touches enchantments and reanimation, these color pairs may need to play more defensively early and then go for a burst in the mid to late game. The white-green part was covered extensively in the preliminary article, so I won't repeat it here.

I hope everyone finds their own combo in DSK Limited. Have fun!
