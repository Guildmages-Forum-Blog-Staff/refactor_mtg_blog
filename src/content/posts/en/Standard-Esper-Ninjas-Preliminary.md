## Why Esper Ninjas?

[MIT#11](https://melee.gg/Tournament/View/385576) was the first week RC (Regional Championship) after the official release of [TMT](https://scryfall.com/sets/tmt). The environment was unpredictable: were the new cards the answers of the format, or traps!? Testing time was extremely limited, which made it a very challenging tournament. Readers who follow my fan page [cephille's MTG World](https://www.facebook.com/Pignosemtgworld) know that I'm a long-time Dimir midrange player at tournaments. And my one constant understanding when playing this deck is --

**[Kaito, Bane of Nightmares](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/220/kaito-bane-of-nightmares) is the strongest "single card" in Standard!**

This understanding was already laid out very clearly in Petr Sochůrek's earlier [article](https://www.tcgplayer.com/content/article/Dimir-Midrange-Standard-MTG-Deck-Guide-Card-Choices-Sideboarding-and-More/8eec2696-c570-4792-9366-0d6ccad96e73/) on TCGPLAYER. Readers interested in this archetype are welcome to study it as foundational material before reading the rest of this article.

> Article link: [Dimir Midrange Standard MTG Deck Guide - Card Choices, Sideboarding, and More!](https://www.tcgplayer.com/content/article/Dimir-Midrange-Standard-MTG-Deck-Guide-Card-Choices-Sideboarding-and-More/8eec2696-c570-4792-9366-0d6ccad96e73/)

And Dimir midrange is a deck that only holds together because of the strength of {% mtglink "Kaito, Bane of Nightmares" %}. But its problem is also obvious: performance with and without Kaito is often worlds apart. Kaito's own strength alone can carry Dimir midrange to Tier 1 to Tier 2 performance. So what if there were an archetype that could fully unleash Kaito's real power? Thus, by adding the new Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles cards from TMT onto the foundation of Dimir midrange, Esper Ninjas was truly born.

## Land Configuration

The mana base for a three-color aggro deck is always a big subject of study, and 24 lands is the result of many rounds of testing. But how many lands that produce black/blue/white are enough? What land sequence is acceptable each turn? Do we still have room for utility lands and basic lands? These questions must all serve your main game plan, which is the "main theme" we mentioned above -- **one-drop on turn one, Sneak on turn two, ninjutsu Kaito on turn three**.

### Mana Requirements of the Main Theme

- **Turn 1 (one-drop)**: Must have <span style="color:#808080">Black</span> (if playing a <span style="color:#808080">black</span> creature) or <span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span> (if playing a <span style="color:#006DB3">blue</span> creature).
- **Turn 2 (Sneak burst)**: Must have <span style="color:#C3B270">White</span> and/or <span style="color:#808080">Black</span> (for the Sneak requirements of {% mtglink "The Last Ronin's Technique" %} or {% mtglink "Dark Leo & Shredder" %}).
- **Turn 3 (Kaito arrives)**: Must have <span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span> + <span style="color:#808080">Black</span> (pay $1 {<span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span>}{<span style="color:#808080">Black</span>}).

| Target timing | Required color combination | Probability achieved (7-card opening hand) | Notes |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| **T1** | 1 <span style="color:#808080">Black</span> or 1 <span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span> | >98% | High probability, since there are 15 + 16 <span style="color:#808080">black</span>/<span style="color:#006DB3">blue</span> lands |
| **T2** | 1 <span style="color:#808080">Black</span> and 1 <span style="color:#C3B270">White</span> | ~71.5%<sup>[Note 1](#quote1)</sup> | Main bottleneck, limited by 14 <span style="color:#C3B270">white</span> lands |
| **T3** | 1 <span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span> and 1 <span style="color:#808080">Black</span> | ~89.0%	| Quite stable, thanks to 17 <span style="color:#006DB3">blue</span> / 20 <span style="color:#808080">black</span> lands |

From the analysis above we can see that the current deck's mana base is acceptable, but the probability of producing black on turn one is slightly below the 16 sources suggested by Frank Karsten<sup>[Note 2](#quote2)</sup>, and the probability of producing white on turn two is also insufficient. After the tournament I worked with AI to adjust this into a version I'm more satisfied with, provided in a [later section](#land-base-optimization). | Verge lands | {% mtglink "Bleachbone Verge" %} | 2 | Turn 1 produces <span style="color:#808080">Black</span>, turn 2 produces <span style="color:#808080">Black</span>/<span style="color:#C3B270">White</span> |
| Fixing lands | {% mtglink "Multiversal Passage" %} | 3 | Turn 1 produces <span style="color:#808080">Black</span>/<span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span>, turn 2 produces <span style="color:#C3B270">White</span>; locked in on entering and can't produce other colors |
| Fixing lands | {% mtglink "Starting Town" %} | 1 | Turn 1 produces <span style="color:#808080">Black</span>/<span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span>/<span style="color:#C3B270">White</span> |

**Notes:**
1. When keeping a hand, if you only have two lands and they are {% mtglink "Gloomlake Verge" %} + {% mtglink "Multiversal Passage" %} or {% mtglink "Starting Town" %}, **you can't produce both <span style="color:#808080">black</span> and <span style="color:#C3B270">white</span> on turn two**. If your turn-two Sneak card is {% mtglink "Dark Leo & Shredder" %}, you may be forced to mulligan or delay your development.
2.  {% mtglink "Floodfarm Verge" %} is primarily white with secondary blue, and absolutely must not be included in this deck (because this deck's turn-one requirement is to produce <span style="color:#808080">black</span>/<span style="color:#006DB3">blue</span>, and this land producing <span style="color:#C3B270">white</span> on turn one doesn't fit at all).
3.  When you have a Verge in hand, {% mtglink "Multiversal Passage" %} should preferentially name the type that "activates the Verge" (for example: if you have a Dimir Verge, choose Swamp/Island).
4.  If your opening hand has both a one-drop creature and {% mtglink "Restless Reef" %}, unless you urgently need Reef to produce color next turn, always play the creature on T1 and play Reef after T4. Don't sacrifice tempo for Reef.
5. Starting Town is great in the first three turns, but terrible if drawn after turn four, so it has the largest gap between ceiling and floor. Therefore, on T1-T3, if you have a choice, prioritize playing the Starting Town in your hand.
6. To keep a three-color aggro deck running, expect to lose 4-6 life yourself in the first three turns just from playing lands. This is quite unfavorable against decks like Mono-Red aggro that win by pressuring your life total.

### Probability and Balance of the Main Theme

| Mulligan strategy | Probability with 8 Sneak cards | Probability with 6 Sneak cards | Sneak count comparison |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| **Keep 7** | 11.2% | ~8.4% | Going down to 6 Sneak cards drops the main theme from once every 9-10 games to about once every 12 games |
| **Mulligan to 6** | 21.5% | ~15.2% | With 6 Sneak cards, the main-theme rate is still reasonable and still a strong threat in BO3 | 
| **Mulligan to 5** | 30.4% | ~23.1% | Efficiency of going for the ceiling drops significantly | 

Having seen the theory, we still have to return to actual testing. In the current meta, when you're on the draw against Izzet Prowess or Mono-White Momo, you're basically the defender; and in the current meta, running four of each turn-two Sneak card greatly increases the chance that your Sneak cards are stuck in hand when you lose the roll, which is quite unfavorable. In response to the current meta, running three of each tested as a relatively reasonable choice; the ceiling is lower, but raising the deck's resilience is the direction we want. I'll plant a seed here though: if the meta shifts in the future so that Esper is the protagonist of the environment, running four of each turn-two Sneak card will be a necessary pivot.

### Card Choices

{% mtgcard "Kaito, Bane of Nightmares" %}

Whether in Dimir midrange or this version of Esper Ninjas, Kaito is the core and strongest single card. If in Dimir midrange Kaito single-handedly carries the whole family of junk, then in Esper Ninjas Kaito has finally found partners who can fight alongside him!

First, Kaito's [+1]: You get an emblem with "Ninjas you control get +1/+1." Unlike before, when after Kaito entered you had to open with [+1] just to protect yourself and lower the risk of being killed in response by {% mtglink "Sear" %}, in this version, with ninja-themed cards you can Sneak out on turn two to go wide, so the turn-three Kaito [+1] is no longer just a defensive choice but also a highly aggressive strategy! When ahead on board, using [+1] twice in a row basically seals the win, and also turns a Sneak-activated {% mtglink "The Last Ronin's Technique" %} into a lethal blow! For Dimir midrange that wins rather slowly, the quick wins Esper Ninjas can achieve are very noticeable.

As for the [0], surveil 2, and if the opponent lost life this turn, draw a card. In ninjas with their front-loaded, dense curve, it's easier to draw that card. Even if you can't get through, manually slamming a {% mtglink "Dream Beavers" %} to drain 1 can help you draw it.

And the [-2] puts two stun counters on a target creature; whether to clear the way, prevent a counterattack, or buy time when behind on board, Dimir midrange players can always make the best choice for the current situation. You can also combine it with a {% mtglink "Floodpits Drowner" %} that has lost summoning sickness to do some tricks.

#### One-Drop Creatures

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Esper-Ninjas-Preliminary/008b4d91ae50.webp)

Choosing Esper Ninjas means adding the two-mana Sneak burst. To reliably Sneak on turn two, one-drop creatures that can attack are indispensable. Under this strategy, increasing the one-drop count is necessary, so the earlier question of whether {% mtglink "Spyglass Siren" %} or {% mtglink "Dream Beavers" %} is better is no longer an issue: **playing both, as many as possible, is the optimal answer**.

Comparing the cards, everyone is familiar with Siren: flying is just the baseline, and the Map token can help a two-land Dimir opening hand develop toward the third land ~~what do you mean you only have one land? Mulligan!~~ and exploring into a nonland card to grow its body is also quite key; a 1/1 flyer versus a 2/2 flyer is worlds apart in the damage race.

Then looking at Dream Beavers, at first I thought drain 1 + scry 1 was inconsequential, but after real testing I found it's indeed an important addition that TMT brought! The reasons are:

- You gain an edge in the damage race, even from turn one!
- In Esper's painful three-color mana base, you recover 1 life this way, and that little breathing room lets you handle the rest of the game with more composure.
- It has the ability to finish off a low-life opponent! With ninjutsu and Sneak support, once a Dream Beavers shows up it may enter a second or even third time!
- Scry 1 looks little different from {% mtglink "Faerie Dreamthief" %}'s surveil 1, but putting an unneeded card on the bottom of the library avoids becoming a target for {% mtglink "Superior Spider-Man" %}; also, if {% mtglink "Doomsday Excruciator" %} mills you down to 6 cards in library, the cards you scried down might even save your life.
- Compared with Faerie Dreamthief, paying life to draw is not great on a three-color base; and paying three mana to draw is not the tempo this deck wants.

These two Dimir bruisers establish our first-turn offensive point and provide extra hidden resources that make development after turn two go more smoothly.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Esper-Ninjas-Preliminary/fd70b34bb7c7.webp)

In our mana base, which hurts no matter how we configure it, we can make flipping Cecil on turn three no problem. And the 2/3 deathtouch one-drop body makes him quite reliable on both offense and defense. After flipping into a 4/4 lifelink Paladin, he can also give other creatures indestructible when attacking, making subsequent ninjutsu and Sneak more likely to succeed!
{% mtglink "Flitterwing Nuisance" %} is the weakest one-drop on the curve, but its activated ability lets us avoid maxing out the clunkier {% mtglink "Enduring Curiosity" %}, which is the main reason I chose him. Don't forget, our deck design gives him chances to activate his ability multiple times after being returned to hand; even {% mtglink "Requiting Hex" %} helps reactivate him. If all goes well, the card advantage from this back-and-forth is considerable.

Besides strengthening our resistance to aggro, these two creatures, with a little extension, can even bring us far greater resource returns than other one-drops!

{% mtgcard "The Last Ronin's Technique" %}

The second addition TMT brought! The turn-two Sneak tempo greatly improves on the traditional Dimir turn two, which was mostly passive defense, or flashing in {% mtglink "Floodpits Drowner" %} to disrupt + pave the way for a turn-three Kaito. Three 1/1 white ninja tokens look ordinary at first glance, but unless the opponent responds by sweeping them with {% mtglink "Fire Magic" %} or {% mtglink "Pyroclasm" %}, a turn-three Kaito is inevitable! And the ninja creature type is crucial: you can even skip the turn-two Sneak, use [+1] twice in a row after Kaito arrives on turn three, then Sneak with another creature on turn four, at which point it's three unblocked 3/3 ninjas + a 5/6 Kaito, for a total of 14 damage!

Also, don't forget this is an instant: when you need to defend, "flashing" in 3 creatures for four mana will completely disrupt the opponent's plans, and if you've used Kaito's [+1] once or more beforehand, it's even deadlier!

{% mtgcard "Dark Leo & Shredder" %}

A black-white Sneak card that can also be cast normally on turn two, and is less prone to getting stuck in hand than Technique. The 1/3 body may look unremarkable but is actually cleverly designed. 3 toughness can usually survive a small red sweeper, giving us a chance to keep developing. The deathtouch on attacking ninjas is a static ability, so you don't even need him to attack, which is especially important when you can't get through after playing Technique first. We can first attack with a group of 1/1 ninjas + a 1/1 flyer; after the opponent's creatures block the ninjas, we return the flyer and Sneak in Dark Leo, and now the blocked ninja has deathtouch; after combat damage, we've freeloaded off one of the opponent's blockers, and because of Dark Leo's ability, the "lost" ninja is made again, and can even block!

Dark Leo is a card I was extremely satisfied with after real testing. I also believe he's the last missing piece in Esper Ninjas!

{% mtgcard "Floodpits Drowner" %}

Drowner is our old comrade in Dimir, so he needs no special introduction here. But I must stress that in today's meta, a Drowner on the play can completely suppress green-based decks: keeping the mana dorks and Earthbending land creatures from standing up for a turn always stalls the opponent's development, which is the main reason I can't part with him.

{% mtgcard "Aang, Swift Savior // Aang and La, Ocean's Fury" %}

Aang was another surprise from splashing white! The original slot was {% mtglink "Tishana's Tidebinder" %}, and Aang performed even better! The main reasons are that Aang flies, and a 2/3 body is far better than a 3/2. Also, Aang can mostly take part in blocking, indirectly saving you some life.

And Aang can do far more than that. As I understand it, his tactical roles are at least the following four:
1. **Path-clearer (proactive):** Airbend can proactively exile the opponent's blockers, and even keep tokens or non-Earthbend creature lands from ever coming back. It can also increase the chance that your one-drops go unblocked, in turn activating ninjutsu or Sneak.
2. **Delaying disruption (reactive):** When the opponent taps out to slam a high-cost threat, Airbend can interrupt and delay the opponent's plan. Against sweepers, this can even be the key to winning or losing!
3. **Umbrella for creatures or spells (reactive):** Flash in Aang in response to removal to Airbend your own creature; at first glance it costs tempo, but making sure our threats can keep dealing damage is very important. If you exile something like {% mtglink "Enduring Curiosity" %} that costs more than two, it's even a gain. And Aang's Airbend can also exile a target spell; when the opponent responds by countering your important spell, Aang lets you escape and recast it for two.
4. **Plan B finisher (proactive):** Beyond the ETB ability mentioned above, Aang's Waterbend 8, when the game is deadlocked or Kaito isn't making an impact, makes a flipped, attacking Aang not only a 6/6 vigilance, trample source of damage, but also able to put +1/+1 counters on all tapped creatures. This lets your one-drop flyers and your ninja tokens that never grow become huge threats under Aang's leadership even in the late game.

Before putting Aang in, I had used TMT's new ninja {% mtglink "Turncoat Kunoichi" %}. But after I started investing in Aang, I increasingly believed he's this deck's {% mtglink "Spell Queller" %}, and an enhanced one at that.

{% mtgcard "Enduring Curiosity" %}

As mentioned earlier, the 2 copies of {% mtglink "Flitterwing Nuisance" %} serve as tempo filler for the blue cat, but the real Enduring Curiosity is still indispensable. Though I only run one, one is enough. I tested a 2-copy version before; believe me, you usually won't, and don't need to, draw a second.

{% mtgcard "Sygg, Wanderwine Wisdom // Sygg, Wanderbrine Shield" %}

Sygg's slot is a flex slot, a so-called Pet Card. But let me still say what Sygg can do.

- Besides turn-two proactive Sneak or reactive defense, provides a third proactive plan of attacking and drawing cards with a one-drop curve.
- Unblockable by itself; unless the opponent has removal, it ensures the inevitability of a turn-three ninjutsu or Sneak.
- The flip-side ability can give a creature protection from all five colors. You can not only make an invincible Drowner that attacks and blocks, but also target a Kaito that's a creature on your turn, ensuring he survives on the opponent's turn! (Even if Kaito turns back into a planeswalker, the protection from all colors lasts until your turn, at which point Kaito is hexproof again!)
- Although Sygg repeatedly flipping is powerful, it still requires paying a bit of white/blue as the cost, so we should prioritize the ninjutsu, Sneak, or spell we can execute and need right now, and not put the cart before the horse.

#### Removal Spells in the Deck

{% mtgcard "Requiting Hex" %}

Hex, as extremely reliable one-mana removal, can stop the starting points of all kinds of decks before turn two. Such as Mono-White Momo's {% mtglink "Momo, Friendly Flier" %}, nearly all of the creatures in the standard Izzet Prowess builds, Simic Rhythm's {% mtglink "Badgermole Cub" %}, or {% mtglink "Llanowar Elves" %} that green-based decks use for a one-turn kill. Don't forget, in response to your creature about to be removed, Blight 1 kills the opponent's small creature to gain 2 life, which is extremely important in a three-color deck.

{% mtgcard "Shoot the Sheriff" %}

After the environment shifted toward Mono-White Momo, green-based decks, Izzet decks, and even Dimir Demons and multicolor Elementals, this card, once criticized for killing nothing, has become quite close to a two-mana clean kill. If I could choose again, I might put a third copy in the main deck or sideboard, to address the current shortage of kill spells among the 75 cards.

{% mtgcard "Get Lost" %}

Running {% mtglink "Bitter Triumph" %} in a three-color deck is often suicide, and we should take the chance of splashing white to use kill spells that don't cost life. Get Lost even has the added function of destroying enchantments, offering possible main-deck enchantment removal against Izzet Lessons and Mono-Green Landfall, reducing the risk of sudden death. But it must be said that giving the opponent two Map Tokens, when you have no sweeper, can sometimes still cause a considerable disadvantage. Get Lost isn't a removal spell you can use mindlessly; think a few steps ahead before using it.

#### Why Run Spider-Sense?

{% mtgcard "Spider-Sense" %}

For this conditional counterspell slot, everyone's choice differs. Previously most people would choose the relatively middling {% mtglink "Phantom Interference" %}, or, based on their read of the meta, counterspells like {% mtglink "Spell Pierce" %} or {% mtglink "Spell Snare" %}. However, in a tightly curved Esper Ninjas deck, I think there's no room for a two-mana counterspell. Put another way, if you leave two mana up for a counter on turns two and three, your early board must be super weak or nonexistent; unless you can counter and then follow up with two creatures to set up, or jump to the blue cat, blind defense equals certain defeat. And the ninjas' board-development ability is both an advantage and a disadvantage, the disadvantage being punishment from sweeper spells or sweeper effects. Web-slinging, at the cost of one {<span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span>} + returning a tapped creature to hand, can handle the following unfavorable situations:

- Countering sweeper spells/triggered abilities, such as the {% mtglink "Living End" %} effect of "casting" {% mtglink "Bringer of the Last Gift" %}, or the return-all-nonElemental-creatures effect of "casting" {% mtglink "Sunderflock" %}.
- Countering spot removal.
- Countering triggered abilities that could be lethal if not countered, such as {% mtglink "Cosmogrand Zenith" %}'s making two 1/1s or putting counters on the whole team in Mono-White tempo; and {% mtglink "Sunspine Lynx" %}'s effect of dealing damage based on how many nonbasic lands you control.
- Various Elementals' ETBs, or {% mtglink "Doomsday Excruciator" %}'s effect of exiling your library down to six cards.

In short, the purpose of Spider-Sense isn't to reliably counter a particular spell, but to avoid situations where, in the main game when I'm ahead on board, a triggered ability or sweeper spell flips the game. Although this card is extremely weak against an empty board, the ninja build itself is meant to keep snowballing board advantage and convert it into a winning position, and under that premise I chose Spider-Sense.

#### Small Tip: Interactions Between Sneak, Ninjutsu, and Web-slinging

When designing this deck, in the spirit of play, I included all the cards from mechanics derived from ninjutsu. Let me use {% mtglink "Floodpits Drowner" %} to explain how to play some tricks.

Prerequisites:
1. A Drowner that has lost summoning sickness
2. The opponent has a creature with one or more Stun Counter(s) on it
3. Drowner attacks and is unblocked

The interaction begins:
1. Activate Drowner's ability with {1}{<span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span>}
2. In response, before the ability resolves, cast a Sneak spell, or activate Kaito's ninjutsu ability to return Drowner to hand (returning Drowner is part of the cost, so the opponent can't respond to it)
3. Resolve Drowner's ability, but only the opponent needs to shuffle the targeted creature with Stun Counter(s) into their library. **Note that you can't, and shouldn't, shuffle** (a spell or ability with two targets still performs its effect as long as one of the targets is legal)
4. Don't forget: a **Sneak spell** only lets you return an unblocked creature during the declare blockers step in order to **cast** it; but a **ninjutsu ability** lets you **activate** it any time after the creature becomes unblocked and before the end of the combat phase!

And if you want to cast {% mtglink "Spider-Sense" %} for {<span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span>} via Web-slinging, you need a tapped creature, whether from attacking or by other means. Incidentally, this is also one of the reasons Spider-Sense is used in Simic Rhythm or Azorius Momo, because they have many ways to tap their own creatures.

## Sideboard Strategy

Below I'll explain my sideboard strategy around these 4 key points:

### 1. Identify Difficult Matchups

Three-color decks definitely have a hard time against red-based decks. Besides the archnemesis {% mtglink "Sunspine Lynx" %}, some even carry the nonbasic-land hater {% mtglink "Zhao, the Moon Slayer" %}, which is absolutely a difficult matchup for us. By the same logic, Boros Dragons, which can blow up our lands + have a chance to develop quickly, is also a major enemy.

Among the three pillars of Izzet, Izzet Prowess depends on being on the play or draw and whether the corresponding cards come in time; the key for Izzet Lessons is whether {% mtglink "Gran-Gran" %} lives long enough and whether the lethal talent and the monument come out in time; Izzet Elementals are basically a good matchup for us.

However, not noticing and practicing enough against the Mono-White Momo that rose a week before the event directly led to my poor performance at this tournament. But failure is the mother of success, and if I face Mono-White Momo again, I also have the corresponding sideboard cards and strategy to deal with it.

### 2. Maximize the Benefit of Splashing White

{% mtgcard "Pest Control" %}

Before the tournament I noticed that the share of Izzet Prowess kept climbing, and splashing white lets us run this low-cost sweeper! If I had noticed Mono-White Momo in time, I'd have given it enough respect -- putting a second Pest Control in the sideboard. Pest Control's strength is that it can flip a Mono-White deck's starting points with one card:
- {% mtglink "Momo, Friendly Flier" %}
- {% mtglink "Springleaf Drum" %}
- The tokens made by {% mtglink "Cosmogrand Zenith" %}
- Other one-drop creatures
- Even {% mtglink "Seam Rip" %}, after which we also get back the one- and two-drops it had pinned down.

When the Mono-White side doesn't have enough board, or against a god-draw opening of turn-one drum, turn-two Momo + {% mtglink "Sage of the Skies" %} as a pair, a successfully resolved Pest Control blows up not just part of the opponent's board but also cuts off their subsequent tempo.

{% mtgcard "Requisition Raid" %}

When still playing Dimir midrange, we always struggled to cover everything against Izzet Lessons. But with white mixed in we have {% mtglink "Requisition Raid" %}, and even if the opponent goes turn-one Gran-Gran, turn-two {% mtglink "Artist's Talent" %}, turn-three {% mtglink "Monument to Endurance" %}, we can still steadily use this card to two-for-one them. And even if we only destroy one side, if we have some creatures left on the board we can also choose to put counters on the whole team to increase board attack power, speed up the clock, and raise our resistance to small red sweepers.

The advantages of a sideboard containing white go far beyond this and deserve further study.

### 3. Risk Management

{% mtgcard "Tishana's Tidebinder" %}

A card I once loved. Although it's no longer strong enough today, Tidebinder still does a fine job of preventing sudden death against combo decks. Don't forget, if the opposing Elementals deck wants to sweep our board with {% mtglink "Ill-Timed Explosion" %}, we can respond to the damage trigger after the opponent finishes discarding and jump in Tidebinder to counter it.

{% mtgcard "Annul" %}

2 Annul are still necessary against Mono-Green Landfall and Izzet Lessons, able to counter key threats and swing the tempo back.

{% mtgcard "Requiting Hex" %}

The fourth Hex is especially key against Simic Rhythm, Mono-Red aggro, and Izzet Prowess. Mono-White always bursts out with a one-to-three jump; on the play you can leave it out, but on the draw you can decide case by case, and if the sideboard has no other removal you may still have to bring it in.

{% mtgcard "Shoot the Sheriff" %}

In the current meta, we may truly need a third two-mana black kill spell to stay alive. If we can promptly remove the {% mtglink "Quantum Riddler" %} that the opponent brings in from the sideboard, we won't easily lose from falling behind on cards.

### 4. Versatility

{% mtgcard "Disdainful Stroke" "pcbb" %}

2 Disdainful Stroke are quite useful against the meta's other dimension of midrange, control, Mono-Green Landfall, Izzet Elementals and similar decks.

{% mtgcard "Essence Scatter" %}

A creature counterspell is a highly versatile card. You can bring it in against almost everything except Izzet Lessons and Jeskai (non-Dragon) control, and considering the current meta there's even a need for 1 main deck and 1 sideboard; on the draw against Mono-White, a god-draw {% mtglink "Sage of the Skies" %} can be pinned down with this card. Also, against Izzet Prowess or Izzet Lessons, which may bring in {% mtglink "Quantum Riddler" %} from the sideboard, the existence of this creature counterspell also keeps us from over-boarding Disdainful Stroke.

{% mtgcard "Glen Elendra Guardian" %}

This card is quite middling against fast decks, but performs very well when brought in against slower decks. The part that counters noncreature spells can withstand a removal spell, sweeper, or planeswalker. After removing the -1/-1 counter, the 3/4 flying body is also quite deadly in these matchups. Although we'd mostly take out {% mtglink "Requiting Hex" %} in these matchups, you can also keep 1 as the situation allows, for a possible {% mtglink "Voice of Victory" %} or {% mtglink "Bitterbloom Bearer" %}.

### Sideboard Cards I Wouldn't Play If Choosing Again

{% mtgcard "Spider-Sense" %}

The second Spider-Sense in the sideboard is actually not good; swapping in {% mtglink "Negate" %} or something else would be a bit better, and I rarely brought this sideboard card in.

{% mtgcard "Subterranean Schooner" %}

My reason for reactivating this card was inspiration from the previous Standard's Esper midrange; the slot used to be {% mtglink "Loch Mare" %}, but most of the time I didn't have room to pay to draw. Considering the Esper color combination's tighter curve, I wanted a card like Schooner that lets me trade for resources without paying extra. However, this card is poor against Requiting Hex, and in most matchups where I'd want to bring it in to strengthen the board, like Dimir Excruciator, they never take out a single Hex. If going down this route, one should look toward three-mana Vehicles or Spacecraft.

And with those 2 freed-up slots, I could put in the second {% mtglink "Pest Control" %} and a third {% mtglink "Shoot the Sheriff" %}.

Oh right, I seem not to have mentioned a sideboard plan for Dimir midrange at all. But Esper Ninjas is fundamentally an upgraded replacement for Dimir midrange in its deck design, and as long as we can develop according to our game plan, with lands and the related tempo not being overly disrupted, Dimir midrange has a hard time standing up to us.

## Post-Tournament Review

With hindsight, **the version of Esper Ninjas I played may have been a version that appeared a week too early. With the champion being Dimir Excruciator and the runner-up being the newly rising Mono-White Momo this week, Esper Ninjas running into all sorts of Mono-White Momo is quite hard to play against. But if next week or later the meta shifts toward Dimir Excruciator or other midrange-focused decks, it will land right in the sweet spot of this version of Esper Ninjas.** Of course, it still has many areas to improve, but the core and framework of this version are worth continuing to refine. The unsatisfying result comes from my insufficient understanding of the meta and my lack of proficiency with Mono-White Momo, not from the deck itself.

### How to Handle Mono-White Momo's Turn-Two God-Draw on the Draw?

**If Mono-White is on the play: pretty much hopeless.** The only way is to bring {% mtglink "Intimidation Tactics" %} in the sideboard, and draw it together with {% mtglink "Pest Control" %} in your opening hand. Incidentally, this hand can mulligan down to two to find it.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Esper-Ninjas-Preliminary/2f56cdb25d08.webp)

- **Mono-White T1**: Play Plains + Drum
- **Esper T1**: Play the Orzhov Verge + Intimidation Tactics to take Sage of the Skies (if the opponent has 2 Sages, or topdecks a second, it's still hopeless, but we won't discuss that scenario).
- **Mono-White T2**: Play Plains, Momo + Warp out {% mtglink "Starfield Shepherd" %}, find {% mtglink "Nurturing Pixie" %}, tap the Angel + Drum for white, play Pixie to return the Angel.
- **Esper T2**: Cast Pest Control to blow up the Drum, Momo, and Pixie.
- **Mono-White T3**: If Mono-White doesn't have a third land, they may have to Warp the Angel to find a Plains, and the tempo will break a bit here. After that it depends on how both sides draw, and the outcome is still up in the air.

**If Esper is on the play: besides the opening hand above, you can also consider keeping a hand of Siren, Technique, Aang + 3 right lands, with the 7th card as you like, tolerating a mulligan to six**.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Esper-Ninjas-Preliminary/654fc443c6e2.webp)

- **Esper T1**: Play Dimir Verge + Siren
- **Mono-White T1**: Play Plains + Drum
- **Esper T2**: Play the Azorius shock land + after Siren attacks, Sneak Technique to make 3
- **Mono-White T2**: Play Plains, Momo + Sage of the Skies as a pair
- **Esper T3**: Play Passage, then pass.
- **Mono-White T3**: Play Cosmogrand Zenith + Seam Rip to kill one Token, Flurry to put +1/+1 counters on the whole team. Suppose two 3/4 lifelink flyers attack; flash in Aang to airbend away the Sage copy, and if the opponent misattacks with Momo too, we can block and kill Momo, and if not, we take 3.
- **Esper T4**: Play the fourth untapped land + replay Siren, then activate Aang's waterbend 8 (at this point we have 3 untapped lands, Siren, 2 Map tokens, and 2 1/1 ninja tokens); even without a land drop, you can already defend and then activate Waterbend 8. Decide based on hand and life total. But if you activate on T4 and Aang attacks and grows things, T5's attack can create at least 17 damage on board; if you choose to defend, it's only 13 damage on board.

The above doesn't account for the cards not yet mulliganed away, or the 3 cards drawn later; if you have 4 extra cards of preparation in hand, our survival rate against a Mono-White god-draw on the draw is still not small. You can all test it in practice and feel it out.

### Land Base Optimization

**Core goals:**
1. Don't affect the main theme of "**one-drop on turn one, Sneak on turn two, ninjutsu Kaito on turn three**"
2. Average life lost from playing lands within three turns should not exceed 4
3. Over six turns, the cumulative probability of drawing a Starting Town that must enter tapped must not exceed 10%

## Conclusion: Designing Your Own Deck Is Still Fun, but Watch the Meta Shifts

Although the result of the MIT#11 main event was far from ideal, organizing this article, and discussing my deck-building thinking back and forth with AI until it converged into a more systematic guide, was also a great takeaway. Although the AI's database is a bit outdated, and its technical professional advice is often outdated and incorrect, discussing the probability questions and article logic it's good at unexpectedly sparked extra enjoyment and yielded quantitative evidence.

If you, like me, want to take on big tournaments with a self-built deck, I hope the content of this article can help you a bit, and I hope readers can learn from the detours I took and the mistakes I made and grow further. Finally, as a tournament player, we must respect the meta and study and understand it in depth. The more thoroughly you prepare, the better your grasp during matches will be, and as you keep competing, your average win rate will get better and better.
