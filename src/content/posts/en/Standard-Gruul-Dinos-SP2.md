## Introduction

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/3902e1fcb1af.webp)

At the end of 2023, [The Lost Caverns of Ixalan](https://scryfall.com/sets/lci?as=grid&order=set) brought the Dinosaur tribe back to Standard, complete with dedicated one-mana accelerants and outsized bodies. A 6/6 on turn two and a 5/3 with ward 2 on turn three would have been an unignorable threat in any Standard of the past. Even with such a strong foundation, the deck has vanished from the tournament scene. In an era where deck power creeps up by the day, can a two-year-old archetype still keep up?


## Deck Evolution

When [The Lost Caverns of Ixalan](https://scryfall.com/sets/lci?as=grid&order=set) was released, I published a Dinosaur deck on this site; see [Wild Awakening: Standard Gruul Dinosaurs](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/Standard-Gruul-Dinosaurs/). As Standard rotated, the Dinosaurs from [March of the Machine](https://scryfall.com/sets/mom?as=grid&order=set) and [March of the Machine: The Aftermath](https://scryfall.com/sets/mat?as=grid&order=set) left us, but later sets still gave the tribe some reinforcements. **Core cast: Ixalli's Lorekeeper, Belligerent Yearling, Pugnacious Hammerskull, Hulking Raptor, Triumphant Chomp, Restless Ridgeline**

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/572de6109e3b.webp)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/0e691e0e7e58.webp)

[The Lost Caverns of Ixalan](https://scryfall.com/sets/lci?as=grid&order=set) gave us a solid set of cheap Dinosaurs: the one-mana accelerant [Ixalli's Lorekeeper](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/194/ixallis-lorekeeper), the one-mana removal spell [Triumphant Chomp](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/170/triumphant-chomp), the explosive two-drop [Belligerent Yearling](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/133/belligerent-yearling), the outsized three-drop [Pugnacious Hammerskull](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/208/pugnacious-hammerskull), and the four-drop [Hulking Raptor](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/191/hulking-raptor) with built-in ward and ramp. Together they form a brutal early curve. The red-green manland [Restless Ridgeline](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/283/restless-ridgeline) also gains the Dinosaur type once animated, so it benefits from the tribal bonuses.

**Bloomburrow: Hunter's Talent**

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/3a473472df1c.webp)

The critter set [Bloomburrow](https://scryfall.com/sets/blb?as=grid&order=set) has no buzzkill Dinosaurs, but we still got an excellent [Hunter's Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/179/hunters-talent). It bites an opposing creature when it enters, gives a target attacking creature +1/+0 and trample on the second combat phase, and draws you a card at end step if you control a creature with power 4 or greater. It covers every need of the green-based creature aggro plan.

**Foundations: Llanowar Elves, Ghalta, Primal Hunger**

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/68b66b9d7520.webp)

The core set [Foundations](https://scryfall.com/sets/fdn?as=grid&order=set) reprinted the tried-and-true [Llanowar Elves](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/227/llanowar-elves), giving the Standard Dinosaur tribe a very fortunate eight one-mana accelerants. Also reprinted was the elder Dinosaur [Ghalta, Primal Hunger](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/222/ghalta-primal-hunger). Its cost reduction based on total power wasn't great in the old Ixalan environment, where bodies were comparatively puny. Now that Dinosaur bodies have jumped up three tiers, a couple of creatures on the battlefield is enough to slam it down with ease.

**Aetherdrift: Regal Imperiosaur, Agonasaur Rex**

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/feb1697f2270.webp)

The racing set [Aetherdrift](https://scryfall.com/sets/dft?as=grid&order=set) unexpectedly patched in two powerful Dinosaurs with little connection to the set's theme: the three-mana Dinosaur lord [Regal Imperiosaur](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/177/regal-imperiosaur) and the five-mana 8/8 trampler [Agonasaur Rex](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/151/agonasaur-rex). [Regal Imperiosaur](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/177/regal-imperiosaur) fills out the three-drop slot, letting you apply pressure more reliably starting on turn two. [Agonasaur Rex](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/151/agonasaur-rex) looks a bit slow on its own, but it has a nice cycling trigger: put two +1/+1 counters on up to one target creature or Vehicle, and that permanent gains trample and indestructible until end of turn. That covers trample, growth, and protection from removal all at once.

**Avatar: The Last Airbender: Ba Sing Se, Bitter Work, Secret Tunnel, Origin of Metalbending**

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/9cf7e85392f5.webp)

Red-green creature aggro has always suffered from running out of gas, and Dinosaurs are no exception. Especially against black or white control decks packed with spot removal and sweepers, a few removal spells or a sweeper often leave you with nothing but lands and mana creatures. Even with the red-green manlands, that is usually a drop in the bucket, since one removal spell takes them out. However, the earthbending of [Avatar: The Last Airbender](https://scryfall.com/sets/tla?as=grid&order=set) looks like it can plug this hole. The land [Ba Sing Se](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/266/ba-sing-se) is a hard-to-answer earthbending engine, endlessly producing land creatures to pressure control opponents. Besides carrying a built-in earthbend 4, [Bitter Work](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/210/bitter-work) fits the theme well, since attacking with power 4 or greater creatures draws cards. Next is the unblockable-granting [Secret Tunnel](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/278/secret-tunnel): besides earthbending for steady damage, it makes two Dinosaurs unblockable, which adds up to a lot of damage. Finally, [Origin of Metalbending](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/187/origin-of-metalbending) not only handles the artifact enchantments that are all the rage right now, but can also put counters on a creature to grant indestructible, a big boon for a Dinosaur deck full of behemoths.

## Match Reports

{%youtube RsQkH0Orf1Q %}

The usual MTGA playtest video is above. What's different this time is that a Kastolo MITQ happened to come up, and I had all the cards for this deck on hand, so I built it and tested it in a real tournament.

**26-01-10 Kastolo MITQ (21 players) Quick Report**

**Round 1 vs Simic Pot Snakes 2-0**

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/0bf0c831268d.webp)

Simic Pot Snakes, which excels at snowballing, is a tough matchup for ordinary creature decks, but the Dinosaurs still land faster. With [Triumphant Chomp](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/170/triumphant-chomp) killing something for one mana to disrupt the opponent's setup, and [Hunter's Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/179/hunters-talent) forcing damage through with trample on the second combat phase, it played out with surprisingly little pressure. Remember to choose your opening hand carefully, since having no removal is dangerous, so mulligan aggressively.

*Sideout: 2 [Origin of Metalbending](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/187/origin-of-metalbending), 1 [Bitter Work](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/210/bitter-work), 1 [Ghalta](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/222/ghalta-primal-hunger)*
*Sidein: 2 [Iroh's Demonstration](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/141/irohs-demonstration), 2 [Nibelheim Aflame](https://scryfall.com/card/fin/146/nibelheim-aflame)*

**Round 2 vs Sultai Control 1-2**

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/654131b9dadd.webp)

Black-based control is a bad matchup for Dinosaurs. The opponent ran a discard-and-removal-heavy build with [The Rise of Sozin // Fire Lord Sozin](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/117/the-rise-of-sozin-fire-lord-sozin) and [The End](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/87/the-end). Its answers have long reach, [Nowhere to Run](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/111/nowhere-to-run) cuts through ward and hexproof, and in the late game there is [Morlun, Devourer of Spiders](https://scryfall.com/card/spm/59/morlun-devourer-of-spiders), which gains a big chunk of life on entering. It was awful to play against. In game 1, I got controlled by removal and sweepers and conceded early. In the sideboarded games, I switched plans and made lands the main attackers, using manlands plus indestructible tricks to grind with them. I stole one game, but ultimately lost 1-2.

*Sideout: 4 [Chomp](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/170/triumphant-chomp), 2 [Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/179/hunters-talent), 2 [Lorekeeper](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/194/ixallis-lorekeeper), 1 [Ghalta](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/222/ghalta-primal-hunger)*
*Sidein: 4 [Snakeskin Veil](https://scryfall.com/card/tdm/159/snakeskin-veil), 2 [Intrepid Paleontologist](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/193/intrepid-paleontologist), 2 [Origin of Metalbending](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/187/origin-of-metalbending), 1 [Vivien Reid](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/234/vivien-reid)*

**Round 3 vs Sultai Midrange 2-1**

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/56fb1d4cc3e2.webp)

A creature-based Sultai midrange deck, built from the Sultai colors' long-reach removal plus solid resource and disruption creatures. In game 1, I forced a win by running the opponent over with the [Agonasaur Rex](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/151/agonasaur-rex) + [Ghalta](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/222/ghalta-primal-hunger) combo. In the first sideboarded game, the opponent ramped with [Kellan, Inquisitive Prodigy // Tail the Suspect](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/212/kellan-inquisitive-prodigy-tail-the-suspect), got flying and trample, and sacrificed its own artifact token to draw cards. On my side, I drew a few extra mana sources, my Dinosaurs all got removed, and I never found a manland. By the time I drew Dinosaurs, my life total was already within flyer lethal range, and the decline was irreversible. In the last game, [Secret Tunnel](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/278/secret-tunnel) gave [Agonasaur Rex](https://scryfall.com/card/dft/151/agonasaur-rex) unblockable, passing straight through the creature defenses to deal a crushing blow and win.

*Sideout: 1 [Lorekeeper](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/194/ixallis-lorekeeper)*
*Sidein: 1 [Nibelheim Aflame](https://scryfall.com/card/fin/146/nibelheim-aflame)*

**Round 4 vs Simic Pot Snakes 2-0**

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/28d6bc925ab6.webp)

The match went much like the first one. The difference was that in the sideboarded game, [Iroh's Demonstration](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/141/irohs-demonstration) caught the opponent off guard, sweeping away two [Mockingbird](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/61/mockingbird)s that were copying [Llanowar Elves](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/227/llanowar-elves) in a one-sided fashion. Then, with [Hunter's Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/179/hunters-talent) backing it up, the **Imperiosaur Triple Star** unleashed the **Jet Yearling Attack**, illustrated below:

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/8b5b562be0f9.webp)

Two big ones and a small one swung in for a total of 25 damage, 18 of it trample damage. After doing the math, the opponent conceded.

*Sideout: 2 [Origin of Metalbending](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/187/origin-of-metalbending), 1 [Bitter Work](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/210/bitter-work), 1 [Ghalta](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/222/ghalta-primal-hunger)*
*Sidein: 2 [Iroh's Demonstration](https://scryfall.com/card/tla/141/irohs-demonstration), 2 [Nibelheim Aflame](https://scryfall.com/card/fin/146/nibelheim-aflame)*

**Round 5 0-0 Intentional Draw**

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/d393f3d8bd01.webp)

After four rounds my record was 3-1-0. The opponent also had three wins, so we took the intentional draw and boarded the Despicable Train toward Top 8.

**Top 8 vs Sultai Control 0-2**

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/de6d0d94d738.webp)

The opponent was the Sultai Control player from the Swiss rounds. Since my countermeasures had already been figured out, I had no chance at all in the rematch. In the end I was finished off by [Koma, World-Eater](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/121/koma-world-eater) and was swept 0-2 without any suspense.


## Conclusion

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Gruul-Dinos-SP2/87efe4221f69.webp)

With new sets coming out at an accelerated pace in recent years, many decks get buried under the flood of new cards after just a few plays, and vanish completely from tournament and online metagame lists. It feels like they've fallen behind the times... but that's not actually true. With a little thought and some adjustments, they can still have the potential to beat up the latest tech.
