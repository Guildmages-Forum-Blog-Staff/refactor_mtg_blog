## Before We Start

After the June 30, 2025 B&R announcement, the Standard environment saw its largest set of bans since 2020. Dimir midrange, left completely untouched, leapt from being suppressed by red aggro in the previous season to the hot favorite of the tournament scene... Uh, maybe that's a bit overheated?

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Dimir-Midrange-Overview/ac5117b7be63.webp)
> MTGGoldfish confirmed date: July 5, 2025

If we only look at the week of June 30 to July 6, it seems to be the biggest blight in this Standard environment! But this deck did not enjoy such a scene from the very start. With Standard about to rotate, readers may not be very interested in this topic, but I still want to try to organize the thread of this process, as a summary of my year and a half of journey.

While writing, I often thought of the [player interviews](/en/categories/Interview/) that [faintmama](/en/authors/faintmama/) and I published on the Forum. We focused on players who have already proven themselves, but what about the times before they were "seen", and the many more players who are still working hard? Do those moments have anything worth looking at? How do we view and feel about "winning" and "losing"?

This article is dedicated to the [Sheoldred, the Apocalypse](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/107/sheoldred-the-apocalypse) in my deck. Thank you for accompanying me these two years, and congratulations on graduating XD ~~but what do I do when my draws no longer gain life~~

## Why Did I Choose Dimir Midrange?

It wasn't because this deck is the strongest, though of course that was a consideration at the very beginning. A deck's strength changes as new sets keep releasing, but the core way of playing does not.

Dimir midrange is a deck with lots of early interaction and mid-game threats, building a unique tempo-counterattack deck through the three components of blue-black: draw, counter, kill. The deck has no super bomb that decides the game in one blow, and no combo that nearly wins the game once assembled; what leads you to victory is that you constantly make subtle adjustments to your main deck and sideboard according to the current meta. You could put it this way: to play Dimir midrange well, you must keep a sharp instinct for the meta at all times, and use understanding and game plans to generate card advantage and then win.

A deck like this is like an amoeba: change a few cards, or even change none but approach playing it with a new idea, and you can have a brand-new game experience. And the Dimir midrange mirror may also be one of the hardest mirrors in the environment. Play or draw, the choice of opening hand, how to disrupt the opponent's proactive plan, whether we can follow the disruption with a threat to take back tempo... there are so many variables that the mirror often can't give a clear game plan, and improvising and thinking your way to victory is the biggest key.

What attracts me most about playing Dimir midrange is that the more time you invest, the more you can bring out unique tactics gained through practice in certain situations, and every adjustment feels like an evolution. You can always do "better".

## 2024 to 2025: My Dimir Midrange Journey

Looking back, in this year and a half I have written 23 posts on my [personal fan page](https://www.facebook.com/Pignosemtgworld) with Dimir midrange as the theme, some of which were also organized into articles on the Forum. To write this retrospective, I picked them up again and relived the feelings I had when writing them. And after the dregs of these emotions settled, some crystals with a faint glow have been repeatedly sending through my mind the thoughts they want to tell.

Behind the glittering major tournaments, how many such crystals in the dark are calling out, struggling, and fading? Allow me to magnify my own sentimentality a little and try to speak my own "voice of the defeated".

#### [20240113 Magic Star MITQ](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/1BBs6oUjoM/)

In the 2023 season I used UW Soldiers, a tempo deck that I have always liked and habitually used. During the Forum's early days I also wrote this piece, [The Evolution and Outlook of Standard UW Soldiers](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/Standard-UW-Solider/), to briefly describe my own understanding. However, at the end of 2023, after witnessing black-based decks with a double dose of Sheoldred, the overwhelming force of playing on turns two, three, and four in a row and making Magic look so easy, plus the weakness of the Soldier tribe not getting enough support after LCI, I decided to switch to Dimir midrange for the following Standard seasons.

Although I didn't play this MITQ very well, I came to appreciate more deeply the power of [Gix, Yawgmoth Praetor](https://scryfall.com/card/tdc/181/gix-yawgmoth-praetor) and of Sheoldred.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Dimir-Midrange-Overview/7af814cd9868.webp)

#### [20240127 ALG MITQ - 豬鼻子's Standard Dimir Midrange Analysis and Tournament Report (Qualified for MIT#6)](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/Standard-Dimir-Midrange/)

Fortunately, I earned my MIT#6 qualification without playing too many rounds, and I therefore confirmed the outstanding disruptive strength of [Tishana's Tidebinder](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/81/tishanas-tidebinder) in the Standard environment. For the following seasons, Tidebinder was always an important member I would not take out of my 75 cards.

This period was basically a process of repeated testing and constantly cutting the deck's original core, [Gix, Yawgmoth Praetor](https://scryfall.com/card/tdc/181/gix-yawgmoth-praetor), down to zero copies. With the rise of Convoke decks, the meta made me more and more anxious the more I played, and my win rate was basically under forty percent. By early May, the original Dimir midrange build, with Convoke blocking the road ahead and control/Ramp evolving behind us to also bring [Tishana's Tidebinder](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/81/tishanas-tidebinder) as anti-counterspell tech, fell into a dilemma, caught between advancing and retreating and nearly ready to give up and switch to playing Convoke...

#### [20240529 豬鼻子's MIT#6 Preparation and Tournament Report: Sultai Midrange](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/MIT-6-Review/)

In the end it still wasn't tuned well. Three colors was a nice change, but it still didn't respect the meta's Convoke enough, and I was thoroughly punished in the win-and-in-for-top-eight match. If I fast-forward to now, a year later, I would say that one main, two sideboard [The Filigree Sylex](https://scryfall.com/card/one/227/the-filigree-sylex) was necessary. I did see similar builds back then, but I let it pass without studying the usage scenarios in depth.

#### [20240914 桌遊侍 Standard Store Championship](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/1C3LmpEXsZ/)

Two weeks before the release of [DSK](https://scryfall.com/sets/dsk), I still didn't know that Dimir midrange was about to receive the biggest boost of its Standard life. Although I was beaten silly by the various small-animal decks of [BLB](https://scryfall.com/sets/blb), the gain was that in this tournament I established the versatile flexibility of [Three Steps Ahead](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/75/three-steps-ahead), and after that it rarely left my 75 (though it was always in the sideboard; nothing to be done, the environment kept getting faster).

#### [20241030 MTGA Mythic](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/16auybDBWP/)

[DSK](https://scryfall.com/sets/dsk) arrives in force!!!

Vraska's? [Unctus, Grand Metatect](https://scryfall.com/card/one/75/unctus-grand-metatect)? Gone are those days. If you ask, the answer is to play [the blue cat (Enduring Curiosity)](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/51/enduring-curiosity)!
In this version of the build, because I always topdecked [Spyglass Siren](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/78/spyglass-siren) and it got worse the more I used it, I was so annoyed that I just pulled it out, and the new stars [Floodpits Drowner](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/59/floodpits-drowner) and [Kaito, Bane of Nightmares](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/220/kaito-bane-of-nightmares) were still in the testing phase, so I politely played only 2 copies of each. I later found out that was a big mistake: before the rise of red aggro, they were extremely important deck cores starting at 3 copies each.

Later, Kaito also proved how perverse a planeswalker that comes down with ninjutsu on turn three, is super hard to kill, and can keep attacking really is. Because of this, Siren was also filled back up to 4 copies, with the aim of spreading out as many decoys as possible on turns one and two so Kaito can come down smoothly. As soon as a single [Enduring Curiosity](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/51/enduring-curiosity) resolves, it turns into Doraemon and grants all my wishes, not to mention having two or more on the battlefield at once! Floodpits Drowner not only plays the extremely important role of defender in later matchups against red aggro, its excellent performance of removing and attacking at once makes it an important blocker for Dimir midrange on the draw to stop the opponent's turn-three ninjutsu! After this, the story of the DSK three musketeers was about to be written with great fanfare, and this was only the prelude.

#### [20241103 CM APAC Standard](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/1Cw5ABWUFg/)

Takeaway this time: [Nowhere to Run](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/111/nowhere-to-run) is better removal than [Cut Down](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/89/cut-down), and I also used 2 main and 1 sideboard in my deck. However, what I didn't make clear here is that this was an environment dominated by aggro decks like Boros Mice and Gruul Prowess that carried tons of protection spells, with [Sheltered by Ghosts](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/30/sheltered-by-ghosts), [Innkeeper's Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/180/innkeepers-talent), and [Overprotect](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/185/overprotect) used in large numbers. Otherwise, one-mana Cut Down is far more efficient than two-mana Nowhere to Run.

#### [20250223 卡士多 MITQ](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/1AheLVYfjF/)

## Reflections on Winning and Losing

![56](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Dimir-Midrange-Overview/8e4d2e476d8e.webp)

On Day 1 of the Spotlight, at the moment when it was already certain I would not make Day 2, I stood on the second floor of the venue, about to take the escalator down to the first floor, from a high vantage point looking at the bustling crowd taking part in the Spotlight. Besides the 2,250 players in the main event, there were three times or more other players, their friends and family, judges, vendors, and staff of the organizers who had come to the venue. A tournament has only one champion, and even counting the top eight, the people who can be called "winners" are truly few. Competitive events are a zero-sum game, and I know that very well.

So what were those many, hundreds of times more people doing here?

In fact I already knew the answer by heart: competition is not the only way to enjoy this game. Entering big tournaments, exchanging with players from all over, finding rare treasures at the venue, complaining together with your friends and then fighting on again the next day... Everyone is different, and everyone who comes to the venue has their own goals. Even if one of them goes unfulfilled, we can draw energy and joy from other directions. Amazingly, the switch between them is so natural. It's a bit of a cliche, but the key is still what our "heart" is like in the way we see the world.

I have been obsessed, and I have also had sudden enlightenment; several times I felt bitter at coming home defeated, and I was also angry at games that gave me no chance of winning, so angry that I left the venue roaring loudly... These feelings were all real, but emotions pass and growth accumulates. When you stop now and then to look around, you find a group of friends supporting one another and spurring each other on to climb out of the valley of disappointment; I found that I could also be honestly happy for others' victories, and feel immensely fulfilled by someone's "god-tier play".

How fortunate we are not to live in an age of samurai duels with real swords or bloody storms in the martial world; our failures do not need to cost precious lives. We fail again and again, yet also grow stronger again and again. I have written 23 records, but the games I have lost and won are far more than that number, and the me writing this retrospective now has more refined skill and understanding than the me at every cross-section in time back then, and I will keep getting stronger and will not stay at this stage. Having finished this article, I will keep moving forward again.

## Conclusion

The reason a game is fun is that it is only a game. It is not a promotion at work, not others' recognition, not some title for winning... those illusory things that you cannot control and that make you anxious about gains and losses. We must always play this game for ourselves, to enrich our own deep experience, and then, the luckiest part is that there is a group of people who play together with you!

Interaction between people is an endless treasure, so let's play Magic: The Gathering together! May you who have read this far also find such a deck to accompany you through a wonderful journey.
