## Before We Start

Among Standard's current one-mana black removal spells, those whose cost does not include X and whose range exceeds -1/-1 are only two: [Cut Down](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/89/cut-down) and [Disfigure](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/91/disfigure). The former has long been the benchmark for one-mana black removal in Standard, while the latter is a completely different story, and many people may not even remember it is in the Standard card pool. Card prices reflect this perfectly. Going by MTGGoldfish prices (USD) at the time of writing, [Cut Down](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/89/cut-down) is $3.5, which is a ceiling-level price for an uncommon one-for-one removal spell; by comparison [Disfigure](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/91/disfigure) is only $0.15, which is negligible.

However, these two cards have been in Standard for about two years, going through one Standard rotation in between, and the threats in the environment have long been reshuffled. Is [Cut Down](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/89/cut-down) really still the superior option to [Disfigure](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/91/disfigure)?

## The Difference in Range Between Cut Down and Disfigure

The two cards are actually not far apart in range. Let's set aside the cases where both kill or both fail to kill, and look at which specific threats only one of the two removal spells can handle.

[Cut Down](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/89/cut-down): Its main advantage is that it can handle 2/3 creatures, and a few high-level threats at 1/3 and 1/4 are also left to it.

[Disfigure](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/91/disfigure): No matter how high the power, anything with toughness 2 or less dies to it. If we want to find the difference from Cut Down, it can handle creatures starting from 4/2 or 5/1.

Standard's two-mana black removal is currently very powerful, nearly kill-spell level. With that as the premise, investing in a one-mana removal spell means this removal is positioned as very tempo-oriented, requiring that you can interact with your opponent's threats early enough.

The choice of removal is certainly tied to the threats in the environment, so next we want to clarify which prominent threats in the current environment make a real difference in choosing Cut Down versus Disfigure.

Of course, here we first rule out the premise that your own deck has extra synergy. Suppose your deck's main configuration includes [Jace, the Perfected Mind](https://scryfall.com/card/one/57/jace-the-perfected-mind): then Cut Down's range ceiling is undoubtedly higher than Disfigure's, the two removal spells are not at the same level, and there is no need to hesitate.

## Why Was Cut Down Strong Back Then?

First, let's analyze why, until the BLB Standard rotation, everyone used Cut Down and gave Disfigure no consideration at all. In fact, a single image says it all:

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Structure-Evolution/8a670a3bb86c.webp)

When MID through SNC were still in Standard, the Esper-based decks led by [Raffine, Scheming Seer](https://scryfall.com/card/snc/213/raffine-scheming-seer) were always the absolute mainstream of the environment. Raffine is a super powerful creature with good tempo and MAX threat level that had to be removed right away, and because of ward 1, two-mana removal might not even come in time, so Cut Down became the only choice for one-mana black removal at the time.

[Dennick, Pious Apprentice](https://scryfall.com/card/mid/217/dennick-pious-apprentice-dennick-pious-apparition) was also a regular in the environment back then, often showing up alongside Raffine. It too is something Cut Down can remove but Disfigure cannot, though this was not the main selection pressure for one-mana removal. After all, once you casually remove it, it just disturbs back onto the battlefield and starts generating cards, so you would deal with it in a more roundabout way depending on timing anyway. With a lower need for tempo, the pressure is also lighter.

In summary, one of the main defining cards of the pre-rotation environment was [Raffine](https://scryfall.com/card/snc/213/raffine-scheming-seer), and no other threat of the same level could be handled only by Disfigure, so Cut Down really was the optimal choice at the time.

Once Monastery Swiftspear triggers prowess even once, it goes completely beyond Disfigure's range, but as long as the spell that triggered prowess is not a pump spell cast on Monastery Swiftspear itself, it is still within Cut Down's range. Overall, you can see that in the current Standard environment, Cut Down has no very clear high-payoff targets, though as a one-mana removal spell it really is good enough.

### Disfigure's Advantage

Since Cut Down's advantages over Disfigure all show up in some seemingly trivial, scattered situations, does Disfigure have any advantage that shows major gains in the environment?

In fact, the situations where Disfigure has the advantage can roughly all be classified under the same kind of opponent, one with a presence that is hard to ignore in the current environment: the red-based aggro decks built around valiant and led by [Heartfire Hero](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/138/heartfire-hero).

![Leyline of Resonance](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Structure-Evolution/33a6b236acfb.webp)

[Heartfire Hero](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/138/heartfire-hero) is undoubtedly the benchmark of red one-mana threats right now. Even the more traditional red aggro decks that emphasize burn like [Shock](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/144/shock) and [Lightning Strike](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/137/lightning-strike) include a certain number of valiant support pieces to make use of Heartfire Hero, to say nothing of the extreme decks using [Leyline of Resonance](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/143/leyline-of-resonance). So why does Disfigure perform better against red-based aggro decks?

![RDW creatures](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Structure-Evolution/c14abd385df3.webp)

Start with the root of all evil, [Heartfire Hero](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/138/heartfire-hero). Every kind of red-based aggro deck has it, and when it dies it deals damage equal to its power, which alone already widens the gap between Disfigure and Cut Down. When you use Disfigure to remove Heartfire Hero, you can gain a 1 to 2 point life swing, and against an aggro deck that has to count its life total carefully, this may be the difference between life and death. In an extreme deck of Leyline of Resonance + [Callous Sell-Sword](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/221/callous-sell-sword-burn-together) throwing with its Adventure, a one-mana [[雜音惡獸]](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/138/heartfire-hero) with a death ability nearly equivalent to Heartfire Hero's is added, and Disfigure has even richer situations to shine in.

Next is the strongest two-drop curve play right now, [Slickshot Show-Off](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/146/slickshot-show-off). Its pump ability lets it easily exceed Cut Down's range, and since these trigger sources are all instant-speed spells, it is not easy to catch it precisely at a weak moment, but because Slickshot Show-Off's ability only boosts power, Disfigure is instead the more reliable removal choice.

>Perhaps some red Leyline builds that pursue extreme opening hands have given up on two-drop creatures, but that is only the personal choice of some players. Overall, Slickshot Show-Off still sees a certain amount of play, to say nothing of the traditional aggro decks that are not Leyline-based, which certainly feature it.
>
![RDW pump spells](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Structure-Evolution/5290551709a6.webp)

In the prowess-style red aggro decks of the past, the pump spell might be just a [Monstrous Rage](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/142/monstrous-rage) and that was about it, with the rest of the spells still mainly burn. After entering the Heartfire Hero era, valiant support spells like [Might of the Meek](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/144/might-of-the-meek) and [Rabid Gnaw](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/147/rabid-gnaw) gradually entered builds, and now Leyline pushes the burst of the valiant archetype to the limit, with builds already seen replacing traditional burn with [Turn Inside Out](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/160/turn-inside-out) and [Felonious Rage](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/125/felonious-rage), with a game plan of the most extreme One-Punch Man style burst.

Looking closely at these pump spells, you will find that apart from [Monstrous Rage](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/142/monstrous-rage), the other spells only boost power and not toughness, just like Slickshot Show-Off's ability. So in situations where you have the ability to respond, Cut Down is very likely to have its target become illegal because the opponent responds with a pump spell, while Disfigure's risk is much smaller.

Also, these pump spells are the reason the difference was somewhat diluted when I mentioned [Monastery Swiftspear](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/144/monastery-swiftspear) above. As long as the opponent's response is a pump spell that enlarges Swiftspear, neither Cut Down nor Disfigure can kill it, though Disfigure can at least reduce some damage. Of course, when dealing with Swiftspear, Cut Down is still stronger, as it can at least restrict the type or target of their spell, instead of letting any spell they toss out dodge the removal.

### Disfigure's Other Small Perks

Disfigure has these small perks relative to Cut Down, but they are truly better-than-nothing added value and do not form a basis for choosing removal.

- Combining multiple copies to hard-kill a big creature
Against something like a 3/3 [Glissa Sunslayer](https://scryfall.com/card/one/202/glissa-sunslayer), Cut Down is simply useless, whereas you can use two Disfigures to kill it. Although it is card disadvantage, it beats a dead card.

- Using it as a combat trick
When two 4/3 [Hostile Investigator](https://scryfall.com/card/big/10/hostile-investigator)s fight, casting a Disfigure on the other one lets you trade one-sidedly. Of course, for non-aggro Standard decks, this kind of combat-trick use is very inefficient, but it still beats a dead card.

- Buying time against the Forge
As time accumulates, the tokens produced by [Urabrask's Forge](https://scryfall.com/card/one/153/urabrasks-forge) will exceed Cut Down's range, but are always within Disfigure's range, and none of the red pump spells listed above can pump high enough to dodge Disfigure. But after all, the root of the problem, the Forge, still exists. All you can say is that surviving one more turn is one more turn, and if you cannot remove the Forge, you are still on a dead-end road.

## Conclusion

[Cut Down](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/89/cut-down) and [Disfigure](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/91/disfigure) differ little against most kinds of decks today, but against red-based aggro decks of the [Heartfire Hero](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/138/heartfire-hero) archetype, Disfigure shows a huge advantage, and this hypothetical enemy happens to be the number one target in the current environment.
