## What Is Standard Rotation?

Standard is a limited-card-pool format, and rotation is the process of making cards from certain older sets unusable in Standard after a fixed period, keeping Standard varied and lively. Since the official article [Revitalizing Standard](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/revitalizing-standard) was published on May 7, 2023, Standard's lifespan has been extended from the original 2 years to the 3 years we know today.

Starting from the Edge of Eternities (EOE) prerelease on July 25, 2025, we will also welcome the second rotation since Standard was extended to 3 years. Cards from the following 5 sets will no longer be usable in Standard (of course, they can still be used in eternal formats outside Standard, such as Pioneer, Modern, Legacy, or above):

1. [Dominaria United (DMU)](https://scryfall.com/sets/dmu)
2. [The Brothers' War (BRO)](https://scryfall.com/sets/bro)
3. [Phyrexia: All Will Be One (ONE)](https://scryfall.com/sets/one)
4. [March of the Machine (MOM)](https://scryfall.com/sets/mom)
5. [March of the Machine: The Aftermath (MAT)](https://scryfall.com/sets/mat)

## Banned Cards in Standard

![Banned cards](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Influence-After-EOE-Release/da031a4a741d.webp)

In the [June 30, 2025 Banned and Restricted announcement](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/banned-and-restricted-june-30-2025), the 7 cards pictured above were banned in Standard. Bans usually happen when one or a few cards, and the decks built around them, take up too large a share of a given format and make the format unhealthy, squeezing out the survival space of other decks that have potential but a power-level gap. At such times, the official side uses routine or even emergency ban announcements to ban the cards causing the situation.

We specifically mention Standard bans because these 7 banned cards can also be seen as the official side believing that their continued presence in Standard would harm the overall diversity of the environment, so they were rotated out early.

> If you count the online version, MTG Arena, in its BO1 format (Best of One, a format without sideboarding in which the first player to win one game wins), {% mtglink "Leyline of Resonance" %} was also banned. But this is different from the paper Standard format.

## Possible Effects of the EOE Standard Rotation

> This section is mainly written for players who already have some understanding of pre-rotation Standard and have invested in it for a while. For new players who started around [FIN](https://scryfall.com/sets/fin), I suggest simply waiting until [EOE](https://scryfall.com/sets/eoe) is released, then looking at decks on MTGGoldfish and choosing one you are interested in and can afford.

#### But EOE reprints the 5 shock lands!

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Influence-After-EOE-Release/cc4992408b8c.webp)
<p class="text-center"><a class="small">5 shock lands, lands that can enter by paying 2 life (like getting hit by a shock) to enter untapped and produce two colors of mana</a></p>

I have to say that the color pairs that got strong fixing lands printed first after rotation will be the front-runners that win the new Standard at the starting line. Like a buff in an online game, these five color pairs are the Tier 1 that gets the smooth mana development buff. The other five color pairs that haven't been printed yet (perhaps later sets will print them, who knows) will be suppressed to a degree, and unless a groundbreaking card or theme is developed, the disadvantage of lagging behind in fixing will be very noticeable in deck building.

### 2. Black-Based Midrange May Be Weakened

#### The quality one- and two-mana removal is gone, especially Cut Down

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Influence-After-EOE-Release/c58e1221f002.webp)

Even with the restriction that it can't kill artifact creatures, {% mtglink "Go for the Throat" %} is still the two-mana removal with the widest reach in Standard, and {% mtglink "Anoint with Affliction" %} is the most reliable sideboard removal against specific decks, and it wouldn't even be strange to use them together in the main deck. After rotation, although the environment still has two-mana kill spells with restrictions or side effects such as {% mtglink "Shoot the Sheriff" %}, {% mtglink "Bitter Triumph" %}, and {% mtglink "Long Goodbye" %}, if you don't want side effects, BLB's {% mtglink "Feed the Cycle" %} can also be a backup plan.

However, compared with the gap among two-mana removal, the departure of {% mtglink "Cut Down" %} will deal a heavy blow to black-based decks. In both breadth of removal and tempo, the one-mana Cut Down could hold off the toughest first three turns for black-based decks, which the existing one-mana instant removal {% mtglink "Stab" %} cannot match at all, and this shortcoming shows even more against must-kill creatures like {% mtglink "Voice of Victory" %}, {% mtglink "Fear of Missing Out" %}, or {% mtglink "Vivi Ornitier" %}.

#### The black four-drop curve changes hands

In addition, the four-mana {% mtglink "Sheoldred, the Apocalypse" %} and {% mtglink "Archfiend of the Dross" %} have basically monopolized the four-drop slot in black decks, and this will also change with rotation. Now is the time for a comeback!

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Influence-After-EOE-Release/6cedaeb6124a.webp)

The biggest impact above is still the departure of Cut Down. Whether black-based midrange decks can maintain high competitiveness and quick adaptability in the post-rotation Standard will depend on whether a one-mana removal as strong as Cut Down gets printed in the future.

### 3. The Omniscience Combo Will Fall Silent for the Time Being

After the bans, the Omniscience combo is also losing quite a few key cards to the coming rotation, and it will likely be quiet for a while, or you could say {% mtglink "Long Goodbye" %} to it. (Unless [EOE](https://scryfall.com/sets/eoe) can bring it a little hope, but the new dig effect, {% mtglink "Rescue Skiff" %}, costs six.)

#### Scrollshift

{% mtgpick "mom" 34 %}

{% mtglink "Temporary Lockdown" %}, often blinked at instant speed with itself, repeatedly clears the board while also refreshing the ETB abilities of low-cost permanents.

Its interaction with the [DSK Overlords](https://scryfall.com/search?as=grid&order=name&q=Overlord+oracle%3AImpending+%28game%3Apaper%29+set%3Adsk) also gives this card a special status that can't be ignored. As long as the deck has a certain number of permanents suited to refreshing ETB abilities, the small add-on system of {% mtglink "Scrollshift" %} + [Overlord](https://scryfall.com/search?as=grid&order=name&q=Overlord+oracle%3AImpending+%28game%3Apaper%29+set%3Adsk) alone can provide enough winning power.

The deck that takes this line of thinking furthest is the {% mtglink "Omniscience" %} Combo. If this deck were purely a combo deck, it would have many fragile points to target. That it got as far as needing a ban ({% mtglink "Abuelo's Awakening" %}) owes a great deal to the Plan B mode of {% mtglink "Scrollshift" %} + [Overlord](https://scryfall.com/search?as=grid&order=name&q=Overlord+oracle%3AImpending+%28game%3Apaper%29+set%3Adsk).
(Of course, for the Omniscience deck, what is strong is less the interaction with the [Overlords](https://scryfall.com/search?as=grid&order=name&q=Overlord+oracle%3AImpending+%28game%3Apaper%29+set%3Adsk) than the interaction with the whole deck.)

> By the way, regarding the interaction with impending Overlords, besides the aforementioned {% mtglink "Zur, Eternal Schemer" %}, {% mtglink "Render Inert" %} is also rotating out, so using sneaky tricks to quickly turn an impending Overlord on the battlefield into a creature will be much harder.

In fact {% mtglink "Scrollshift" %} has quite a wide range of uses and isn't purely an {% mtglink "Omniscience" %}-specific weapon, but the following two cards rotating out together are important support cards inseparable from the {% mtglink "Omniscience" %} Combo deck.

#### Repair and Recharge & Ephara's Dispersal

![Omniscience](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Influence-After-EOE-Release/2ce992b2c6c6.webp)

{% mtglink "Repair and Recharge" %} was the most efficient replacement after {% mtglink "Abuelo's Awakening" %} was banned. With it gone too, digging for {% mtglink "Omniscience" %} becomes harder still. The remaining graveyard-digging spells either cost more or have messier color requirements. Although level 3 of {% mtglink "Builder's Talent" %} is also a 5-mana dig, the leveling process requires extra mana, and overall operating efficiency is far below {% mtglink "Repair and Recharge" %}.

{% mtglink "Ephara's Dispersal" %} is only a soft-removal bounce effect, but for a combo deck, being able to hold off one attack for 1 mana while also getting a surveil 2 to speed up finding combo pieces is mostly far better than a 2-mana hard kill. And with {% mtglink "Omniscience" %} on the battlefield, it can also bounce your own creatures with ETB abilities as a link in the chain.

In addition, {% mtglink "Fallaji Archaeologist" %} and {% mtglink "Oracle of Tragedy" %} are also among the pieces rotating out this time, but these were only at the level of picks in some builds and are not very necessary for the Omniscience deck.

### 4. Certain Themes Transforming or Even Disappearing

#### Land Theme

![Lands](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Influence-After-EOE-Release/eea767f50346.webp)

Although {% mtglink "Up the Beanstalk" %} was banned, that only pulled this deck from a state of excess resources to a relatively fair one. But with the departure of the core card {% mtglink "Zur, Eternal Schemer" %}, transforming into a DSK Overlords-style ramp deck should be a temporary option, and with FIN adding {% mtglink "Yuna, Hope of Spira" %} and a whole group of Saga creatures, enchantment-based Ramp decks still have great prospects.

#### Boros Convoke

![Convoke](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Influence-After-EOE-Release/b8b61897bf2f.webp)

The key cards of Boros Convoke, {% mtglink "Gleeful Demolition" %} and {% mtglink "Knight-Errant of Eos" %}, will both rotate together. With the double blow of losing both burst and staying power, the deck will need to transform after rotation.

#### Poison and Toxic Counters Theme

![Poison](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Influence-After-EOE-Release/6e5dab70dc06.webp)

It rose with ONE and will leave with ONE's rotation. Goodbye to the nightmare of being poisoned from afar by {% mtglink "Venerated Rotpriest" %}.

#### Legendary Theme

![Legendary](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Influence-After-EOE-Release/b8af19d2ec37.webp)

Since the core cards are all in the sets rotating out this time, this theme will basically disappear after rotation.

### 5. Powerful Color-Hate Sideboard Cards

![MOM color-hate cards](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Influence-After-EOE-Release/7eec8bb9f802.webp)

These quality sideboard cards will leave us, but every generation has its own talent, and there will always be new cards entering players' sideboard arsenals!

### 6. Other Important Cards

#### Temporary Lockdown

{% mtgpick "dmu" 36 %}

Since its debut, {% mtglink "Temporary Lockdown" %} has been an important answer to aggro in white, and some blink-package decks also use it as one of their working parts. Although it will rotate out this time, EOE also brings a successor with a very similar function, {% mtglink "Pinnacle Starcage" %}. Note that {% mtglink "Pinnacle Starcage" %} can only exile creatures and artifacts, so it can't remove {% mtglink "Proft's Eidetic Memory" %} or {% mtglink "Insidious Roots" %}, but likewise it won't hit your own {% mtglink "Authority of the Consuls" %} or {% mtglink "Rest in Peace" %}, so it has both pros and cons.

Against red-based aggro, not clashing with your own {% mtglink "Authority of the Consuls" %} is very important, but since it is an artifact, red does have a chance to destroy it.

#### Jace, the Perfected Mind

{% mtgpick "one" 57 %}

A big weapon against control and other slow decks, especially non-blue Token control. Unless you exile it with {% mtglink "The Stone Brain" %}, you are almost dead on contact. Although there is still {% mtglink "Riverchurn Monument" %} for winning by mill alone, it can't affect the board or earn extra card resources, and is pure milling, not as well-rounded as {% mtglink "Jace, the Perfected Mind" %}.

#### Monastery Swiftspear

{% mtgpick "bro" 144 %}

Whether this card rotating out will actually affect the environment is honestly hard to say. {% mtglink "Monastery Swiftspear" %} has major historical significance for red-based aggro, but in recent builds it was clearly an optimized-out, phased-out option. Especially before the [June 30, 2025 ban update](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/banned-and-restricted-june-30-2025), pure red aggro's one-drop damage came from {% mtglink "Heartfire Hero" %} + {% mtglink "Hired Claw" %}, while red-blue prowess had already shifted to midrange, needing only {% mtglink "Stormchaser's Talent" %} for its one-drop damage. Even after {% mtglink "Heartfire Hero" %} was banned, {% mtglink "Monastery Swiftspear" %}'s employment rate recovered in a subtle way: red-blue doesn't necessarily need it, red-green may choose green creatures like {% mtglink "Pawpatch Recruit" %}, and even in pure red aggro some people switched to {% mtglink "Greasewrench Goblin" %}.

Overall, {% mtglink "Monastery Swiftspear" %} is no longer the absolute core damage source supporting red-based aggro, but because of its important historical significance, the author feels it is still worth a mention.

#### Urabrask's Forge

{% mtgpick "one" 153 %}

For red aggro and all kinds of red-based decks, it has always been a powerful Plan B win condition. Although losing it won't drastically change the nature of the deck, it is no longer possible, as before, to rely on {% mtglink "Urabrask's Forge" %} as a powerful win condition that switches to noncreature in post-board games against opponents with lots of creature removal.

#### The Stone Brain

{% mtgpick "bro" 247 %}

An important answer card against certain special decks in the environment, especially combo decks. The only current replacement is {% mtglink "Ancient Vendetta" %}, but its exile at the fastest is one turn slower than {% mtglink "The Stone Brain" %}, and only black decks can use it.

## Conclusion: Treat Standard Rotation as a Change of Seasons!

Although it is sad to see old friends leave, the arrival of new friends also brings new ways to play and chemistry between decks. You wouldn't want Standard to be forever dominated by a single deck, and if that really happens, the [June 30, 2025 bans](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/banned-and-restricted-june-30-2025) are a recent warning. The unchanging face of Standard is always the limited experience that comes with rotation, and the unforgettable memories we accumulate as players as the seasons change. And don't forget, Foundations (FDN) will accompany us in the Standard seasons for a full five years! Change or no change, we will keep playing just the same!
