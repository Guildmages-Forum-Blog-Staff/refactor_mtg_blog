## Introduction

Welcome to our usual first look at the new Standard card pool. Foundations (FDN) is a very unusual set, and I suspect many of you still can't make sense of its product lineup: which cards are legal in Standard? Which ones can you open in booster packs? And how long will this set stay Standard-legal?

So before we get into the decks, let's talk about the set itself.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FDN-Standard-Preliminary/79504078dfeb.webp)


### The Foundations Card Pool

The Foundations card pool consists of the following three products:

- Regular booster packs
- [Beginner Box](https://mtg.fandom.com/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Foundations/Beginner_Box)
- [Starter Collection](https://mtg.fandom.com/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Foundations/Starter_Collection)

Regular booster packs are the ones we crack every day, and they make up the entire Limited card pool. The other two are extra products, but they still count as part of the FDN set, so those cards enter Standard as well.

The general rule is to check a card's set symbol and set code <font color="#AAAAAA">(the three-letter abbreviation in the bottom left corner)</font>. If the set symbol and code say FDN, the card is Standard-legal.

To check the complete FDN card pool, see: [FDN full card list](https://scryfall.com/sets/fdn).


### Foundations Rotation

According to the current announcements, FDN cards will stay legal in Standard until 2029.


### Combat Rules Change

Starting with FDN, the combat phase gets a major rules change. Previously, after blockers were declared in the declare blockers step, if there were multiple blockers you had to immediately **"order the blockers"** for damage assignment. That process is gone: players no longer declare any damage assignment order, and can divide damage freely when damage is dealt. For more details on the combat rules, see the article: [Turn Structure Explained](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/Round-Process/).

### 4 Colors Gates

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6740599)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FDN-Standard-Preliminary/17cf839e191b.webp)

[Maze's End](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/727/mazes-end) is back! This deck comes from [約力昂大叔 Uncle Yorin](https://www.facebook.com/profile.php?id=100068751771443) and was tweaked slightly by the GF team. Let [Loot, Exuberant Explorer](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/106/loot-exuberant-explorer) and [Glarb, Calamity's Augur](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/215/glarb-calamitys-augur) help you play a pile of lands, gain a ton of life with [Archway Angel](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/566/archway-angel), hold the board together with sweepers, and then run the maze for a surprise win!
Don't forget that Standard has an 11th Gate, too: [Thran Portal](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/259/thran-portal) can make your maze run much smoother. It is an early-game land that plays almost like a fast land, though tapping it for mana costs 1 life. Since this deck is designed to start going off around turn three or four, a Gate that comes down on time matters a lot. Recommended for control players who like to win in unusual ways!

> At its core this is Bant Gates control. Black is only there to add [Glarb, Calamity's Augur](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/215/glarb-calamitys-augur) and the boosted mode of [Tear Asunder](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/183/tear-asunder).

### Golgari Elvish Snowball

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6740624)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FDN-Standard-Preliminary/eef43289e7cd.webp)

FDN pushes the Elf tribe hard. Besides [Llanowar Elves](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/227/llanowar-elves), this list runs all four Elf lords available in Standard ([Elvish Archdruid](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/219/elvish-archdruid), [Imperious Perfect](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/719/imperious-perfect), [Dwynen, Gilt-Leaf Daen](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/217/dwynen-gilt-leaf-daen), and DMU's [Leaf-Crowned Visionary](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/167/leaf-crowned-visionary)), then throws in the strong legendary Elves such as [Glissa Sunslayer](https://scryfall.com/card/one/202/glissa-sunslayer), [Lathril, Blade of the Elves](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/242/lathril-blade-of-the-elves), [Tyvar the Pummeler](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/202/tyvar-the-pummeler), and [Tyvar the Bellicose](https://scryfall.com/card/mat/48/tyvar-the-bellicose). Since Elves should make plenty of mana, the reprinted [Genesis Wave](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/221/genesis-wave) goes in too. On the green side, [Innkeeper's Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/180/innkeepers-talent) fits the deck very well, and while we're at it, why not add [Vraska, Betrayal's Sting](https://scryfall.com/card/one/115/vraska-betrayals-sting) for an instant-win combo?

For lands, apart from the full four [Cavern of Souls](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/269/cavern-of-souls), there is still some room for fine-tuning. The one-drop slot is also a bit thin with only four [Llanowar Elves](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/227/llanowar-elves), but if you manage to jump from one to three and then from three to infinite, the snowball starts rolling very quickly... right?

That said, this version still has a few problems that today's Standard card pool can't fix well:

1. No second one-drop mana dork. [Elvish Mystic](https://scryfall.com/card/m15/173/elvish-mystic) is a real loss;
2. No two-drop like [Elvish Warmaster](https://scryfall.com/card/khm/167/elvish-warmaster) that steadily produces more Elves;
3. No big flexible payoff like [Chord of Calling](https://scryfall.com/card/m15/172/chord-of-calling) to find the key creature.

Treat this build as a basic prototype for everyone to keep brewing with. Maybe future sets will fill in these missing pieces!

Finally, here is a midrange Golgari Elves version. With a mana dork on turn one, [Glissa Sunslayer](https://scryfall.com/card/one/202/glissa-sunslayer) on turn two, and [Lathril, Blade of the Elves](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/242/lathril-blade-of-the-elves) or [Wilt-Leaf Liege](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/668/wilt-leaf-liege) on turn three, the attacking tempo is already strong enough! Using [Llanowar Elves](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/227/llanowar-elves) as the starting point for a green-based midrange ramp plan may well perform better than the clunky pure-Elf approach!

### Rakdos Lizard

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6739214#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FDN-Standard-Preliminary/229ef8b5c81b.webp)

The real change to this deck is one extra creature, [Viashino Pyromancer](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/634/viashino-pyromancer). The Lizard deck was missing a good two-drop curve, so some people used things like [Deep-Cavern Bat](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/102/deep-cavern-bat) to fill the curve. ### Boros Burn

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6739220#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FDN-Standard-Preliminary/983ae185f5a4.webp)

This time we got [Boltwave](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/79/boltwave), [Burst Lightning](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/192/burst-lightning), and [Boros Charm](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/721/boros-charm), three efficient burn spells in one go. [Ball Lightning](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/618/ball-lightning) is a creature, but it is essentially no different from a one-shot burn spell. (If you wanted, you could also fit in a [Viashino Pyromancer](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/634/viashino-pyromancer), but there are already so many good cards that I don't know what to cut.)

In the past, you could fight burn with lifelink creatures or other lifegain sources and survive until the burn ran out of ammunition. However, DSK printed [Screaming Nemesis](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/157/screaming-nemesis), which stops the opponent from gaining life for the whole game, and you can trigger it with any burn spell of your own.

Honestly, the author really wonders whether Boros Burn will exceed what the current Standard can handle. Unlike [Leyline of Resonance](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/143/leyline-of-resonance) red aggro, which has a very high ceiling but crashes as soon as it eats a removal spell, Boros Burn is both highly efficient and hard to stop, and the main-deck threats alone can answer the answers. Sure, this set also printed hate cards against aggro and burn like [Authority of the Consuls](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/137/authority-of-the-consuls) and [Crystal Barricade](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/7/crystal-barricade), but outside of respecting red aggro their general usefulness is actually terrible, so it's hard to say whether you can find enough slots just to respect red aggro.

### Mono White Gain Life

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6739222#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FDN-Standard-Preliminary/267884f148c6.webp)

An aggro lifegain deck. Triggering lifegain frequently quickly powers up [Ajani's Pridemate](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/135/ajanis-pridemate), [Essence Channeler](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/12/essence-channeler), and [Exemplar of Light](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/11/exemplar-of-light) (which can even draw cards), and it also easily meets the condition for [Resplendent Angel](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/32/resplendent-angel) to make an Angel token. The reason for playing painlands is to give [Essence Channeler](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/12/essence-channeler) flying + vigilance. As for leaving out [Leyline of Hope](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/18/leyline-of-hope), it is a bit too Win More: when you're ahead you seem to win without it, when you're behind it does nothing, and what I care about is the number of lifegain triggers, so a larger amount per trigger matters much less.

This is also one of the few decks that can reasonably main-deck [Authority of the Consuls](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/137/authority-of-the-consuls). It not only buys aggro tempo in creature combat, it also triggers lifegain more often, which helps build your board. Most importantly, it lets you show red aggro full respect ❤️ Since red aggro's [Screaming Nemesis](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/157/screaming-nemesis) only needs to resolve one instance of ability damage to shut this deck down completely, the sideboard runs a full four [Surge of Salvation](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/41/surge-of-salvation) as that respect. It is also a good protection spell in ordinary removal-heavy matchups (for pure protection [Crystal Barricade](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/7/crystal-barricade) is stronger, but it fits the deck's tempo less well).

> PS: [Hinterland Sanctifier](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/730/hinterland-sanctifier) is a new card, but it is not in the regular booster pack pool; it is printed in the Starter Collection instead.

### Mono Blue Tempo

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6742550#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FDN-Standard-Preliminary/dc8232d11e55.webp)

This is basically a remake of the blue tempo deck from before the last Standard rotation. ### Rakdos Demonic Pact

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6742440#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FDN-Standard-Preliminary/8e61d823df34.webp)

When FDN was previewed, [Demonic Pact](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/602/demonic-pact) & [Harmless Offering](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/625/harmless-offering) were shown on the same screen, and everyone burst out laughing; the intent was far too obvious. Although this combo really is a viable way to win, its requirements are still steep. We don't recommend building the deck around winning with this combo. Instead, focus on [Demonic Pact](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/602/demonic-pact)'s own powerful ability to generate resources. The game plan is still a normal midrange resource battle, and you don't need to think about how to successfully hand a [Demonic Pact](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/602/demonic-pact) that has run through all three modes to the opponent with [Harmless Offering](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/625/harmless-offering). You just need to make sure you don't get killed by [Demonic Pact](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/602/demonic-pact).

Unlike in 2016, when this combo first appeared, there are now so many ways to sacrifice your own enchantment that you couldn't fit them all in one deck. Here the author went for WOE's bargain ability. Because sacrificing an enchantment is a cost, not an effect, no matter whether your spell is cast successfully, you can at least be sure you won't be killed by [Demonic Pact](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/602/demonic-pact). I especially want to stress the pairing with [Beseech the Mirror](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/82/beseech-the-mirror): it not only helps you shed [Demonic Pact](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/602/demonic-pact), it can also eat other fodder to enable [Harmless Offering](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/625/harmless-offering) and send the [Demonic Pact](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/602/demonic-pact) away. Eating one [Demonic Pact](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/602/demonic-pact) to find a new [Demonic Pact](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/602/demonic-pact) is also a valid play. Thanks to [Beseech the Mirror](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/82/beseech-the-mirror), we only need to play a single [Harmless Offering](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/625/harmless-offering) to make the combo viable enough. As for the other bargain spell, [Torch the Tower](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/153/torch-the-tower), it is the kind of one-mana removal a normal red deck should be running anyway, so putting it in costs nothing; we just need to make sure the deck has enough bargain fodder.

### Mono Blue Artifact

[Decklist link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6747384#paper)
![decklist](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FDN-Standard-Preliminary/86a4cef68c28.webp)

A deck where I let myself off the leash. After [Solemn Simulacrum](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/257/solemn-simulacrum) was reprinted, you get itchy to build a deck with it, but traditional Ramp decks are already very complete now. In acceleration speed, a four-mana [Solemn Simulacrum](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/257/solemn-simulacrum) certainly can't compete, and in resources, [Up the Beanstalk](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/195/up-the-beanstalk) draws more satisfyingly, so it had to find another way out. Looking around, it seems only an artifact theme gives [Solemn Simulacrum](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/257/solemn-simulacrum) room to shine: at least it works with plenty of artifacts, and there are clear post-Ramp end targets like [Cityscape Leveler](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/233/cityscape-leveler) and [Portal to Phyrexia](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/240/portal-to-phyrexia). As for why not build Izzet or Azorius and instead pick mono blue? It's purely because I wanted to test the possibilities of a single color. If you want to play it safe, I still recommend building a normal Azorius or Izzet deck and mixing in quality cheap removal.
