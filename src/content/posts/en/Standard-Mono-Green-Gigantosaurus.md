## Before We Start

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Mono-Green-Gigantosaurus/c4db5b2de6e7.webp)

Five green pips, a 10/10 body: [Gigantosaurus](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/185/gigantosaurus), carrying the dreams and hopes of green fans, returns to Standard in *[Foundations](https://scryfall.com/sets/fdn?as=grid&order=set)*. At the same time, M19's [Vivien Reid](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/234/vivien-reid) and [Ghalta, Primal Hunger](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/222/ghalta-primal-hunger) are back too. How exactly should we play it? Let's dream back to 2018 and build a new-era [Gigantosaurus](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/185/gigantosaurus) deck!


## Card Analysis

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Mono-Green-Gigantosaurus/2f9fcc90a5eb.webp)

No keywords, no obscure abilities, only five green pips, huge muscles, and the Dinosaur type. If you want to play [Gigantosaurus](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/185/gigantosaurus), these are the only points to brainstorm from. The green-pip part has no mechanic in Standard to pair with, so we have to develop the latter two.

[Decklist](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6818769#paper)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Mono-Green-Gigantosaurus/98dca1d66866.webp)

The deck concept is similar to the earlier [Draconic Roar: Standard Mono-Red Dragon Burn](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/Standard-Red-Dragon-Wins/) deck: ramp to five mana and slam our protagonist [Gigantosaurus](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/185/gigantosaurus). Ramp is green's specialty, and with the return of one-mana ramp [Llanowar Elves](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/227/llanowar-elves), [Gigantosaurus](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/185/gigantosaurus) can show up as early as turn three. For other low-cost ramp, we use [Intrepid Paleontologist](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/193/intrepid-paleontologist) and [Glimpse the Core](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/186/glimpse-the-core). The former both recovers Dinosaur cards and hates on graveyards, while the latter is ramp that is hard to disrupt, so you do not need to worry about the opponent's removal slowing your tempo, and it also thins the deck to reduce the chance of drawing lands later.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Mono-Green-Gigantosaurus/371569d7cace.webp)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Mono-Green-Gigantosaurus/4fe1d7e3de28.webp)

Considering creature quality and [Intrepid Paleontologist](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/193/intrepid-paleontologist)'s tribal support, the creature cards are naturally mostly Dinosaurs. I use [Pugnacious Hammerskull](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/208/pugnacious-hammerskull), [Thrashing Brontodon](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/560/thrashing-brontodon), [Hulking Raptor](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/191/hulking-raptor), and [Gigantosaurus](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/185/gigantosaurus)'s good friend [Ghalta, Primal Hunger](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/222/ghalta-primal-hunger). [Pugnacious Hammerskull](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/208/pugnacious-hammerskull) is a three-mana monster body that not only hits hard but also forces the opponent to spend more spells to trade when facing aggro. [Hulking Raptor](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/191/hulking-raptor) is ramp that is hard to remove, letting you fire off big plays smoothly next turn, and [Gigantosaurus](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/185/gigantosaurus) into [Ghalta](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/222/ghalta-primal-hunger) becomes not hard at all. In functionality [Thrashing Brontodon](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/560/thrashing-brontodon) is not as good as [Tranquil Frillback](https://scryfall.com/card/mat/24/tranquil-frillback), but it wins on body size, cannot be easily picked off by a single burn spell, and can raise the player's survival rate against aggro.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Mono-Green-Gigantosaurus/07498d1e1451.webp)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Mono-Green-Gigantosaurus/a96e26033e60.webp)

As for other spells, having [Gigantosaurus](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/185/gigantosaurus) as the only ramp target is clearly not enough, so I add [Vivien Reid](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/234/vivien-reid) and [Season of Gathering](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/192/season-of-gathering). The former is a stable source of staying power and can also remove the currently rampant enchantments, artifacts, and all kinds of big fliers, and the paper price is still pocket change, so there is no reason not to play her. [Season of Gathering](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/192/season-of-gathering) can provide trample, sweep artifacts and enchantments, and draw based on greatest power in a big-creature deck. Paired with [Gigantosaurus](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/185/gigantosaurus), you can draw ten cards or trample for 10 or more damage.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Mono-Green-Gigantosaurus/204b0133370b.webp)

Finally, for creature removal, I mix the land-fetching [Bushwhack](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/215/bushwhack) with the versatile [Hunter's Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/179/hunters-talent). [Bushwhack](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/215/bushwhack)'s main use is fetching a land for one mana early, and in the mid-to-late game with enough lands it is a one-mana fight. [Hunter's Talent](https://scryfall.com/card/blb/179/hunters-talent) at level one is one-sided direct damage, level two gives your creatures trample, and level three draws cards in the end step. As long as you have enough mana and control a creature with power 4 or more, basically every effect you need is provided. For lands, besides basic Forests, I also include two Cave [Hidden Nursery](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/276/hidden-nursery) for [Glimpse the Core](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/186/glimpse-the-core) to recycle, and you can choose to add [Cavern of Souls](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/269/cavern-of-souls) to defend against the opponent's counterspells.

## Gameplay Demo

{%youtube KUuH08dL1ao %}

Although this video has three games, there is actually nothing in the play that especially needs explaining: ramp into [Gigantosaurus](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/185/gigantosaurus) or [Ghalta](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/222/ghalta-primal-hunger) to beat the opponent to death, or [Vivien](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/234/vivien-reid) earning full value. The only two things to note are probably to prioritize [Glimpse the Core](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/186/glimpse-the-core) for two-mana ramp ([Intrepid Paleontologist](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/193/intrepid-paleontologist) getting removed would slow your tempo), and not to overextend the board when the opponent might have a sweeper. By the way, the opponent in the last game played an interesting and powerful graveyard deck. Although it was forcefully run over by my [Gigantosaurus](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/185/gigantosaurus), it is still worth watching.

## Conclusion

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Mono-Green-Gigantosaurus/a75edace8a37.webp)

Like the earlier [Draconic Roar: Standard Mono-Red Dragon Burn](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/Standard-Red-Dragon-Wins/) deck, this [Gigantosaurus](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/185/gigantosaurus) deck has a simple color structure and a clear strategy, and correspondingly big blind spots too. In some unfavorable matchups, you may not find an effective way to fight back. Fortunately, two-color and multicolor lands are plentiful now, so players can easily adjust the colors themselves. Interested readers, please do try it out, and let's work together on a more perfect version.
