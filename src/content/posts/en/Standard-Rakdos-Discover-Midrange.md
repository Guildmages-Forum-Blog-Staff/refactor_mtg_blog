## Introduction

Hi everyone, I'm 荒野騎士. At the end of last year I heard that 2024 would be the year of Standard, and back in December I had already picked out the deck I wanted to play. Over these two months I've watched this deck go from off-meta to a Tier 1 deck, and from its original build to the current configuration, so I suppose I count as an OG player of this deck?

## Why You Should Pick Rakdos Discover

Simple: you pick black for the bats, Gix, and Preacher of the Schism, and you pick red for the Discover creatures. I think that, apart from a clearly unfavorable matchup against Domain, this deck has a slight edge against everything else. Unlike Domain, it doesn't lack early interaction, and it still has plenty of mid-to-late-game threats. Overall, it's a deck that overperforms at every stage of the game.

### Deck Core

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Rakdos-Discover-Midrange/545d5560c7a4.webp)

#### 4 [Deep-Cavern Bat](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/6/9/69c68c95-b788-43b1-9f22-1b22c5a00b25.jpg?1699044121):

A black supernova. A 1/1 flying lifelinker is already good, and taking a card from the opponent's hand makes it the top two-drop choice for any deck that plays black.

#### 2 [Gix, Yawgmoth Praetor](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/2/c/2c76f7e0-37e7-4e87-93a3-a25ba0674645.jpg?1675567458):

The bat's best friend. Drawing cards when it deals damage is strong on its own, and the activated ability lets you rise from the ashes when behind and press the advantage when ahead.

#### 4 [Geological Appraiser](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/7/f/7f9c1a82-695b-4df2-8e51-2d71a62e7baf.jpg?1701690520):

The Standard version of [Bloodbraid Elf](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/e/2/e2f12f6f-9383-47e6-a44f-2834ad130e51.jpg?1673148385). It has no haste, but being able to put the card you flip into your hand is better than Bloodbraid Elf. Note that its ability **only triggers when you cast it**.

#### 4 [Trumpeting Carnosaur](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/e/d/edc035ca-f0a3-4814-9405-d6dc6f048315.jpg?1699044357):

The deck's best finisher. Discover 5 is already good enough, a 7/6 trampler puts huge pressure on the board, and it can also be used as a removal spell. In short, super strong.

#### 2 [Sheoldred, the Apocalypse](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/d/6/d67be074-cdd4-41d9-ac89-0a0456c4e4b2.jpg?1674057568):

A powerful card. It is very strong when the opponent hasn't drawn removal, and you can even win the game directly through her, but that is on the premise that the opponent has no removal. The current environment demands immediate impact on the turn a card enters, and Sheoldred clearly lacks that, so for this reason Geological Appraiser is sometimes the better turn-four play. Compared with the dominating presence she had in Pioneer, in the current Standard environment she mostly serves as a life-gain card and a defensive blocker.

#### 3 [The Cruelty of Gix](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/3/d/3d6e5eb3-25ab-4e9e-96e8-655e0ce74675.jpg?1673307042):

The deck's lubricant. Chapter one is hand disruption, which performs well against Domain; chapter two is a tutor, find whatever you're missing, no need to elaborate; chapter three is the essence of this card: you can dig up [Trumpeting Carnosaur](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/e/d/edc035ca-f0a3-4814-9405-d6dc6f048315.jpg?1699044357) for maximum value, or dig up the opponent's powerful creature to gain an advantage.

### Support Cards

#### Removal:

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Rakdos-Discover-Midrange/33a217446943.webp)

- The standard 2 to 3 copies of [Cut Down](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/7/5/753db072-5d6a-4f37-8f7d-255572ecd3bd.jpg?1673307061)
- 2 to 3 copies of [Go for the Throat](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/5/4/5446e1ba-c745-45b2-ad05-b22abf04daec.jpg?1682209037)
- Paired with 1 copy of [Anoint with Affliction](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/f/7/f7dc55a8-290f-4666-90ce-aa632e87c5e7.jpg?1675957006)
- I personally added one extra [Molten Collapse](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/2/4/2487d124-210b-4808-888c-cd0a78aebd90.jpg?1699044556) as planeswalker removal, so that a planeswalker landing doesn't cost me tempo

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Rakdos-Discover-Midrange/6e73e9c40956.webp)

#### [Bloodtithe Harvester](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/f/0/f0192cf7-3391-4720-b9c8-72dec5dde01e.jpg?1643593808):

A two-mana, three-power attacker that can also act as removal, and the Blood token helps filter your hand. Remember that it reduces the opponent's power and toughness, which lets Cut Down kill creatures it otherwise couldn't.

#### [Preacher of the Schism](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/8/9/89345f55-2b32-4356-945a-d56dded39909.jpg?1699044158):

Another black supernova. Getting a bonus just from attacking is practically ghost-like. A 2/4 deathtoucher is not only hard to block profitably, it is also exceptional on defense, and it is one of the few creatures that can actually hold off [Subterranean Schooner](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/9/4/94b6881a-b00e-4e90-92e6-602ed8e0e090.jpg?1699043964) early. Make good use of the bonus it gets when attacking: a 2/4 deathtoucher is of course excellent on defense, but attacking is how it delivers its maximum value.

#### [Aclazotz, Deepest Betrayal](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/88/aclazotz-deepest-betrayal-temple-of-the-dead):

An excellent five-drop that performs well against midrange and aggro decks. The bat god, a 4/4 flying lifelinker, is very strong on both offense and defense, and the advantage is even more obvious when both sides are racing in life totals. However, it has no enter-the-battlefield value and is relatively weak against Domain decks, so we usually take it out in sideboarded games against Domain. The most eye-catching part is its recursion: in midrange and aggro matchups, both sides usually run out of cards in hand quickly, so it is easy to meet the condition for it to come back, which makes it an extremely valuable card.

### Land Choices

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Standard-Rakdos-Discover-Midrange/00a4973af948.webp)

In the past most lists played 25 lands, but recently 26 lands has become the norm. I think the main reason is that with 25 lands the chance of getting stuck on lands is still too high. After all, even Pioneer Rakdos midrange, whose mana curve tops out at 4, plays 25 lands, so with this many five-mana and even six-mana spells, playing only 25 lands really makes no sense. Here is the breakdown of each land:

- [Haunted Ridge](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/b/2/b200e822-be36-4e59-bd0e-9d0188a68df8.jpg?1655879799): The best dual land in Standard. Play a full set.
- [Restless Vents](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/e/6/e628e89b-bee9-408d-bb05-1784fda6b8a1.jpg?1699044708): Although it enters tapped, the deck has only two one-drops, so there is just enough time to play this. As a creature, its menace and looting ability are excellent. Play a full set.
- [Cavern of Souls](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/3/a/3aad15a2-8a1b-4460-9b06-e85863081878.jpg?1706137652): Although the deck has many different creature types, since the red creatures all need two red mana, in addition to making creatures uncounterable it also happens to help with color fixing. In my testing, 4 copies was a bit too many and 3 was just right.
- [Sulfurous Springs](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/d/f/dfd5450a-6490-417f-9aea-b6fca6f380d7.jpg?1673308373): A dual land that can enter untapped. I didn't go up to 4 copies because drawing two of them puts pressure on your life total against creature decks. Playing too few causes color problems, though, and this land can also be used with Preacher of the Schism to adjust your life total for greater value.
- [Blackcleave Cliffs](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/1/4/1441fba4-fe06-4b5f-a103-aa6cf59a3859.jpg?1675957263): A dual land that can enter untapped under certain conditions. Since the cards in the deck still cost quite a lot, you can't play many; 2 copies is enough.
- [Takenuma, Abandoned Mire](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/4/9/499037cc-a577-41cb-8ca2-5e117945634f.jpg?1654569039): A very good card. Early on it is almost no different from a Swamp, and late in the game it can be recycled; retrieving powerful creatures late in the game matters a lot. Play 1 copy.
- [Sokenzan, Crucible of Defiance](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/a/a/aa548dcd-c1dd-492d-a69f-c65dfeef0633.jpg?1654569016): Same as Takenuma, early on it is almost no different from a Mountain, and late in the game it can be recycled, good for both offense and defense. Play 1 copy.
- Basic lands: **6 Swamp**, 2 Mountain. **This deck is still built on a black foundation, so you need more Swamps.**

## Common Matchups

### Mirror

The old saying applies to the mirror: "whoever draws better wins," though of course player skill and deck construction matter somewhat. Whoever can land a turn-two bat and a turn-three Gix has almost the winning formula for this matchup. If neither deck gets stuck on lands, the matchup can be extremely long, so when you gain an advantage you have to find ways to expand it. In sideboarded games [Liliana of the Veil](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/a/c/ac506c17-adc8-49c6-9d8d-43db7cb1ec9d.jpg?1562835390) performs well; the opponent has almost no answer to it, and it also gives you the option to protect Liliana and win with her ultimate.

### Domain

A very bad matchup, but you still have a chance. In game 1, all we can do is apply enough life-total pressure, disrupt with the bat and The Cruelty of Gix, and find a way around their removal, sweepers, and [Atraxa, Grand Unifier](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/4/a/4a1f905f-1d55-4d02-9d24-e58070793d3f.jpg?1676519555). Note that a turn-two [Bloodtithe Harvester](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/f/0/f0192cf7-3391-4720-b9c8-72dec5dde01e.jpg?1643593808) is better than [Deep-Cavern Bat](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/6/9/69c68c95-b788-43b1-9f22-1b22c5a00b25.jpg?1699044121), because a body with 3 power helps the early racing plan far more than one with 1 power, and we can wait until turn four to start using [Deep-Cavern Bat](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/6/9/69c68c95-b788-43b1-9f22-1b22c5a00b25.jpg?1699044121) for disruption. After sideboarding our chances are better: we lose the removal we can't use and some expensive, useless spells, and gain more hand disruption and [Breach the Multiverse](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/d/a/daf51a76-7a57-4462-ae18-a19e817e49e5.jpg?1682203621). Our game plan stays the same: steadily deploy creatures and win the game together with hand disruption. If your opponent draws exactly what they need, then all you can do is throw up your hands and let them win...

### Mono-Red Aggro

A favorable matchup; we have a lot of removal and our creatures are strong enough. As the environment shifts, Mono-Red will put a certain amount of removal and cards for the mid-to-long game into the main deck, so I don't think purely playing defense is the right choice. Once your life total and the board stabilize, you should switch from the defensive role to the attacking role. [Preacher of the Schism](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/8/9/89345f55-2b32-4356-945a-d56dded39909.jpg?1699044158) holds a crucial position in this matchup, because while attacking it also makes a 1/1 lifelinker to play defense. After sideboarding, we take out some useless or expensive cards and bring in more removal and sweepers. I think there are two things we need to watch out for: [Urabrask's Forge](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/6/8/68f9f9d8-9ea0-4608-a79c-a09a87918186.jpg?1675957116) and planeswalkers. For the former we have artifact removal, and the latter can be dealt with by creatures, but as I said, they have quite a few cards aimed at the mid-to-long game, so please don't just play defense.

### Esper Midrange

Today's Esper midrange comes in two versions. One is a pure midrange deck that uses [Virtue of Loyalty (White Virtue)](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/e/a/ea7e7daf-7c06-4c74-8bcf-e42c1f611861.jpg?1692936837) and [Wedding Announcement](https://scryfall.com/card/vow/45/wedding-announcement-wedding-festivity) as its finishers; the other is a tempo deck built on blue-black with a splash of white, adding [Raffine, Scheming Seer](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/7/1/716a44b4-f6b0-4f14-a270-6442aed3251f.jpg1664413581) and [Destroy Evil](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/4/f/4f7862ef-2c8d-4d28-9e50-7cc41861f245.jpg?1673306430).
Against the former, it is a midrange mirror-style matchup where nobody has a particular edge. Our [Trumpeting Carnosaur](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/e/d/edc035ca-f0a3-4814-9405-d6dc6f048315.jpg?1699044357) and [Aclazotz, Deepest Betrayal](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/88/aclazotz-deepest-betrayal-temple-of-the-dead) can easily end the game and generate resources, although the opponent also has some over-the-top cards, especially enchantments, and we may fall behind because we have no answer to enchantments. Still, we have room to maneuver (since White Virtue buffs creatures, just kill all the creatures). After sideboarding, we have enchantment answers and more removal and lose some useless cards, but so does the opponent, so what we do is minimize mistakes and press the advantage when ahead.
Against the latter it is more of a tempo matchup: their early offense is stronger, and in exchange their late-game threats are weaker. This is good for us, because we have lots of creature removal and our late-game threats are stronger than theirs. As long as we carefully play around counterspells, we have a very high chance of winning. This is even more true after sideboarding: we have more removal and fewer dead cards. Simply put, it's a favorable matchup.

### Bant Toxic

The opponent is basically a more extreme Mono-Red; we have a lot of removal and our blockers are big enough. Watch out for the opponent's noncreature spells, especially [March of Swirling Mist](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/1/0/100171d8-7436-44c8-b4cb-0101ffa05c25.jpg?1654566768). The most common way you lose is that they land creatures on turns one and two and then use only sorceries and instants to maneuver against us and take us down. Just remember that this is how they beat us, and don't let them get away with it. Also note that, as with Mono-Red, their late-game win condition is mostly to poison us out with proliferate spells, so don't let the game drag on too long either; once the board stabilizes, start attacking.

### Golgari Midrange

The concept of this matchup is almost exactly the same as the mirror, but this time we have a bit more of an edge, because they don't gain advantage as much as we do. The only variable is their planeswalkers: we lack ways to deal with planeswalkers, so if a planeswalker stays on the battlefield too long, we are very likely to lose the game. After sideboarding, we have ways to deal with planeswalkers and more cards that help in this matchup. Unlike the mirror, Liliana can't do much in this matchup, because with the opponent's [Mosswood Dreadknight](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/9/8/9869ac70-5907-45fa-952c-31aef70c5066.jpg?1692939707) around, Liliana is merely a one-sided drain on our resources.

### Dimir Midrange

Their early offense is strong, but they lack late-game threats. All we have to do is defend until we have enough mana to deploy our threats. We may struggle in game 1, since we have too many clunky spells that will be gone for good after hitting a counterspell, but after sideboarding we can fix that problem.
