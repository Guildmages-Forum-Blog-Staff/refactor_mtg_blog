### Temur Rhinos

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Meta-Inspection-PreMKM/34edf365aa37.webp)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Meta-Inspection-PreMKM/6f436e145425.webp)

The two decks are basically the same, differing by just one card in the main deck, where one chose [Murktide Regent](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/52/murktide-regent) and the other chose [Mystical Dispute](https://scryfall.com/card/eld/58/mystical-dispute); the sideboards also differ by one card, where one chose [Flame of Anor](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/203/flame-of-anor) and the other chose an extra [Boseiju, Who Endures](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/266/boseiju-who-endures).

What's more interesting is that both players chose to play one [Ertai's Scorn](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/48/ertais-scorn) in the sideboard, mainly for the cascade mirror when on the draw.
Let's assume both sides have smooth hands, and the player on the play has [Violent Outburst](https://scryfall.com/card/arb/63/violent-outburst) available on turn three. They will usually pass the turn and cast the cascade during the opponent's upkeep, because the opponent usually has no mana to counter it, and [Force of Negation](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/50/force-of-negation) can't be cast for free on your own turn, so they can only watch helplessly as the opponent successfully resolves [Crashing Footfalls](https://scryfall.com/card/mh1/160/crashing-footfalls). This should be the only card that can counter [Crashing Footfalls](https://scryfall.com/card/mh1/160/crashing-footfalls) for two mana without blocking your own cascade.

The sideboard is all toolbox cards.

### Izzet Breach

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Meta-Inspection-PreMKM/6f9277d8f8e6.webp)

This was the deck that impressed me most across two days of watching the stream. It doesn't play the combo of [Grinding Station](https://scryfall.com/card/5dn/127/grinding-station) and [Thassa's Oracle](https://scryfall.com/card/thb/73/thassas-oracle), and unlike Murktide it has no mid-to-late-game creatures, so it is a thoroughly tempo deck. In the mid-to-late game, once there is enough mana and graveyard, it drops [Underworld Breach](https://scryfall.com/card/thb/161/underworld-breach) and keeps escaping [Lightning Bolt](https://scryfall.com/card/clu/141/lightning-bolt) to burn away the opponent's life.

I'd say this deck is more like Murktide, just with the finisher replaced by [Underworld Breach](https://scryfall.com/card/thb/161/underworld-breach), but the mana requirement for the kill is too high, so I still prefer the version with [Murktide Regent](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/52/murktide-regent), or otherwise a more controlling deck.

### Izzet Murktide

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Meta-Inspection-PreMKM/983845378014.webp)

In contrast to Murktide being wiped out in Europe, one Murktide deck survived here in Brazil.
I think that in the current environment this matchup is no longer that hard to play, at least you won't get turn-one monkey, turn-two DRC DRC and then get swept away by a backhanded Fury scam, and there isn't such great life-total pressure either.
And because of the extra card filtering, compared with other decks it is relatively easier to handle a turn-one Grief scam.

Also, this build chose to play one [Test of Talents](https://scryfall.com/card/stx/59/test-of-talents) in the sideboard, which should be quite comfortable in the mirror or in cascade matchups.

Also note that this deck is a 62-card build, and I can't currently think of a reason to build it that way.

### Amulet Titan

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Meta-Inspection-PreMKM/480ba065d555.webp)
![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Meta-Inspection-PreMKM/160cddb0f782.webp)

One is a very standard Titan list, and the other list is a bit more interesting, adding lands not usually seen as a backup / faster mana acceleration, which lets it more reliably land Titan on turn three without necessarily relying on the Amulet, since in the current environment some matchups are won as long as you have a Titan as a blocker.

### Golgari Yawgmoth

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Meta-Inspection-PreMKM/4043e9401922.webp)

A pretty standard Yawgmoth build. Choosing [Zulaport Cutthroat](https://scryfall.com/card/clb/775/zulaport-cutthroat) is presumably to dodge the protection from everything of [The One Ring](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/246/the-one-ring).
But honestly, Yawgmoth should be able to win during the opponent's upkeep, and [Blood Artist](https://scryfall.com/card/lcc/182/blood-artist) also triggers when the opponent's creatures die, so it should be somewhat better.

> Let me add a note on the advantages of [Blood Artist](https://scryfall.com/card/lcc/182/blood-artist) and [Zulaport Cutthroat](https://scryfall.com/card/clb/775/zulaport-cutthroat).
> [Blood Artist](https://scryfall.com/card/lcc/182/blood-artist): drains whenever a creature of either side dies
> [Zulaport Cutthroat](https://scryfall.com/card/clb/775/zulaport-cutthroat): can get around hexproof / does not need to target
> According to the Yawgmoth players on our writing team, each has pros and cons, depending on personal choice.

## Predictions for the Upcoming RCs

I've roughly looked through the MKM card list, and it shouldn't affect the Modern environment much.
So far it looks like only Rhinos might use the new surveil lands to replace the earlier triome slot, since Rhinos easily has nothing to do on the first two turns, and using fetch lands to find a surveil land and filter the top of the library would be a good choice. Murktide or other slower decks (such as control) can also consider playing them.

> Personally, though, I don't much like how it performs in Murktide, which we'll talk about when we have the chance (digging myself another hole).

Also, I'm not very familiar with the player community distribution in other countries, but combining the meta performance of these two weeks and the RC results, Rakdos Scam and Temur Rhinos should be the biggest shares.
If I were to go into detail, it would look like this:

1. Rakdos Scam and Temur Rhinos are the biggest shares, together probably around 35% ~ 40%
2. Yawgmoth, Murktide, and Titan won't be small either, probably each around 10%, with Yawgmoth slightly higher
3. Burn and Tron, decks that don't need much practice, should still show up, together around 10%
4. Coffers, Scales, and Hammer Time should also appear sporadically
