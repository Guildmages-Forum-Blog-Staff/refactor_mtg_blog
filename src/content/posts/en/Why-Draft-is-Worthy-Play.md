## Before We Start

Over the more than thirty years of Magic: The Gathering (MTG) history, Limited, and Draft in particular, has always been seen as a game mode that both tests a player's skill and brings surprise and fun. In recent years, however, whether at card shops or in the community, we have felt that **getting together a full 8-person draft pod has become harder and harder.**

But "hard to organize a draft" does not mean "draft is not worth playing," nor does it mean "the era of draft is over." In fact, it is quite the opposite: **draft remains one of the most precious and most fun cores of MTG.**

Below I want to revisit what draft means from the angles of player experience, learning and growth, community value, and even the market and the game's ecosystem.

## I. The Fun of Draft: A Timed Adventure That Never Repeats

Playing Constructed is like venturing out with a refined signature weapon, while draft is like a roguelike where you walk into unknown territory empty-handed and pick up weapons at random on the spot to put together something usable for the fight. This makes draft a puzzle game of both strategy and intuition, and that is exactly what makes it addictive:

- Even in the same set, with different people, different cards opened, or even simply passing left or right, the experience never repeats.
- Building an orderly theme out of a disordered card pool is the most charming part of draft.
- Players need to read the flow in front of them and make the right decisions, and every choice has a deep impact on what follows.
- Unlike Constructed, which pursues an "optimal configuration" mindset, draft values "managing limited resources" and "theme synergy."

Pulling off a super combo with underrated cards? No problem! Winning the game in a rare way? There are plenty of ways! As long as you understand the card pool well enough and your thinking is unique enough, you can always find a way to play that is all your own. More than simply winning, the satisfaction when you pull off a magical play with a pile of seemingly ordinary cards is something only draft can give you.

## II. Draft Is the [Training Grounds](https://scryfall.com/card/roe/91/training-grounds) for Competitive Players to Grow Quickly

After reading many interviews with players who performed well in major tournaments, we can notice one thing in common: the vast majority of veterans who have played for more than 10 years are not only strong at Constructed but even better at draft! And in the interviews, they all independently mention how important draft is for understanding individual cards and for attacking and defending in games. If you want to truly **understand the underlying logic of MTG: resources, tempo, offense and defense, and the value of interaction, draft is the best teacher.** You will naturally practice:

- Reading and analyzing individual cards (in what situations is this card great, and in what situations is it not?)
- A sense of deck tempo (do I need more removal? More early creatures? Or a reliable finisher?)
- Mana base fundamentals (how many utility lands can a one- or two-color deck play? How do I arrange lands in a multicolor deck? Do I need one fewer or one more land?)
- Deck resilience (still winning with a reliable backup plan when you do not draw your theme)
- Adaptability (when your opponent has a hand full of removal, I should not curve out and fall into the tempo they expect)
- The ultimate either/or (should I take the creature or the removal? Training to make the best decision)
- Advanced gamesmanship: reading cards and deduction (what is my opponent's behavior revealing?)

These are core skills that top players all share, and we can gradually build them through draft. Whether or not you want to become a competitive player, or you just want to play casually, playing more draft will help you understand the depth of this game faster.

## III. But... Draft Really Is Not Perfect

The following issues are not saying draft is bad, but rather **telling new players what draft is**, so that everyone can decide whether to join after understanding it.

### 1. Luck and variance are much greater than in Constructed

Draft can indeed run into:

- A barren card pool (lacking curve, removal, or color fixing)
- Not enough support cards for a theme (some themes lack key cards and cannot be played)
- A scarce color, where switching colors takes skill (your main color gets cut by your neighbors)
- Building a deck with both a main plan and a curve, only to be blown out by a bomb (fate is in your own hands, but do not force what is not meant to be)

The first two are common in 4-person drafts with few players, while in 8-person drafts, because the card pool is larger, their impact is relatively smaller. But for the last two, even if you manage your limited resources well and maximize the value of the cards you draw, you will sometimes inevitably lose a few matches.

### 2. The skill barrier is higher than beginners imagine

Draft is not just about luck, nor is it simply opening packs and picking by curve. In fact, it requires a lot of implicit knowledge, including:

- The strength of each theme in the set
- The speed and tempo of the set
- Trap cards/themes that look strong but are actually weak
- Situational strong cards

If new players have no experience or do not do their homework, it is easy to fall into traps and misconceptions. So for people who are unfamiliar with the new set, or who dislike making lots of decisions on the spot, the barrier to draft really is higher.

> But this is also one of the things Guildmages' Forum is working on. Since [WOE](/categories/WOE/), every Standard set has had a Limited primer article, and readers are welcome to click [Limited](/categories/Limited/) to look them up.

### 3. The time cost is less friendly than Constructed

For busy players, preparing a Constructed deck requires more upfront investment, but afterward it can be reused, and even with less than an hour you can find a friend for some simple practice games. Finishing a draft event, on the other hand, needs less preparation, but the event itself takes at least 2 to 3 hours, so some players short on time want to play but cannot.

### 4. Not everyone pursues uncertainty

Some players like a stable experience while others like uncertainty, and the former may not enjoy draft. Many things that are strengths for players who like draft are too high a barrier for players who just want to play simply, so when promoting draft, we also need to pay attention to feedback from different kinds of players.

## IV. Why Is Draft Harder to Organize Now?

From my own observation, I think at least the following four points have led to the current situation where drafts are hard to organize:

1. **Play Booster prices went up** → The cost of one draft is higher than in the past. Although many stores have switched to handing out only Promo packs to offset the entry fee, the effect is not yet significant, and students already have limited entertainment budgets, so a price increase may make them even less willing to join.
2. **More entertainment options fragment players' time** → It is not just different MTG formats or other card games; social media, streaming platforms, Steam and other games/mobile games all take a share of the time players could put into draft.
3. **The player age range is gradually getting older** → People need to allocate time to family, and the communities that used to draft together regularly are gradually dispersing.
4. **Online draft is extremely convenient (such as MTGA)** → It diverts a large number of players who cannot find anyone to play paper draft with.

There is also a degree of reverse causality among these four points. Especially the last one: drafting on MTGA is convenient, but anyone who has done it knows that online is not as fun or as representative as paper, and online is more like a substitute you have to use.

## V. Draft Is the "Heart" of the Entire MTG Ecosystem — The Real Key to Stirring Liquidity

This may be a long-neglected perspective, and now the impact of not playing draft on the overall market is growing. Many players who do not draft and rarely open packs always claim: "I'll just buy singles."

But where do the singles come from?

As of TLA, the volume of singles cracked from packs by Taiwanese stores, as well as the number of stores running a singles market, has been getting smaller and smaller, and this is reflected in prices. Even worse, in the past two years the price increases on new-set cards with Constructed uses have been outrageous. Just look at the recent price surge of TLA's {% mtglink "Badgermole Cub" %} and EOE's {% mtglink "Quantum Riddler" %}, which I think everyone has seen, and this is one of the effects of not playing draft.

The current MTG market already shows the following cycle:

〔Players do not draft → fewer cards from new sets get opened → new cards find a use and prices rise〕

And the deadlock of too few new cards on the market cannot be gotten around, so new card prices get pushed even higher, to an unbelievable degree.

If we want to break this situation, the key is for players to start playing draft. My argument is as follows:

### 1. Draft gets cards opened in large numbers, supporting market supply

Every draft consumes many regular booster packs, and the supply of the R/MR cards and the useful C/U cards opened from them can increase substantially, lowering the difficulty for players to obtain them and stabilizing the supply across the whole market.

### 2. Draft naturally promotes trading

Players sell or trade the cards they do not need, which speeds up the movement of supply and demand, drives liquidity in singles that have been stagnant for a long time, and helps with the current situation where commonly used singles have a price but no volume.

### 3. In an environment with draft, card prices are healthier

If most of the people opening packs on the market are collector-type players, then the price trends are just what we see:

- Popular cards get more expensive
- Affordable useful cards get harder to obtain
- Foil and non-foil prices are barely different
- Commons may be harder to get than foils or alternate-art cards

And the volume of cards opened and the liquidity from draft can naturally balance these problems.

From an economic perspective: **MTG without draft becomes more "scarce in cards, uncontrollable in prices, and low in singles liquidity," and that is exactly the current state of the Taiwanese MTG community.** Draft is not just a way to play; it is a whole circulation system, and it is the heart of the MTG market.

## VI. Draft Is Also the Easiest Way for Players to "Enter the Community"

Playing Constructed with strangers or entering official tournaments always requires a higher psychological barrier, and the time and energy each person can invest varies; however, drafting with strangers is always much easier, as long as everyone can find time to sit down together. In a draft where card strength is relatively balanced, the following always happen:

- Someone opens a rare card and everyone gathers around to look
- During the match you share deck ideas and your picking process with your opponent
- Someone pulls off a bizarre combo and is happy whether or not it wins, then gets surrounded and photographed
- You meet new friends, and then arrange to draft together again next time
- Repeat the cycle above, and new players gradually blend into the local community

Draft can bring card shops to life, let players meet each other, and make MTG not just cards but a shared memory for a group of people.

## Wrap-Up: We Have the Motivation, but How Do We Build a Paper Draft Culture?

We have discussed the pros and cons of draft, the difficulty of putting it into practice, card liquidity, community, and the effects of the new event policy. Since paper draft is so fun and so important, how do we make it happen?

### Limited-scale drafts at a fixed time and place

- Not multiple per week, but at least make one draft a week a habit for local players
- The cost of participation becomes manageable because of the fixed frequency
- Practicing on MTGA improves players' understanding of draft, so every paper match is better prepared and more challenging
- Once a regular draft population forms, there is a better chance of calling more players to participate

### Stores and players working together

- Stores provide affordable deals or subsidies (especially for students)
- Reviving the draft pack-sending (booster pack consignment) economy (plans and feasibility depend on each store's announcements)
- Players actively invite friends to join, so that a 4-person draft has a chance to grow into an 8-person draft!
- Build a culture of discussing and practicing draft, so that draft is not just an event but a community culture

The final goal is:
**To make "one draft per week" no longer just a slogan but a habit that is actually kept up. When out-of-town players visit a card shop, they will also have the chance to get to know the local community through a draft, promoting more friendly exchanges.**

As an entertainment activity, draft is in essence absolutely no less fun than anything else. And Magic always brings great energy and a sense of belonging when it is gathering, to say nothing of the fact that we now have the higher pursuit of See the World!

## Conclusion: Let's Play a Draft Together

Perhaps draft costs more now, has fewer players, and is hard to organize, but none of that means it is not worth playing. Whenever I am worn out from Constructed, an occasional draft with a few friends always refills my passion for MTG.

If you have never played draft, I sincerely invite you to try it once.
If you once loved draft, I hope you will step up and call your friends to make it happen once more.

Let's play a draft together!!!
