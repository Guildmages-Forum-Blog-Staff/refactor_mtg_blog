## Introduction

Season 5 of the RCQ (Regional Championship Qualifier, called MIT in Taiwan) has already been going for a while, and starting this week I will also be running around card shops to fight for RCQs. Because I once shared an article on my personal page that billed itself as the most detailed Pioneer Rakdos midrange article in all of Taiwan, I received an invitation from the [Guildmages' Forum](https://www.facebook.com/guildmagesforumtw) team asking me to use half of the skill I had then to introduce this Modern deck that I have played for nearly a year and have won with in the US until it is nearly worn out.

## Why You Should Choose Temur Rhinos

If you ask me what to play in Modern right now, I will answer without hesitation: "Temur Rhinos." I think that in most matchups this deck is the proactive side, and no deck can easily stop two 4/4 trampling Rhinos on turn three. Given that, opponents have to make trades that are unfavorable for them, not to mention we also have plenty of counterspells and removal. Another point is that apart from the deck's main core, every other slot can be changed to follow the environment. This trait makes the deck's adaptability to the environment exceptionally strong. As long as the environment's strength doesn't surpass the essence of the deck itself or a key card isn't banned, I think this deck can last a long time without needing to swap decks too often.

## About Temur Rhinos

As the title says, Temur Rhinos is a deck that is a "perfect blend of aggression and control." On the aggressive side, it can build a board early to put heavy pressure on the opponent and quickly lower their life total. On the control side, the whole deck is full of various counterspells and removal spells, so the opponent cannot develop smoothly in the early and middle game.

### Deck Core

These are the cards I think cannot be cut, including the following:

![Rhino](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Crashing-Footballs/2c8cdf6bbdea.webp)

[Crashing Footfalls](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/a/8/a8cca2a2-69e3-4136-936c-7a2774c19351.jpg?1562202050) / [Shardless Agent](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/b/0/b0824e77-c84b-464a-aa0c-44af5f6faa50.jpg?1626100788) / [Violent Outburst](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/1/b/1b58856a-f88e-4625-8636-62b5c717b956.jpg?1562639969): The deck's operating system, no explanation needed.

![Cookie Cards](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Crashing-Footballs/a0cbe6007930.webp)

[Fire // Ice](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/0/e/0e09b054-4d33-4a12-bf2a-9b0009f33044.jpg?1660845942): The "perfect" supporting card. The Temur Rhinos deck can't start operating until turn three, and this card effectively makes up for our weak first two turns. For Fire, cheap removal and direct damage are exactly what we need. For Ice, a punctual 2-mana disruption can slow down the opponent's tempo, and also lets us draw to find our Cascade cards.

[Dead // Gone](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/4/5/45b090c7-f1ba-4656-8b51-915fc1876922.jpg?1666020618): Dead is a lot like Fire, but more efficient. Gone is a bit awkward in the current environment, though, because most creatures now have ETB abilities, so bouncing the opponent's creatures is not very useful: the opponent can recast it and the problem remains unsolved. I originally wanted to put this card in the next section because it isn't as useful in today's environment as it used to be, but given that it has always been in Temur Rhinos, I finally decided to put it here.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Crashing-Footballs/591642d7b1ba.webp)

[Force of Negation](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/1/8/1825a719-1b2a-4af9-9cd2-7cb497cd0317.jpg?1673147298): It's a 0-mana disruption. In Modern there are few decks that run only a small number of noncreature spells, but in theory Force of Negation always has room to shine. Furthermore, as long as Temur Rhinos continues to survive in the meta, other decks will definitely bring in matching sideboard cards to target us, and Force of Negation can usually help me effectively dodge those hate cards.

[Lórien Revealed](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/0/c/0ce44270-a684-4489-9077-521456e6dfaa.jpg?1687210977): Early on it acts as a de facto land search that isn't disrupted by [Blood Moon](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/d/0/d072e9ca-aae7-45dc-8025-3ce590bae63f.jpg?1599706217), and late it is strong card draw, and then there's "it's blue": because of [FON](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/1/8/1825a719-1b2a-4af9-9cd2-7cb497cd0317.jpg?1673147298), [Subtlety](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/7/0/701256d5-1389-48b7-9581-d6037209bd06.jpg?1626095059), and [Commandeer](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/d/c/dca0a9a8-5ebc-43a3-8450-420ab6b7b76e.jpg?1689996238), our deck needs plenty of blue cards, which gives it another use besides searching for lands. Of course, after cycling it can serve as material to quickly make [Murktide Regent](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/2/0/20c4aae1-7665-4df7-bd51-a1d95bf8a17d.jpg?1626094651).

### Other Support Cards

![Support Cards 1](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Crashing-Footballs/0f3522942e6b.webp)

[Murktide Regent](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/2/0/20c4aae1-7665-4df7-bd51-a1d95bf8a17d.jpg?1626094651): Our deck stacks its graveyard quickly on its own, and a powerful flying creature gives us an option besides Rhinos trampling people to death. It is useful in many matchups, such as Rakdos Scam, which emphasizes resource trading, and against high-toughness fliers, which Rhinos have a hard time with. Many people run 2, but I run only 1, because I don't think it's necessary, and drawing two in a row is awkward.

[Subtlety](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/7/0/701256d5-1389-48b7-9581-d6037209bd06.jpg?1626095059): Basically a necessary card to fight the environment. It is useful against Rakdos, Elementals, and so on, and being able to softly handle [[小泰]] is also a fairly important factor.

[Fury](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/b/d/bd281158-8180-40b9-a5b7-03cfc712d81a.jpg?1626096626): Removal. Given that the environment has Rakdos Scam and 4-color Elementals, I think this card can't be cut. Besides being usable early as instant-speed removal, what I think is more important is that hard casting it helps our strength in the mid to late game.

![Support Cards 2](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Crashing-Footballs/7b287a64d4ec.webp)

[Mystical Dispute](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/f/b/fbe04cb8-a8b9-4241-baae-b398a2509a3a.jpg?1572489956): Just a counterspell. It performs well in the mirror, against Elementals, [[龍喉]], and Tron, but isn't very good against Rakdos, Hammer Time, and Yawgmoth. I think main deck and sideboard together should total at least 3, and I run 3 main and 1 side. After all, if you run into a situation in game 1 where you can't use it, early on it can also be material for FON and Subtlety, and in the mid to late game it is a serviceable counterspell.

[Dismember](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/3/d/3d286cf6-3e16-4941-9326-1818b1e06d69.jpg?1562261132): A card that came back into use after the Lord of the Rings set came out. Besides removing most creatures in the environment, I think the most important thing is that for a low mana cost and 4 life you can deal with creatures "worth more than the 4 life you pay," for example Rakdos Scam's turn-one Elemental, Sheoldred, Yawgmoth, and Omnath. But that's as far as this card goes: it is a card for answering today's mainstream, and for me paying 4 life isn't worth it most of the time.

[Flame of Anor](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/0/4/04779a7e-b453-48b9-b392-6d6fd0b8d283.jpg?1686969766): My first reaction when I saw this card was: "Damn, no Wizard? And what's the point of a 5 toughness... I'd rather keep filling out Fury, which can also go face." Later I found out I was wrong: destroying an artifact can be the answer to [Chalice of the Void](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/1/f/1f0d2e8e-c8f2-4b31-a6ba-6283fc8740d4.jpg?1562433485), dealing 5 can handle most creatures in the environment just like Dismember, drawing two is very powerful, and it is itself red and blue, so it helps us cast FON, Subtlety, Commandeer, and Fury. The drawback is that it costs 3, so in extreme moments it isn't as good as Dismember. As for the Wizard part, we can run [Mutavault](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/5/2/52cc2f10-142d-4e6a-984e-b25f566cc960.jpg?1674142985) to raise this card's ceiling.

### Land Choices

1 of each shock land, 1 tri-color cycling land: Basically no explanation needed. I see some people run more red-blue shock lands, but I don't think that's necessary.

4 each of red-green and blue-green fetchlands, 1 red-blue fetchland: Also basically no explanation needed. The red-blue fetchland count may vary based on each player's considerations.

[Gemstone Caverns](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/7/f/7f273641-c5f3-48bc-b89e-3cff52d26a0b.jpg?1619399338): This card is here to raise the ceiling. I originally ran 2 main, but I added 1 more Mutavault, so I moved one to the sideboard. I think main and side together totaling 2 is about right in the current environment, and 1 feels a bit low to me, though given that drawing duplicates is a problem I think that's understandable. Running 3 takes a big heart, but on the draw you are almost guaranteed to have one.

[Boseiju, Who Endures](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/2/1/2135ac5a-187b-4dc9-8f82-34e8d1603416.jpg?1654568912): Artifact and enchantment removal that performs well against Tron, Titan, and Hammer Time. 1 to 2 across main and side is fine.

[Otawara, Soaring City](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/4/8/486d7edc-d983-41f0-8b78-c99aecd72996.jpg?1654568960): Much like Boseiju, and worse than Boseiju most of the time, but it is better than Boseiju in that it can deal with planeswalkers and creatures.

[Mutavault](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/5/2/52cc2f10-142d-4e6a-984e-b25f566cc960.jpg?1674142985): This is optional. It simply works with Flame of Anor to raise the ceiling, and can deal damage when necessary, but both situations are somewhat unlikely, and I'm considering removing it.

Basic lands: I run 2 Island, 1 Forest, 1 Mountain. It was originally 2 each of Island and Forest, but recently people around me started playing burn, so I put 1 Mountain back in. I think 2 Islands are necessary in today's environment, so we're sure we can still operate normally against Blood Moon. As for whether to run 2 Forests, that is up to you, and I'm leaning toward putting the 2-Forest version back. The Mountain is there to make sure I don't die from paying too much life, which is especially important when I need to cast Dead // Gone on turn one.

### Common Matchups and Sideboarding Strategy

#### Mirror (Temur Rhinos Mirror Match)

![Mirror](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Crashing-Footballs/2c8cdf6bbdea.webp)

The mirror is the same old line: "whoever draws better wins," though of course player skill and deck construction have some effect. We both have 4 Crashing Footfalls, and usually whoever uses up their 8 Rhinos first loses, so we need to include more cards that can trade with Rhinos or stop Crashing Footfalls from resolving, such as Fury, Murktide Regent, and counterspells. Sometimes even Endurance is worth considering, because it can recycle Crashing Footfalls from the graveyard for reuse. Resource advantage is also one of the key points of the mirror. Flame of Anor performs well in the mirror, and sometimes you can also hold on to Lórien Revealed in hand to ensure late-game resource advantage.
Remember: make sure your own Crashing Footfalls resolves, stop the opponent's Crashing Footfalls, and maintain resource advantage, and leave the rest to your right hand~

##### Sideboarding Strategy

Basically on the draw you bring in Gemstone Caverns, then the next card finds a land. Land changes won't be repeated in other matchups.

|    Side Out     |      Side In        |
| :-------------: | :-----------------: |
| -4 Dead // Gone |       +2 Fury       |
|                 | +1 Mystical Dispute |
|                 |  +1 Flame of Anor   |

Dead // Gone isn't good in this matchup because Dead has almost no targets, and although Gone can effectively deal with a Rhino or temporarily deal with Murktide Regent, I don't think it's worth it. I see some people take out Subtlety but I don't, because the opponent also has Fury and Murktide Regent, and Subtlety can be an answer to those two cards as well as extra damage.

#### Rakdos Scam

![Rakdos Scam](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Crashing-Footballs/ec42d34a9c40.webp)

A matchup that is favorable for us, and usually our topdecks are better. The easiest way to block Undying + [Grief](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/e/6/e6befbc4-1320-4f26-bd9f-b1814fedda10.jpg?1626095577) is a pair of Rhinos. Although we will be forced to discard two cards, it doesn't matter, since our deck has 8 Cascade cards and we will always draw them. If they don't have a turn-one Undying + Grief, they are just an ordinary midrange deck: "destroy everything on the battlefield and ask them what it feels like to be hit by a Rhino horn." Subtlety and Fury perform well in this matchup, both disrupting their game plan, and like Murktide Regent they give us another way to win when we can't produce Rhinos.
In sideboarded games they will bring in Chalice of the Void, [Engineered Explosives](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/4/2/420bf1e9-f2ec-4dff-b540-e64de71e58be.jpg?1599709112), or [Hidetsugu Consumes All](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/8/e/8e910464-329a-4de1-930a-be85b1956676.jpg?1656453394) to fight our Rhinos, but we also have removal and other ways to win. The only problem may be [Sheoldred](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/d/6/d67be074-cdd4-41d9-ac89-0a0456c4e4b2.jpg?1674057568): its 4/5 body can perfectly trade with a Rhino, but we are prepared for it and aren't totally helpless.
All in all, our game plan is simple: "**Destroy everything, gain favorable resource trades through our cards, and win with the remaining resources.**" Don't be in a rush in this matchup, because it stays in our favor even late in the game.

##### Sideboarding Strategy

|       Side Out       |         Side In         |
| :------------------: | :---------------------: |
| -4 Force of Negation |         +2 Fury         |
| -3 Mystical Dispute  |    +1 Flame of Anor     |
|                      |       +1 Subtlety       |
|                      |      +2 Endurance       |
|                      | +1 Boseiju, Who Endures |

We choose to take out all of our counterspells and bring in more removal and card draw, because this matchup quickly enters a topdeck stage, and a counterspell isn't good enough when topdecked. I think Endurance in this matchup is purely a supporting role: it blocks most of their creatures, prevents them from using Scam's fake death, and finally recycles our own Rhinos. If you have a better card, Endurance isn't so necessary.

#### 4-Color Elementals

![4C](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Crashing-Footballs/49b289c6d2b4.webp)

Before [Up the Beanstalk](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/2/d/2d5e991f-23b2-4db0-a452-7755125b1fd2.jpg?1692939184) was included, I thought this matchup was good. Early on they needed to spend more than two cards' worth of resources to answer our Rhinos, and this resource gap could keep us ahead throughout the game, so even if we didn't win early, as long as we kept The One Ring from landing we could let the opponent die slowly through the resource gap.
However, since Up the Beanstalk started being included, this matchup has changed. Their ability to catch up on resources early is far better than before, and we also find it hard to stop the opponent from casting Up the Beanstalk early. In my experience, if the opponent has only one Up the Beanstalk, then we are competing on the same level, and if the opponent has two or even more, the matchup is very bad for us. Because of this, we have to end the game quickly early. In the midgame we still have a chance to finish the match, but a little later and our win rate drops sharply.

##### Sideboarding Strategy

|      Side Out      |       Side In       |
| :----------------: | :-----------------: |
|  -4 Dead // Gone   |       +2 Fury       |
| -1 Murktide Regent |  +1 Flame of Anor   |
|   -4 Fire // Ice   |     +1 Subtlety     |
|                    |    +3 Blood Moon    |
|                    | +1 Mystical Dispute |

The reason for removing Dead // Gone is the same as in the mirror: it barely helps in the matchup. Murktide Regent is too easily answered in this matchup, so it comes out. I think Fire // Ice is also not good in this matchup: Fire can't effectively remove their creatures, and Ice is somewhat weak if you don't cast it on turn two.

#### Yawgmoth

![Yawgmoth](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Crashing-Footballs/c564f3897e76.webp)

Although Yawgmoth has received big boosts from several rounds of new cards, the essence of the deck hasn't changed; it just operates in a more complicated and flashy way.
I think this matchup comes down to three key points: first, understand how the whole deck operates. [Agatha's Soul Cauldron](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/0/1/019b51b0-e5c6-4208-922b-7736686dddcd.jpg?1692939838) brings the deck more ways to win, so be sure to understand all the ways this deck operates, lest you get shot in the face by a flashy sequence of plays from the opponent with no defense. Second, know that the early strength of this deck depends on the number of creatures on the battlefield, so in theory our goal is to kill their creatures with everything we have, giving us time to slam down our Rhinos and put us ahead. Finally, "**do everything to stop the Doctor from landing**." The problem with the Doctor isn't that it can make an infinite combo, but that it earns a large amount of resources while reducing your creatures' toughness. As long as the Doctor doesn't land, this matchup is definitely easy.
In summary: kill their creatures, stop the Doctor from landing, slam down our Rhinos, pressure their life total, then force them into trades that are unfavorable for them, and finally win.

#### Tron

![Tron](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Crashing-Footballs/93439bac45ea.webp)

I don't like running into this matchup, but it isn't as bad as you'd imagine. The quality of what they slam down is far greater than ours, and if we don't counter their cards, the balance of victory quickly tips to them. Also, because we need to leave mana open every turn to handle the opponent's threats, we won't necessarily have a window to make a Rhino and apply pressure. In the end, we get crushed by Tron's high-quality cards with insufficient disruption in hand and no Rhino. This is also why Obsidian Charmaw is great in this matchup, because it can do both "disrupt" and "apply pressure."
Therefore, what we can do is to slam down a Rhino early when we have FON or Subtlety in hand, so that we can both disrupt early and pressure with the Rhino.
