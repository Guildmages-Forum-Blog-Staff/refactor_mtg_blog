Avatar: The Last Airbender holds its prerelease events on November 14, 2025. Want to learn more about card ratings and advice for Limited? Then check out this series of TLA Limited overviews!

- For the mechanics, see this article: [Avatar: The Last Airbender Mechanics Explained](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/TLA-Mechanism/)

Avatar: The Last Airbender Eternal (TLE) is a supplementary subset of the main set TLA. It contains 61 borderless non-foil cards called Source Material, made from anime screenshots, and you can open one in every 26 regular Play Boosters. They can be used in TLA Limited, as well as in the corresponding formats where the card was originally legal.

In addition, these 61 borderless versions have an extra Easter egg: at the very bottom of the card, to the right of the language abbreviation, there's a coordinate that lets players look back at which season and episode of the anime the screenshot comes from.

{% mtgpick "tle" 13 %}

Take this Force of Negation as an example. The scene is from the last episode of the anime's third season, where Aang, awakened as the Avatar, takes away Ozai's bending. Here's how to read the coordinates:

- "**B**" stands for Book, meaning which season. B3 is the third season, and there are three seasons in total
- Each season has an element as its subtitle: the first season is Water (**Water**), the second is Earth (**Earth**), and the third is Fire (**Fire**)
- "**CH**" stands for Chapter, meaning which episode. Here it's episode 61

Alright, now readers know how to tell where a screenshot scene comes from. When you have time, go rewatch the anime! New friends are also very welcome to learn more about the story!

## Rating Scale

5.0: Unconditional first pick, brings a near-winning advantage
4.5: Unconditional first pick if you're in the right color pair, brings a huge advantage
4.0: Worth a first pick, great synergy with the color pair's theme, can be the deciding card for splashing / switching colors or entering the color pair
3.5: Top uncommons and commons of the set and other strong enough cards, good synergy with the color pair's theme, can be a key card for entering the color pair
3.0: Important cards for the color pair's theme and other above-average cards, you'll want them in your final deck
2.5: Average, usually a decent curve filler
2.0 to 1.0: Below average, basically leaning toward not playing; can be used as filler when necessary, but not too many
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable, basically don't play it, it will only help you lose

## Conclusion
Many old friends are reprinted here, along with some long-unseen old flames, and there are even Three Kingdoms cards, which is honestly quite impressive. The one flaw is a pity: the art is shared with the main set. But TLE's overall power level is very high, and if you're lucky enough to draw or open them, they will certainly bring you a great advantage.
