Outlaws of Thunder Junction (OTJ) holds its prerelease events on April 12, 2024, and officially releases a week later on April 19, 2024. Want to learn more about individual card ratings in limited and some possibly fun synergies? Then check out this series of OTJ limited guides!

For the mechanics, see this article: [OTJ Mechanics Explained](https://guildmagesforum.tw/OTJ-mechanism/)

## Rating Scale

5.0: Unconditional must-pick
4.0: First-pick quality
3.5: Strong enough, but not necessarily a first pick
3.0: Above average (you'll really want it in your final deck)
2.5: Average, maybe around 6th or 7th pick in a draft
2.0 to 1.0: Below average (you'd rather not play it than play it)
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable (basically never play it; it will only help you lose)

The Plot design happens to solve the problem of the original Snapcaster Mage not having enough mana in one turn: even if there's no suitable instant or sorcery in the graveyard right now, you can Plot it out first and call it when the graveyard has a suitable play. As long as you've picked up enough instant-speed removal, this is a card advantage point I'd like to see. Also, casting it from exile and giving flashback to an instant or sorcery lets you make two spells from a single card, which also helps cast-multiple-spells-in-a-turn cards work.

Plot happens to solve the pain point of creatures like [Clone](https://scryfall.com/card/m14/47/clone): getting stuck in hand early with no good creature to copy, then coming down late when a quality creature finally shows up, which is slow and makes awkward use of mana. Now you can Plot it for three and set it aside, and cast it for free the moment you have a quality creature to copy, instantly widening your board advantage. It also keeps the Outlaw type, so it can enjoy more synergies.

![271](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/f/d/fd17e8d4-499e-4005-ae3c-bc9c44dc5a67.jpg?1711575181)

**Jru:** 4.0

The beauty of this card is that its first two abilities are both [+1]. Continual card filtering or Plotting helps quite a bit with consistency, and the second ability's Plot in particular is exactly the core of how some blue approaches run.

Remember you can skip the second ability: when your hand is too good to loot or you have no hand and don't want to effectively mill yourself, just choose to skip the second ability. As long as you can stall until you can use the [-6] and cast two spells, that's basically enough value.

## Conclusion

The officially designated limited themes for each color pair in this set are:

- White-blue: didn't cast a spell this turn
- Black-blue: commit a crime
- Red-blue: cast multiple spells in one turn
- Green-blue: Plot with high mana value

As you can see, apart from blue-black, the themes of the other color pairs all run almost entirely on Plot, so when picking blue material in this set's limited, if I consider things from the theme level, I care more about Plot-related cards and about the survival tools that help you get through the early Plot gap.
