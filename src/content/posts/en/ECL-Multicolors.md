## Color Pair Themes

![ECL draft five color pairs card image](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/ECL-Multicolors/26397927c95f.webp)

### <span style="color:#C3B270">White</span>-<span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span> Merfolk

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/ECL-Multicolors/1fe7b2cc1ccc.webp)

The Merfolk theme excels at using the **Convoke** mechanic to tap creatures, and thereby **triggering the effects Merfolk have when they become tapped**. {% mtglink "Merrow Skyswimmer" %} has a chance to enter early as long as you cast a Merfolk in the first few turns, and gives you more creatures to help with convoking later. {% mtglink "Deepchannel Duelist" %}'s own lord effect (other creatures of the same type get +1/+1), besides strengthening the Merfolk on your board, can also untap a target Merfolk to do an extra convoke or tap effect. {% mtglink "Wanderwine Farewell" %}, a Merfolk Kindred sorcery, becomes easier to cast the more Merfolk you have, and can send out even more Merfolk. And {% mtglink "Tributary Vaulter" %}, as a common Merfolk, has an extra effect whenever it becomes tapped, and there are many more Merfolk cards like this in White-Blue.

### <span style="color:#808080">Black</span>-<span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span> Goblins

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/ECL-Multicolors/15a34a99ee83.webp)

The Goblin theme interacts closely with the **Blight** mechanic. You need some **cheap fertilizer** to get rewards from putting -1/-1 counters or from creatures dying. Beyond being a three-mana 3/2 curve filler, {% mtglink "Chaos Spewer" %} is a super high-quality 5/4 body if you have some fertilizer. {% mtglink "Boggart Cursecrafter" %} turns your fertilizer into extra damage when it dies. If the Goblin Kindred enchantment {% mtglink "Boggart Mischief" %} does Blight 1 when it enters, it can send out two 1/1 Goblins as fertilizer, and it also comes with a drain effect when Goblins die. Finally, the common {% mtglink "Elder Auntie" %} is Goblin fertilizer that turns one card into two bodies.

### <span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span>-<span style="color:#C3B270">White</span> Kithkin

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/ECL-Multicolors/a29f300d93b5.webp)

The Kithkin theme encourages you to **go wide**, and many Kithkin cards **get bonuses from other creatures entering**. For example, {% mtglink "Wary Farmer" %} can surveil 1 extra at end of turn because a creature entered, and with {% mtglink "Thoughtweft Lieutenant" %} on the battlefield, every time a Kithkin enters it can strengthen your creatures and give them trample. The Kithkin Kindred enchantment {% mtglink "Clachan Festival" %} sends out two 1/1 Kithkin when it enters, and in the mid to late game, with enough mana, it can keep serving as your arsenal. And once you have enough Kithkin, you can cast {% mtglink "Mistmeadow Council" %} for a low cost and refill your hand.

### <span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span>-<span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span> Elementals

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/ECL-Multicolors/e80bbf9c5b5e.webp)

The Elemental theme focuses on **casting spells with mana value 4 or more**. Don't forget that things like Convoke, the Vivid mechanic, and even the five mythic Evoke Elemental cards have a mana value of 4 or more when cast, but you actually have chances to cast them for less mana. Elementals like {% mtglink "Flaring Cinder" %} and {% mtglink "Kulrath Mystic" %} get extra effects when you cast a spell with mana value 4 or more, and if {% mtglink "Twinflame Travelers" %} is also on the battlefield, they trigger an additional time. As for the Elemental Kindred sorcery {% mtglink "Kindle the Inner Flame" %}, besides being a four-drop itself, its Flashback design lets you use it one more time, keeping your Elemental theme running.

### <span style="color:#808080">Black</span>-<span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span> Elves

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/ECL-Multicolors/3c8edcaf63f1.webp)

The Elf theme **focuses on the graveyard**. Elf cards like {% mtglink "Scarblade Scout" %} all have self-**mill** effects, which helps fill the graveyard faster, and milling cards like {% mtglink "Stoic Grove-Guide" %} gives extra resource returns even from the graveyard. The Elf Kindred enchantment {% mtglink "Morcant's Eyes" %} surveils 1 each turn to fill the graveyard or make your next draw better, and in the mid to late game you can even sacrifice it and send out a large number of Elf Warriors! And besides being an Elf lord, {% mtglink "Morcant's Loyalist" %} can even pick up Elf cards to your hand to reuse when it dies, and it can even pick up Kindred Elf cards too!

## Rating Scale

> **Note**: This ECL Limited format has high requirements for tribal pairings within color pairs. If a creature card has the tribe of that color pair's theme (e.g. Merfolk, Goblins, Kithkin, Elementals, Elves), the rating already reflects its strength in that color pair.

5.0: Unconditional must-pick, brings a near-winning advantage
4.5: Unconditional must-pick if you're in the right colors, brings a huge advantage
4.0: First-pick value, extremely good synergy with the color pair theme, can be the deciding card for splashing / switching colors or moving into a color pair
3.5: Top uncommons, commons, and other sufficiently strong singles in the set, with good synergy with the color pair theme, can be a key card for moving into a color pair
3.0: Important single of the color pair theme, and other above-average singles, you'd want it in your final deck
2.5: Average level, usually a decent curve filler
2.0～1.0: Below average, generally lean toward not playing it, can be used as filler if necessary, but not too many
0.5～0.0: Unplayable, basically don't play it, playing it will only help you lose

Comment: These five are three-mana hybrid creatures of the five featured tribes from [Eclipsed Realms](https://scryfall.com/card/ecl/263/eclipsed-realms). Their function is to look at the top 4 cards of the library on entering and choose one basic land type card of the corresponding color (that's right, besides basic lands you can also take a shock land of the corresponding type!) or one card of that tribe. Since Kindred cards also have a tribe type, if those four cards include a Kindred card of the corresponding tribe or a Changeling, you can take that to hand too. In a reasonable tribal-theme build, the vast majority of the time these creatures' enter abilities are better than simply blindly drawing a card, which fits the definition of 3.5.

Also, there's a point to talking about these five cards together, and it's definitely not because I'm being lazy XD The cost and stats of these cards also reveal a lot of information about the themes:

- The <span style="color:#808080">Black</span>-<span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span> Goblin has a 2/3 body because of its interaction with the Blight theme, and higher toughness helps it be a target of Blight multiple times.
- The <span style="color:#808080">Black</span>-<span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span> Elf has a 3/2 body because the Elf theme cares about the number of Elves in the graveyard, and low toughness helps it go to the graveyard after trading.
- The <span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span>-<span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span> Elemental has a 1/4 body because the Elemental theme cares about whether you cast spells with mana value 4 or more, and since the theme is slower it needs creatures with higher toughness to help you get through early combat.
- The <span style="color:#C3B270">White</span>-<span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span> Merfolk also has a 2/3 body, related to Merfolk tap trigger abilities, and higher toughness helps it survive after combat, or to cast instant-speed Convoke spells after blocking.
- The <span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span>-<span style="color:#C3B270">White</span> Kithkin has a 2/1 body, highlighting that the creatures of this theme are individually weak, but costing only two mana encourages you to lower your curve and win through the board rather than relying on individual creature power.

As for cost, the Blue-Red pair allows a colorless mana as the third mana, which hints at the possibility of Blue-Red mixing in a third color, while the other four tribes all use hybrid mana. This design is easy to cast in a pure two-color deck, but unfriendly to mixing in a third color. When building a non-Elemental theme, you must think carefully before mixing in a third color, so as not to weaken the stability of that color pair.

## Conclusion

Drafts locked into five tribal themes have been rare in recent years (not counting [SPM](https://scryfall.com/sets/spm)), and returning to Lorwyn this time is a bold attempt by the design team. However, are these the only five possibilities? Quite a lot of creatures of different tribes were still printed. Are they all not worth using, or just lower-pick filler?

I don't think so. In an ECL draft, when the five clearly themed tribes have already been taken by others, the remaining players aren't left with only the road to defeat. The Vivid theme cards encourage you to move toward multicolor, and color fixing isn't just a power unique to green. When you turn your eyes to colorless cards, you'll find very different discoveries! Without the support of the two-color land bases players are used to, ECL's multicolor Vivid is surely a path full of challenges, with potentially quite rich rewards.

And besides that, are there other powerful but still unknown lines of thought? There definitely are! Let's explore ECL Limited together!
