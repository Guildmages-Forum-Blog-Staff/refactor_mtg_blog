### Echo of Eons! Your nightmare tonight!!!

#### Where the inspiration came from!

Right after MH3 came out, Dimir Murktide would run flashback cards like [Deep Analysis](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/268/deep-analysis) that can be cast from the graveyard, using the ability of [Psychic Frog](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/199/psychic-frog) to increase the payoff of filling the graveyard. But they didn't work well and were dropped, so I started looking for a good flashback card.

Also, while testing I noticed that the deck is at a significant disadvantage against all sorts of aggro decks, or gets locked out by all sorts of control decks. The weakness of Dimir is also obvious: once the creatures don't show up or get removed, your hand is full of counterspells and removal, and you win very slowly.

I had always wanted to play the [Narset, Parter of Veils](https://scryfall.com/card/war/61/narset-parter-of-veils) + Echo of Eons combo in Modern but never had the chance. With Psychic Frog arriving in MH3, I realized it could be a component of this combo, but in testing I found that, rather than drawing seven and letting the opponent draw one, I could just shoot the opponent dead with [Orcish Bowmasters](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/103/orcish-bowmasters)!?

On August 26, 2024, [Grief](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/87/grief) was banned, which made the main deck [Subtlety](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/67/subtlety) non-essential, and the freed-up slots could be used to experiment with a new toy!

#### Why play this combo?

Because of love! Because it's fun! ~~Because I want to blow up the opponent with the archers!~~

The above is part of the reason, yes, but another part is wanting to improve Dimir Murktide against the problem of winning too slowly when Murktide and/or Frog get removed. Adding the [Echo of Eons](https://scryfall.com/card/mh1/46/echo-of-eons) combo gives the deck another dimension of winning, a powerful card that can turn the game around in topdeck mode when both sides have traded away their resources.

#### Combo explanation

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Dimir-Murktide/c93ed3297650.webp)

Basically, alongside the 4 [Orcish Bowmasters](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/103/orcish-bowmasters) that Dimir Murktide already plays, we add 1 extra [Sheoldred, the Apocalypse](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/107/sheoldred-the-apocalypse), for a total of 5 powerful creatures that deal extra damage whenever the opponent draws. Then 2 copies of [Echo of Eons](https://scryfall.com/card/mh1/46/echo-of-eons) shuffle your hand and graveyard into your library and draw 7. As long as one of the above creatures is on the battlefield when [Echo of Eons](https://scryfall.com/card/mh1/46/echo-of-eons) resolves, you get the following combo:
- [Orcish Bowmasters](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/103/orcish-bowmasters): deal 7 damage in total to any targets, divided however you like, and Amass 7 at the same time.
- [Sheoldred, the Apocalypse](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/107/sheoldred-the-apocalypse): the opponent loses 14 life and we gain 14 life, simple and brutal.

In the Bowmasters case above, if you already have an Amass 1 + Orc Army token from an earlier Bowmasters, you will pump it to 8/8 or bigger as well. And as long as you did this in your first main phase, that giant can then attack, and if things go well, deal about as much damage as the Sheoldred case.

Then, don't forget these effects stack. With two or more Bowmasters, the joy doubles!

#### Why 2 copies of Echo of Eons?

Although it is a combo, it uses 3 flexible slots of Dimir as combo pieces, and we can't play more without hurting the deck's structure. In addition, Echo of Eons is not essential to the deck's makeup; its role is to give us an unfair way to win, or a way to widen our advantage all at once. We assume it works best as a card that turns the game around, or even seals it, once we get into topdeck mode. So we don't need to play a full set, since a full set would only clog your hand, and 1 copy is no better than none, so we settled on 2 as a reasonable number.

#### Isn't Sheoldred too clunky?

True, at four mana she can be answered by the common [Fatal Push](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/93/fatal-push) (with revolt), or by [Unholy Heat](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/145/unholy-heat) after delirium, among others. But we don't mainly win through [Sheoldred](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/107/sheoldred-the-apocalypse); the single copy is there to raise the combo's chance of winning. After discussion we agreed that an extra Sheoldred makes our combo win more comfortable, and a Sheoldred resolved in topdeck mode is deadly, giving us more haters against the opponent's [The One Ring](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/246/the-one-ring), which is extremely important in the current environment. Her draw-punishing life gain is also something Dimir Murktide badly needs. So our conclusion is that Sheoldred is not too clunky, and she fits perfectly into our game plan, further raising the deck's power.

#### How to use Echo of Eons

In our build, there are several ways to cast Echo of Eons:

- Discard it with the ability of [Psychic Frog](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/199/psychic-frog), then cast it with flashback. (Frog on turn two, Bowmasters on turn three, Echo of Eons on turn four! Awesome!)
- Put it in the graveyard with the surveil effects of [Consider](https://scryfall.com/card/clu/84/consider) or [Undercity Sewers](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/270/undercity-sewers), then cast it with flashback.
- Hard-cast it for six mana, and cast it again if needed.
- If there's no time, feed it to [FON](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/50/force-of-negation) or [Subtlety](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/67/subtlety) (though I don't play it).

Of these, apart from the first two, which cheat on mana a bit, it is worth explaining the hard-cast for six. This usually happens because the card is stuck in hand with no other discard outlet, but it can also be a mid-to-late-game topdeck. With that in mind, I usually don't mindlessly feed lands to the Frog, unless I am very sure the game will end quickly, so that I keep the flexibility of hard-casting for six when I draw Echo of Eons later with no Frog to discard it to.

#### Other fun interactions

By the way, besides drawing 7, doesn't Echo of Eons also shuffle your graveyard back into your library? In our build, this effect leads to a few interesting things:

- When we have [Murktide Regent](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/52/murktide-regent) on the battlefield, every instant/sorcery card we shuffle back puts one more +1/+1 counter on Murktide!
- When we have [Psychic Frog](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/199/psychic-frog) on the battlefield, remember to respond to the shuffle effect when casting Echo of Eons by discarding the cards you can't use to the froggy, then fly away as needed, since you are about to draw 7 anyway!
- When we have an important card in the graveyard that we want to reuse, shuffling it back might just get it drawn among the 7!
- When we have been milled until our graveyard is bigger than our library, don't worry: Ech-o of E-ons! Whoa! Suddenly the library is tall again! Amazing grace!

### Are there any fixed slots?

Besides the [7 combo pieces mentioned earlier](#combo-explanation), the following are the parts of the Dimir Murktide build that I won't touch:

- 3 [Murktide Regent](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/52/murktide-regent): the centerpiece of the deck. We don't play a fourth because we are not the other version with [Thought Scour](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/65/thought-scour), the fast Murktide, and 3 is enough.
- 4 [Psychic Frog](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/199/psychic-frog): the Frog is too strong, always a full set.
- 4 [Counterspell](https://scryfall.com/card/cmm/81/counterspell): Counterspell is always a full set.
- 2 [Force of Negation](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/50/force-of-negation): 2 is the minimum, and 3 is the usual count.
- 4 [Fatal Push](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/93/fatal-push): always a full set. It is black's best removal; without it, don't bother playing against energy.
- 2 [Spell Snare](https://scryfall.com/card/dis/33/spell-snare): a quality card with tons of targets in Modern besides Eldrazi. 2 is the minimum, and 3 is the usual count.
- 3 [Preordain](https://scryfall.com/card/m11/70/preordain): usually a full set, but I swapped one for [Lórien Revealed](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/60/l%C3%B3rien-revealed).
- 1 [Sink into Stupor](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/241/sink-into-stupor-soporific-springs): it can be a land or a soft removal/counter. Although [高橋優太 thought three weeks ago that this card is not very good and that an Island is better](https://cardrush-media.com/mtg-yutatakahashi-20240803/), I find it quite good, and our build wants more lands anyway.
- 2 [Shoot the Sheriff](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/106/shoot-the-sheriff): these 2 slots are for hard removal and can be adjusted by the meta, but it has to be 2 copies.
- 1 [Toxic Deluge](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/277/toxic-deluge): one main-deck sweeper to keep things safe.
- 1 [Cling to Dust](https://scryfall.com/card/thb/87/cling-to-dust): this card might be the only good-luck charm exclusive to Modern Dimir. It gains life, draws cards, and hoses graveyards.

The cards above are ones I would absolutely never cut from the main deck so far. They make up the deck and its important new pieces.

### How to choose the flexible slots?

- The third [Spell Snare](https://scryfall.com/card/dis/33/spell-snare) is added against the common two-mana threats in the environment.
- 1 [Spell Pierce](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/63/spell-pierce) replaces the usual third FON, because good players will basically play around your FON, and when you keep one mana open, sometimes this Spell Pierce is worth far more than a third FON. In the mirror or against other counter-heavy control decks, this Spell Pierce can often be a surprise ambush!
- 1 [Lórien Revealed](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/60/l%C3%B3rien-revealed) replaces the fourth [Preordain](https://scryfall.com/card/m11/70/preordain). Since most of our early Preordains are used to find lands, a Lórien Revealed that can find lands at instant speed is something I would rather have. And since our deck wants to hit land drops steadily and keep six mana available to hard-cast Echo of Eons, five-mana hard-cast draw 3 in the mid to late game is great flexibility to have when needed, even though we don't expect to go that long.
- Whether to play [Shoot the Sheriff](https://scryfall.com/card/otj/106/shoot-the-sheriff) or other removal is worth discussing, but considering that energy, mirrors and Eldrazi are the mainstream in the current meta, two-mana hard removal is still quite reliable. Compared with [Go for the Throat](https://scryfall.com/card/moc/250/go-for-the-throat), which may miss artifact creatures, the only mainstream creature Shoot the Sheriff can't kill right now is [Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/138/ragavan-nimble-pilferer), but it shouldn't be something we worry about, as it basically doesn't survive. Some people play [Drown in the Loch](https://scryfall.com/card/clb/842/drown-in-the-loch) in this slot, but I don't want a removal spell with a somewhat higher ceiling but an extremely low floor. Face a mirror with a Frog on the battlefield and you'll understand what I mean.
- The 2 [Harbinger of the Seas](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/63/harbinger-of-the-seas) in the main deck improve our bad matchups, such as Eldrazi, Mardu Energy, and opponents who don't dutifully fetch basic lands. Hitting Amulet Titan and Domain Zoo is just a bonus XD.

## Game Play

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Dimir-Murktide/21cfbc24f19a.webp)

### Understanding the tempo of Dimir Murktide

When to rush? When to turtle? When to go all-in with your hand to the Frog? When to tap out for Murktide?

These are the fated homework and soul-searching questions for every Murktide player. Different situations call for different plays, and getting it wrong can be the difference between winning and losing. There are simply too many scenarios, and different decks call for different responses. Below I only explain a few perhaps more common ones. You still need a lot of practice to grasp the tempo, and I am still learning too.

#### On the play, you don't have to Frog on turn two

When facing a mirror, if you are on the play with a Frog in hand but no FON, and the opponent has a fetchland that can find a blue or black land, it is possible that when you cast Frog they counter it with [Spell Snare](https://scryfall.com/card/dis/33/spell-snare), or kill it with [Fatal Push](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/93/fatal-push) after it resolves. That would cost you tempo, and it may be better to pass while holding the cards that can counter or answer the opponent's turn-two Frog. Of course, if you have both [Orcish Bowmasters](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/103/orcish-bowmasters) and Frog in hand, and you both pass, you can jump out Bowmasters, see how the opponent responds, and then cast Frog on your own turn three while holding Snare or [Spell Pierce](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/63/spell-pierce). Even if nothing resolves successfully after that round, Murktide's arrival is not far off.

#### When to go all-in with your hand on the Frog?

Basically, although the Frog can draw cards and fly, without pumping it, chipping away one point at a time is far too inefficient.

Generally, I suggest pumping the Frog to at least 2/3 to speed up the clock, and against decks with red removal, pumping it to 3/4 to survive at least one [Lightning Bolt](https://scryfall.com/card/clu/141/lightning-bolt), or a [Galvanic Discharge](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/122/galvanic-discharge) without extra energy. And against black opponents with [Fatal Push](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/93/fatal-push), keeping it at 2/3 and jumping up when the chance arises is the recommended play.

Of course, if you are sure the opponent's hand is full of counterspells with no removal, then go all-in boldly! The window to win doesn't wait for anyone!

#### You basically can't Murktide on turn two

We don't have quick graveyard-stuffing tools like [Thought Scour](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/65/thought-scour) as the fast Murktide does. Cards go into our graveyard one by one, so generally, casting Murktide on turn three while holding Spell Pierce or FON, or on turn four while holding Counterspell, is the more common and steady approach.

> In response to fast Murktide mirrors, [Subtlety](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/67/subtlety) has returned to this type of Dimir Murktide build, which is bad for us in spots where we have no Counterspell when casting Murktide. Don't forget that the faster the environment (for example, the meta when its siblings [Grief](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/87/grief) and [Fury](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/126/fury) were still around), the better Subtlety performs. That is no exception for fast Murktide.

#### Landing a big threat after resources are spent often settles the game

This refers not only to mirrors and counter wars between control decks, but also to facing an opponent with lots of removal who has killed all your early threats. At that point, tapping out to resolve a Murktide or Sheoldred, or even a [Lórien Revealed](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/60/l%C3%B3rien-revealed), can swing the game your way. Of course, the opponent's [The One Ring](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/246/the-one-ring) or other threats may be sharpening their knives in hand or on top of the library. However, sometimes, if the known resources on the table have been traded roughly evenly, resolving your own threat early is always reasonable.

But how do you know they have really been traded evenly? That depends on accumulated experience and on-the-spot reactions. Deeper mind games aren't discussed in this article; you can imagine more similar situations and ask yourself!

### Understanding the tournament meta

After understanding yourself, we need to understand your opponents.

Based on the tournament meta listed in the [Changes in Modern](#changes-in-modern) section above, before entering a tournament, what I do is take the top 15 to 20 decks of the past 7 to 14 days, pick the best-performing deck in MO Challenges or even bigger events to look at, see whether they have added anything new, and think about whether my main deck and sideboard need adjusting. For the top few decks I look at a few more lists to make sure no tech has slipped through and put up results.

This is only the most basic, armchair-player routine, but quite effective. After you have seen these decklists, you can think about the strategic plan in each matchup, which hands to keep or mulligan, how to plan sideboarding, and even adjust your main deck and sideboard to some degree.

This is only a big-picture concept; the practice is up to each person.

### Gameplan: better to push forward with one thought than to stop at one thought

I quote a line from the movie *The Grandmaster* as the section title, mainly hoping readers realize how important a far-sighted game plan, and sticking to it after deep thought, truly is!

Imagine you have only two untapped lands that make blue on the battlefield, Murktide in hand, and 4 cards in the graveyard. You have activated a fetchland and found a surveil land, planning to surveil and then cast Murktide. When you surveil, you see a card you need on top of the library, such as [Psychic Frog](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/199/psychic-frog) or [Counterspell](https://scryfall.com/card/cmm/81/counterspell). If you **stop** here, and, possessed, tell yourself it would be nice to keep this card and leave it on top, then when you come to your senses you realize: oh no! My Murktide can't come down this turn...

If you have already decided you will cast Murktide this turn, why would you trip yourself up? If you predict, or know, that the opponent has a counterspell or Subtlety in hand, you wouldn't choose to cast Murktide, unless you want to use it as bait... This kind of thinking three steps ahead is what distills your own gameplan. Once you have thought it through, unless a change significant enough to alter the gameplan occurs, we should stick to our gameplan as much as possible.

The above is only a very decontextualized example, but decisions like this are situations every Murktide player meets often. We must keep asking ourselves and strengthen ourselves through deep practice.

## Matchup Tips

### Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury 

You have a Frog on the battlefield, the opponent hard-casts Phlage, and they have four mana open to escape it afterward. You should first activate the Frog's ability to get a trigger, then counter Phlage, and then, in response to your own Frog ability, before it resolves, use graveyard hate to exile Phlage, so you don't give the opponent priority to escape it.

## Sideboard Strategy

### Boros Energy

| Side Out | Side In |
| :-: | :-: |
| -2 {% mtgcard "Force of Negation" tooltip=true %} | +2 {% mtgcard "Stern Scolding" "ltr" tooltip=true %} |
| -1 {% mtgcard "Sink into Stupor // Soporific Springs" tooltip=true %} | +1 {% mtgcard "The Meathook Massacre" "mid" tooltip=true %} |
| -1 {% mtgcard "Spell Pierce" tooltip=true %} | +1 {% mtgcard "Legion's End" "m20" tooltip=true %} |
| -3 {% mtgcard "Preordain" tooltip=true %} | +1 {% mtgcard "Toxic Deluge" "mh3" tooltip=true %} |
|  | +2 {% mtgcard "Surgical Extraction" tooltip=true %} |

Basically, we take out the FON, which loses cards, and the {% mtgcard "Spell Pierce" tooltip=true %}, which is weak against their small threats, and bring in more sweepers. The sideboard {% mtgcard "Legion's End" "m20" tooltip=true %} is both removal and disruption in one, and works quite satisfyingly. With this swap we only have four real Counterspells left to deal with the opponent's hard-to-handle Ring and Phlage, so Counterspell should basically be saved for those, while creatures are handled with sweepers and removal. {% mtgcard "Surgical Extraction" tooltip=true %} exiles the opponent's Ring, Phlage, or important two-mana threats like {% mtgcard "Ajani, Nacatl Pariah // Ajani, Nacatl Avenger" tooltip=true %} and {% mtgcard "Amped Raptor" tooltip=true %} when they hit the graveyard, reducing our later worries. But we still need to keep watching the key cards; even with one side cut away, the opponent can still beat us with the remaining threats.

In addition, {% mtgcard "Spell Snare" tooltip=true %} is still the MVP of this matchup.

|  | +2 {% mtgcard "Surgical Extraction" tooltip=true %} |

The sideboarding is the same as against Boros Energy, but Mardu plays {% mtgcard "Fatal Push" "2xm" tooltip=true %}, {% mtgcard "Thoughtseize" "2xm" tooltip=true %}, and {% mtgcard "Orcish Bowmasters" "ltr" tooltip=true %}, which target us more. This also makes our combo easier for the opponent to interrupt, so when we go for the combo we must watch whether the opponent might jump out a Bowmasters.

Don't forget, when both players are at 7 life or less and we do the Echo of Eons combo and get responded to with a Bowmasters, our Bowmasters effect goes on the stack first and the opponent's goes on after it. On resolution, the ability put on the stack last resolves first, so we get shot dead by the opponent's Bowmasters first XD

### Jeskai Control

| Side Out | Side In |
| :-: | :-: |

In this matchup, {% mtgcard "Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury" "mh3" tooltip=true %} basically has to be countered and then exiled, and we try not to let it resolve at all. If it does resolve, {% mtgcard "Consign to Memory" "mh3" tooltip=true %} can counter its life-gain effect, while we also watch {% mtgcard "The One Ring" "ltr" tooltip=true %} and the enter-the-battlefield effects of the white-blue dual-element cards. Our creatures are basically better than theirs apart from Phlage, so if we hold the ground we can push back.

{% mtgcard "Spell Snare" tooltip=true %} in this matchup basically can only target the opponent's Counterspell, so considering its value we keep only 1 copy.

### Goryo's Vengeance

| Side Out | Side In |
| :-: | :-: |
| -1 {% mtgcard "Sheoldred, the Apocalypse" "dmu" tooltip=true %} | +2 {% mtgcard "Nihil Spellbomb" tooltip=true %} |
| -3 {% mtgcard "Preordain" tooltip=true %} | +2 {% mtgcard "Surgical Extraction" tooltip=true %} |
| -1 {% mtgcard "Murktide Regent" "mh2" tooltip=true %} | +1 {% mtgcard "Legion's End" "m20" tooltip=true %} |

Take out the slower Sheoldred and 1 Murktide, take out the sorcery-speed Preordain, and bring in 4 graveyard hate cards and 1 {% mtgcard "Legion's End" "m20" tooltip=true %}.

Don't forget, as mentioned earlier, our Echo of Eons can shuffle away the opponent's graveyard. However, in this matchup every time you tap out you risk paying a painful price. Even if you have FON, the opponent will usually play around it, or clear the way with Thoughtseize and then follow up with Goryo's, so the key in this matchup is not to let the opponent's graveyard hold any threat. Prioritize Goryo's with Surgical Extraction to stay safe.

Today's Goryo's lists play {% mtgcard "Emperor of Bones" "mh3" tooltip=true %} and {% mtgcard "Priest of Fell Rites" "mh2" tooltip=true %} as backup plans, but we never take out a single removal spell, and wait there for them.

You have to stay calm in this matchup. Sometimes cutting the opponent's {% mtgcard "Solitude" "mh2" tooltip=true %} to make sure your Murktide survives and ends the game quickly is also an option.

### Living End

| Side Out | Side In |
| :-: | :-: |
| -4 {% mtgcard "Fatal Push" "2xm" tooltip=true %} | +2 {% mtgcard "Nihil Spellbomb" tooltip=true %} |
| -3 {% mtgcard "Spell Snare" tooltip=true %} | +2 {% mtgcard "Surgical Extraction" tooltip=true %} |
|  | +3 {% mtgcard "Consign to Memory" "mh3" tooltip=true %} |


This matchup isn't that fast, so I won't take out Preordain, but even if we resolve one or two graveyard hate cards, we still won't win easily. Resolving an Echo of Eons early is equivalent to making the opponent's plan of activating {% mtgcard "Living End" "tsr" tooltip=true %} start over.

Although the opponent looks like a four-color deck at first glance, apart from the double Cascade ({% mtgcard "Shardless Agent" "mh2" tooltip=true %}, {% mtgcard "Ardent Plea" "arb" tooltip=true %}), they are not really stuck by Harbinger of the Seas turning all their lands into Islands, and the opponent can still leisurely fill the graveyard, then use bounce effects to return Harbinger to hand to Cascade, or even use {% mtgcard "Press the Enemy" "ltr" tooltip=true %} to bounce plus Living End from hand. If we resolve Harbinger of the Seas, we should remove the opponent's graveyard as soon as possible so the opponent can't calmly accumulate resources.

{% mtgcard "Teferi, Time Raveler" tooltip=true %} is still our lifelong enemy. We can't let him resolve, a high-priority Extraction target. {% mtgcard "Spell Pierce" tooltip=true %} may look a bit silly against a pile of free spells, but it still has to be kept for Teferi and the on-time turn-three Cascade into Living End. After all, the even worse {% mtgcard "Fatal Push" "2xm" tooltip=true %} and {% mtgcard "Spell Snare" tooltip=true %} have to be shown the door first.

Don't forget to watch out for the opponent's {% mtgcard "Colossal Skyturtle" "neo" tooltip=true %}.

### Yawgmoth (Golgari)

| Side Out | Side In |
| :-: | :-: |
| -1 {% mtgcard "Echo of Eons" "mh1" tooltip=true %} | +2 {% mtgcard "Stern Scolding" "ltr" tooltip=true %} |
| -2 {% mtgcard "Harbinger of the Seas" "mh3" tooltip=true %} | +2 {% mtgcard "Surgical Extraction" tooltip=true %} |
| -3 {% mtgcard "Preordain" tooltip=true %} | +1 {% mtgcard "Toxic Deluge" "mh3" tooltip=true %} |
| -1 {% mtgcard "Spell Pierce" tooltip=true %} | +1 {% mtgcard "Legion's End" "m20" tooltip=true %} |
|  | +1 {% mtgcard "The Meathook Massacre" "mid" tooltip=true %} |

This is a tough matchup. The opponent has a vast number of ways to win, while we must proceed carefully and answer with the most appropriate removal.

Our opening hand must be able to deal in time with {% mtgcard "Delighted Halfling" "ltr" tooltip=true %}, and the subsequent {% mtgcard "Agatha's Soul Cauldron" "woe" tooltip=true %}, {% mtgcard "Grist, the Hunger Tide" tooltip=true %}, and {% mtgcard "Yawgmoth, Thran Physician" tooltip=true %}. The turn-one Halfling must die, or what dies is the counterspells in our hand, and then us.

Don't forget, {% mtgcard "Grist, the Hunger Tide" tooltip=true %} is a creature card outside the battlefield, so we should use {% mtgcard "Stern Scolding" "ltr" tooltip=true %} to counter it, not FON or {% mtgcard "Spell Pierce" tooltip=true %}

Sometimes using Surgical Extraction to cut {% mtgcard "Young Wolf" "dka" tooltip=true %} can break the opponent's infinite combo, but no matter how bad things get, they still have a Plan B of pushing through on the ground, and then sweepers are crucial. {% mtgcard "The Meathook Massacre" "mid" tooltip=true %} is usually not too slow.

## Conclusion

Although my results went up and down along the way, I am very happy to have found friends to develop the combo with. Even after losing, we can go out for a meal and a walk together, then go home to keep practicing and think of new builds and sideboard strategies. Along the way I also met several impressive players. Losing doesn't matter; I stand behind them and watch how they play, follow the flow of their games, and think about how I would play each hand, and I have learned a lot of valuable experience from that.

Behind a single win there are often countless defeats. But so what? We are all still moving along the road of improvement! I hope this article gives some encouragement to interested players, and to friends who keep fighting in competitive formats but have yet to prove themselves! Good luck, everyone!
