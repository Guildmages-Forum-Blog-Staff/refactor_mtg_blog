The Lost Caverns of Ixalan (LCI) holds its prerelease events on November 10, 2023, and releases officially a week later on November 17, 2023. Want to learn more about how individual cards rate in Limited and some possibly fun synergies? Then check out this LCI Limited overview series!

For the mechanics, see this article: [The Lost Caverns of Ixalan Mechanics Explained](https://guildmagesforum.tw/LCI-mechanic/)

## Grading Scale

5.0: Must-pick, unconditionally
4.0: First-pick quality
3.5: Strong enough, but not necessarily a first pick
3.0: Above average (you'll really want it in your final deck)
2.5: Average, maybe around the 6th or 7th pick in a draft
2.0～1.0: Below average (you'd rather not play it than play it)
0.5～0.0: Unplayable (basically don't play it, it will only help you lose)

**Jru**: 3.5

Instant-speed, two mana for 3 damage. As cheap removal there's nothing to complain about, and since LCI has an extremely high density of artifacts, to the point you could say it rivals a real artifact environment, the ability to remove artifacts is worth more here than in a regular set.

By the way, Abrade seems to be on its way to becoming a standard evergreen common like Negate and Duress.

**Jru**: 1.5

Just a very ordinary combat trick. Because of the Discover mechanic, combat tricks, which depend heavily on timing, may become even harder to use. Also worth mentioning: a number of Craft cards in this set use exiling artifacts as the cost, and besides producing mana as usual, Treasure tokens can also be used as Craft material.

**Jru**: 3.0

As a two-drop, this body plus trample is quite acceptable, and Dinosaurs are a common tribe on Ixalan, so there are plenty of chances to trigger its power-boosting ability.

![134](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/2/2/2220ed60-3f8f-4dd2-8319-6a06896a5350.jpg?1698183724)

**Jru**: 5.0

An unquestionable bomb. A five-mana 5/5 flying first striker already has terrifying combat power, and the triggered ability earns absurd value: just one [Reckless Impulse](https://scryfall.com/card/vow/174/zht/%E9%AD%AF%E8%8E%BD%E8%A1%9D%E5%8B%95) per turn already snowballs into a terrifying resource gap, and exiling cards creates extra resources; it doesn't even require you to play the card to get the token. Basically it's a bomb that wins half the game if it survives one turn.

![135](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/LCI-Red/e4481248f1eb.webp)

**Jru**: 5.0

This set's all-purpose glue card. It enters with discard N, draw N+1, letting you loot without losing cards while also supporting Descend. The transform condition is descending three times before the end step of your own turn, and this card already provides one Descend when it enters, so reaching three isn't hard. After transforming it's a super resource-expansion engine that can easily overpower the game with a huge amount of resources.

![136](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/8/e/8e9e0c66-f64a-428c-be13-a52691833df1.jpg?1699044235)

**Jru & Kappa**: 2.0

On offense it can potentially act as a three-mana 4/4. Two artifacts aren't hard to gather in this set, but many of them are slower cards or Equipment that need an equip cost, so it's hard to turn it into a 4/4 while your teammates also provide enough early pressure.

Mono-red artifacts aren't enough to make it a playable card, but mixed with white's pile of cheap artifacts or artifact creatures, it can become an effective 4-power attacker, and it's no great loss if it gets removed. A vanilla 3/2/3 isn't terrible either, but that still can't hide the problem that it only works in specific color pairs. If the ability were a static one that works whenever the condition is met, instead of only when attacking, it could go up another 0.5 to 1 point; as things stand, with no support, it is always a below-the-line card.

**Jru**: 4.0

Excellent as a standalone card. A three-mana 3/3 first striker puts a lot of pressure on in Limited creature combat, and the three-mode attack trigger is also very useful. Just note that LCI has far fewer Pirates this time: the entire set, including this red card, has only 14 Pirates, some of which are weak, so don't expect too much from a Pirate tribal build.

Dinosaur teammates aren't hard to find in this set (commons and uncommons already have 18 of them, 14 of which are in the red-green pair), but there is a limit after all. However, we can also view its ability as a stronger [Bushido 1](https://mtg.fandom.com/wiki/Bushido?so=search); in a deck with many Dinosaurs it can often trade with a 3-toughness creature or put more attacking pressure on the opponent, making it an excellent common in red-green.

Draft is a bit better, but this set's Cave theme doesn't have as complete a systemic payoff as KHM's snow or DMU's domain, so don't expect to pick Cave cards highly.

**Kappa**: 1.5

You can look forward to the Cave archetype becoming a five-color Cave deck in draft, like the domain strategy in the DMU era. This card is essentially an AOE, and very powerful in a Cave deck; imagine [Gates Ablaze](https://scryfall.com/card/rna/102/gates-ablaze) in a [Maze’s End](https://scryfall.com/card/dgm/152/mazes-end) deck to understand it better. In draft, Caves currently show up in 2 out of every 3 packs; the rating isn't high yet, but the future looks promising.
In sealed it's probably completely dead.

**Jru**: 2.5

Descending once is acceptable, and from the second Descend on it starts to pay off. Since this set has a fairly high proportion of big-bodied creatures, it takes extra effort to pump it up enough to hold an advantage in creature combat.

![141](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/a/1/a144d0b3-678d-4f4c-a9b0-22af19f5cf9f.jpg?1699044253)

**Jru**: 2.0 (sealed) / 2.5 (draft)

Its strength depends entirely on the density of Discover cards. Copying into a Discover the first time only breaks even on the cost of the card; the second time onward is the real profit. To play this guy, first make sure your deck has a high enough chance of getting two Discovers after it enters, otherwise don't play it. In sealed it's also hard to put together a full Discover theme; in draft you can take some Discover cards highly for better consistency, but remember not to overpick weak Discover payoffs just for this card.

![142](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/d/9/d95018a4-be10-4c46-b14d-4b1eba838bc0.jpg?1699044254)

**Jru**: 2.0

Its use is close to making all the opponent's creatures unable to block, and it can easily be the last straw that breaks the opponent. The benchmark cost for this kind of card has historically been four mana; this one costs five, but it comes with a Discover 4, and judged on its own, paying one extra mana for Discover 4 is quite a gain. However, this card is very timing-dependent: you usually use it to deliver the final blow to an opponent already on the deadline, so your basis for deciding whether to cast it should be the state of the board, not the random follow-up value of Discover 4. Even whatever the Discover 4 flips will most likely not change that turn's attack, so it ends up as an inefficient card that costs one more than the benchmark card and can't completely strip the opponent's ability to block.

![161](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/c/0/c035250e-6f6e-4d0f-b4fd-2a53d6069aa7.jpg?1698435084)

**Jru & Kappa**: 5.0

A super-rewarding card for artifact strategies. As long as you get two Dinosaur tokens, it has paid for itself, and from the third on it's pure profit. This set has quite a few Dinosaur creatures, and you can also expect to get a few Treasures along the way. On the flip side, when you don't draw these specified types of cards it's completely useless, so its floor and ceiling depend heavily on the distribution of your card pool.

Its real value is that the first artifact each turn comes with a creature that can both attack and block. Among this set's commons and uncommons there are 50 artifacts in total: red has 9, colorless has 14, so it's hard to completely miss out, and mixing with other colors gives even more: white has 10, blue has 9. Dinosaurs are mainly in red-green; the Treasures produced by this card's Dinosaur attacks help with color fixing, and casting artifacts makes Dinosaurs, so once this card gets going it is hard to stop unless the Enchantment is destroyed. In summary, it's a big bomb worth building a deck around, and you can specifically build red-green-white or red-green-blue; considering it can make Dinosaurs on its own, you might not even need to splash green, as red's own Dinosaurs or the ones it makes itself can sustain the first effect.
Note that because casting an artifact spell immediately produces a Dinosaur, even if you know the opponent can respond at instant speed to destroy the Enchantment, you can slam this Enchantment for four mana and then immediately slam a one-mana artifact, and the opponent still can't stop the first Dinosaur from being produced; either way you can force a 1-for-0 against the opponent.

A card with a wildly floating floor and ceiling.
