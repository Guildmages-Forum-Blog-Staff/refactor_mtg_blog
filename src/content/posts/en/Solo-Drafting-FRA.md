## Introduction

With entertainment options as countless as the stars these days, getting eight people together for a draft pod is harder and harder. For this project, I used Reality Fracture (FRA) as the showcase set. I mostly weighed deck strength and thematic synergy, and tried to complete the challenge of drafting by myself!

![Seating chart](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Solo-Drafting-FRA/1c82dbe50a66.webp)
<p style="text-align: center;"><a class="small">Seating chart for this draft. Image generated by Gemini 3.1 Pro</a></p>

The seating is shown above.
> Please note that the seat order in this article differs from a real draft. In a proper draft, players 1 through 8 should be seated clockwise in order. I had misunderstood this, and by the time I noticed, all the photos had already been taken, so I decided to keep it as is and leave this note. If you are interested, see this official article: [Bahr: Here's What the Optimal Draft Looks Like](https://wpn.wizards.com/en/news/bahr-heres-what-optimal-draft-looks)

The format follows the usual eight-player draft rules: pack one passes left, pack two passes right, and pack three passes left again. In each booster, everything except the last card (the token / advertisement card, which isn't passed) goes around, so 14 cards get passed. There was no time limit, and I was allowed to look at any player's picked cards at any time. Throughout the process, I tried to complete the picks and deck construction of eight independent "players" within what a single human brain can handle.

## Let's get started!

### Pack 1: The picks

![1](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Solo-Drafting-FRA/c27d6aa79377.webp)
Ta-da! The first-pick reveal! Except for players 1 and 2, the other six players all took the R/MR card from their pack. Player 1 skipped {% mtglink "Puppet Crafting" %} because it needs support, and [FRA](https://scryfall.com/sets/fra) is not an artifact/enchantment-themed set. Player 2 passed on {% mtglink "Codie, Ravenous Codex" %} mainly because {% mtglink "Eye of Jace" %}, which surveils 1 every turn, is the more well-rounded effect.

> Draft tip: In pack one, first picks should usually prioritize raw card power and color openness, with theme second. At the moment of the first pick, it is hard to be sure the theme will really come together and that there will be enough playables for it.

![2](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Solo-Drafting-FRA/4705b54f5ea4.webp)
- Player 1 first-picked {% mtglink "Titanbones, Towering Heart" %}, so wants a white-green lifegain plan. Compared with the one-shot lifegain of {% mtglink "Emergency Phytomedic" %}, {% mtglink "Koth of the Homestead" %}, which gains life repeatedly off land drops and can even grow creatures, is a better fit.
- Player 2 first-picked {% mtglink "Eye of Jace" %}. Getting passed {% mtglink "Puppet Crafting" %} here was very tempting, so player 2 decided to raise the priority of later artifacts/enchantments and head toward a green-based aggro [Ensoul Artifact](https://scryfall.com/card/m15/54/zht/%E7%A5%9E%E5%99%A8%E7%B7%98%E9%9D%88-(ensoul-artifact)) deck.
- Player 3 first-picked {% mtglink "Variable Chaser" %}, and this pack had plenty of red-blue gold cards. Among {% mtglink "Way of the Pyromancer" %}, {% mtglink "Way of the Cryomancer" %} and {% mtglink "Violent Echoes" %}, player 3 chose the first one, the red Way, to pair with acceleration.
- Player 4 first-picked {% mtglink "Liliana the Faultless" %} and likewise went down the white-green lifegain path. Between {% mtglink "Vigorbloom Charm" %} and {% mtglink "Way of the Wildspeaker" %}, player 4 took the more flexible {% mtglink "Vigorbloom Charm" %}.
- Player 5 first-picked {% mtglink "Hexhaven Invigorator" %} and basically takes green whenever it is available. This pack had little to choose from, so player 5 took {% mtglink "Inspired Tethermage" %}. 
- Player 6 first-picked {% mtglink "Sanctum Lurker" %} and stayed relatively open. Player 6 decided to stay black and took {% mtglink "Ferocity of the Hunt" %} as a trick. {% mtglink "Woodwork Prodigy" %} would also have been fine, but it felt too early to commit to a second color.
- Player 7 first-picked {% mtglink "Lyra, Archangel of Dawn" %}, also locking in white, and took {% mtglink "Saheeli, Consul of Oversight" %}. 
- After player 8 first-picked {% mtglink "Uldaros Theorix" %}, the deck leaned toward a black-based graveyard-recursion plan. The passed {% mtglink "Liliana the Repentant" %}, {% mtglink "Extended Absence" %} and the blue {% mtglink "Surveillance Phantasm" %} were all quite good, but after weighing rarity and the deck's development, player 8 took {% mtglink "Liliana the Repentant" %}.

![3](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Solo-Drafting-FRA/5f6d6eedefeb.webp)
Player 2, locked into the artifact theme, took {% mtglink "Warrior's Blades" %}. After the blue {% mtglink "Surveillance Phantasm" %} was passed from the previous seat, player 7 is showing signs of shifting toward a blue-white surveil theme. Player 8 hesitated between [two Beast Way enchantments](https://scryfall.com/search?as=grid&order=name&q=Way+of+the+%28oracle%3A4%2F4+oracle%3ABeast%29+%28game%3Apaper%29+set%3Afra+prefer%3Abest) and the black-green {% mtglink "Hapatra, the Desert Fang" %}, and in the end chose the black-green Hapatra, which can be dug back onto the battlefield.

![4](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Solo-Drafting-FRA/baadb904d4b5.webp)
Player 2 leaving the artifact creature {% mtglink "Keeper of the Quiet Hour" %} to take {% mtglink "Winter, Tormented Loner" %}, and player 4 not continuing with the green lifegain engine {% mtglink "Greenhouse Propagator" %}, were both thematically inconsistent. Player 7 taking the third-color {% mtglink "Way of the Wildspeaker" %} was more or less forced, since the other blue-white cards available had too little impact. Players 5, 6 and 8 each got an important card for their theme on this pick.

![5](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Solo-Drafting-FRA/06e433023342.webp)

Player 1 starts to diverge from the deck's theme. The five-mana {% mtglink "Fateshaper Aspirant" %} is a solid card, but it neither supports the lifegain plan nor interacts with the {% mtglink "Generous Revival" %} taken at pick 3. What this deck lacks most right now is a two- and three-drop curve, so taking a five-drop is not the best choice. {% mtglink "Afterthought Sentry" %}, which can take off and attack in the mid to late game, may have been better. Player 7 moving into a fourth color and taking the two-red-pip {% mtglink "Violent Echoes" %} was also not a sensible choice, especially when the same pack even had a blue-white dual land available. Staying in two colors is still the safer approach. Player 8 ignored the plentiful blue-black gold cards and picked {% mtglink "Tinybones, Pocket Nuisance" %}, continuing to stock up on creatures that can be reused from the graveyard.

![6](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Solo-Drafting-FRA/7e888528799e.webp)

Player 1, affected by the neighbors on both sides fighting over green cards, faced the decision of splashing a third color in this pack. Between the red {% mtglink "No Admittance" %} and the black {% mtglink "Extended Absence" %}, player 1 chose the sturdier latter (which also triggers lifegain), but the deck inevitably moves toward a clunkier build. Meanwhile, player 2 ignoring {% mtglink "Afterthought Sentry" %} to take the unsynergistic {% mtglink "Mabel, Bitter Recluse" %} clearly shows they have not yet realized that green should be their main color. Player 3 choosing {% mtglink "Arni, Humble Scribe" %} while lacking bodies is not practical. A non-creature-themed deck must get through the early game with removal, disruption, or creatures with better stats, so that the mid and late game has room for the theme to work. Taking other red creatures with better stats, or even {% mtglink "Icy Reception" %} as a two-mana disruption piece, would have been better. 

![7](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Solo-Drafting-FRA/2dae5cf07741.webp)

This pack is relatively dull, but player 5 got {% mtglink "Codie, Ravenous Codex" %}, which gives a chance to copy a prepare spell one more time. {% mtglink "Budding Insurgent" %} was also available in the same pack, but in a draft I still recommend taking the card with the higher ceiling.

![8](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Solo-Drafting-FRA/16ac9b9206eb.webp)

Player 2 finally snapped out of it and took a red curve card, beginning to lock into red-green and watching for a chance to splash white. It was pure luck that player 5 could take a second {% mtglink "Woodwork Prodigy" %}. After player 6 took {% mtglink "Koth, the Geomancer" %}, they now have three engine creatures that trigger on noncombat damage, and the red-black shell is already quite clear. After player 8 took {% mtglink "Extrapolate the Impossible" %}, their later pick strategy shifted a little too, leaning slightly toward expanding the usability of prepare cards.

![9](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Solo-Drafting-FRA/16d8ed1d558f.webp)

Players 1 and 6 were lucky enough to get their own {% mtglink "Greenhouse Propagator" %} and {% mtglink "Stingerquill Charm" %} back.

![10](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Solo-Drafting-FRA/5d3a6acdcea4.webp)
Player 2 taking the red-green dual land and letting {% mtglink "Medic's Kitesail" %} go cannot be called wrong, but it is not a pick that raises the deck's ceiling.

![11](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Solo-Drafting-FRA/d09b8aefe798.webp)
The {% mtglink "Living Library" %} that player 2 took adds early defense, increases thematic synergy, and is also a good hidden mid-to-late-game answer. 

![12](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Solo-Drafting-FRA/82b3b5addf8a.webp)

Player 4 getting {% mtglink "Medic's Kitesail" %} was a pleasant surprise. 

![13~14](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Solo-Drafting-FRA/9ed8e1219f09.webp)

In the last few picks, player 6 saw what came back around in this set. Even {% mtglink "Stinging Vitriol" %} was passed all the way to the end, so it is almost certain that player 6 is the only one at the table playing red-black. Barring surprises, they can build a very complete red-black deck.

## Match results

The matches were an 8-player single-elimination bracket, best of three. Thanks to the anonymous players who helped complete the matches.

### Round 1
Table one pitted player 1, white-green splashing black, against player 8, playing blue-black. Player 8's two wins were basically uncontested mid-game resource crushes. The one loss came from player 8 getting stuck on four lands and being run over by player 1's curve.

Table two was more of a tug-of-war. In game one, player 7, playing blue-white, quickly won with {% mtglink "Yuriko, Blade of the Mighty" %} and flyers. But in games two and three, player 2, playing red-green artifacts, stabilized the board and then counterattacked to win.

Table three was also one-sided. Player 3, playing red-blue, saw their defenses quickly pierced by player 6's tightly curved red-black deck. On top of that, red-blue has no way to gain life, and had no chance to turn things around against red-black's steady noncombat damage offense.

And at the last table, table four, player 4's white-green lifegain strategy, even in a game where the curve came out smoothly, quickly could not keep up the pressure against player 5's creature size advantage. Once ramped past, it was basically a lost game.

### Round 2

In the first table of round two, player 8 faced player 2. Even when both sides developed smoothly, player 2's game plan could not effectively take down player 8, who has the long-game resource advantage.

At table 2, player 5, playing mono-green, faced player 6, playing red-black. Red-black took game one with a fast curve and direct damage to the face, but in the next two games, facing mono-green's huge acceleration and the big disadvantage of burn spells against the opposing ace {% mtglink "Hexhaven Invigorator" %}, it finally lost to mono-green's heavy assault.

### Round 3 (Finals)

The finals were a real tug-of-war, with both sides reaching one game apiece. At the key turn, mono-green pushed blue-black's life down to 4, but blue-black summoned the ace {% mtglink "Uldaros Theorix" %}, choosing the 4-mana sorcery {% mtglink "Rewrite Regrets" %} and 2-mana instant {% mtglink "Last Gasp" %} in the graveyard to remove the opponent's creature and dig {% mtglink "Apex Witchstalker" %} out of the graveyard, restoring life to 6 and making two big blockers. Mono-green discarded {% mtglink "Sureshot Sower" %} to eliminate the blue-black Sphinx, then played a Forest to trigger {% mtglink "Roiling Canopy" %}'s +3/+3, turning a 2/2 trampler into a 5/5 attacker; blue-black blocked with {% mtglink "Apex Witchstalker" %} and life went back up to 7. After that, both sides entered a standoff, until blue-black summoned an attack-capable {% mtglink "Lyra, Tolarian Archangel" %}. With only 6 cards left in the library, they used {% mtglink "Theoretical Necromancer" %} from the graveyard to pick up {% mtglink "Fblthp, Impossibly Lost" %} and put it onto the battlefield. After Lyra attacked successfully, in response to {% mtglink "Fblthp, Impossibly Lost" %}'s triggered ability they used {% mtglink "Theorix Charm" %} to mill three and draw one, then drew the last two cards of the library to win.

## Conclusion: Can an "Omniscient" view create eight "Omnipotent" draft decks?

Completing an eight-player draft alone is much harder than I imagined. Even with no time limit and the ability to repeatedly check what each "player" had previously taken, I was still limited by my understanding, preferences and fatigue in the moment, and made choices that looked less than ideal in hindsight. The draft also forced me to keep switching among eight lines of thinking, and with my limited brainpower, I found many blind spots from the time while writing this retrospective. For that reason, this attempt gave me a chance to repeatedly re-examine the whole draft from different perspectives.

By "omniscient", I do not really mean being able to foresee what each pack will open, but grasping as much "known" information as possible: who is fighting over the same colors, whose theme is taking shape, who lacks a curve or interaction, and what signal each pick sends to the neighbors on either side. And by "omnipotent" here, I do not mean building eight of the most well-rounded decks. Rather, it means giving each deck the basic conditions needed to win matches: a clear theme, a reasonable curve, and enough interaction.

Looking back over the whole draft, what truly decides a deck's success or failure is often not taking the strongest single card on some pick, but whether, at the right moment, you make tradeoffs consistent with the deck's theme. The theme provides draft direction, the curve determines the game's tempo, and interaction determines the margin for error. When these elements mesh with each other, the 42 cards chosen go from a pile of "not bad cards" to a module that really works.

A well-designed, highly playable set should be like [FRA](https://scryfall.com/sets/fra). The official color-pair themes are only a starting point; when you piece together different components, you will find many more possibilities worth exploring. Drafting is a process of putting imagination into practice, while actual matches are used to confirm whether that imagination can withstand the test of reality.

If you happen to have a box of boosters on hand, try drafting eight players by yourself too. You will find that the hardest part is not picking the "strongest" card on each pick, but making reasonable choices for eight different players within the limits of your own blind spots. At that moment, your thinking no longer serves a single deck, and you must use a near-"omniscient" bird's-eye view to weigh signals, themes, curve and interaction at the same time, building eight decks with different themes and similar power, each with its own plan to win.

{% mtglink "The Theorist, Jace Beleren" %}, in the story, gradually walked toward "[Omniscience](https://scryfall.com/card/m19/65/zht/%E5%85%A8%E7%9F%A5%E5%85%A8%E8%83%BD-(omniscience))" with a mortal body, and finally reached that state. Drafting eight players alone may also be a small step toward that goal: not to pursue knowing everything and being able to do everything, but to face your own biases more honestly each time you switch perspectives.
