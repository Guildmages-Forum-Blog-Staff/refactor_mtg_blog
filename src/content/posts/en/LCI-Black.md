The Lost Caverns of Ixalan (LCI) holds its prerelease events on November 10, 2023, and is officially released a week later on November 17, 2023. Want to learn more about card ratings in Limited and some potentially interesting synergies? Then take a look at this series of LCI Limited introductions!

For the mechanics, see this article: [The Lost Caverns of Ixalan Mechanics Explained](https://guildmagesforum.tw/LCI-mechanic/)

## Rating Scale

5.0: Must-pick, unconditionally
4.0: Worth a first pick
3.5: Strong enough, but not necessarily a first pick
3.0: Above average (you'd really like it in your final deck)
2.5: Average, maybe around the 6th or 7th pick in a draft
2.0 to 1.0: Below average (you'd rather not play it than play it)
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable (basically don't play it; it will only help you lose)

**Jru**: 2.0

When ahead it can be a bomb, but when behind it may become a dead card, so it performs very unstably. Sometimes your own creatures are even better than the opponent's, and then you wish it were a 6/6 vanilla. If you want to play it, it's best to make sure your deck has enough fodder creatures to give it.

![088](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/LCI-Black/cac9bae2d8d7.webp)

**Jru**: 5.0

A god-tier bomb. A 5-mana 4/4 flyer with lifelink has a body that fights well, and once it starts attacking it rolls out a hopeless resource gap and even gains a life differential on the side. It is the kind of bomb where, if you don't remove it within a turn, that alone is enough to decide the game. What's even more hopeless is that this Bat God is hard to remove cleanly. The land on the back needs only any player to have 1 or fewer cards in hand to return to the front-side Bat God, which in Limited is hardly a condition at all. Normally, once the board reaches five mana everyone's hands are about played out, and even if the opponent deliberately holds cards to keep two or more in hand, you can just play whatever you draw and burn your own hand down to one or fewer. Conventional damage and destroy effects have almost no answer to the Bat God.

![089](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/9/9/99009400-ffa4-40af-8305-78295ae605ac.jpg?1699044065)

**Jru**: 2.0

The sacrifice theme in this set is thin in the traditional sense; red doesn't even have an [Act of Treason](https://scryfall.com/card/m20/124/act-of-treason) effect. Although the set has many ways to produce artifact tokens, sacrifice outlets are also plentiful, and this one is among the less efficient ones. On top of that, for a three-mana creature the base body is somewhat feeble, so it is a low-priority filler.

![090](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/2/0/20d9eb9f-2fcc-49f0-99c1-bad748239466.jpg?1699044076)

**Jru**: 3.0

Returning two creatures to hand at instant speed for three mana is a decent way to gain a resource edge, and a controllable self-mill of two cards has special meaning in this set. When you need a source to trigger descend, this card can very likely do it, and after filling the graveyard there are mechanics like descend 4, descend 8, fathomless descent, and craft to reuse resources. Also note that this card has no target; the two creature cards to return from the graveyard are chosen on resolution.

![091](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/0/5/05bdd22c-3e11-4c29-bdfa-d3dfc0e90a9f.jpg?1699044085)

**Jru**: 3.0

A removal spell with a very heavy cost, but at least it is a two-mana instant-speed outright kill. Also, when you need to trigger descend or have other graveyard-filling needs, make good use of the additional cost of discarding a card; instantly filling the graveyard in this set may produce other gains to offset the cost of discarding a card.

**Jru**: 2.0

A creature version of [Living Death](https://scryfall.com/card/v14/8/living-death), with a mandatory trigger on entering. Although it's a 6/6 flyer that fights well, at 8 mana it is too heavy any way you look at it, and it only makes sense if your own graveyard is stocked better than the opponent's. Although black is good at actively filling the graveyard, it is still an extra condition. Also, if you're ahead on board you may not want to call it. It is a high-ceiling dream card, but there are quite a few situations where you can't afford to cast it or the board state doesn't suit it.

![095](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/0/8/08318a16-a9ed-42c8-9433-876b7a72e368.jpg?1698262986)

**Kappa**: 2.5

Without **descend** it's a four-mana 4/3, a passable body; with it, the bonus is creating an unblockable 1/1 every turn. If you can cheaply descend every turn, it works as a continuous token-generating machine, and the tokens can be used for sacrifice, beans, tapping, craft, and so on; even if they can't block, they're not necessarily completely useless. But you must pay attention to whether your deck can withstand a heavy amount of mill. Conversely, if descend support is insufficient, it becomes a 4/3 vanilla, and in combat its 3 toughness is easily traded off by two- and three-mana creatures, and when you want to use it as a production machine you naturally fight less. All in all, it is a mainstay creature with obvious strengths and weaknesses.

**Jru**: 1.5

Fortunately it has an instant-speed seven-mana ability to turn itself into a 4/3 flying token, so in the mid to late game it won't be a completely dead card.

Although the range of what its enter-the-battlefield effect can remove may be limited, as long as it removes something it's a gain, and used well it will be a black [Flametongue Kavu](https://scryfall.com/card/jmp/319/flametongue-kavu). Used normally it is already a creature with a three-mana body, and more descends extend the ETB's reach; at the extreme, without actively descending, just through simple early creature trades it can already remove creatures with 2 or 3 toughness, and since the ETB has already done this creature's job, the next step is to use its high power to trade for another creature, achieving a 2-for-1.

![098](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/5/f/5f661095-3645-4e44-ac39-752e417c2174.jpg?1699044112)

**Jru & Kappa**: 5.0

The body can be seen as a three-mana 3/2 haste creature, and once you descend once it's a gain, and it can create board pressure that is extremely uncomfortable for the opponent. Plus enchantments are not easy to deal with in this set, making it a payoff card worth actively fighting for continuous descending.

One of the biggest prizes in this Limited format's descend system, a semi-permanent generator of 3/2 haste creatures. If you can descend steadily, you can produce a 3/3/2 harasser every turn; even if it can't get through, you can keep casting this and descending each turn, slowly raising a large army of 3/2 skeletons. Not to mention that with two or more copies, each skeleton's clock gets higher and higher. A semi-infinite resource with simple conditions is absolutely a bomb in this Limited format.

![126](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/LCI-Black/61d65869acee.webp)

**Jru**: 5.0

When you have enough fodder it is a sustainable resource engine, and when your hand is mostly spent it can convert into a super card-advantage machine; being able to cast a creature from the graveyard every turn gives a resource gap enough to drown the opponent, and lands are a category extremely hard to answer. It is a big bomb that can help you win steadily.

Also note that its front face is an artifact, so be careful against opponents with plentiful artifact removal. When you can transform safely, don't be greedy about the chance to sacrifice and draw; the back face earns you plenty anyway. Conversely, when the opponent may respond by destroying the artifact, be careful, because discarding for the transform ability is a cost and transforming is the effect, so if you painfully discard a card to activate the transform ability and get answered by artifact removal, you'll be left crying with no tears.

Also, creatures recurred by the back face get a finality counter, and a creature with a finality counter is exiled when it dies, so don't fantasize about endlessly freeloading some big guy from the graveyard.

But in the vast majority of cases it is a powerful sweeper, very much worth a first pick.

As a [Trial of Ambition](https://scryfall.com/card/akh/113/trial-of-ambition)-like removal on its front face it isn't efficient, but at least it is a two-mana removal spell, and its yield is better early in the game or after baiting the opponent's small creatures into trades. The craft fodder requires just one creature card, which you can normally find in the graveyard, and once successfully transformed it is a death countdown for the opponent; as long as you find a way to hold the line, that's enough to win.

**Jru**: 3.5

The most important and numerous cards in this set are creatures and artifacts, so restricting discard to those two types isn't much of a problem. Although the craft cost is a bit heavy, the transformed dinosaur has enough combat power too, and since craft materials can be pulled from the graveyard, it is in effect a good card that creates an additional card-advantage point as long as you pay.

**Jru**: 3.0

Among sacrifice outlets it is a relatively good one. The ability can be activated at instant speed with no tap or once-per-turn limit, and the gains are permanent +1/+1 counters. As long as you still have mana, the opponent mostly has to prioritize removing this guy, or else whoever the opponent removes next, you eat, and it will always become a threat that's hard to deal with. Don't forget that a sacrifice outlet is also a potential descend engine.
