## Rating Scale

> **Note**: This ECL Limited format places a very high demand on creature-type synergy within each color pair. If a creature card has the theme creature type of a color pair (for example: Merfolk, Goblin, Kithkin, Elemental, Elf, etc.), its rating already reflects its strength in that color pair.

5.0: Always take it; gives you a near-winning advantage
4.5: Always take it if you're in the right colors; gives you a huge advantage
4.0: First-pick quality; excellent synergy with the archetype's theme, and can be the deciding card for splashing, switching colors, or entering a color pair
3.5: Top uncommons and commons of the set, plus other strong cards; good synergy with the archetype's theme, and a key card for entering a color pair
3.0: Important cards for an archetype's theme, plus other above-average cards; you'll want them in your final deck
2.5: Average, usually a decent curve filler
2.0 to 1.0: Below average; you generally prefer not to play them, and can use them as filler if necessary, but not too many
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable; basically never play it, it will only help you lose

## Conclusion
I can't tell if it's an illusion, but the cards in this set seem generally strong in Limited, and removal already has a higher priority in Limited, let alone this set's removal, which comes with a permanent -1/-1 debuff (at least against opponents who can't remove counters).
Overall, the author has very high expectations for black this time. Both red-black Goblins and black-green Elves look like very good creature-type decks, each with good quality creatures and fun cards you can do flashy things with; there are also some blue-black Faerie ingredients, but the quantity doesn't look as plentiful as Goblins and Elves. If black is your main color, you'd still rather end up in red-black or black-green.
