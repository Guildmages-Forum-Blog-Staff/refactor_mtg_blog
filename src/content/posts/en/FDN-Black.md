## Grading Scale

5.0: Unconditional first pick, gives you a nearly game-winning advantage
4.5: Unconditional first pick if you are in the right colors, gives you a huge advantage
4.0: Worth a first pick, synergizes extremely well with a color pair's theme, and can be the deciding card for splashing/switching colors or settling into a color pair
3.5: Top uncommons and commons of the set and other sufficiently strong cards, synergize well with a color pair's theme, and can be key cards for settling into a color pair
3.0: Important cards for a color pair's theme, plus other above-average cards that you want in your final deck
2.5: Average, usually a decent curve filler
2.0 to 1.0: Below average, generally not played, can be used as filler if necessary but not too many
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable, basically never play it, doing so only helps you lose

Score: 2.5
Comment: This card prompted quite a few different opinions in our team's internal discussion. First, every set's Limited occasionally has a three-mana discard-two-cards type card (usually with an additional effect), but cards of this type are harder to pin down in power rating, and the user's play style makes their performance fluctuate quite a bit.

Personally I lean toward giving it a score of around 3.0, because the awkward point of three-mana discard-two cards has always been that it is awkward in the mid-to-late game or when topdecked, but being able to do something very strong on time on turn 3 or 4 is a two-for-one. The second effect of this card gives it room to still be useful when drawn in the mid-to-late game, so I think it is a good card above average.

Some team members rated this card 2.5 or below, for several main reasons: Limited is a battlefield of creature fights, and discarding cards from hand is not as useful as in constructed, and sometimes it just makes the opponent throw away a land or something useless at the moment, uh; also, the second effect has very limited impact on the board situation in the late game, so they lean toward not playing it in the deck.

All in all, if you are good at using discard effects to control the tempo in your Limited style, this is a good card worth putting in your deck; conversely, if you are better at creature fights, or find the timing of discard hard to grasp, you can move this card's pick order back a bit.

## Conclusion
Overall, the card strength of Foundations (FDN) is generally a bit higher this time, and most black creatures are not bad. At the same time, many small synergies that can be found within black alone give black more to look at in Limited. Taken together, compared with the generally weak black of the past several sets, this time black should be a color very much worth considering.
