子安 and I walked out of [CardMaster](https://www.facebook.com/cardmaster.tw) together, planning to head to the MOS Burger across the street to meet up with 柏宇. [cephille](/authors/cephille/) and [Jeff](/authors/jeffchen/) were heading toward Nanmen Market and said goodbye to us. 子安 and 柏宇 are friends I met through the Conquest format, and we hang out from time to time, playing around with all kinds of formats. It was around August, I think? This game is really hard.

For a while I struggled so much I nearly went mad, but somehow I got used to the feeling of being the underdog, and without realizing it I became very calm. There was nothing to lose anyway. Gradually I could enjoy the pleasure of playing again, and even began to figure out a quirky style of play that suits me ~~if you don't lose, you have a chance to win~~.

I didn't say any of this to 子安 and 柏宇 in particular, but I believe they could feel it to some degree.

I found 柏宇 on the second floor of MOS Burger. He's easy to spot; we've seen that broad back countless times. 柏宇 is still waiting and watching this season. Everyone has their own life to deal with, so he's not competing in Standard for the time being. Of course, this doesn't affect our friendship at all; nobody falls out of the group because of it.

The three of us sat down at an affordable pasta restaurant, and after ordering and chatting idly for a bit, we slowly fell silent. It wasn't that we had nothing to say; we simply knew each other too well, so well that we could relax around each other and rest a little.

Congratulations to 彥融 for qualifying at [鬥樂一中店](https://www.facebook.com/DouLeTCG) this week. Hard work pays off ~~but still take care of your liver~~. His seriousness and skill are plain for all to see, and it's a blessing to know a young person like that.

I knew he'd brought only 16 lands that day, with 1 on the battlefield, so the odds of topdecking a land were just 15/53, but that's how things go. Someone wins the lottery jackpot, so there's nothing to complain about.

At the time I kept a faint smile as best I could, but inside I still felt a little down. So I guessed 子安 probably felt the same way today.

"Whose is the Golden Garlic Mixed Seafood Risotto?" The waiter's voice pulled me back to reality.

Without him, I wouldn't have the [Urabrask's Forge](https://scryfall.com/card/one/153/urabrasks-forge) in my deck, and without [Urabrask's Forge](https://scryfall.com/card/one/153/urabrasks-forge), I couldn't have beaten 子安 today, because 子安's deck also has [Urabrask's Forge](https://scryfall.com/card/one/153/urabrasks-forge). Earlier, before the result was settled, we were laughing as we shared a single token and beat each other up...

The three of us started chatting idly again, and bursts of laughter rose and fell. I said this season's Standard is really hard. 4-2-0 (four wins, two losses) only got me 11th place, missing the top-8 playoff. Mainly because turnout was huge; if I remember right, 45 players took part, and the competition was intense.

My Rakdos [[骷髏妖]] deck and I didn't seem to have done anything wrong. Did we? The more I think about it, the less sure I am. Maybe the deck itself... is a mistake?

I'd poured out the same self-pitying complaints earlier to my senior [蹦蹦](https://www.facebook.com/UncleJund), who patted my arms lightly and said quietly, "Keep going next time." Those few words, together with the earlier chance to interview him and the many times I've seen his resilient mindset in pulling off win streaks after two losses, made me glad that I bravely picked up Magic: The Gathering at thirty-five, and that I got to know these people. Of course, next time we keep going. I tried to convince myself: this was only my second time going out to play a Standard tournament, so it could count as a satisfying result.

A big reason I can keep going is also that the tournament atmosphere in the north is good. I can't say whose credit it is, but promoting exchange has always been Guildmages' Forum's goal, and being in the middle of it, I'm of course very happy. In one round at [CardMaster](https://www.facebook.com/cardmaster.tw), I sat next to the well-known Taipei player [SamuelChang](https://www.facebook.com/profile.php?id=100063848411689), listening to him talking with young players, and I thought he was trying, within reason, to convey the logic and importance of a few details. Everyone has a different social circle, but the desire to promote this game can't be hidden. Once there's been contact, today's active players will at least greet each other, and things seem to be getting better and better.

I couldn't help recalling the tournament at [Magic Star](https://www.facebook.com/MAGICSTAR01) a week ago, where I chose to play Azorius enchantments, a deck I had poured a lot of effort and love into. No teammates were there that day, and it was my first time in a Standard competition. Since I was really unfamiliar with the environment, in a daze I made two major mistakes (with [Enduring Curiosity](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/51/enduring-curiosity) on the battlefield, I drew three fewer cards at damage resolution; and I put three fewer +1/+1 counters before and after [Optimistic Scavenger](https://scryfall.com/card/dsk/21/optimistic-scavenger), and in the end I happened to miss winning by just three life), so I could only barely slide into the top 8 with a 2-0-3 record (two wins, zero losses, three draws), and then immediately ascended to heaven, leaving a regretful "the deck was stronger than the player" ending.

Even so, that day I had plenty of exchange with Allen, whom I'd met briefly once before, and 老曹 (sertyple), whom I met on site. The two even helped watch my games, and I'm truly grateful. Looking back, it's exactly these friendly-rival relationships that shifted the center of Magic exchange from some BBS board to the real world. I truly believe the environment now really is much better and much more fun.

When I came to my senses, I had followed 子安 and 柏宇's footsteps to [牌塔](https://www.facebook.com/totarmory). Before we formally stepped into competitive play, he had already become reasonably familiar with the fine details of the rules. Thanks to him often playing the role of teacher, I've been able to keep improving at this game and come this far.

So deep that we have to keep discussing and verifying with each other to keep playing, and in the process of supporting each other, we naturally walked into each other's lives.

The second match was 柏宇 against 子安. I didn't care how it went; I was truly tired. Rather than wanting to play cards, I was really just extending my time with friends.

The night of 11th place was somewhat interesting, but not entirely pleasant.

On the ride home, I thought of the novel *Hibana* (《火花》) by 又吉直樹. Within the framework of competition, losers always outnumber winners, or rather, in the end there's only one winner. If so, what is the point of everyone else's existence?

I think that, beyond the competing and after the competition, northern Taiwan has already proven everything through its present state. The players have indeed built a culture that goes beyond winning and losing, and through more sincere communication, brought everyone together closer to the depths of this game.

If, in the distant future, this land can produce another World Champion, that person will surely be a winner jointly forged by the competitive pressure each of us has invested. That glory belongs to them, but also to each of us who, from this year on, has tried to be a little kinder to the environment around us.

So, dear fellow players, close friends and strangers alike, we keep going next time, and next year.
