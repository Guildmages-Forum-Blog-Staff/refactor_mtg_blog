## Rating Scale

5.0: An unconditional first pick that gives you a near-winning advantage
4.5: An unconditional first pick if you're in the right colors; gives you a huge advantage
4.0: Worth a first pick; fits the archetype's theme extremely well and can be the deciding card for splashing, switching colors, or moving into the archetype
3.5: The set's top uncommons and commons, plus other strong cards that fit the archetype's theme well and can be key to moving into the archetype
3.0: Important cards for the archetype's theme, plus other above-average cards you'll want in your final deck
2.5: Average; usually a decent curve filler
2.0 to 1.0: Below average; you generally prefer not to play them, but they can fill out the deck if needed, just not too many
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable; basically never put it in your deck, because it will only help you lose

## Archetype Themes

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FIN-Multicolors/4a2691c6e6e6.webp)

Starting from the official draft overview, let's expand a bit on the core cards and interactions of the ten color pairs. In addition, each of the ten color pairs has 2 legendary uncommon creatures that serve as the core of the mechanic or reinforce it.

> If you aren't familiar with Final Fantasy and want a better sense of what makes each legendary character special, feel free to watch this video:
> [歴代主人公 vs 全ラスボス。迫力の戦闘シーン - Dissidia Final Fantasy NT 日本語 & English Battle movie](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TDcMzfFyceU)

### <span style="color:#C3B270">White</span>-<span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span> — Artifacts

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FIN-Multicolors/51baf7b1fa26.webp)

Keep casting **artifacts** or making **artifact tokens**, and your {% mtglink "Tidus, Blitzball Star" %} keeps growing and walks into the end zone unopposed! The other legendary uncommon, {% mtglink "Cid, Timeless Artificer" %}, encourages you to grab every copy of him you can see, building up Cids on the battlefield and in the graveyard through looting and combat trades, so your **Hero** tokens and artifact creatures get the huge bonuses that time has accumulated! Don't forget that **Equipment with Job select** first creates a 1/1 colorless Hero when it enters, and Cid will be a great mentor to those little heroes!

### <span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span>-<span style="color:#808080">Black</span> — Control

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FIN-Multicolors/a2a87347f230.webp)

Blue-black returns to the most traditional control theme, and besides the control staples of **card filtering, counterspells, and removal**, it can also gain benefits from **building up your graveyard**. The legendary uncommons {% mtglink "Locke Cole" %} and {% mtglink "Ultimecia, Time Sorceress" %} filter your cards as they attack, and once the graveyard reaches 8 or more cards late in the game, you can transform Ultimecia to take an extra turn.

### <span style="color:#808080">Black</span>-<span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span> — Black Wizard Aggro

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FIN-Multicolors/11e6b738cfed.webp)

> Here we use the MTG creature type Wizard rather than the Mage used in the FF games, to avoid confusing readers.

The main mechanic is making **black 0/1 Wizards**, and pairing them with the legendary uncommon {% mtglink "Black Waltz No. 3" %} and lots of **noncreature spells** to win through noncombat damage. This archetype needs the rare {% mtglink "Kuja, Genome Sorcerer" %}, who keeps making 0/1 Wizards and, once transformed, doubles the damage those Wizards deal. These two cards will be the core cards that make black-red run.

As for the other legendary uncommon, {% mtglink "Garland, Knight of Cornelia" %}, he has no connection to the archetype theme. He's more of a card that blue-black and black-green, the two graveyard-value archetypes, will want, and readers can try him out in actual Limited games.

### <span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span>-<span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span> — Landfall Aggro

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FIN-Multicolors/a9ea9d9c4bf3.webp)

In red-green, **landfall** is a very important theme. Of the two legendary uncommons, {% mtglink "Rydia, Summoner of Mist" %} helps you filter out cards you don't need, while {% mtglink "Gladiolus Amicitia" %} fetches a land when it enters, which triggers landfall, and landfall giving other creatures +X/+X and trample is exactly what red-green needs. In this archetype you need to include some extra ways to fetch lands and put extra lands onto the battlefield, so that you can trigger landfall multiple times.

In addition, Rydia's second ability hints at another theme: **Saga creatures**. This set has 15 enchantment creature cards that are Sagas on the front face, and no noncreature Sagas, which makes giving them haste quite important. After all, without haste, a three-chapter Saga creature can basically only attack on chapter II, when it's past summoning sickness; once it reaches chapter III, it gets sacrificed.

There's also one more theme that the legendary uncommons don't mention but that works as a subtheme in red-green: **Birds**. There are 4 Bird cards in total (6 if you add white), plus 5 noncreature spells that make 2/2 Bird tokens, and the vast majority of the Birds either benefit from landfall or help landfall happen, so they're worth readers' attention in Limited too.

### <span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span>-<span style="color:#C3B270">White</span> — Go Wide

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FIN-Multicolors/d3d26d7cd6d3.webp)

Although green-white's mechanic is going wide, these two legendary uncommons can't add much to that theme. {% mtglink "Rinoa Heartilly" %} needs you to spread out your board first before her offensive pump effect works; {% mtglink "Garnet, Princess of Alexandria" %} is more of a member of the small Cleric subtheme, and also lets your Saga creatures survive a bit longer before the countdown.

To go wide effectively, you'll need to borrow green's Bird theme, as well as white cards that create 1/1 Hero tokens, such as {% mtglink "Aerith Rescue Mission" %}.

### <span style="color:#C3B270">White</span>-<span style="color:#808080">Black</span> — Artifact & Creature Sacrifice

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FIN-Multicolors/ddd8a3cb235d.webp)

White-black focuses on **effects when artifacts and creatures die**. The legendary uncommon {% mtglink "Judge Magister Gabranth" %} can easily grow very large without you noticing, while the other, {% mtglink "Rufus Shinra" %}, implies that this archetype needs to make some artifacts//creatures to serve as **sacrifice fodder**.

In addition, the mythic {% mtglink "Sephiroth, Fabled SOLDIER // Sephiroth, One-Winged Angel" %} and his signature weapon {% mtglink "The Masamune" %} can be seen as a **sacrifice engine**, or an amplifier for one. Also, apart from {% mtglink "Judge Magister Gabranth" %}, the other three cards mentioned above are all from FFVII. Interested readers can look further into the story (FFVII has a remake too!).

### <span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span>-<span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span> — Big Noncreatures

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FIN-Multicolors/d1a5e5e172d1.webp)

In blue-red, {% mtglink "The Emperor of Palamecia // The Lord Master of Hell" %} is a very efficient noncreature-spell [mana dork](https://scryfall.com/card/fdn/227/llanowar-elves) that helps you **spend four or more mana to cast noncreature spells**, which also makes him bigger and even transforms him; once transformed, he cares about the number of noncreature, nonland spells in your graveyard to deal extra damage. {% mtglink "Shantotto, Tactician Magician" %}, on the other hand, benefits from noncreature spells you cast by spending four or more mana: besides getting +4/+0 or more, she also draws cards to refill your hand.

Many of blue-red's sorceries/instants have **Flashback**, and the flashback costs are all at least four mana, which also helps you meet the archetype's theme. Casting **Vehicles that cost four or more** counts too, so you can use that to fill out part of your creature curve, but I don't recommend playing too many.

### <span style="color:#808080">Black</span>-<span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span> — Graveyard Value

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FIN-Multicolors/13a81a900b94.webp)

Both uncommons of black-green care about the **number of permanent cards in your graveyard**. That means that for this archetype to work well, there's an implicit message that you shouldn't play **too many sorceries/instants**. However, looking at the reported cards such as {% mtglink "Exdeath, Void Warlock // Neo Exdeath, Dimension's End" %} and {% mtglink "Cloud of Darkness" %}, both are a bit mediocre filler before they hit their threshold, so I suggest approaching it from a different angle.

If black-green's graveyard value is about **recursion from the graveyard**, then cards that **mill yourself** to fill the graveyard, like green's {% mtglink "Town Greeter" %} and black's {% mtglink "Shinra Reinforcements" %}, become very important, since every permanent card you put in there has a chance to turn into a resource in the mid-to-late game. Don't forget that Saga creatures also go to the graveyard after their final chapter resolves, which makes them well suited as mid-sized threats in black-green.

### <span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span>-<span style="color:#C3B270">White</span> — Equipment Aggro

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FIN-Multicolors/42e0a641566f.webp)

This set has 26 Equipment cards (plus 1 more, {% mtglink "Sidequest: Play Blitzball // World Champion, Celestial Weapon" %}, whose back face is an Equipment), including 16 **Equipment with Job select**, so building a deck around them is definitely fine. The legendary uncommon {% mtglink "Giott, King of the Dwarves" %} is well suited to carrying Equipment himself, and his extra rummage effect lets you filter your hand effectively; as for the Dwarf subtheme, there are only 3 creatures, so you can basically treat it as nonexistent. The other one, {% mtglink "Zidane, Tantalus Thief" %}, has less to do with the Equipment theme, but his enters effect of {% mtglink "Act of Treason" %}, and the Treasure you get when you give the creature back, both create extra value.

### <span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span>-<span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span> — Town Ramp

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/FIN-Multicolors/86331657cc00.webp)

This set has 23 Town cards. Since the dual lands themselves are Towns, even if you don't play blue-green you'll still end up using 2 to 3 Towns; this also means Towns will be popular in the draft, because everyone takes them. So to **get into the blue-green Town theme, you need to raise the pick order of Town cards to make sure you have enough of them**.

The legendary uncommon {% mtglink "Ignis Scientia" %} can basically ramp you a land when he enters, and the more Towns you have, the bigger the impact {% mtglink "Omega, Heartless Evolution" %} has on the game when you cast it late. As for the rare legend {% mtglink "The Wandering Minstrel" %}, besides giving your Towns the effect of {% mtglink "Amulet of Vigor" %}, his own passive ability reveals that **this theme starts to kick in at five or more Towns**; considering that you won't necessarily get every Town in your deck onto the battlefield, 6 to 7 Towns, or even more, is probably a safe number.

## Conclusion

[Final Fantasy](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E6%9C%80%E7%B5%82%E5%B9%BB%E6%83%B3%E7%B3%BB%E5%88%97), as a heavyweight IP spanning more than 35 years, is a truly phenomenal product, both in the longevity of the IP and in the historic achievements it has made in the RPG world; its standing there is perhaps comparable to Magic: The Gathering's place in the TCG (Trading Card Game) world. ~~The [Lord of the Rings](https://scryfall.com/sets/ltr) set is even older, a true elder~~ This crossover can also be seen as a team-up of two great IPs from East and West, and I hope it sparks something different and lets more new players get to know Magic: The Gathering and keep playing.

Like [Cid, Timeless Artificer](https://scryfall.com/search?as=grid&order=released&q=oracleid%3A87050537-99c9-4993-a770-4329b2e749e4+include%3Aextras&unique=prints), a character who runs through FFII to FFXIV, 15 games in total, and appears in different forms, appearances, and personalities, including as a non-player helper, party member, and villain. Magic also has many legendary creatures and Planeswalkers who appear in different sets in similar ways, clashing and cooperating to drive the story forward; or, as seasons pass, their descendants, heirs, and inheritors of their ideals continue to appear in later stories.

Let's close with the FFVIII ending theme, Faye Wong's "[Eyes On Me](https://youtu.be/06Za71UUdps?si=Mdr-n4hSo4sKiWZ5&t=328)". The stories of FF and MTG will keep being written, never ending...
