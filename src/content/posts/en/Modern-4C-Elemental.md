## Introduction

Modern Four-Color Elementals is a deck I only decided to collect and practice this year.
Before it rose to become a meta deck, it was still stuck at the version built around [Risen Reef](https://scryfall.com/card/m20/217/zht/%E9%86%92%E8%BD%89%E6%9A%97%E7%A4%81) and [Eladamri's Call](https://scryfall.com/card/mh1/197/zht/%E8%89%BE%E6%8B%89%E9%81%94%E5%8A%9B%E7%9A%84%E5%8F%AC%E9%9B%86), and after MAT brought [Nissa, Resurgent Animist](https://scryfall.com/card/mat/22/nissa-resurgent-animist), people gradually started developing new ways to play Elemental decks.
But none of that is what made Elementals take off. What really made Elementals mainstream was the release of the LTR set this June. After adding [Delighted Halfling](https://scryfall.com/card/pltr/402/delighted-halfling) and [The One Ring](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/246/the-one-ring), the deck's power clearly went up by a lot. Uncounterable legendary spells and mid-game card advantage turned this deck, which badly needs its spells to resolve normally and to build a resource lead, into one of the top-tier decks.

## Strengths and Weaknesses of the Deck

As one of the frontrunners of the metagame, Four-Color Elementals is bound to be a deck many players choose for MITQs/RCQs, so let's briefly discuss the deck's ways to win and its weaknesses.

### Ways to Win

1. **Resource lead**
   This is one of the advantages of all four-color decks. This deck starts to build a clear resource lead in the mid-game: besides drawing cards, it has individual cards that trade one-for-many, and the resources you gain not only let you get ahead on cards but also set up the following two ways to win.
2. **Board**
   One way to win: use the resource lead to call down individual creature cards that trade one-for-many, namely your Elementals, to clear the board and take control of it, and eventually the opponent loses because they have no way to deal with the board.
3. **Control**
   Apart from earning resources, this deck is busy controlling the game. When you can build a resource lead and keep the opponent's win conditions under control, the opponent, short on resources and almost unable to win, will usually concede on their own.

### Weaknesses

1. **Attacks on "lands"**
   A big part of how the deck runs relies on finding the right lands to cast those spells that need at least two specific colors, thereby maintaining the tempo and advantage it should have. So it is quite afraid of individual cards that can target lands, with common ones being [Blood Moon](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/346/blood-moon) and [Sundering Titan](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/292/sundering-titan).
2. **Slow speed**
   A common problem of multicolor decks. Against decks that can finish their game plan faster than you, such as `Boros Burn` or `Creativity` or `Belcher`, unless you topdeck exactly the right combination of answers, you have almost no resistance in game 1.
3. **Long matches**
   The deck spends most of its time controlling the board and trading resources, and the point at which it really starts attacking with Elemental creatures is usually after turn 5. However, although these Elemental creatures have powerful board-clearing ability, they are also very fragile, so it often happens that you just finished clearing the board with Elementals but they are easily removed by the opponent, making the situation a stalemate and lengthening the match. This is especially obvious in the mirror.

## Decklist & DeckTech

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-4C-Elemental/7fcbfc2afa7d.webp)
[Deck link](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/visual/5730979) is attached as well.

This is a modified version of the 75-card Four-Color Elementals build that recently went 9-0 at an MO Super Qualifier (one [Lightning Bolt](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/117/lightning-bolt) and one [Fury](<https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/126/zht/%E6%80%92%E7%81%AB）改放兩張[艾拉達力的召集](https://scryfall.com/card/mh1/197/zht/%E8%89%BE%E6%8B%89%E9%81%94%E5%8A%9B%E7%9A%84%E5%8F%AC%E9%9B%86)>) swapped out for two Eladamri's Call). There is no special reason for raising the count to 75 cards other than to go with the new card [Up the Beanstalk](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/195/up-the-beanstalk) and play the greediest resource war, so as many Elemental board-clearing cards and card-advantage cards as possible are included, while the mana base relies on [Wrenn and Six](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/296/wrenn-and-six) for stability.

Because four colors can reach a very wide range, there is a lot of freedom in how the deck is put together and in card choices, but basically some specific cards are still always played at 3 to 4 copies, with the rest depending on personal preference.

First, let's briefly talk about how this deck works.
You can see that the early-curve cards in the deck are nothing but board-clearing cards or packages that stabilize the mana base, so you can tell the deck's early goal: depending on the situation, either clear the board or stabilize mana, and then expand once the game reaches the mid-game.
Overall it is a slow midrange-control deck, with a certain level of resistance against fast decks.

From here on, I'll introduce the deck's composition and go through the individual cards.

### Artifacts

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-4C-Elemental/f064f40fd67e.webp)
The Ring itself, with very strong card draw, plus, if you cast it, protection from everything for a turn that protects its owner.
Although keeping it around has a life loss side effect every turn, you can offset it with [Omnath, Locus of Creation](https://scryfall.com/card/znr/232/zht/%E7%94%9F%E6%A9%9F%E6%A0%B8%E6%AD%90%E9%82%A3%E6%96%AF)'s life gain, or by using [Teferi, Time Raveler](https://scryfall.com/card/war/221/zht/%E7%90%86%E6%99%82%E6%B3%B0%E8%8F%B2%E5%8A%9B) to bounce it back to hand.
Remember that its draw condition is to first put a burden counter on it, and then see how many counters are on it to decide how many cards you can draw. If the Ring leaves the battlefield for some reason before you put the counter on, the number of cards you can draw depends on how many counters were on it before it left.
(EX: The Ring has 2 counters and you activate the ability, and the opponent exiles it in response. What would have drawn 3 becomes drawing 2, because you can't put one more counter on the Ring.)

### Enchantments

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-4C-Elemental/fb63275d047e.webp)
A new card from Wilds of Eldraine, which lets your resources explode faster in an Elemental deck.
Whenever you cast a spell with mana value 5 or greater, you draw a card.
The qualifying range includes [Leyline Binding](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/24/leyline-binding), [Fury](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/126/zht/%E6%80%92%E7%81%AB), [Solitude](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/32/zht/%E5%B9%BD%E5%AF%82), [Elesh Norn, Mother of Machines](https://scryfall.com/card/one/10/elesh-norn-mother-of-machines), or [Prismatic Ending](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/25/zht/%E8%99%B9%E5%BD%A9%E7%B5%82%E5%B1%80) with X equal to 4.
There are a few things to note when using it:

1. **You really can't draw any more**
   In some stalemate matches where time gets stretched out, having too many of it on the battlefield actually carries the risk of decking yourself. If your main deck doesn't include [Endurance](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/157/endurance) to shuffle the graveyard back into your library, you may have to think about how many of them you put onto the battlefield.
2. **Draw punishment**
   If the opponent has [Orcish Bowmasters](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/103/orcish-bowmasters) or [Sheoldred, the Apocalypse](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/107/sheoldred-the-apocalypse) on the battlefield, too many Beanstalks actually become a double-edged sword. The main reason is that the Beanstalk's ability is a "**cast trigger**": when you use board-clearing cards that cost 5 or more, because of how the stack works, [Orcish Bowmasters](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/103/orcish-bowmasters) and [Sheoldred, the Apocalypse](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/107/sheoldred-the-apocalypse) trigger their abilities before they get removed. With multiple Beanstalks it could even help the opponent push your life total into their lethal range, so please be extra careful against black decks.

### Lands

Since this is a four-color deck, the mana base has to be very carefully chosen, so let's explain which lands are included and why.

#### Fetchlands

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-4C-Elemental/f49d72a171f5.webp)
First, for the fetchlands, we include the three above.
Because color fixing is so badly needed, we play around 11-12 fetchlands to keep a sufficient density.
There are two reasons for choosing these three:

1. The only basic lands in the deck are Island, Forest, and Plains, so we must avoid, as much as possible, situations where we need those basics and can't find them, especially in matchups where we must prioritize fetching basics to avoid getting wrecked by [Blood Moon](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/346/blood-moon).
2. In most situations, these three fetchlands can find the tri-lands and shocklands we need.

#### Shocklands

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-4C-Elemental/2014050a72de.webp)

#### Tri-lands

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-4C-Elemental/587915745f9a.webp)

### Sideboard Choices

Compared with Standard and Pioneer, Modern is more of a format that emphasizes sideboard flexibility and power. The following are cards the deck often uses in the sideboard, and of course the counts depend entirely on personal preference and the matchups the player wants to specifically target.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-4C-Elemental/d717b9b98676.webp)
[Chalice of the Void](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/f/a/fab5f3b1-dc9f-4529-8283-941f60fa016e.jpg?1646195971) puts different numbers of charge counters on it in different matchups, but the shared goal is always to lock down the way the opponent's deck operates.
Putting on a given number of counters counters every spell of that mana value cast by any player. (EX: Putting on one counter automatically counters all one-mana spells.)
Remember that Chalice of the Void's ability applies to your own spells too. If the opponent sets their own Chalice and then foolishly casts a spell with the same mana value as the counters on it, remember you can't let it through.
Likewise, if you set a Chalice yourself and then cast a spell with the same mana value as the counters and forget to trigger your own Chalice's ability, if the opponent calls a judge afterward, you will very likely be penalized, so don't take the risk.

**Suggested matchups:**
Hammer Time (X=1), Murktide (X=1), Death's Shadow (X=1), Boros Burn (X=1), Cascade decks (X=0)

### Game Plan Thinking

1. **Stabilize the mana base**
   For a four-color deck this is the most important point. Most spells in the deck need at least two mana of specific colors to cast, so stable color fixing and finding the right land at the right time is what players most need to practice when picking up this deck.

2. **Don't be afraid of counterspells**
   Against tempo or control decks, you can't do nothing out of fear of counterspells; that would simply throw away the win.
   You are a deck that needs to stabilize the tempo, so judge which cards in your hand will become targets for the opponent's counterspells, decide which cards need to be sacrificed and in what order to cast them, or take a big gamble when you have no choice, because if you don't play cards, the opponent's counterspells will just sit in their hand and do nothing to help them.
   Conversely, whenever the opponent needs to pay for a counterspell but a spell they didn't counter resolves, the headache will be theirs.

3. **How to play unfavorable matchups**
   Falling behind means the opponent can complete their game plan earlier than you. In that situation, unless you have no choice, mindlessly casting spells on curve doesn't help you. Finding ways to disrupt the opponent's game plan and drag the tempo in the direction you're comfortable with is the better way to play.
   So mulligan aggressively to find answers you can use, and slow the tempo down to counter the opponent's plan; that is the approach I recommend.
