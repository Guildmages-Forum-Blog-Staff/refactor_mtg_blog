## Pre-Tournament Preparation

### Before MKM releases

[MITQ#5\_桌遊侍\_Temur Rhinos_David Yeh](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/5963553#paper)

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/f5e0ee84e7b6.webp)

The picture above is the deck I used at the 桌遊侍 MITQ#5. That day I beat four Rakdos Scam decks and two Burn decks, and it felt pretty good. However......

[DECEMBER 4, 2023, BANNED AND RESTRICTED ANNOUNCEMENT](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/december-4-2023-banned-and-restricted-announcement)

Who knew that the day after I qualified at the MITQ, [Fury](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/126/fury) would get banned? I had been cutting corners by playing only [Fury](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/126/fury) and no [Dead // Gone](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/161/dead-gone) to deal with small creatures, and now that was gone.

The tasty Scam decks became rarer, and so did the annoying 4C Omnath. Without Fury, you can expect more small-creature decks, so the stronger Hammertime, Hardened Scales, Humans, Golgari Yawgmoth and Selesnya Company decks should show up more; and rare animals like Faeries, Merfolk, Affinity, Infect, Death & Taxes and Bant Cloudshift should appear too. Originally I just wanted to replace [Fury](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/126/fury) with [Bonecrusher Giant](https://scryfall.com/card/eld/115/bonecrusher-giant-stomp)......

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/379f6fb7fcaf.webp)

### After MKM releases

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/bc3f7b35b686.webp)

But Murders at Karlov Manor (MKM) changed a huge amount in Modern. First is [Leyline of the Guildpact](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/217/leyline-of-the-guildpact), which pushed Domain Zoo into the top five, and the US RC also saw a lot of the new Domain Rhinos. With the combination of [Scion of Draco](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/323/scion-of-draco) + [Leyline of the Guildpact](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/217/leyline-of-the-guildpact), [Scion of Draco](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/323/scion-of-draco) benefits from the Leyline's effect and has vigilance, hexproof, lifelink, first strike and trample on top of its own flying, so unless you remove [Leyline of the Guildpact](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/217/leyline-of-the-guildpact), you simply can't race it.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/1d1899fc29cd.webp)

Also, the surveil lands that carry two basic land types interact well with fetch lands, which upgraded the existing [Living End](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/121/living-end) and [Goryo's Vengeance](https://scryfall.com/card/bok/67/goryos-vengeance) decks a notch.

![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/f/5/f58cfb23-4d99-4133-bf4b-d7e7c7d17cea.jpg?1708201628)

With more Domain Zoo and Domain Rhinos around, many decks started playing MKM's best common, [Pick Your Poison](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/170/pick-your-poison), in the sideboard, which is a simple and effective way to hit [Scion of Draco](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/323/scion-of-draco) and [Leyline of the Guildpact](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/217/leyline-of-the-guildpact). In addition, besides Pick Your Poison, Izzet Murktide decks splashing green have also started playing the ancient beast [Tarmogoyf](https://scryfall.com/card/fut/153/tarmogoyf). This deck is naturally good at assembling delirium, and using Goyf to hold off Rhinos and [Territorial Kavu](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/216/territorial-kavu) is more than enough.

### The Decision Before MIT#5

Honestly, I originally planned to play Domain Rhinos at this MIT, with this list: [MIT#5_Domain Rhinos_David Yeh](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/6229524#paper)

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/0e44b9eb94d6.webp)

While building it, the deck felt packed to the brim, with really few flexible slots. Take the [US RC 7th-place Domain Rhinos](https://melee.gg/Decklist/View/363672) in the picture below as an example. It is probably still the most common main deck and sideboard configuration from before [Violent Outburst](https://scryfall.com/card/arb/63/violent-outburst) was banned, a uniform deck that everyone plays the same.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/af1a351e0cb9.webp)

The most obvious differences in Domain Rhinos are probably:

1. Choosing between [Xander's Lounge](https://scryfall.com/card/snc/260/xanders-lounge) and [Indatha Triome](https://scryfall.com/card/iko/248/indatha-triome) as the three-color land, since the fetch lands and shock lands / surveil lands that go with these two lands are naturally different.
2. Whether the sideboard has four [Territorial Kavu](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/216/territorial-kavu): LSV played [Indatha Triome](https://scryfall.com/card/iko/248/indatha-triome) at the RC. Personally I think [Indatha Triome](https://scryfall.com/card/iko/248/indatha-triome) with [Steam Vents](https://scryfall.com/card/rvr/288/steam-vents) + [Thundering Falls](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/269/thundering-falls) should be better than [Xander's Lounge](https://scryfall.com/card/snc/260/xanders-lounge) with [Temple Garden](https://scryfall.com/card/rvr/290/temple-garden) + [Lush Portico](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/263/lush-portico). After all, Domain Rhinos is still mainly red, blue and green, and you probably don't want two white-green lands.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/8e2056e5a8fa.webp)

I also thought about another combination of three-color and two-color lands. Black is the least useful, so it should be tied to the three-color land. That way, on turn one [Raffine's Tower](https://scryfall.com/card/snc/254/raffines-tower), and on turn two either [Stomping Ground](https://scryfall.com/card/rvr/289/stomping-ground) or [Commercial District](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/259/commercial-district) works, and with white and blue on turn two you can cast either [Leyline Binding](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/24/leyline-binding) or [Mystical Dispute](https://scryfall.com/card/eld/58/mystical-dispute). The drawback, though, is that you might not be able to cast [Violent Outburst](https://scryfall.com/card/arb/63/violent-outburst) on turn three. ~~(Doesn't matter, it got banned anyway.....)~~

Also, playing [Territorial Kavu](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/216/territorial-kavu) in the sideboard is rare. After siding in, the deck becomes more like Domain Zoo, which can dodge hate cards like [Chalice of the Void](https://scryfall.com/card/a25/222/chalice-of-the-void); it is big enough to beat Rhinos in combat, and when attacking you can discard excess Leylines to loot for what you need, and against Living End it can also exile a few creatures from the graveyard. (Although getting hit by it once still probably means death......)

In the two weeks before MIT I gathered the cards for Domain Rhinos, and although having Leyline on turn one into Scion on turn two feels great, and even just a turn-two Scion feels pretty good, it still felt a bit off no matter how I played it. I always felt it had fewer tricks than the old three-color Rhinos and was less flexible, and the lands were just three-color lands and surveil lands.

![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/1/0/10bf4693-668e-4498-a652-a9d5eb77f9b7.jpg?1674074201)

I considered playing one [Zur, Eternal Schemer](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/228/zur-eternal-schemer), which turns the deck's eight Leylines into a [Wurmcoil Engine](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/308/wurmcoil-engine)-like threat with hexproof, deathtouch and lifelink; on the other hand, Zur is blue and can also be pitched to [Force of Negation](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/50/force-of-negation) and [Subtlety](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/67/subtlety).

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/97ac8c9b5e5d.webp)

Or other excellent multicolor cards such as [Omnath, Locus of Creation](https://scryfall.com/card/znr/312/omnath-locus-of-creation), [Aragorn, the Uniter](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/192/aragorn-the-uniter) and [Nahiri, the Harbinger](https://scryfall.com/card/cmm/929/nahiri-the-harbinger). In any case, I spent several days daydreaming, trying to make the deck less monotonous. ## Analysis of the Temur Rhinos Build Used at MIT#5

### Main Deck Introduction

First, as at the MITQ, I don't play [Dead // Gone](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/161/dead-gone). The consideration is that before you make a Rhino on turn three, the only thing you really need to kill is [Dauthi Voidwalker](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/81/dauthi-voidwalker), and the decks that play Dauthi are the few black-based Scam decks, a few Mono-Black Coffers decks, and Goryo's Vengeance decks. If you don't remove [Dauthi Voidwalker](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/81/dauthi-voidwalker) and force through a Rhino anyway, every one of its [Not Dead After All](https://scryfall.com/card/woe/101/not-dead-after-all) can bring a Rhino back to life......

> If you have [Force of Negation](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/50/force-of-negation) in hand, consider forcing out a Rhino to get exiled by Dauthi, then using [Force of Negation](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/50/force-of-negation) to counter the exiled Rhino~
> In addition, another card that must be answered before turn three is [Teferi, Time Raveler](https://scryfall.com/card/war/221/teferi-time-raveler), but that has nothing to do with [Dead // Gone](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/161/dead-gone); you have to rely on [Force of Negation](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/50/force-of-negation) and [Subtlety](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/67/subtlety).

![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/d/4/d42a57b2-fbe9-4024-a593-f6dc711a2e9d.jpg?1571323269)

Three Borrowers are still very useful. Turn one, smooth out your lands; turn two, bounce a threat; turn three, make a Rhino, and the tempo is on our side. Borrower can also effectively deal with hate cards you meet in the main deck, such as [Chalice of the Void](https://scryfall.com/card/a25/222/chalice-of-the-void), [Roiling Vortex](https://scryfall.com/card/znr/156/roiling-vortex), [Dauthi Voidwalker](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/81/dauthi-voidwalker) and [Boromir, Warden of the Tower](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/4/boromir-warden-of-the-tower). Although Borrower's biggest fear is [Orcish Bowmasters](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/103/orcish-bowmasters), only Golgari Yawgmoth and black-based Scam should be using it, and if you meet it, just don't flash it in carelessly; think a little more before casting card-draw spells.

![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/6/0/604e2bfc-655d-4d3e-98aa-374780ca4016.jpg?1699044881)

![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/e/8/e882ef7d-ddff-40f5-a4f5-bff5c8169dc1.jpg?1686972130)

Four [Flame of Anor](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/406/flame-of-anor) alongside four Tidebinders and [Mutavault](https://scryfall.com/card/m14/228/mutavault); some of these could be swapped for one [Dismember](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/322/dismember), but I feel Temur has no life gain, so it would be a heavy burden. I also looked for powerful Wizards to pair with Flame, but the two-mana [Snapcaster Mage](https://scryfall.com/card/pr23/2/snapcaster-mage) doesn't work, [Magus of the Moon](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/175/magus-of-the-moon) and [Arcane Proxy](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/75/arcane-proxy) might be good for one copy, and [Vannifar, Evolved Enigma](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/373/vannifar-evolved-enigma) might be the best choice, but she doesn't suit Rhinos......

![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/7/0/701256d5-1389-48b7-9581-d6037209bd06.jpg?1626095059)

Three [Subtlety](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/67/subtlety): Subtlety was good against the old Rakdos Scam but is bad against Temur Rhinos and Living End. Against Domain Zoo and Domain Rhinos, it can push back the [Scion of Draco](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/323/scion-of-draco) the opponent plays on time on turn two, which is acceptable. If decks with [Teferi, Time Raveler](https://scryfall.com/card/war/221/teferi-time-raveler) like 4C Omnath and Domain Rhinos keep increasing, a full set of [Subtlety](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/67/subtlety) might be better.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/6086f2d318d1.webp)

One [Generous Ent](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/169/generous-ent) replaces [Lórien Revealed](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/60/l%C3%B3rien-revealed), just hoping that when I face Living End in game one and unluckily get my graveyard hit, I at least have an Ent to hold off the [Oliphaunt](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/139/oliphaunt). And the fun thing in practice is that sometimes if you hard-cast the big tree, the opponent's Scion of Draco or [Murktide Regent](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/52/murktide-regent) can't get through. Lórien Revealed can also be pitched to [Force of Negation](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/50/force-of-negation) and [Subtlety](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/67/subtlety), so it's up to personal preference.

![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/c/4/c49690c7-c282-4eb4-8da3-5e0c46a80fc4.jpg?1707428999)

[Kellan, Inquisitive Prodigy](https://scryfall.com/card/mkm/212/kellan-inquisitive-prodigy-tail-the-suspect) is something I first saw Japanese players put in their sideboards on the Hareruya site. In practice it is a super-powered [Serra Angel](https://scryfall.com/card/dmr/25/serra-angel), looking especially mighty when you face Hammertime, Scales, Amulet and Green Tron. When you really want to draw cards, you can also blow up your own [Shardless Agent](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/292/shardless-agent) to farm the opponent and draw cards, and on turn two when you have nothing to do and want to ramp, you can cast it via its adventure as Tail the Suspect. It is both blue and green, so it can be pitched to [Force of Negation](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/50/force-of-negation), [Endurance](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/157/endurance) and [Subtlety](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/67/subtlety), which is perfect. On top of that, it's legendary, so it reduces the channel cost of [Boseiju, Who Endures](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/266/boseiju-who-endures) and [Otawara, Soaring City](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/271/otawara-soaring-city), which also makes unfamiliar players miscount mana. Its 3/4 body is slightly bigger than [Subtlety](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/67/subtlety), [Dragon's Rage Channeler](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/121/dragons-rage-channeler) and [Brazen Borrower](https://scryfall.com/card/eld/281/brazen-borrower-petty-theft), and it isn't afraid of being swept by a sideboard [Brotherhood's End](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/128/brotherhoods-end).

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/a18cd133e949.webp)

One of the [Fire // Ice](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/290/fire-ice) was changed to Bonecrusher Giant, which has a chance to punch through [The One Ring](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/697/the-one-ring)'s protection when it enters. If the field has a lot of 4C Omnath, Green Tron and Amulet Titan, I'd reduce the number of [Fire // Ice](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/290/fire-ice) to add more Bonecrusher Giant.

Lands: 22 lands + 4 cycling fetch-style lands. I don't want to go with just 21 or even 20 lands; in a big tournament I would rather draw three lands in a row and then topdeck my way out than get stuck on lands on turn three and be done.

The basics are one each of the blue-red, red-green and blue-green shock lands, [Boseiju, Who Endures](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/266/boseiju-who-endures), [Otawara, Soaring City](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/271/otawara-soaring-city), [Gemstone Caverns](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/280/gemstone-caverns), [Mutavault](https://scryfall.com/card/m14/228/mutavault), and eight fetch lands that find green ([Misty Rainforest](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/250/misty-rainforest), [Wooded Foothills](https://scryfall.com/card/ktk/249/wooded-foothills)). Since I don't play Dead // Gone, I chose two Islands and two Forests as basics, to guarantee that [Force of Negation](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/50/force-of-negation) and [Endurance](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/157/endurance) can always be cast outright at key moments.

The new set's blue-green and red-blue surveil lands are also fixed inclusions, but I thought for a long time about whether to play the red-green surveil land. Only a few lists play all three surveil lands, or they just put back one [Ketria Triome](https://scryfall.com/card/iko/250/ketria-triome) to avoid color problems,

![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/a/b/abde5bed-dc4e-4b2b-820c-18d4d0cf8042.jpg?1699044703)

In the end I decided to add more punch with a creature land, but two [Mutavault](https://scryfall.com/card/m14/228/mutavault) could clog up the deck (after all there is also [Gemstone Caverns](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/280/gemstone-caverns)). After considering six dual creature lands and [Treetop Village](https://scryfall.com/card/10e/361/treetop-village), I decided on [Restless Ridgeline](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/283/restless-ridgeline). Four lands animate it plus itself; it has 3 power itself and gives the neighboring creature +2 power, so five lands add up to 5 attack power.

This land really does force the opponent to be wary (opponents often picked it up to read it). When your life total is too low and all your lands are tapped (for example, when they sweep your Rhinos), at end of turn flashing in something like [Brazen Borrower](https://scryfall.com/card/eld/281/brazen-borrower-petty-theft), [Tishana's Tidebinder](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/335/tishanas-tidebinder), [Endurance](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/157/endurance) or [Subtlety](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/67/subtlety), then animating the lands plus one 3-power creature is 8 damage, and if before end of turn it was [Violent Outburst](https://scryfall.com/card/arb/63/violent-outburst) with two Rhinos, animating the lands and attacking along with them for 13 usually kills. At MIT that day I animated it only once, but with [Mutavault](https://scryfall.com/card/m14/228/mutavault) I hit for 7 and killed an unfamiliar opponent (pressing palms together).

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/ea5b6ccb8000.webp)

Next, the sideboard choices. First, the cards I didn't play: [Commandeer](https://scryfall.com/card/cmm/80/commandeer) is mainly for stealing the opponent's [The One Ring](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/697/the-one-ring), but the decks that play the Ring now, like 4C Omnath, Amulet Titan and Azorius Control, are fewer than the Cascade decks, and besides, [Tishana's Tidebinder](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/335/tishanas-tidebinder) can already handle the Ring. And [Obsidian Charmaw](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/137/obsidian-charmaw) is only aimed at Tron-style furniture-lands decks like Green Tron; in the past I would also bring it against Hammertime and Scales with four [Inkmoth Nexus](https://scryfall.com/card/mbs/145/inkmoth-nexus) and four [Urza's Saga](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/259/urzas-saga). But there aren't many of those decks, and relying on counterspells and removal should be enough.

#### Sideboard Cards Used

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/13646bafce87.webp)

The sideboard I chose mainly follows [the US RC champion Minh Nguyen's deck](https://melee.gg/Decklist/View/366769). I wanted my sideboard to have at least four counterspells, four removal pieces for hate cards, and four [Endurance](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/157/endurance).

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/053b12c5c1df.webp)

The four counterspells are a combination of [Mystical Dispute](https://scryfall.com/card/eld/58/mystical-dispute) and [Ertai's Scorn](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/48/ertais-scorn). You must have at least one [Ertai's Scorn](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/48/ertais-scorn): besides being able to counter a Cascade spell on turn two on the draw, in most matchups the opponent assumes that after you've used up your [Force of Negation](https://scryfall.com/card/2x2/50/force-of-negation), anything that gets around [Mystical Dispute](https://scryfall.com/card/eld/58/mystical-dispute) will resolve, and then Ertai's Scorn, which works just like [Cancel](https://scryfall.com/card/m21/46/cancel), is very useful.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/e5c6ce81f3b5.webp)

The four removal pieces are a combination of [Force of Vigor](https://scryfall.com/card/mh1/164/force-of-vigor) and [Brotherhood's End](https://scryfall.com/card/bro/128/brotherhoods-end). I decided on at least two Brotherhood's End: besides destroying Hammers and Scales, against Domain Zoo it can also sweep 8 cats and 4 monkeys ([Wild Nacatl](https://scryfall.com/card/ala/152/wild-nacatl), [Nishoba Brawler](https://scryfall.com/card/dmu/174/nishoba-brawler), [Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/315/ragavan-nimble-pilferer)), [Lavinia, Azorius Renegade](https://scryfall.com/card/rvr/195/lavinia-azorius-renegade), [Meddling Mage](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/210/meddling-mage) or [Drannith Magistrate](https://scryfall.com/card/iko/11/drannith-magistrate). Against Yawgmoth it can also sweep away [Grist, the Hunger Tide](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/202/grist-the-hunger-tide), [Delighted Halfling](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/158/delighted-halfling) and [Soulless Jailer](https://scryfall.com/card/one/241/soulless-jailer).

![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/e/b/eb0e0404-4846-4891-acfa-bd0951ecf9c6.jpg?1626097375)

The four [Endurance](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/157/endurance) are against the deck's natural predator, Living End, and for when you feel the game is dragging on and you need more Rhinos shuffled back in.

![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/7/c/7c9bd75c-9606-4607-bfa6-d6acdee12820.jpg?1619397276)

[Magus of the Moon](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/175/magus-of-the-moon) is just one copy, there to scare opponents when they look at the Open List; I didn't think there would be many Green Tron decks this time. I chose the creature Magus of the Moon rather than [Blood Moon](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/118/blood-moon) because, first, removal is very strong now, [Force of Vigor](https://scryfall.com/card/mh1/164/force-of-vigor) is impossible to guard against, and [Boseiju, Who Endures](https://scryfall.com/card/neo/266/boseiju-who-endures) can't stop it either; second, he is a Wizard and can go with [Flame of Anor](https://scryfall.com/card/ltr/406/flame-of-anor).

![](https://cards.scryfall.io/large/front/a/3/a32dbdc6-3321-4d77-8d2f-acbcb1a29090.jpg?1682720438)

Last is [Kogla and Yidaro](https://scryfall.com/card/mom/311/kogla-and-yidaro), which many people asked about. I first saw this card in Amulet Titan sideboards. I think the idea is similar to some Amulet decks putting [Trumpeting Carnosaur](https://scryfall.com/card/lci/171/trumpeting-carnosaur) in the sideboard: using a creature's ability to get around counterspells to deal with artifact // enchantment haters like [Blood Moon](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/118/blood-moon) or [Chalice of the Void](https://scryfall.com/card/a25/222/chalice-of-the-void). And the two abilities of hard-cast Kogla when it enters cover quite a lot, so I play one as a universal sideboard card.

- The fight from hard-casting it at six mana can deal with haters like [Dauthi Voidwalker](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/81/dauthi-voidwalker), [Soulless Jailer](https://scryfall.com/card/one/241/soulless-jailer), [Lavinia, Azorius Renegade](https://scryfall.com/card/rvr/195/lavinia-azorius-renegade), [Drannith Magistrate](https://scryfall.com/card/iko/11/drannith-magistrate), [Meddling Mage](https://scryfall.com/card/2xm/210/meddling-mage), or [Magus of the Moon](https://scryfall.com/card/tsr/175/magus-of-the-moon);
- And when undisturbed, it can even attack with haste to take out [Teferi, Time Raveler](https://scryfall.com/card/war/221/teferi-time-raveler), or kill an opponent with a low life total in one hit.

#### Sideboarding Strategy

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/Modern-Temur-Rhinos-LD/9f681f555593.webp)

Next is my sideboarding strategy. The picture above is one of my sideboard notes. With the internet being so developed now, you can find guide videos or articles for every deck, so I went through the strategies of all the major decks against Rhinos. For this I prepared strategies for sixteen decks, knowing what they are thinking, roughly which cards they will take out, and whether the cards I bring in are still effective.

With the sideboard strategy well prepared, during the tournament you can tell from the Open List what they will bring in, and you can guess fairly accurately what they will take out; you can even infer from the cards they kept that they probably aren't familiar with the matchup. I also read the Complete Guides for Amulet and Yawgmoth twice, so I roughly know what to watch for in different situations. In short, the more time you spend studying your opponents before the tournament, the less panicked you'll be when you meet them.

## Conclusion

Some people say that what Taiwanese players do best is copy a Top 8 deck and then ruin it with their own tinkering before they're willing to take it to a tournament 😆

I don't think so. In a national team qualifier Standard event I once played [Basilisk Collar](https://scryfall.com/card/wwk/122/basilisk-collar) in Jund, going 6-0 in the constructed rounds; and in White-Blue Spirits I played [Loxodon Warhammer](https://scryfall.com/card/mrd/201/loxodon-warhammer) and beat up a bunch of mirror matches. Spending more time testing and fine-tuning a deck is definitely better than copying a deck while only half understanding it.

I got into Magic in high school, and twenty years have gone by like one day. I've played in plenty of the old national team qualifiers, but I never imagined I'd win a championship during the busiest time of life with three little ones. I've taken this Rhino deck to tournaments only three times, which is truly very lucky. I hope to keep winning glory for Taiwan at Worlds!
