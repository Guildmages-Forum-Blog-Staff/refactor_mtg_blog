### Top-Down Design

> This content mainly draws on the [TDM](https://mtg.fandom.com/wiki/Tarkir:_Dragonstorm) entry on the MTG Wiki, with its structure adjusted to better fit each clan's representative traits.

"Top-down" design means set design led by the setting and flavor. I learned this concept from the official article [Nuts & Bolts #10: Creative Elements](https://magic.wizards.com/zh-Hant/news/making-magic/nuts-bolts-10-creative-elements-2018-03-26). Why do we need to understand top-down design? Because it is the fastest shortcut to understanding the themes and structure of a Limited format! And I think TDM's design this time is quite representative.

From the three-color Spirit Dragons at mythic rare and rare, we can imagine the strongest possible form of each clan in Limited; from the fixing creatures at common and the clan monuments at uncommon, we can see how these basic cards serve each clan to make sure the clan mechanics run smoothly. Running through all of it are the clansfolk living in the Tarkir timeline, their leaders, and the legends of past eras (linking to the story of the 2015 Tarkir trilogy), as well as the Stormborn creatures born from the Dragonstorm.

## Seeded Booster Returns — The Limited-Friendly Design of the TDM Prerelease Box

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/TDM-multicolors/da089680a3ba.webp)

Playing Limited in a three-color environment is relatively difficult, but fortunately this time the TDM prerelease kit has a special design that basically locks you into a three-color combination as soon as you choose a theme. Each prerelease box includes the following:

- 5 regular TDM packs (Play Boosters)
- **1 clan-themed TDM Seeded Booster, containing 14 cards**
- 1 prerelease foil
- 1 cardboard deck box
- 1 twenty-sided die corresponding to the five TDM clans

The Seeded Booster is not a new design; it was already used in Return to Ravnica (RTR) in 2012 and Khans of Tarkir (KTK) in 2014, and its return this time is to lower the difficulty of building decks in the three-color Limited environment.

> Note: Interested readers can further read this article: [WotC Seeds a Volatile Old Trick into Tarkir: Dragonstorm Prerelease Kits](https://draftsim.com/tarkir-dragonstorm-seeded-prerelease-kits/)

## Rating Scale

5.0: An unconditional must-pick, bringing a near-winning advantage
4.5: An unconditional must-pick if you're in the right colors, bringing a big advantage
4.0: Has first-pick value, has excellent synergy with the color combination's theme, and can be the deciding card for going multicolor / switching colors or entering the color combination
3.5: The set's top U and C cards and other cards of sufficient power, with good synergy with the color combination's theme, and can be a key card for entering the color combination
3.0: An important card for the color combination's theme, and other above-average cards that you'd want in your final deck
2.5: Average, usually a decent curve filler
2.0 to 1.0: Below average, generally leaning toward not playing; can be used as filler when necessary but not too many
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable, basically don't play it, since playing it only helps you lose

### Five-Color Spell — Summoning the Spirit Dragons

<!---174: Dragon-soul indestructible enchantment--->

However, I don't think it is a useless 0.0 card, because of its effect. Giving dragons indestructible is actually a very strong effect in this Limited format. Your dragons can unilaterally dominate the air, and that's not all: at the beginning of your upkeep it can make your indestructible dragon pack bigger. Think about this scenario: you control a three-color dragon that has indestructible because of this enchantment, and each upkeep you can put 3 more +1/+1 counters on it!

Small conclusion: This is not a card worth taking early, but in a dragon-themed deck where you have a chance to cast it, once it resolves you may gain a huge advantage from it. It's a card that is slow but wins. Of course, if you can't assemble five colors, don't consider it.

## Conclusion

More than a decade ago we saw the dragonless [Khans of Tarkir](https://scryfall.com/sets/ktk) wage war on one another; and in the story of [Fate Reforged](https://scryfall.com/sets/frf/), Sarkhan traveled a thousand years into the past to save Ugin from his fated death, turning the Tarkir timeline into an era ruled by the Dragonlords. The khans declined, the names of the old five clans were even forbidden, replaced by names bearing the two-color Dragonlords: [Dromoka](https://scryfall.com/card/dtk/217/dragonlord-dromoka), [Ojutai](https://scryfall.com/card/dtk/219/dragonlord-ojutai), [Silumgar](https://scryfall.com/card/dtk/220/dragonlord-silumgar), [Kolaghan](https://scryfall.com/card/dtk/218/dragonlord-kolaghan), and [Atarka](https://scryfall.com/card/dtk/216/dragonlord-atarka).

And now, returning to Tarkir, things are the same but the people are different. The clan leaders and the Spirit Dragons born from the new Stormborn ritual joined forces to defeat the five Dragonlords and ended the era of [Dragons of Tarkir](https://scryfall.com/sets/dtk). Sarkhan and Narset, once the core figures of the story, were both stripped of their sparks after the New Phyrexia invasion, and Ugin and Nicol Bolas were both released from the meditative realm that had imprisoned each other since [War of the Spark](https://scryfall.com/sets/war); a new conspiracy will unfold across the Multiverse.

For me, the Tarkir timeline is a special timeline with a real "sense of history." Not only does its storyline span a thousand years and two world lines, witnessing the rise and fall of dragons and humans, but its setting also passes through the features of ancient China (Jeskai), Siberia (Temur), Mongolia (Mardu), West Asia (Abzan), and the confluence of Southeast Asia (Sultai), blended into a historical conflict reenactment rich in the flavors of Asia and ceaselessly fighting among themselves.

Although Wizards clearly favors some products over others in marketing resources, as a returning timeline, whether in its tribute to the old Tarkir, the care put into the design of the three-color Limited environment, or the development potential of its cards in constructed, [TDM](https://scryfall.com/sets/tdm) will be one of the few great works in recent years! I believe that for upcoming constructed play, and for players who want to experience three-color Limited, TDM will be a series well worth coming back to enjoy!
