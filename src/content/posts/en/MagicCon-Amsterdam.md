## Before I Start

I've always had a longing for big events like MagicCon, always hoping that one day I could be there in person and see for myself how overseas players shape the MTG community and put on such large events.

> In 2023 I also wrote a simple guide for MagicCon: Las Vegas. If you don't mind it being a bit dated, you can take a look: [Article link](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1HHCvjgeHLfXNw0XOmtNg3gxc2vtEYZqo2Gh1__ptZLY/edit?usp=sharing)

Around Lunar New Year this year, I saw that MagicCon: Amsterdam had a [New Perspectives Grant application](https://wpn.wizards.com/zh-Hant/news/about-the-2024-new-perspectives-grant-helping-players-reach-magiccon), so I decided to apply and see if I had a chance. Even though there were only 10 spots, I applied without much hope. On March 1 I received a notice that it was still under consideration, but on March 16 I received the thank-you card below anyway.

> If you're interested, you can also look at the [content](https://forms.office.com/Pages/ResponsePage.aspx?id=Pt0ecKjHiUexzookNiDWjyM9dwf3-hpEsCTCctrXY_xUMFpSM1ROMkdDV0VaRVcwOUJQTVkyNFBBNy4u) of the 2024 application.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/b34ac307f019.webp)

The moment I received the thank-you letter, a voice in my heart suddenly said I should still make this trip. Over the following week I booked my flight to Amsterdam, my lodging, and my MagicCon entry badge. After that, all that was left was to try for a chance at qualifying as a PT player through MIT#5 or #6, but unfortunately I didn't make it in either.

> You can read my MIT#6 preparation and tournament report here: [豬鼻子's MIT#6 Preparation and Tournament Report: Sultai Midrange](https://guildmagesforum.tw/en/MIT-6-Review/)

## 20240627 (Thu) Day 0

My flight left Taiwan at 11:55pm on the night of June 26. I could barely sleep during the 13-hour flight, and arrived in Amsterdam at around 8am. After taking the train to Amsterdam Centraal Station, I headed straight to the hotel where I'd be staying that night, found a locker to drop my large luggage, and began sightseeing in the city. Since it was my first time there and I didn't want to spend €19 renting a bicycle, I walked to the venue, RAI Amsterdam, to pick up my badge.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/814ef47c72d7.webp)

Weekend badge get!

Amsterdam has a great many canals, and the bikes on the road go pretty fast. As I walked, it got hotter and hotter toward noon, so I bought a few things at a supermarket and had a picnic by the roadside. Then, since I'd walked so much and it was so hot, I was sucked into the [Heineken Experience](https://www.heinekenexperience.com/nl/) by the road. I came out slightly tipsy after two glasses of draft Heineken, picked up the souvenirs I needed to buy on the way, and finally walked back to Amsterdam Centraal Station. By the time I took the train back to the hotel, washed up, and went to sleep, it was past 8pm, and the sky still wasn't dark, which felt super weird XD

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/8aa17c4ff24d.webp)

A view of an Amsterdam canal, with lots of tour boats on it and many people rowing. Apart from the water probably being cleaner, this scenery is actually quite similar to Kaohsiung's Love River.

This photo was taken at 6:30pm while I was walking to buy dinner. I'd walked too much in Amsterdam today and my back pain got worse. Traveling alone and finding everything expensive made me not feel like eating much. Even an ordinary meal was €18 to €25. After looking around, everything seemed poor value for money, so in the end I decided on Domino's. On the walk back to the hotel with the pizza, it gradually got cold as night fell, and I suddenly really wanted to go back to Taiwan and get a good sleep.

My impression after the first day in Amsterdam: this is a city I'd recommend visiting with friends. If four of you could come together and stay in a hostel, draft or practice together at night, order a pizza and share it, it would be a lot more fun than coming alone. For someone like me who finds museums and art galleries too expensive to enter and isn't interested in nightlife, the red-light district, or smoking weed, it was actually pretty boring. All I could do was look forward to tomorrow's MagicCon making me feel the trip was worth it!

### Trading

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/a56bb1e7790c.webp)

Many card shops had set up booths at the venue, and the trading atmosphere was quite lively. Each shop displayed its buylist, and you could also ask the staff directly for an appraisal. In the end I handed the cards I'd brought to the Italian card shop [CardGame Corner](https://www.cardgamecorner.com/en/) for appraisal and sold them to them. The clerk who appraised them was very friendly, and it was a very pleasant trading experience. After the appraisal, the clerk gives you a quote sheet or check-like paper like the one in the photo. Once you agree, both sides sign on it, and then you take it to another group of staff to collect your money.

### Artist Signatures

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/2063603bc77b.webp)

More than 40 artists were at the venue, and each artist's signature costs about €3 to €5. Double and Shadow mean roughly the same thing: basically adding a shadow or signing twice, and naturally the price is higher too. The photo shows Magali Villeneuve's price list at this event. I passed by several times and there were always tons of people, and over the next two days I also went around to look many times, but gave up each time because of the crowds. On the last day, they even put a sign next to the booth saying you'd have to wait at least 3 hours, to discourage players. Worthy of a famous artist...

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/fb9f8d31c791.webp)

The one above is the signing price list of another famous artist, John Avon. What's special is that John's signing is limited to a quota of 10 signatures per person per visit, and if you want a Shadow signature it uses up 2 of the quota at once. He himself was a bit troubled, saying he'd written this so clearly, yet many people still didn't understand what he meant.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/298e051a3d70.webp)

And John himself was very nice. He agreed to let me take this photo while he was signing, and others were filming too. In short, very easygoing. But you could feel that this kind of signing event is still quite a burden on him. Besides taking regular breaks, during the signing you could see him often stretching to relax.

> If you're a fan of his, all I can say is: sign while you can, and cherish it.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/1bfc12b39759.webp)

After signing, he puts your cards on a kraft paper bag, and you carry the bag with your cards off to the side like a customer picking up a meal, waiting for the ink to dry. John has probably seen quite a few tragedies where customers didn't wait for the ink to dry after signing. Besides the written notice, he personally told every customer to please wait at least five minutes for the signature to dry XD

### Trying My Hand: MH3 Swiss Draft

Since I didn't know what to do back at the hotel anyway, I figured I might as well stay at the venue and play a draft! The entry fee is €35. You first go to the registration counter to buy a €5 voucher, then find a table, and once eight players have gathered, a judge comes over to collect the vouchers and the draft begins.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/91ef98d97dee.webp)

The draft picks, from top to bottom and left to right, are packs 1 to 3 and picks 1 to 14 in order. I ended up with a not-so-great black-white deck, and didn't get any of the most important removal.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/d939d2b4557b.webp)

My final built deck is above.

The draft itself was pretty peaceful. A 13-year-old kid sat two seats down from me, and in my third pack I opened [Eladamri, Korvecdal](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/149/eladamri-korvecdal), which I couldn't use, so I passed it to him.

In R1 my opponent was a Swede playing red-black sacrifice. Under the guard of my countless little fliers and lifelink creatures, he couldn't get through at all, and he never drew any of his removal, really sorry about that. 2-0.

In R2 my opponent was a German who had drafted a very complete red-green Eldrazi deck. He looked like an experienced player, and he'd also be playing in Day 2's PTQ. 2-1.

In R3 my opponent was that 13-year-old kid, who is American but lives in Germany, with his dad cheering him on from the side. He had drafted blue-green Ramp, and I had no ability to resist under the tyranny of his [Springheart Nantuko](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/171/springheart-nantuko) copying a pile of big Reach creatures. 0-2.

After playing, you go sign a yellow match slip and get your respective prize tix. With three wins you sign your name in the "Undefeated" column. The kid held the match slip like a certificate and had his dad take a photo, and he even asked the staff, quite innocently, whether three wins got an extra prize. The staff said no at first, but seeing how disappointed he was, they left for a while, and when they came back they gave him a piece of candy XDD

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/b7779d055f7b.webp)

You get 1,200 points of prize tix for each win and 400 for a loss. After playing, my 2-1 record earned me 2,800 points. I gave 200 points to the Swedish player from round one so he could exchange for 3 packs of MH3, and with the remaining 2,600 points I exchanged for 2 packs of MH3 and 1 Lord of the Rings draft pack.

- 1,000 tix: MH3 Play Booster

## 20240629 (Sat) Day 2

At 6am I was again woken by my roommate's snoring. I got up, ate breakfast as usual, and got ready to go to the venue for the main event of this trip, the Pro Tour Qualifier, an 8-round MH3 Sealed format.

The PTQ started at 11:30am with 570 players. Judges came over to hand out boosters, six MH3 boosters wrapped in a yellow zip-lock bag. Sitting across from me was a girl from the Czech Republic who seemed to be playing her first competitive-level Sealed event, so I told her a bit about how things work. But she opened really well, with [Ulamog, the Defiler](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/15/ulamog-the-defiler) and [Six](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/169/six)...

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/cdbc9de9eaf4.webp)

After opening, I thought the pool was awful. The black-white removal was great but the creatures were terrible, yet I had no choice but to main-deck black-white. At least the removal kept up, and I could hold my own a bit against all the Eldrazi.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/06017c0506f4.webp)

After sideboarding, depending on tempo or being on the play, in a few games I'd switch to red-green Eldrazi, but I kept getting stuck on three lands, and after getting stuck again in R5, I stopped using it.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/055028905624.webp)

The rest are the cards I didn't use. Two cards didn't get photographed, namely [Genku, Future Shaper](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/186/genku-future-shaper) and [Nadier's Nightblade](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/275/nadiers-nightblade).

After finishing this day I reviewed my deckbuilding a bit. In terms of overall power, building Temur Eldrazi might have been better, but the blue mana-fixing in the pool was a big problem. Otherwise I'd have splashed blue in my black-white deck for [Genku, Future Shaper](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/186/genku-future-shaper).

### R6 Opponent No Show

After reporting the result I saw there was still more than half an hour left, so I decided to have dinner at the ramen shop across the street from the venue. My takeaway after eating: don't eat ramen in Europe, but the grilled skewers at the ramen shop were okay.

### R7 Mardu Resonance 2-1

After eating ramen I still made it back in time for R7! Yay! Both sides were competing over who could kill more, and the one who did would win, so I won XD Just kidding. Mainly the synergy of the opposing deck wasn't very good, and once a few main creatures were removed the pressure became small. Although the main reason I could win was that I had more removal and drew it all, the opponent's deck was built rather loosely, and the late-game threats weren't big, which let me keep widening my advantage. That was also a major point.

### R8 Boros Energy Mixed with Eldrazi 2-1

The opponent had a ton of removal, but in game one he got value from [Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/40/phelia-exuberant-shepherd). After I learned my lesson, I held my removal and played slowly and won.

In total, 4 wins, 3 losses, and 1 draw. In fact, starting from 1 win, 2 losses, and 1 draw I was already out of the prize range of the top 128, so I was simply there to get more chances to play against overseas players. Overall my performance in this PTQ wasn't ideal, but that's just how it goes.

## Dealing Damage: Trip Expense Subtotal

Since I made this trip, some of you might be curious about roughly how much it cost, so let's calculate the damage here!

> The following doesn't include money I spent on personal souvenirs, cards, or other gifts, and only counts the expenses of the four days in Amsterdam.
> The euro to NT dollar exchange rate is taken as 35:1

### Transportation

The main expense of flying to Europe is the **airfare**. I took a KLM direct flight from Taipei to Amsterdam, bought three months before departure, **costing NT$45,416**. However, when buying the ticket I chose a specific seat, which added about 1 to 2 thousand, so buying normally could be a bit cheaper.

**Local transportation** was mainly by metro and on foot. Apart from Day 0, each day I basically bought a combined metro and bus ticket for the round trip between the hotel and the venue, once each way, €3.4 each, so €6.8 over 4 days. In addition, there was the €4.9 one-way train from the airport to Amsterdam Central Station, €9.8 for the round trip, **totaling NT$1,295**.

Another thing worth mentioning is Amsterdam's bicycles. The hostel I stayed in also offers rentals. Although I didn't rent one, I can still mention it. The price is €14 for 3 hours, €19 for one day, and €18 per day for two days or more. A one-day rental costs about the same as a normal meal in Amsterdam. If you want to experience the Amsterdam scenery, it's actually worth considering. When crossing the road, besides watching for cars you also need to watch for bikes. Bikes have dedicated lanes, usually painted brick red, so be especially careful when crossing. The bikes here, much like scooters in Taiwan, mostly don't yield XDD.

### Lodging

For lodging I stayed in a hostel called [Meininger Hotel](https://book.meininger-hotels.com/reservations/rooms), a four-person room. Three nights, including the 12.5% city tax, came to €240, **totaling NT$8,400**. I did add the €11.9 daily breakfast, though. A full breakfast means no worries during the tournament. At first glance this price looks expensive, but Amsterdam is just this expensive...

Also, because I'd be away from the hotel for long periods for the tournament, I **rented a locker** every day, €6 per day, €24 for 4 days, **totaling NT$840**.

For **internet**, I signed up for Taiwan Mobile roaming, **NT$699 for 7 days**.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/MagicCon-Amsterdam/14857e6601b4.webp)
Here's a picture of the four-person room. The Wi-Fi was free and fast enough!

### Food

Since I ate breakfast at the hotel, I ate relatively cheaply over the day. I also don't know much about Dutch food, and a random meal at €18 to €25 was something I really couldn't stomach, so I mostly bought simple things at the local supermarket Albert Heijn and occasionally had Domino's, with a 9" pepperoni pizza costing around €8. But by the last two days I couldn't stand it anymore, so I went to the ramen shop across from the venue. The total for meals over the four days was €96, **totaling NT$3,360**. But I must say I really ate quite cheaply these four days. A normal person coming for four days would probably spend at least 1.5 times my food expense.

## Q&A with Myself

This last section has nothing to do with MagicCon. During my days at the venue, I kept asking myself two questions over and over:

### 1. The Financial Burden of MTG in Europe and America vs. Taiwan

At this MagicCon, an artist signature cost €5 to €10, playing one MH3 Swiss draft was €35, and opening one MH3 Play Booster was €11. But a simple pizza at the venue was at least €15, and a can of Coke €5. Although I was in Amsterdam, the most expensive place, and spending inside the venue is correspondingly higher than at a typical European card shop, playing one MH3 draft is about the cost of a somewhat decent meal for the average European or American player, while in Taiwan it's at least equal to two or three meals. From daily necessities to entertainment, MTG in Taiwan really can't be called an entertainment expense anyone can afford, and this leads to my next question.

### 2. The Mindset of Taiwanese Players Playing MTG

Precisely because MTG being expensive in Taiwan is an established fact, it's understandable for players to want to make their money back. But when behavior stemming from this mindset is reflected in Taiwan's MTG community environment, it pushes the current gaming environment in Taiwan into a market situation that is inward-turning, struggles to absorb new players, and has seriously imbalanced domestic demand. However, this is just the adjustment Taiwanese players make according to their own circumstances in order to keep experiencing this game, but it really is an increasingly serious negative cycle. And it is the complete opposite of the positive community atmosphere, with Europeans and Americans loving this game, that I experienced at this event.

## Conclusion

If you ask me whether paying out of pocket to make a trip to Amsterdam was worth it, I'd tell you I had a rather barren time and the trip wasn't really worth it for me. But if you ask me whether paying out of pocket to attend this MagicCon: Amsterdam was worth it, I'd say it was super worth it!!!

MagicCon is a highly concentrated MTG festival. Whatever you're interested in about MTG, you can find your own reason to linger at this event! Of course, if you get the chance, coming in a group with a few friends would surely be even more fun. After playing and taking part in events all day, you can chat and discuss freely with friends, maybe have a beer, and the day's fatigue or any frustrations from the tournament can always vanish!

Lastly, factors like per capita income, values, and habits can't be changed overnight, but mindset can instantly change your view of the world and your actions. I hope more players begin to hold a relatively positive attitude toward facing this game environment and influencing the people around you. Only then does change have a chance to happen.
