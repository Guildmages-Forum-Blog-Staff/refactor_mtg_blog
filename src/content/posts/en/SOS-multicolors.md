## Archetype Themes

![SOS draft five-college card art](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SOS-multicolors/aef6ef1d9d56.webp)

### <span style="color:#C3B270">White</span>-<span style="color:#808080">Black</span> Silverquill College

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SOS-multicolors/eebec879f98f.webp)

Signature mechanic: **Repartee**

Repartee triggers when you cast an instant or sorcery that targets a creature, so in Limited there are at least two trigger situations:
1. When you use removal / a tapping effect and **target a creature an opponent controls**, creatures with Repartee abilities trigger;
2. When you use a beneficial spell and **target a creature you control**, creatures with Repartee abilities trigger.

So our game plan is very clear. Use the "Prepared" state you get after casting a creature with {% mtglink "Abigale, Poet Laureate" %} to produce a copy spell that can place counters, gaining multiple benefits: widening the board, growing your creatures, and frequently triggering Repartee. As for single-card synergies, the white-black common {% mtglink "Inkling Mascot" %} gains flying and surveil 1 through Repartee, which makes your later draws better; the uncommon {% mtglink "Killian's Confidence" %} amplifies the snowball value of this archetype and has potential for reuse. If you need a stable, low-rarity trigger source, the white {% mtglink "Dig Site Inventory" %}'s flashback ability lets it count as two spells, while the black {% mtglink "Scathing Shadelock" %} reliably produces copies, making sure Repartee abilities are maximized every turn.

All of the scenarios above are the Repartee mode that benefits your own side. On the other hand, you can also hold a pile of removal and use creatures with powerful Repartee effects to out-argue your opponent into speechlessness!

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SOS-multicolors/3fbd856ac663.webp)

### <span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span>-<span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span> Prismari College

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SOS-multicolors/1e691010ebba.webp)

Signature mechanic: **Opus**

So how do we reliably get the maximum payoff from the Opus mechanic? First, let's find some clues from the two-color uncommon legend. {% mtglink "Sanar, Unfinished Genius" %} can tap to make a Treasure when you trigger Opus, which helps you cast high-cost instants/sorceries. Also, his Prepared copy spell costs five, which also helps you fully unlock Opus payoffs. The instants/sorceries you find continue the cycle of triggering Opus + making Treasures. The common {% mtglink "Elemental Mascot" %}'s Opus payoff, besides increasing its power, gives an extra reward if you trigger Opus by casting a spell with five mana. Another card, {% mtglink "Abstract Paintmage" %}, provides two extra mana, making it easier to cast high-cost instants/sorceries. There are also quite a few creatures with Prepare effects like Sanar; a Prepared creature with its own five-mana spell, like {% mtglink "Campus Composer" %}, can come down as a curve creature first, and if you have room, you can then cast a copy of the Prepare spell. Finally, spells with an X cost, like {% mtglink "Fractalize" %}, can also serve as flexible Opus trigger sources, so don't forget to pick them up early when building a blue-red deck.

But don't forget that the five mono-colored Lesson sorceries with Paradigm, after the first cast, only trigger the base effect of Opus on their next free copy, and don't satisfy the extra condition of being cast with five or more mana.

### <span style="color:#808080">Black</span>-<span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span> Witherbloom College 

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SOS-multicolors/7ad9b185b1dc.webp)

Signature mechanic: **Infusion, i.e., having gained life this turn**

{% mtglink "Lluwen, Exchange Student" %} is an exchange student from the plane of Lorwyn. Besides repeatedly exiling creature cards from your graveyard to get Prepared again and again, the 1/1 Pest tokens it makes not only fill the board but, through the life gain they provide when attacking, let the later Infusion cards deliver their full benefit. For the common I picked {% mtglink "Bogwater Lumaret" %} rather than {% mtglink "Pest Mascot" %}, because this card is simply too important in black-green: not only itself, but any creature entering the battlefield gains you life, and the gap between the upper and lower limits of your Infusion cards in black-green decks with or without it is huge! Another card, {% mtglink "Essenceknit Scholar" %}, makes a Pest when it enters, and when those Pests die after attacking you also draw an extra card. Among the Infusion cards, {% mtglink "Poisoner's Apprentice" %} is just a 3-mana 2/2 when it enters on its own, but if it enters while Infused, it becomes an efficient removal spell. This shows how important life gain is in black-green. And with {% mtglink "Thornfist Striker" %}, your creatures' combat strength differs enormously whenever you are Infused, and this can even happen on blocks!

> Infusion is a checking effect: it only looks at whether you gained life this turn, and if so, the Infusion card's effect resolves in its Infused state, with no extra triggers or use of the stack. Therefore, cards like {% mtglink "Pest Mascot" %} keep the regular rules text of a normal life gain effect.

### <span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span>-<span style="color:#C3B270">White</span> Lorehold College

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SOS-multicolors/7f1939958aaf.webp)

Signature mechanic: **Flashback**

{% mtglink "Kirol, History Buff" %} becomes Prepared whenever cards leave your graveyard, and the Prepare spell copy lets you mill + strengthen your creatures. The common {% mtglink "Spirit Mascot" %} simply grows whenever cards leave your graveyard, simple and brutal. Another flashback spell, {% mtglink "Molten Note" %}, is a reliable removal spell by itself, and flashback triggers a graveyard-leaving effect, letting you earn extra resources from flashback cards in your graveyard in the very late game; two for the price of one is great. Among the red-white commons, the red {% mtglink "Rubble Rouser" %} and the white {% mtglink "Stone Docent" %} both have the ability to proactively remove cards from the graveyard, and bringing a few makes your red-white deck run more smoothly!

> For ease of description, we at the Forum use "**graveyard-leave**" here to refer to the effects of "When/Whenever one or more cards leave your graveyard..." Please note this is not an officially used keyword ability.
> 
> All of the following situations trigger "graveyard-leave" effects:
> - Exiling from the graveyard: exiling a target card from a target graveyard / your graveyard.
> - Returning to hand: returning a target card from a target graveyard / your graveyard to your hand.
> - Reanimating: returning a target creature/permanent card from a target graveyard / your graveyard to the battlefield.
> - If {% mtglink "Ark of Hunger" %} is on the battlefield, when we tap it to mill a card and use that card this turn, that also triggers graveyard-leave.
> 
> It seems Wizards went to considerable trouble to give Lorehold students some history to study, but if such a long sentence could be consolidated into a single keyword, that would be even better.

### <span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span>-<span style="color:#006DB3">Blue</span> Quandrix College

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SOS-multicolors/ce6edcb3f720.webp)

Signature mechanic: **Increment**

To understand more deeply what green-blue is about, we should go back to the two-color uncommon legend {% mtglink "Tam, Observant Sequencer" %}. The effect is simple: play a land to trigger Landfall and become Prepared, then cast the Prepare copy spell to draw cards (the life gain is incidental). This implies two other messages: ramp, and that drawing cards will bring new finishers. Another card, {% mtglink "Paradox Surveyor" %}, mainly supports the ramp side, whether you fetch a land to keep ramping or, with enough lands, fetch an X-cost spell like {% mtglink "Pterafractyl" %} to end the game.

Before explaining the outlet for card draw, let's look at another dimension: counters (most commonly +1/+1 counters, which we at the Forum often call "beans"). Not only does Increment place beans; cards like {% mtglink "Growth Curve" %} can also place lots of beans, and cards like {% mtglink "Fractal Tender" %} have both: you can cast a high-cost spell to trigger its Increment, or actively place beans on it, or even, getting a bit wicked, use cards like {% mtglink "Fractal Mascot" %} to put stun counters on it! And once you can keep placing beans on it, your Fractal tokens will soon crush the opponent.

Finally, drawing cards is great, sure, but besides making the opponent collapse on their own with a huge card advantage, are there other ways to end the game after drawing a pile of cards?

{% mtgcard "Fractal Anomaly" %}

This is the one: the more cards you draw, the better this card's effect. Of course, be careful not to draw your library dry first! This set has no effects that shuffle the graveyard back into the library!

### The Savior of Sealed — Converge

Have you ever opened a <span style="color:#808080">Black</span>-<span style="color:#4A8A3D">Green</span> prerelease box in Limited but all your other packs were <span style="color:#AF2321">Red</span>-<span style="color:#C3B270">White</span>? In other sets, you might be forced to play three or more colors with an unstable mana base, praying for a start with a normal, functional curve. In SOS Limited, the situation above can still happen, but the existence of Converge cards means that even if your colors are wrong, you can still put up a decent, above-average fight.

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SOS-multicolors/ad8fc06cff58.webp)

In two-color decks, Converge cards only deliver their base floor, with roughly filler-level power or worse; but in decks of three or more colors, each additional color when casting a Converge spell noticeably boosts its power!

![](https://images.guildmagesforum.tw/posts/SOS-multicolors/5ebe567798d0.webp)

Counting the Mystical Archive (SOA) this time, there are 5 colored Converge instants/sorceries in total. When your sealed pool is a wreck, don't forget to look their way; maybe you'll find new possibilities!

## Rating Scale

5.0: Unconditional first pick, brings an almost game-winning advantage
4.5: Unconditional first pick if in the right colors, brings a major advantage
4.0: Worth a first pick, excellent synergy with the archetype theme, can be the deciding card for splashing / switching colors or entering the archetype
3.5: Top uncommon, common, or other sufficiently strong card with good synergy with the archetype theme, can be a key card for entering the archetype
3.0: Important card for the archetype theme, and other above-average cards, which you'll want in your final deck
2.5: Average, usually a decent curve filler
2.0 to 1.0: Below average, basically you'd rather not play it; can be used as filler when necessary but not too many
0.5 to 0.0: Unplayable, basically never play it; it will only help you lose

## Conclusion

Locking into five colors in drafts is becoming more and more common; I hope this isn't a sign of Wizards getting lazy with Limited XD Returning to Strixhaven, I strongly feel that card power has grown explosively over these four or five years. Is that a good thing? I'm not sure. But even so, Limited's variety is still fun, and every two-color theme surely still has many undiscovered tactics and game plans, so let's keep experiencing them!
